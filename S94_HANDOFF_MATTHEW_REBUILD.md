## §B. Three-tier architecture — corrected at S94 close

**The prior handoffs' tier-naming was wrong, and Yoshi caught it. The corrected model is three-tiered, not two-tiered. This section is the verbal-answers-transcribe of the late-S94 conversation that settled it. Every prior handoff that called matthew-N-short.md "the $4.99 layer" and matthew-N.md "the $9.99 layer" was missing the existence of the free tier entirely — which IS the live-rendered look at bible.remnantofpromise.org today.**

### Tier 1 — FREE (New Testament commentary, all-canon cross-references)

**Commentary substance per chapter.** A paragraph or two of running prose under the chapter — the shape of the 1 Corinthians 11 commentary currently rendered live on the site (rendering-rationale + framework-substance compressed: *why this English departs from the standard, lexical/idiomatic decisions, the contextual key.*) The Matthew free-tier paragraph(s) can follow the same shape — both rationale (where the rendering carries decisions like *son of Adam* over *son of man,* or *Yahusha* over *Jesus,* or *Yashar'el* over *Israel*) and substance (the chapter's framework move in two or three sentences). Roughly 250-400 words per chapter. Easy compression work from the existing matthew-N-short.md long-form for each chapter that has the short-form authored.

**Cross-reference scope.** All canonical scripture — Tanakh AND New Testament — clickable and navigable. A free user reading Matthew 12:8 and seeing the Genesis 2:3 cross-reference clicks the link and lands on Genesis 2. Same for the Mark 2:27 link, the Hebrews 4:9 link, the Isaiah 42 link, the Deuteronomy 23:25 link, all of it. Free tier sees and clicks every canonical cross-reference in the apparatus. The restored library (Apocrypha, Pseudepigrapha, 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Jasher, Adam-Eve Conflict, Apocalypse of Abraham, Cepher additions, Sonnini's *Acts of Paul,* etc.) is OUT of the free tier's scope — those cross-references either don't show or paywall on click.

**Free-tier panel shape (currently live).** Per chapter:
1. The chapter text (canonical scripture in the restored rendering).
2. A divider.
3. **COMMENTARY** sub-header with the paragraph-or-two running prose.
4. **TANAKH SOURCES FOR [chapter]** panel — per-verse cross-references with each target scripture fully quoted in italics, clickable arrows to navigate. (Currently rendering with snippet-truncation. **The snippet truncation must be fixed before launch** — see §D.)
5. **Thread cards** — each thread card with title, anchor verse range, framework paragraph, *Show more* link, and the per-verse member rows under it. (Currently rendering with a **MEMBERS IN THIS CHAPTER** sub-header on each card. **That sub-header must be retired before launch** — see §D.)

### Tier 2 — $4.99 (longer commentary, all-texts cross-references)

**Commentary substance per chapter.** The running-prose long-form commentary I have been building as `matthew-N-short.md` — 5,000-6,500 words per chapter, walking each framework moment with every load-bearing verse quoted in stand-alone italics. The come-and-see strict-read deliverable. Already built for matthew-1 through matthew-12.

**Cross-reference scope.** All texts — canonical scripture plus the full restored library. A $4.99 user clicks a 1 Enoch cross-reference and navigates to 1 Enoch; clicks a Jubilees cross-reference and lands on Jubilees; clicks a Jasher reference and lands on Jasher. Everything in the bidirectional scripture-level library is reachable. Per the voice skill's locked architecture in DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md §17: scripture-level texts are bidirectional, historical-witness texts (Josephus, Apostolic Fathers, M.R. James's apocryphal-NT collection) are deferred from V1 entirely and not in the $4.99 pool either.

### Tier 3 — $9.99 (DEFERRED — not user-facing at launch)

**Substance.** The audited sectioned long-form commentary I have been building as `matthew-N.md` — body §1-§N + cross-reference apparatus block at §N+1 with the staging-note pattern. Already built for matthew-1 through matthew-12.

**Critical clarification — matthew-N.md is doing double duty.** The file serves two purposes: (a) authoring-source for the cross-reference apparatus that gets ingested into the database for `cross_references`, `cross_reference_threads`, and `cross_reference_thread_members` (needed for EVERY tier because cross-references render at every tier), and (b) potential user-facing $9.99 paid commentary view. We defer (b) without losing (a). The apparatus block is the source-of-truth for the cross-reference data regardless of whether the audited body is ever rendered to users as a $9.99 product.

**Forward implication.** matthew-N.md authoring continues across S95+ as the database-source for cross-references. The user-facing $9.99 commentary tier ships later — once more of the NT is rebuilt and the $9.99 product has substantive corpus to justify the price-point. Until then the matthew-N.md files are infrastructure; the matthew-N-short.md files are the paid commentary substance shipping at the $4.99 tier.

### Cross-reference data — engineering-side tier-tagging

The apparatus tables in `matthew-N.md` files list cross-references by row (Source verse → Target verse → Member note) without a tier column. Engineering-side tagging is the clean approach: the ingestion script for `cross_references` and `cross_reference_thread_members` infers the tier from the target book (Tanakh book → free; NT book → free; restored-library book → $4.99). No author-side change needed; the existing apparatus blocks work as-is. `canon.json` already knows the canonical-status of each book, so the script can read it.

Edge case to watch: any cross-reference that points to a multi-canon range (rare but possible) — the ingestion script picks the most-restrictive tier the range hits. A row pointing to *Genesis 6:1-4 and 1 Enoch 6-7 together* would be $4.99-gated because the 1 Enoch portion lives in the restored library.

## §C. UX patterns landed at S94

**Cross-reference link interaction.** Locked: **short-tap = navigate** to the cross-referenced chapter; **long-press = action menu** with highlight / bookmark / note / more options. Keeps the read-and-explore navigation flow fast (the dominant use-case) and surfaces the deeper actions only when the user actually wants them. The action menu can grow over time without disrupting navigation.

**Highlight / bookmark / note data paths.** All three actions need to attach polymorphically to three different element-types: (a) scripture verses, (b) commentary text (any tier — free paragraph, $4.99 running prose, $9.99 sectioned body), and (c) cross-reference rows. Schema sketch: three tables (`user_highlights`, `user_bookmarks`, `user_notes`) each with a polymorphic foreign-key column pointing at one of `verse_id`, `commentary_element_id`, `cross_reference_row_id`. The long-press action menu surfaces on any clickable element, not just verses, because users will want to highlight commentary and cross-references too.

**Paywall popup mechanic.** Free users clicking out-of-tier cross-references (any restored-library reference) get a paywall popup: *Upgrade to follow this cross-reference into the restored library — the books the Reformation cut, where the framework's deepest moves are anchored.* The exact copy is a UX-and-marketing decision for later; the sales-pitch-in-itself shape is locked. The Reformation-canon-cutting diagnostic the voice skill carries is the substance the upgrade-copy can lean on (in plain descriptive prose, NOT the internal-only canannited vocabulary).

**(2) MEMBERS IN THIS CHAPTER sub-header — RETIRE.** Currently every thread card renders with a *MEMBERS IN THIS CHAPTER* sub-header above the per-verse rows. The fix: drop the sub-header. The per-verse rows render directly under the thread card's framework paragraph and *Show more* link, with a thin divider if visual separation is needed. The word "members" is internal-engineering vocabulary; the reader doesn't need to see it.

Both fixes are visual / rendering / template changes — no schema change, no data migration. They should ship in the next engineering pass before any commentary launch.

## §G. Post-launch major work-stream — Blind-user mode

Yoshi raised the blind-user dedicated experience at S94 close and decided to defer it as a major post-launch work-stream. **This is not a checkbox accessibility item.** It's a parallel UI track that requires substantive engineering and product design. The work is captured here so future sessions don't lose it.

**TTS pronunciation overrides for the full restored-names lexicon.** Standard TTS engines mangle every restored Hebrew name — *Yahuah* will be read *Yah-OO-uh* or *Yahweh,* *Yahusha* will be read various wrong ways, *Yashar'el / Yahudah / Yahudim / Yahudi / Ruach HaKodesh / Melek Tsadiq / El Shaddai / El Elyon / Ehyeh asher Ehyeh* etc. all need pronunciation overrides. A blind user listening to the scripture is getting EVERYTHING through audio; mispronounced restored names hand the same flattening to the blind listener that the printed translators handed the sighted reader. The TTS layer has to carry the third commandment forward — *honor the name with the right sound.* Per the voice skill: *Yahuah* pronounced Ee-Ah-Oo-Ah (four vowel sounds per Josephus *Wars* 5.5.7). The full pronunciation lexicon needs to be built. This is framework-distinctive engineering.

**Parallel UI track.** Linear/hierarchical menus that announce cleanly via screen reader. No visual-only affordances. No gesture-only navigation. Every action reachable via screen-reader gesture or simplified menu. Audio playback for scripture AND for commentary (free paragraph, $4.99 long-form). Cross-references that announce as audio prompts and navigate via simplified controls. Bookmark / highlight / note via simplified voice command or screen-reader-friendly gestures. The "I am blind" mode selector at first launch (with the choice persisted) routes the user into the parallel track instead of the default sighted track.

**Park as roadmap track.** Post-launch priority. Substantial work-stream. Not S95 work; capture for the multi-month roadmap once launch is in the water.
