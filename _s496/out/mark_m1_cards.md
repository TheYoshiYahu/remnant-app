## NEW THREAD `mark-9-the-son-of-adam-must-suffer-and-be-killed-isaiah-53-zechariah-13` — Mark 9:9–32
**The Son of Adam must suffer and be killed — despised, taken from judgment, the shepherd smitten**

It ain't new. Coming down from the mountain with the glory still on them, Yahusha (Jesus) tells the three to hold their tongues until the rising.

*And as they came down from the mountain, he charged them that they should tell no man what things they had seen, till the Son of Adam were risen from the dead.* (Mark 9:9)

What lies between the glory and the rising had been written already, in the picture of a man the people would not own.

*He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not.* (Isaiah 53:3)

Then, passing through Galilee, he stops hinting and teaches the twelve plainly.

*For he taught his disciples, and said unto them, The Son of Adam is delivered into the hands of men, and they shall kill him; and after that he is killed, he shall rise the third day.* (Mark 9:31)

Isaiah has the same road, and says whose transgression stands behind it.

*He was taken from prison and from judgment: and who shall declare his generation? for he was cut off out of the land of the living: for the transgression of my people was he stricken.* (Isaiah 53:8)

Zechariah has the flock's side of it, the shepherd smitten by the sword Yahuah (the LORD) of hosts calls up, and the sheep scattering after.

*Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts): smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones.* (Zechariah 13:7)

So when Mark says the twelve *understood not that saying*, the fault was not that the saying was new. The despised man, the one taken from judgment and cut off from the living, and the shepherd with the scattering flock were already on the page they had been reading all their lives.


## NEW THREAD `mark-12-more-than-all-whole-burnt-offerings-the-scribe-says-what-the-prophets-said` — Mark 12:28–34

## NEW THREAD `mark-13-signs-and-wonders-do-not-vouch-for-the-prophet` — Mark 13:22–22
**Signs and wonders do not vouch for the prophet**

It ain't new. Yahusha (Jesus) warns the four who asked him privately on the Mount of Olives that the deceivers will not come empty-handed:

*For false Christs and false prophets shall rise, and shall shew signs and wonders, to seduce, if it were possible, even the elect.* (Mark 13:22)

Moses had already told Yashar'el (Israel) that a sign proves nothing by being a sign. What matters is where the man who gives it is leading:

*If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder,* (Deuteronomy 13:1)

*And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them;* (Deuteronomy 13:2)

The answer Moses gives is the commandments themselves:

*Ye shall walk after Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God), and fear him, and keep his commandments, and obey his voice, and ye shall serve him, and cleave unto him.* (Deuteronomy 13:4)

So a wonder that comes to pass is no proof. The test is whether the one who works it keeps the people walking in the commandments or draws them away.

John saw wonders of this kind in Revelation, in his own vision:

*And he doeth great wonders, so that he maketh fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men,* (Revelation 13:13)

And Paul, writing to the Thessalonians, says where such power comes from:

*Even him, whose coming is after the working of Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders,* (2 Thessalonians 2:9)

Moses, John and Paul agree with what Yahusha (Jesus) says in Mark: signs and wonders can be seen, and they can still seduce.


## NEW THREAD `mark-13-the-son-of-adam-coming-in-the-clouds` — Mark 13:26–26
**The Son of Adam coming in the clouds**

Yahusha (Jesus) answers the four who asked him privately by naming himself as the one Daniel saw:

*And then shall they see the Son of Adam coming in the clouds with great power and glory.* (Mark 13:26)

Daniel saw him in a night vision, and what he saw was one like the Son of Adam:

*I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of Adam came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him.* (Daniel 7:13)

Daniel goes on to say what was given him:

*And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.* (Daniel 7:14)

Matthew tells the same coming and adds the mourning of the tribes:

*And then shall appear the sign of the Son of Adam in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of Adam coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.* (Matthew 24:30)

John, in Revelation, says every eye will see him:

*Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Even so, Amen.* (Revelation 1:7)

And Zechariah, long before, has the speaker say of the pierced one:

*And I will pour upon the house of David, and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the spirit of grace and of supplications: and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son, and shall be in bitterness for him, as one that is in bitterness for his firstborn.* (Zechariah 12:10)

The one Daniel saw and the one Mark names are one, and the seeing ends in mourning for the one who was pierced.
