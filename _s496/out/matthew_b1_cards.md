*In the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria took Samaria, and carried Yashar'el (Israel) away into Assyria, and placed them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes.* (2 Kings 17:6)

Then Yahudah (Judah) was taken in her turn, and the land was emptied of nearly everyone who could lead it.

*And he carried away all Jerusalem, and all the princes, and all the mighty men of valour, even ten thousand captives, and all the craftsmen and smiths: none remained, save the poorest sort of the people of the land.* (2 Kings 24:14)

Jeremiah had already said how long it would last, so the captivity was not an accident that broke the line.

*And this whole land shall be a desolation, and an astonishment; and these nations shall serve the king of Babylon seventy years.* (Jeremiah 25:11)

Some did go home, but it was a partial return to Jerusalem and Yahudah (Judah), and nothing like the gathering of both houses that the prophets describe.

*Now these are the children of the province that went up out of the captivity, of those which had been carried away, whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away unto Babylon, and came again unto Jerusalem and Yahudah (Judah), every one unto his city;* (Ezra 2:1)

That is why Matthew counts the way he does.

*So all the generations from Abraham to David are fourteen generations; and from David until the carrying away into Babylon are fourteen generations; and from the carrying away into Babylon unto Messiah (Christ) are fourteen generations.* (Matthew 1:17)

The count runs straight through the carrying away and ends at Messiah (Christ). The line of the king is shown walking through exile, with the northern house still scattered and the return still ahead.

### Herod and the old hatred of Esau for Jacob (Genesis 27; Numbers 20; Obadiah; Psalm 137; Malachi 1; Ezekiel 35)
`matthew-2-herod-and-the-old-hatred-of-esau` — anchor Matthew 2:3–16

It ain't new. When Herod the king hears that a King of the Yahudim (Jews) is born, the scripture says he is shaken:

*When Herod the king had heard these things, he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him.* (Matthew 2:3)

The reader who looks back finds the same hatred in the same family, running from the first brothers to this king. Esau's heart was set on murder because of the blessing:

*And Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing wherewith his father blessed him: and Esau said in his heart, The days of mourning for my father are at hand; then will I slay my brother Jacob.* (Genesis 27:41)

The prophets name Edom's violence against Jacob, and give it its answer:

*For thy violence against thy brother Jacob shame shall cover thee, and thou shalt be cut off for ever.* (Obadiah 1:10)

The psalmist carries the cry that was made over the city on the day it fell:

*Remember, O Yahuah (LORD), the children of Edom in the day of Jerusalem; who said, Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation thereof.* (Psalm 137:7)

Over all of it stands the word of Yahuah (the LORD) that does not move with the kings:

*I have loved you, saith Yahuah (LORD). Yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us? Was not Esau Jacob’s brother? saith Yahuah (LORD): yet I loved Jacob,* (Malachi 1:2)

Herod's order shows where the old hatred lands. He cannot find the child, so he reaches for every child he can find:

*Then Herod, when he saw that he was mocked of the wise men, was exceeding wroth, and sent forth, and slew all the children that were in Bethlehem, and in all the coasts thereof, from two years old and under, according to the time which he had diligently enquired of the wise men.* (Matthew 2:16)

Herod was of Edom's house, an Idumean set on the throne in Jerusalem, and the prophets had already spoken to that house: Moses' message to the king of Edom at the border, the word given to Ezekiel against Mount Seir, the psalm that remembers the day the city fell. What Matthew shows in Herod is the old hatred of the blessing, sitting on a throne. It is the throne and the hatred the scripture names, and the word that stands over both is the one Malachi wrote down: *yet I loved Jacob*.

### The Jordan, where the people crossed and the prophets worked
`matthew-3-the-jordan-joshua-elijah-naaman` — anchor Matthew 3:13–13

It ain't new. The river was already carrying this, and Matthew sets Yahusha (Jesus) where the older scripture had set the great turnings of the people.

*Then cometh Yahusha (Jesus) from Galilee to Jordan unto John, to be baptized of him.* (Matthew 3:13)

The people of Yashar'el (Israel) first came into their land through this water, with the ark going ahead of them.

*And it came to pass, when the people removed from their tents, to pass over Jordan, and the priests bearing the ark of the covenant before the people;* (Joshua 3:14)

The word that sent them over was spoken to a man whose name is the same name Yahusha (Jesus) carries.

*Now after the death of Moses the servant of Yahuah (LORD) it came to pass, that Yahuah (LORD) spake unto Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ minister, saying,* (Joshua 1:1)

Then the prophets worked at the same river. Elijah was sent there, and Elisha went with him and would not turn back.

*And Elijah said unto him, Tarry, I pray thee, here; for Yahuah (LORD) hath sent me to Jordan. And he said, As Yahuah (LORD) liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee. And they two went on.* (2 Kings 2:6)

And a sick man was told to wash in it by the word of the prophet, and he came up clean.

*Then went he down, and dipped himself seven times in Jordan, according to the saying of the man of Elohim (God): and his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child, and he was clean.* (2 Kings 5:14)

So when John baptizes in the Jordan and the people come out confessing, the water is not a new place for a new thing. It is the river of the first entry, of the prophet's errand and of cleansing at a prophet's word, and Yahusha (Jesus) comes to it as the one leading the people home.
