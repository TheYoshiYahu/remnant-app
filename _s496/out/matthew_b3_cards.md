## `matthew-14-the-mountain-where-he-goes-apart` — The mountain where he goes apart to pray (anchor Matthew 14:22–23)

The chapter shows Yahusha (Jesus) going apart twice. When he heard that John was dead, he left by ship for a lonely place, and then, after the crowds had been fed and sent away, he went up a mountain by himself.

*And when he had sent the multitudes away, he went up into a mountain apart to pray: and when the evening was come, he was there alone.* (Matthew 14:23)

Those two verses hold the same movement, the withdrawing to the Father after a death and after a crowd, and the mountains elsewhere in Matthew carry the same marks. At the opening of the Sermon the multitudes bring him up the hill and the disciples come to him there.

*And seeing the multitudes, he went up into a mountain: and when he was set, his disciples came unto him:* (Matthew 5:1)

*Then the eleven disciples went away into Galilee, into a mountain where Yahusha (Jesus) had appointed them.* (Matthew 28:16)

Each of these is a place where he is apart from the crowd and with the Father, whether he is praying alone, teaching his disciples, standing with Moses and Elijah, or sending out the eleven. The mountain in Matthew 14 is the first of these where he is wholly alone, and it comes straight after the feeding in the wilderness.

## `matthew-15-plants-the-father-did-not-plant-and-blind-guides` — The plant my heavenly Father hath not planted, and the blind leaders (anchor Matthew 15:13–14)

It ain't new. The Father has always had a planting, and the older scriptures already knew the difference between what he planted and what was sown over it. When his disciples tell him the Pharisees were offended, Yahusha (Jesus) says:

*But he answered and said, Every plant, which my heavenly Father hath not planted, shall be rooted up.* (Matthew 15:13)

The plant he means is the one in his own parable of the field, where one man sowed and an enemy sowed after him.

*Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field:* (Matthew 13:24)

*But while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way.* (Matthew 13:25)

When the disciples ask for the parable to be declared, he does not leave it a riddle.

*The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one;* (Matthew 13:38)

So a plant the Father did not plant is what the enemy sowed, and what is rooted up is judged at the harvest and not by us. Isaiah says what remains standing when it is done.

*Thy people also shall be all righteous: they shall inherit the land for ever, the branch of my planting, the work of my hands, that I may be glorified.* (Isaiah 60:21)

The next words in Matthew turn from the plants to the guides who lead people among them.

*Let them alone: they be blind leaders of the blind. And if the blind lead the blind, both shall fall into the ditch.* (Matthew 15:14)

He had already said why such blindness stands.

*Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand.* (Matthew 13:13)

And later he shows what the guiding looked like, in the oath rule that put the gold first.

*Woe unto you, ye blind guides, which say, Whosoever shall swear by the temple, it is nothing; but whosoever shall swear by the gold of the temple, he is a debtor!* (Matthew 23:16)

It is the system and its rule that he names. The ones who follow it are the people in danger of the ditch, and that is why he speaks of them as he does.


## `matthew-18-binding-and-loosing-by-two-or-three-witnesses` — Binding and loosing on the word of two or three witnesses (anchor Matthew 18:18–20)

It ain't new. The binding and loosing in this chapter is the court of Moses, and Yahusha (Jesus) is speaking from inside it. He has just said what is done when a brother will not hear the private word:

*But if he will not hear thee, then take with thee one or two more, that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established.* (Matthew 18:16)

That is Deuteronomy's own rule, and it was old when he said it:

*One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.* (Deuteronomy 19:15)

In Moses the matter then goes before a court, where the two sides stand in the presence of Yahuah (the LORD) himself:

*Then both the men, between whom the controversy is, shall stand before Yahuah (LORD), before the priests and the judges, which shall be in those days;* (Deuteronomy 19:17)

Then Yahusha (Jesus) hands the verdict to the disciples together, in words he had spoken to Peter alone:

*Verily I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven.* (Matthew 18:18)

So the binding and loosing is the judgment of a matter that has been heard on its witnesses, and it is carried by the ones he sends, as he says after the rising:

*Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained.* (John 20:23)

Then comes the verse that is so often lifted out of its place:

*For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.* (Matthew 18:20)

Read where he put it, these are the two or three witnesses of verse sixteen, standing where Moses stood the two men, before Yahuah (the LORD) and the judges, and he is in the midst of that hearing. Malachi shows the kind of people who are heard when they speak to one another:

*Then they that feared Yahuah (LORD) spake often one to another: and Yahuah (LORD) hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared Yahuah (LORD), and that thought upon his name.* (Malachi 3:16)

Nothing here turns a gathering into a room where anything asked is granted, and nothing gives men a power over heaven. A matter established on the mouth of witnesses is bound as the Torah already binds it, and the one who sits in the midst of it is the one who gave the rule.
