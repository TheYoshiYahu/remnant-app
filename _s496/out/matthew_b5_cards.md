## `matthew-26-the-plot-and-the-passover-lamb` — The plot against Yahusha (Jesus) and the passover lamb (Matthew 26:1–5)

It ain't new. Two days before the feast, Yahusha (Jesus) says to his disciples:

*Ye know that after two days is the feast of the passover, and the Son of Adam is betrayed to be crucified.* (Matthew 26:2)

The feast he names is the one Yahuah (the LORD) gave to Moses and Aaron in Egypt, and it begins with a lamb taken for each house:

*Speak ye unto all the congregation of Yashar'el (Israel), saying, In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house:* (Exodus 12:3)

Abraham had already said on the mountain what Elohim (God) would do about the lamb:

*My son, Elohim (God) will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering* (Genesis 22:8)

John the Baptist points at the man walking by and calls him that lamb, and Isaiah had written how the lamb would go:

*Behold the Lamb of Elohim (God), which taketh away the sin of the world* (John 1:29)

*he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth* (Isaiah 53:7)

Paul speaks of the feast as still the feast, the old leaven purged and the lump unleavened, and names the lamb in the middle of it:

*For even Messiah (Christ) our passover is sacrificed for us* (1 Corinthians 5:7)

The feast is not undone by his death, it is filled. Around it the rulers gather and plot. They are the chief priests, the scribes and the elders at the palace of Caiaphas, and the people they fear are not the ones in the plot:

*And consulted that they might take Yahusha (Jesus) by subtilty, and kill him.* (Matthew 26:4)

The psalm had pictured the same gathering:

*the rulers take counsel together, against Yahuah (LORD), and against his anointed* (Psalm 2:2)

And he had told them to their faces where it was heading:

*and some of them ye shall kill and crucify* (Matthew 23:34)

John shows the council before this one, gathered over the miracles, with Caiaphas saying one man should die for the people. The plot belongs to the system that held the court, and the lamb goes to it the way the older words said.

## `matthew-26-bethany-the-anointing` — The anointing at Bethany (Matthew 26:6–13)

It ain't new. In the house of Simon the leper a woman pours ointment on Yahusha's (Jesus's) head, and the disciples call it waste:

*There came unto him a woman having an alabaster box of very precious ointment, and poured it on his head, as he sat at meat.* (Matthew 26:7)

Oil on a head is the oldest way the text sets a man apart. Samuel did it for a captain over Yahuah's (the LORD's) inheritance:

*Then Samuel took a vial of oil, and poured it upon his head* (1 Samuel 10:1)

and for David, in the middle of his brothers:

*Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of Yahuah (LORD) came upon David from that day forward* (1 Samuel 16:13)

The psalm speaks to a king in the same words of oil:

*therefore Elohim (God), thy Elohim (God), hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows* (Psalm 45:7)

So the oil at Bethany sits beside the anointing of kings, and Yahusha (Jesus) says what he saw in it as well:

*For in that she hath poured this ointment on my body, she did it for my burial.* (Matthew 26:12)

When the disciples say the money should have gone to the poor, he answers from Moses, and the commandment in that same verse is not set aside:

*For the poor shall never cease out of the land: therefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land* (Deuteronomy 15:11)

*For ye have the poor always with you; but me ye have not always.* (Matthew 26:11)

John dates the same supper six days before the passover and names the woman Mary. Luke's woman with an alabaster box comes to a Pharisee's table and is a different meal.

## `matthew-26-the-arrest` — The arrest in the garden (Matthew 26:47–56)

In the garden Yahusha (Jesus) is still speaking when the crowd arrives:

*And while he yet spake, lo, Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves, from the chief priests and elders of the people.* (Matthew 26:47)

John says why Judas could lead them there, and that Yahusha (Jesus) was not caught off guard:

*And Judas also, which betrayed him, knew the place: for Yahusha (Jesus) ofttimes resorted thither with his disciples.* (John 18:2)

A disciple draws a sword and strikes the servant of the high priest. Luke adds that Yahusha (Jesus) touched the ear and healed him:

*And Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said, Suffer ye thus far. And he touched his ear, and healed him.* (Luke 22:51)

Then he says to the one who struck:

*Then said Yahusha (Jesus) unto him, Put up again thy sword into his place: for all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword.* (Matthew 26:52)

It ain't new. Noah was given the measure of the sword long before:

*Whoso sheddeth man’s blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of Elohim (God) made he man.* (Genesis 9:6)

He refuses the sword, and not because he cannot call for help:

*Thinkest thou that I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels?* (Matthew 26:53)

John gives the reason in his own words, a life laid down by the one who has it:

*No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father.* (John 10:18)

The swords and staves come from the chief priests and elders, and Mark adds the scribes, the rulers who held the court. Judas chose his part, and the crowd of the people is not the plot. The one they come to take is not taken, he gives himself, as a charge he received from his Father.

## `matthew-26-peters-denial` — Peter's denial and his restoration (Matthew 26:69–75)

At the supper Peter says what he means with all his heart:

*Peter answered and said unto him, Though all men shall be offended because of thee, yet will I never be offended.* (Matthew 26:33)

Yahusha (Jesus) tells him what the night will do:

*Yahusha (Jesus) said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, That this night, before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.* (Matthew 26:34)

It comes as he said. In the palace Peter is asked and denies with an oath:

*And again he denied with an oath, I do not know the man.* (Matthew 26:72)

Matthew ends the scene with the cock crowing and the man outside:

*And Peter remembered the word of Yahusha (Jesus), which said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And he went out, and wept bitterly.* (Matthew 26:75)

Luke says what happened in between, a look:

*And Yahusha (Lord) turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of Yahusha (Lord), how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.* (Luke 22:61)

David had written what meets a heart broken like that:

*The sacrifices of Elohim (God) are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O Elohim (God), thou wilt not despise.* (Psalm 51:17)

Joel had called for the same turn, in the heart and not in the clothes:

*And rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God)* (Joel 2:13)

After the resurrection Peter is asked three times, once for each denial, and each time he is given a charge:

*Simon, son of Jonah, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Adonai (Lord); thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs* (John 21:15)

Peter fell and wept and was restored. He is a sheep that stumbled, and he is not condemned. The other gospels add their own detail, and they agree on the fire, the three questions and the three answers.

## `matthew-27-judas-and-the-potters-field` — Judas, the silver and the potter's field (Matthew 27:3–10)

It ain't new. The silver, the potter and the field were in the Torah and the prophets long before Judas carried the money back. Matthew says the priests would not put it in the treasury:

*And the chief priests took the silver pieces, and said, It is not lawful for to put them into the treasury, because it is the price of blood.* (Matthew 27:6)

The Torah already had a line for such money:

*Thou shalt not bring the hire of a whore, or the price of a dog, into the house of Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God) for any vow: for even both these are abomination unto Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God).* (Deuteronomy 23:18)

Matthew then names Jeremiah the prophet for the thirty pieces and the potter's field. The potter and the bought field are Jeremiah's, and the silver thrown to the potter is in Zechariah, so each text is set down here as it stands:

*So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver.* (Zechariah 11:12)

*And Yahuah (LORD) said unto me, Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price that I was prised at of them.* (Zechariah 11:13)

*Thus saith Yahuah (LORD), Go and get a potter's earthen bottle, and take of the ancients of the people, and of the ancients of the priests;* (Jeremiah 19:1)

David had spoken of the man who turns, and Peter reads him over Judas:

*Let his days be few; and let another take his office.* (Psalm 109:8)

*Men and brethren, this scripture must needs have been fulfilled, which the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) by the mouth of David spake before concerning Judas, which was guide to them that took Yahusha (Jesus).* (Acts 1:16)

Judas chose his side, and the priests who paid him and then refused the money kept a commandment while the innocent man it paid for was handed over.

## `matthew-27-pilate-and-barabbas` — Pilate and Barabbas, the silence and the court (Matthew 27:11–26)

It ain't new. Before Pilate asks his question the prophet had already described the man who would stand silent, and the psalm had already described the rulers gathered against him. Matthew says:

*And when he was accused of the chief priests and elders, he answered nothing.* (Matthew 27:12)

*He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.* (Isaiah 53:7)

Pilate himself did not want the death. Matthew says what moved the crowd, and who moved it:

*But the chief priests and elders persuaded the multitude that they should ask Barabbas, and destroy Yahusha (Jesus).* (Matthew 27:20)

Peter later says the same of Pilate, and puts the handing over where it belongs:

*The Elohim (God) of Abraham, and of Isaac, and of Jacob, the Elohim (God) of our fathers, hath glorified his Son Yahusha (Jesus); whom ye delivered up, and denied him in the presence of Pilate, when he was determined to let him go.* (Acts 3:13)

Mark and Luke each show the same hearing from another side, as the whole council binds him and leads him to the governor, and John shows the rulers outside the hall, careful not to be defiled before the passover. In every telling the weight sits on the court that owned the proceeding, with a governor who knew it was envy and a crowd the rulers had worked on, and the one before them opens not his mouth.

## `matthew-27-the-burial` — The burial and the watch (Matthew 27:57–66)

It ain't new. Isaiah had already said where the servant's grave would be, and why:

*And he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death; because he had done no violence, neither was any deceit in his mouth.* (Isaiah 53:9)

Matthew brings in the man who fits it:

*When the even was come, there came a rich man of Arimathæa, named Joseph, who also himself was Yahusha's (Jesus') disciple:* (Matthew 27:57)

*And laid it in his own new tomb, which he had hewn out in the rock: and he rolled a great stone to the door of the sepulchre, and departed.* (Matthew 27:60)

Luke says what kind of man he was and where he sat:

*And, behold, there was a man named Joseph, a counsellor; and he was a good man, and a just:* (Luke 23:50)

Mark says what hour it was, with the Sabbath coming on, and John says Joseph had been a disciple in secret. The council had condemned Yahusha (Jesus), and a just man from that same council asked for the body.

Then the court seals what it can:

*So they went, and made the sepulchre sure, sealing the stone, and setting a watch.* (Matthew 27:66)

And the next chapter shows what the sealing brought back to them:

*Now when they were going, behold, some of the watch came into the city, and shewed unto the chief priests all the things that were done.* (Matthew 28:11)

The tomb was a rich man's, the stone was sealed by the rulers, and the first report of what happened there went to the system that sealed it.

## `matthew-28-the-watch-and-the-story-they-paid-for` — The watch set at the tomb and the story the rulers paid for (Matthew 28:11–15)

Before the stone was ever moved, the chief priests and Pharisees had gone to Pilate with a fear and a plan.

*Command therefore that the sepulchre be made sure until the third day, lest his disciples come by night, and steal him away, and say unto the people, He is risen from the dead: so the last error shall be worse than the first.* (Matthew 27:64)

The watch was set to keep one story from being told. Then the watch came back with the truth of what had happened, and the same men who had asked for the guard took counsel with the elders and bought the very tale they had feared, with a promise to square it with the governor.

*Say ye, His disciples came by night, and stole him away while we slept.* (Matthew 28:13)

Matthew says plainly how it spread and who began it.

*So they took the money, and did as they were taught: and this saying is commonly reported among the Yahudim (Jews) until this day.* (Matthew 28:15)

The ones doing the teaching are named, the chief priests and the elders, the leadership that owned the court and the guard. The soldiers took money and did as they were taught, and the story went out among people who had no part in making it. Those people were handed it. Acts shows the same office at the same work once the disciples begin to preach.

*Being grieved that they taught the people, and preached through Yahusha (Jesus) the resurrection from the dead.* (Acts 4:2)

And the people did not follow their rulers there.

*Howbeit many of them which heard the word believed* (Acts 4:4)

The high priest's circle goes further than words.

*And laid their hands on the apostles, and put them in the common prison.* (Acts 5:18)

Matthew and Acts side by side show a system working to keep a resurrection from being heard, first by a purchased tale and then by a prison, while the people it was told to are the ones it was told about.
