# S507 — the two thread summaries to read before anything deploys (gethsemane id 7, talents id 13). Model: Claude Fable 5.1

## `gethsemane-cup-of-suffering-and-the-sons-substantial-submission-to-the-father`

**TITLE:** Gethsemane — the cup in the Father's hand, and not as I will, but as thou wilt (Psalm 75; Isaiah 51; John 10; Hebrews 5; Zechariah 13)

It ain't new. The cup he prayed over in the garden was already in the Father's hand in the Psalms and the prophets, and the prayer itself, not my will but thine, is what the Son had said of himself all along. Matthew sets the scene:

*Then cometh Yahusha (Jesus) with them unto a place called Gethsemane, and saith unto the disciples, Sit ye here, while I go and pray yonder.* (Matthew 26:36)

*Then saith he unto them, My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me.* (Matthew 26:38)

*And he went a little further, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.* (Matthew 26:39)

The soul cast down was the psalmist's before it was his:

*Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in Elohim (God): for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance.* (Psalm 42:5)

And the cup is not a figure Matthew made up. It is the cup of fury in the hand of Yahuah (LORD), poured out and drunk to the dregs:

*For in the hand of Yahuah (LORD) there is a cup, and the wine is red; it is full of mixture; and he poureth out of the same: but the dregs thereof, all the wicked of the earth shall wring them out, and drink them.* (Psalm 75:8)

*Awake, awake, stand up, O Jerusalem, which hast drunk at the hand of Yahuah (LORD) the cup of his fury; thou hast drunken the dregs of the cup of trembling, and wrung them out.* (Isaiah 51:17)

*Thus saith thy Yahuah (Lord) Yahuah (LORD), and thy Elohim (God) that pleadeth the cause of his people, Behold, I have taken out of thine hand the cup of trembling, even the dregs of the cup of my fury; thou shalt no more drink it again:* (Isaiah 51:22)

Isaiah says the cup is taken out of Jerusalem's hand. In the garden it is put into his. He had already asked the sons of Zebedee whether they could drink it:

*But Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said, Ye know not what ye ask. Are ye able to drink of the cup that I shall drink of, and to be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with? They say unto him, We are able.* (Matthew 20:22)

Luke adds the angel and the sweat:

*Saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done.* (Luke 22:42)

*And there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him.* (Luke 22:43)

*And being in an agony he prayed more earnestly: and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground.* (Luke 22:44)

Read what the prayer is. It is the Son asking the Father, and the Son's will bending to the Father's will. He is Elohim (God), and he has a Father, and he does the Father's will. He said so before the garden, said it in the garden, and said it from the cross:

*Then answered Yahusha (Jesus) and said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do: for what things soever he doeth, these also doeth the Son likewise.* (John 5:19)

*Now is my soul troubled; and what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour: but for this cause came I unto this hour.* (John 12:27)

*And when Yahusha (Jesus) had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.* (Luke 23:46)

Nobody took the life from him. The cup was drunk because he chose to drink it, and the choosing was the Father's commandment kept:

*No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. This commandment have I received of my Father.* (John 10:18)

Hebrews says plainly what the garden was:

*Who in the days of his flesh, when he had offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared;* (Hebrews 5:7)

*Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered;* (Hebrews 5:8)

And Paul says that same mind is the one we are to carry:

*Let this mind be in you, which was also in HaMashiach Yahusha (Christ Jesus):* (Philippians 2:5)

*And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.* (Philippians 2:8)

Set Abraham beside it. He also rose early and went up the mountain with his only son, and Matthew does not say the word; put the two side by side and read:

*And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.* (Genesis 22:2)

Then the hour comes, the shepherd is struck as Zechariah said, and the sheep run:

*Then cometh he to his disciples, and saith unto them, Sleep on now, and take your rest: behold, the hour is at hand, and the Son of Adam is betrayed into the hands of sinners.* (Matthew 26:45)

*Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts): smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones.* (Zechariah 13:7)

The sheep who scattered that night are the little ones his hand turns toward. They slept while he prayed, and the charge he gave them in the garden is still the charge:

*Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.* (Matthew 26:41)


## `parable-of-the-talents-stewardship-and-the-unprofitable-servant-cast-into-outer-darkness`

**TITLE:** The talents — his own servants, his goods delivered, the reckoning at his coming, and the one cast out (Luke 19; Daniel 7; Proverbs 13; Revelation 2)

It ain't new. A lord who goes away, leaves his goods with his own servants, and comes back to reckon with them: the prophets had the saints taking the kingdom, and the proverbs had the sluggard with nothing, long before Matthew wrote the parable down:

*For the kingdom of heaven is as a man travelling into a far country, who called his own servants, and delivered unto them his goods.* (Matthew 25:14)

*And unto one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one; to every man according to his several ability; and straightway took his journey.* (Matthew 25:15)

Mark who they are. Not strangers, not hired men off the road, but his own servants, each given a share of what is his. Luke tells it with pounds and a nobleman gone to receive a kingdom:

*He said therefore, A certain nobleman went into a far country to receive for himself a kingdom, and to return.* (Luke 19:12)

*And he called his ten servants, and delivered them ten pounds, and said unto them, Occupy till I come.* (Luke 19:13)

Mark has the same house and the same journey, with the charge to watch:

*For the Son of Adam is as a man taking a far journey, who left his house, and gave authority to his servants, and to every man his work, and commanded the porter to watch.* (Mark 13:34)

Then the lord comes back:

*After a long time the lord of those servants cometh, and reckoneth with them.* (Matthew 25:19)

*His lord said unto him, Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord.* (Matthew 25:21)

*Then he which had received the one talent came and said, Lord, I knew thee that thou art an hard man, reaping where thou hast not sown, and gathering where thou hast not strawed:* (Matthew 25:24)

*And I was afraid, and went and hid thy talent in the earth: lo, there thou hast that is thine.* (Matthew 25:25)

*His lord answered and said unto him, Thou wicked and slothful servant, thou knewest that I reap where I sowed not, and gather where I have not strawed:* (Matthew 25:26)

*And cast ye the unprofitable servant into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.* (Matthew 25:30)

The one cast out was given a talent, called a servant, and reckoned with as one of the household. He is not a tare and not one of the nations. He is of the house, and he is judged by what he did with what he was given. Luke says the same of the servant who knew his lord's will:

*And that servant, which knew his lord’s will, and prepared not himself, neither did according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes.* (Luke 12:47)

And Matthew had already said who it is that ends in outer darkness:

*But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.* (Matthew 8:12)

The proverbs had the slothful man's end long before:

*The soul of the sluggard desireth, and hath nothing: but the soul of the diligent shall be made fat.* (Proverbs 13:4)

*Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise:* (Proverbs 6:6)

What the faithful are given is rule, and Daniel had seen whose that rule is:

*But the saints of the El Elyon (most High) shall take the kingdom, and possess the kingdom for ever, even for ever and ever.* (Daniel 7:18)

*And the kingdom and dominion, and the greatness of the kingdom under the whole heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the El Elyon (most High), whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions shall serve and obey him.* (Daniel 7:27)

The twelve thrones and the reign on the earth are that same rule, promised to the same servants, and the one who gets it is the one who keeps his works to the end:

*And Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, Verily I say unto you, That ye which have followed me, in the regeneration when the Son of Adam shall sit in the throne of his glory, ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 19:28)

*And he that overcometh, and keepeth my works unto the end, to him will I give power over the nations:* (Revelation 2:26)

*And hast made us unto our Elohim (God) kings and priests: and we shall reign on the earth.* (Revelation 5:10)

The talents are the gifts, dealt to each by measure, and Peter says what a servant does with a gift:

*As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of Elohim (God).* (1 Peter 4:10)

*For unto every one that hath shall be given, and he shall have abundance: but from him that hath not shall be taken away even that which he hath.* (Matthew 25:29)

The reckoning is of the house, by works, at his coming. The one with his talent in the ground was no stranger, and the door he is put out of was his own lord's. So the parable ends where the chapter before it did:

*Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come.* (Matthew 24:42)
