### Of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham — the stones are the scattered house (Joshua 4; Hosea 1; Ezekiel 37)
*(thread 30413 · `matthew-3-fruits-meet-for-repentance-stones-to-abraham-isaiah-1-ezekiel-18-romans-11`)*

It ain't new. John is standing in the Jordan when he says it:

*And think not to say within yourselves, We have Abraham to our father: for I say unto you, that Elohim (God) is able of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham.* (Matthew 3:9)

The Jordan already had stones that meant something. When Yashar'el (Israel) crossed it, one man from every tribe carried a stone up out of the river bed:

*And Joshua said unto them, Pass over before the ark of Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) into the midst of Jordan, and take ye up every man of you a stone upon his shoulder, according unto the number of the tribes of the children of Yashar'el (Israel):* (Joshua 4:5)

*And he spake unto the children of Yashar'el (Israel), saying, When your children shall ask their fathers in time to come, saying, What mean these stones?* (Joshua 4:21)

Twelve stones, twelve tribes. By John's day ten of those tribes had been gone seven hundred years, scattered among the nations and counted as dead. Hosea had said what Yahuah (God) would do with them:

*Yet the number of the children of Yashar'el (Israel) shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living Elohim (God).* (Hosea 1:10)

Ezekiel saw them as bones and was told who they were:

*Then he said unto me, Son of Adam, these bones are the whole house of Yashar'el (Israel): behold, they say, Our bones are dried, and our hope is lost: we are cut off for our parts.* (Ezekiel 37:11)

*And say unto them, Thus saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD); Behold, I will take the children of Yashar'el (Israel) from among the heathen, whither they be gone, and will gather them on every side, and bring them into their own land:* (Ezekiel 37:21)

Dry bones made a living house; stones made children of Abraham. It is one promise in two pictures, and in both it is the scattered house coming home, not the nations made into Yashar'el (Israel). So *We have Abraham to our father* is answered from two sides at once. The ones who trust their birth and bear no fruit are cut down: *every tree which bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.* (Matthew 3:10) And the children everyone thought were lost are raised up. Those are the people Paul means when he asks *Hath Elohim (God) cast away his people? Elohim (God) forbid.* (Romans 11:1), and the wild olive *graffed in among them* (Romans 11:17) is that same seed, grown wild in the nations but olive from the root. The fruit John asks for is what Isaiah asked of the same house: *Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes; cease to do evil;* (Isaiah 1:16). Repentance is a turning, and the ones who turn are the ones Yahuah (God) raises.

### The mustard seed and the leaven — what grows in the field before the harvest (Daniel 4; Ezekiel 31; Exodus 12)
*(thread 30901 · `matthew-13-the-mustard-seed-the-leaven-the-birds-lodge-ezekiel-17-daniel-4`)*

It ain't new. Two short parables sit between the sower and the explaining of the tares, and both describe the field before the harvest, not after it:

*Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field:* (Matthew 13:31)

*Which indeed is the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among herbs, and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof.* (Matthew 13:32)

A mustard plant is an herb. One that *becometh a tree* has grown past its kind, and the birds that come to lodge in it are the birds this chapter has already named: *And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them up:* (Matthew 13:4), which he explains himself: *then cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart.* (Matthew 13:19). Scripture does have a great tree full of nesting birds, and it is Babylon:

*The leaves thereof were fair, and the fruit thereof much, and in it was meat for all: the beasts of the field had shadow under it, and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof, and all flesh was fed of it.* (Daniel 4:12)

*He cried aloud, and said thus, Hew down the tree, and cut off his branches, shake off his leaves, and scatter his fruit: let the beasts get away from under it, and the fowls from his branches:* (Daniel 4:14)

And Assyria, cut down for lifting itself up:

*All the fowls of heaven made their nests in his boughs, and under his branches did all the beasts of the field bring forth their young, and under his shadow dwelt all great nations.* (Ezekiel 31:6)

The leaven says the same thing plainly:

*Another parable spake he unto them; The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.* (Matthew 13:33)

Nowhere in scripture is leaven a good thing. It is put out of the house at Passover on pain of being cut off (Exodus 12:15); no offering may carry it (Leviticus 2:11); he names it himself, *Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.* (Matthew 16:6), and the disciples understood *the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees* (Matthew 16:12), which Luke calls *hypocrisy* (Luke 12:1); Paul says of the teaching that was pulling the Galatians away, *A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump.* (Galatians 5:9). Three measures of fine meal is what Sarah kneaded for the three who came to Abraham's tent (Genesis 18:6). Someone hid leaven in the offering, and it worked through all of it. That is what the kingdom looks like in this age, told without flattery: a seed grown into something out of its kind where the wicked one's birds lodge, and a doctrine worked quietly through the whole until nothing is left unleavened. The cedar Yahuah (God) plants with his own hand is still to come, *In the mountain of the height of Yashar'el (Israel) will I plant it* (Ezekiel 17:23), and it comes when *I Yahuah (LORD) have brought down the high tree, have exalted the low tree* (Ezekiel 17:24). The tares grow with the wheat until the harvest; these two parables say the same thing in two pictures.

### The gathering of the scattered twelve tribes — Sirach 36, 2 Esdras 13 and Tobit 13 behind *I am not sent but unto the lost sheep*
*(thread 221 · `gathering-of-the-scattered-twelve-tribes-in-sirach-and-2-esdras-and-tobit`)*

It ain't new. He said who he was sent to in one sentence:

*But he answered and said, I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 15:24)

He had sent the twelve the same way: *But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 10:6). Who was lost? Not Yahudah (Judah) in Jerusalem, who could be found any Sabbath. The books the Reformation cut from the library say plainly who:

*Those are the ten tribes, which were carried away prisoners out of their own land in the time of Osea the king, whom Salmanasar the king of Assyria led away captive, and he carried them over the waters, and so came they into another land.* (2 Esdras 13:40)

*But those that be left behind of your people are they that are found within my borders.* (2 Esdras 13:48)

Two groups: the ten tribes beyond the waters, and the remnant inside the borders. Tobit, himself of Naphtali in that captivity, says what comes after the scattering:

*And he will scourge us for our iniquities, and will have mercy again, and will gather us out of all nations, among whom he has scattered us.* (Tobit 13:5)

And Sirach prays for it:

*Have mercy upon us, O Yahuah (God) of all, and behold us:* (Sirach 36:1)

*Gather all the tribes of Jacob together, and inherit you them, as from the beginning.* (Sirach 36:11)

The prophets had said it before them, *I will take the children of Yashar'el (Israel) from among the heathen, whither they be gone, and will gather them on every side* (Ezekiel 37:21), and Moses before the prophets: *will return and gather thee from all the nations, whither Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God) hath scattered thee.* (Deuteronomy 30:3). So when he says he was sent to the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel), the prayer of Sirach 36:11 is being answered in person, and Paul says as much: *a minister of the circumcision for the truth of Elohim (God), to confirm the promises made unto the fathers:* (Romans 15:8). After he rose the sending went *to all nations* (Matthew 28:19), because that is where the sheep are, *until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in.* (Romans 11:25). The woman of Canaan in this same passage, in the coasts of Tyre and Sidon where Elijah's widow had lived, is not an exception to his sentence. She heard, she called him Son of David, and he said *O woman, great is thy faith: be it unto thee even as thou wilt.* (Matthew 15:28). A sheep heard his voice where the house had been scattered. Anyone who holds that there is only Yahudah (Judah) and everybody else, with no ten tribes to be found, has to read every one of these books as if they were never written.

### The Nazareth scroll — the acceptable year read, the day of vengeance left for its day, and the widow of Sidon and Naaman named (Isaiah 61; 1 Kings 17; 2 Kings 5)
*(thread 554 · `nazareth-scroll-the-acceptable-year-stopped-mid-verse-and-the-individual-mercy-contrast-cases`)*

It ain't new. On the Sabbath in Nazareth he is handed Isaiah and reads:

*The Spirit of Yahuah (Lord) is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised,* (Luke 4:18)

*To preach the acceptable year of Yahuah (Lord).* (Luke 4:19)

Then he closes the book. Isaiah's sentence goes on: *To proclaim the acceptable year of Yahuah (LORD), and the day of vengeance of our Elohim (God); to comfort all that mourn;* (Isaiah 61:2). He stopped at the comma. *This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears.* (Luke 4:21) is said of the acceptable year and the liberty to the captives, not of the day of vengeance, which was not that day and is not yet. The first coming is the one; the dreadful day is the other.

They take it well until he says where the word went the last time Yahuah (God) sent a prophet in a famine:

*But I tell you of a truth, many widows were in Yashar'el (Israel) in the days of Elijah, when the heaven was shut up three years and six months, when great famine was throughout all the land;* (Luke 4:25)

*But unto none of them was Elijah sent, save unto Sarepta, a city of Sidon, unto a woman that was a widow.* (Luke 4:26)

*And many lepers were in Yashar'el (Israel) in the time of Elisha the prophet; and none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian.* (Luke 4:27)

Both were outside the land, among the nations, and both answered the word when it came: *Now by this I know that thou art a man of Elohim (God), and that the word of Yahuah (LORD) in thy mouth is truth.* (1 Kings 17:24); *Behold, now I know that there is no Elohim (God) in all the earth, but in Yashar'el (Israel)* (2 Kings 5:15). Neither was an outsider handed a kindness. Each heard and followed, which is how his sheep are known: *My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me:* (John 10:27), and *other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice* (John 10:16). Seed of the house, revealed outside its borders by its hearing. That is what the synagogue could not bear: that the favour had gone out past the land to people they counted as nothing, and passed over a house that trusted its birth. *And all they in the synagogue, when they heard these things, were filled with wrath,* (Luke 4:28) and they led him to the brow of the hill to cast him down.

### Elijah's famine and Elisha's spirit — Sirach 48 behind the widow of Sidon and Naaman
*(thread 636 · `luke-4-the-individual-mercy-contrast-cases-elijah-elisha-and-the-famine-in-sirach-48`)*

It ain't new. The two names he sets before Nazareth are the two prophets Sirach praises back to back. Elijah first:

*Then stood up Elias the prophet as fire, and his word burned like a lamp.* (Sirach 48:1)

*By the word of Yahuah (God) he shut up the heaven, and also three times brought down fire.* (Sirach 48:3)

That shut heaven is the famine he names in the synagogue: *many widows were in Yashar'el (Israel) in the days of Elijah, when the heaven was shut up three years and six months* (Luke 4:25). Then Elisha:

*Elias it was, who was covered with a whirlwind: and Eliseus was filled with his spirit: while he lived, he was not moved with the presence of any prince, neither could any bring him into subjection.* (Sirach 48:12)

Filled with Elijah's spirit, and the one leper he cleansed was *Naaman the Syrian* (Luke 4:27). Sirach knows both prophets as Yashar'el (Israel)'s own and does nothing to soften where their work landed. In the famine the word went to a widow in Sidon, and the leper made clean was a Syrian, while many in the land went without. They were not strangers shown a favour; they were the ones who heard. *Now by this I know that thou art a man of Elohim (God), and that the word of Yahuah (LORD) in thy mouth is truth.* (1 Kings 17:24). *Behold, now I know that there is no Elohim (God) in all the earth, but in Yashar'el (Israel)* (2 Kings 5:15). Seed of the house revealed outside its borders by its hearing, which is how his sheep are always known: *My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me:* (John 10:27). Nazareth heard the same two stories told back to them and *were filled with wrath* (Luke 4:28).

### Corban — the tradition that makes *Honour thy father and thy mother* of none effect (Exodus 20; Isaiah 29; Sirach 3)
*(thread 622 · `mark-7-the-corban-tradition-voids-the-fifth-commandment-in-sirach`)*

It ain't new. He tells them what their tradition does to the commandment, and he begins with Isaiah's words:

*He answered and said unto them, Well hath Isaiah prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.* (Mark 7:6)

*Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.* (Mark 7:7)

Then the case:

*For Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die the death:* (Mark 7:10)

*But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free.* (Mark 7:11)

*And ye suffer him no more to do ought for his father or his mother;* (Mark 7:12)

*Making the word of Elohim (God) of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye.* (Mark 7:13)

A man says the word over what his parents needed, and the ruling sets him free of them. The commandment stands written, *Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God) giveth thee.* (Exodus 20:12), and a ruling of men is laid over it until it does nothing. Sirach had already tied that honour to the fear of Yahuah (God), and to the deed and not the saying only:

*He that fears Yahuah (God) will honour his father, and will do service to his parents, as to his masters.* (Sirach 3:7)

*Honour your father and mother both in word and deed, that a blessing may come upon you from them.* (Sirach 3:8)

*For the blessing of the father establishes the houses of children; but the curse of the mother rooteth out foundations.* (Sirach 3:9)

Word and deed. The corban ruling keeps the word and forbids the deed, and so forfeits the blessing Sirach names. The same thing is done every time a teaching of men is set above a commandment of Elohim (God), whether it is a Pharisee's ruling or a pulpit's, and his verdict on it does not change: *Full well ye reject the commandment of Elohim (God), that ye may keep your own tradition.* (Mark 7:9)

### The rich man and Lazarus — Abraham's bosom, the torment, the great gulf, and *they have Moses and the prophets*
*(thread 571 · `rich-man-and-lazaros-the-framework-honest-sheol-with-two-compartments-and-the-mosheh-and-the-prophets-test`)*

It ain't new. Luke tells it in his own words, and the words are enough:

*And it came to pass, that the beggar died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham’s bosom: the rich man also died, and was buried;* (Luke 16:22)

*And in hell he lift up his eyes, being in torments, and seeth Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom.* (Luke 16:23)

*And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence.* (Luke 16:26)

Abraham's bosom on one side, torments on the other, a great gulf fixed between, and both men awake. The rich man sees, speaks, remembers his five brothers, feels the flame and asks for water. Lazarus is comforted. Abraham answers. Two teachings handed down both have to argue with the page. The one that says the dead know nothing until the resurrection has a dead man holding a conversation. The one that says this flame is the final judgment has Abraham say *now he is comforted, and thou art tormented.* (Luke 16:25) while the five brothers are still alive on the earth and no one has risen. Luke says bosom, torments, gulf, and a man waiting; he does not say the end has come. David had said the grave gives back: *But Elohim (God) will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me. Selah.* (Psalm 49:15)

Then the parable turns to the living:

*Abraham saith unto him, They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them.* (Luke 16:29)

*And he said unto him, If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead.* (Luke 16:31)

That is the test he leaves standing against every gospel that sets Moses aside, and it was tried in the open. Another Lazarus did rise, *he cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth.* (John 11:43), and *the chief priests consulted that they might put Lazarus also to death;* (John 12:10). Men who would not hear Moses were not persuaded by one risen from the dead, exactly as Abraham had said. *For had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me.* (John 5:46)
