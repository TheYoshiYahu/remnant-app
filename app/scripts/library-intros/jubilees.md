The Book of Jubilees is not new. The book is older than the New Testament. It was read in Yashar’el (Israel) before the Messiah (Christ) walked the dust of Galilee. It was preserved at Qumran among the sect that watched for him. It was carried into Ethiopia by the body that received it as scripture and kept it in its canon when no one else would. The early assemblies in the West read it — Jude alludes to it, the framework of his own short letter is unintelligible without the Watchers material it shares with 1 Enoch and Jubilees — and then, at the same Council of Laodicea (363 AD) where the horn moved the Sabbath and pulled 1 Enoch out of the canon, Jubilees was set aside too. Not because it was wrong. Because it said too clearly what the body the horn was building had decided not to say.

What does Jubilees say, that the four houses needed silenced?

It says the Sabbath is older than Sinai. It says the Sabbath was kept in heaven before any flesh kept it on the earth. It says the angels of the presence and the angels of sanctification keep the Sabbath with Yahuah (God) — and that he hallowed a peculiar people above all peoples to keep it together with them. It says the seventh day is the rest of the Creator, sealed at creation, ratified into the calendar of heaven, given to the covenant body as a sign for ever. The day cannot be moved. The horn moved it.

It says the festivals — the Feast of Weeks, the Feast of Tabernacles, the Passover — are not Yahudi (Jewish) holidays Christianity has graduated from. They are appointed times engraven on the heavenly tables before Sinai. The Feast of Weeks was kept in heaven from creation, kept on earth by Noah, kept by Abraham, kept by Isaac, kept by Jacob, re-disclosed at Sinai to a body that had forgotten it in Egypt, kept by the Messiah (Christ) himself, and folded into the architecture of the gathering at the end of the age. The festivals were not retired. The body forgot.

It says the festivals are appointed times engraven on the heavenly tables, marked by the two lights Genesis 1:14 set together for signs and seasons — the moon’s own witness naming the new month as Psalm 81 has it, the seventh-day rhythm Yahuah (God) hallowed at creation. Where the book of Jubilees presses further than this — setting the moon aside and locking the year against its witness — the commentary on chapters 2 and 6 presses back.

It says the Watchers descended in the days of Jared, took wives of the daughters of men, taught the omens of the sun and moon and stars and the metallurgy of war, fathered the giants, and corrupted the orders of every flesh on the earth. It says their offspring drowned in the Flood — but their disembodied spirits are the unclean demons that walk the earth from then on, and the chief of those spirits is Mastema, the prince of accusations, whom Yahuah (God) permitted to retain a tenth of the bound spirits at his command until the appointed end. This architecture is not the church’s architecture. The church flattened the Watchers into the godly line of Seth and the daughters of men into the wicked line of Cain — a fourth-century invention the church needed because it had withdrawn 1 Enoch and Jubilees from the canon and could not afford to keep the original reading. Peter and Jude both refuse the flattening (2 Peter 2:4; Jude 6). Jubilees gives the architecture in full.

It says the holy line descends from Adam through Seth, Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech, Noah, Shem, Eber, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — and that Adam was a priest before there was a priesthood, that the priestly office is older than Levi, that the tithe is older than Aaron, that the festivals are older than the wilderness wanderings the festivals will later commemorate. All of Sinai, in Jubilees’ frame, is the re-disclosure to a covenant body in Egypt of what the body’s fathers had already carried. Sinai is not novelty. Sinai is recovery.

It says the seed of Jacob — the twelve tribes through their patriarch — is the firstborn son of Yahuah (God). The firstborn-son language Jubilees uses in chapter 2:20 is the same language Yahuah (God) will speak to Pharaoh in Exodus 4:22, and the same language the prophets will speak again of the gathered house. The covenant body is real. The covenant body is named. The covenant body has a paternal blood lineage that the prophets traced and promised to gather. Christianity says the church replaced this body. Judaism says only Yahudah (Judah) carries it. Hebrew roots says anyone who keeps Torah replaces it. Islam says the covenant transferred to Ishmael. Four houses. Four denials. Four versions of the same lie.

Jubilees forecloses every one of them. Chapter 15:30–31 is the verse the four houses cannot hold: over Yashar’el (Israel) He did not appoint any angel or spirit, for He alone is their ruler. The covenant people are the seed of Jacob, under the direct rule of Yahuah (God), distinct from the nations who were placed under intermediate authorities, distinct from Ishmael’s line and Esau’s line who are blessed in their own measure but are not the seed of promise. The chapter is unbothered by modern sensibilities. It states the architecture and lets the architecture speak.

And it says — this is the verse the awakening reader must let land — the body that forgot will be gathered. Chapter 1:15 onward names what every prophet later confirms: they will turn to Me from amongst the nations with all their heart and with all their soul and with all their strength, and I shall gather them from amongst all the nations. The gathering is not metaphor. The gathering is not the church. The gathering is the paternal blood remnant of all twelve tribes of Yashar’el (Israel) coming home — Yahudah (Judah) and the scattered house of the north, Ephraim and Yosef and Yashar’el (Israel), the lost sheep that Yahusha (Jesus) said he was sent to (Matthew 15:24), the Lo-Ammi of Hosea 1 to whom the second word came in the same chapter: in the place where it was said unto them, ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, ye are the sons of the living Elohim (God). Paul quotes that verse in Romans 9:25–26. He knew who he was writing to. The scattered seed is being gathered, and this is the second recovery the prophets named.

Chapter 23:26 is the verse the awakening reader will not be able to put down: in those days the children will begin to study the laws, and to seek the commandments, and to return to the path of righteousness. Read it slowly. The recovery does not start in the synagogues of the surviving Yahudim (Jews). The recovery does not start in the academies of institutional Christianity. The recovery starts in the children — the next generation, the ones the systems failed — beginning to study the laws. This is the awakening Jubilees prophesied a hundred and fifty years before Yahusha (Jesus) was born. This is what Hosea 3:5 meant. This is what is happening across the world right now, in homes you have never visited, in lives you will never know, as the scattered seed reaches back across the church’s seventeen centuries of mediation, opens the prophets without the lens, finds the calendar without the liturgical year laid over it, finds the Sabbath without the moved day, finds the festivals without the Christianizing labels, and finds their identity as Yashar’el (Israel) without any of the four replacement readings. The children are studying the laws. Jubilees called it.

And it says, in chapter 50, the closing chapter, that this entire arc — from Adam to Sinai to the gathering to the appointed reign — sits inside a Jubilee architecture. There are forty-nine jubilees from the days of Adam until this day, the angel of the presence tells Moses, with the count holding all the way down. The giving of the Torah at Sinai is the Jubilee of Jubilees from Adam — the great reset, the maximum liberation, the moment the heavenly architecture lands at full weight on a body just brought out of bondage. And the Jubilees keep counting. The jubilees will pass by, until Yashar’el (Israel) is cleansed from all guilt of fornication, and uncleanness, and pollution, and sin, and error, and dwells with confidence in all the land, and there will be no more a Satan or any evil one, and the land will be clean from that time for evermore. The book opens with the gathering. The book closes with the gathering. The architecture is internally seamless.

This volume restores Jubilees to the awakening reader the way it deserves to be restored. The text is R.H. Charles’ 1917 translation — the same translator whose Enoch you may already have on your shelf — modernized into present-day English, scrubbed of the apparatus brackets and obelisks that mark a critical edition rather than serve a reader, and corrected at the specific places where Charles’ theology imposed itself on his word choice. The sacred names are restored in the body of every chapter, with the conventional English in parentheses on every mention so the reader meeting them for the first time is never lost. The sustained commentary that follows each chapter is in the voice of the Remnant of Promise series — patient toward the deceived, plain about the systems, and never trying to write a rulebook over the reader’s life. The book’s job is to lift the lie off the text and let Jubilees say what it actually says. What the reader does with it next is between the reader and the Father.

A word about the four houses. The horn’s pulpit has spent seventeen centuries telling the people in the pews that the Sabbath is a Yahudi (Jewish) ordinance, the festivals are obsolete, the Torah is the curse, the holy seed of Jacob has been replaced by the church, and the prophets’ specific promises of restoration have been spiritualized into a metaphor. None of that is in this book. None of that has ever been in this book. The grandmothers and grandfathers in the pews who have loved the Father with what they were given are not the enemies of this book. They are also the lost sheep. The system that gave them what they were given is what this book — like every book in this series — exists to dismantle. The same patience extends to the synagogue, where the body of Yahudah (Judah) has built its own walls against the plain reading of the prophets concerning the Messiah (Christ) and the gathering of the scattered house; to the Hebrew roots and Messianic communities, where Christian replacement theology often persists in Torah clothing as a doctrine that anyone who keeps the commandments is thereby Yashar’el (Israel) regardless of bloodline — a teaching the prophets and Jubilees both refuse; and to the houses of Islam, where the same denial wears a different costume. All four houses, in different ways, share one foundational lie: that there are no scattered tribes coming home, that the binary is Jew and everybody else, that the prophetic gathering is finished or transferred or metaphorical or replaced. The lie is the antichrist. The people inside the houses are victims. Jubilees was set aside because it makes the lie too plain to maintain. This volume returns the book to where it has always belonged — in the hands of awakening believers who are reading the prophets without the lens.

Read it as one continuous piece if you can. The book is short. The arc is one arc. The gathering at chapter 1 is the gathering at chapter 50, and the chapters in between are the architecture by which Yahuah (God) brought the body from Adam to Sinai and is, in this season, bringing the body from the long exile home. If you have come to this book with the lens of any of the four houses still on you, the commentary will press the lens back where it can be removed. If you have already put the lens down, the commentary will sharpen what you have been seeing. Either way, the text is the text. The book is older than the things that opposed it. The witness has not retired. Read.
