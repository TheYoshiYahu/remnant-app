# Commentary on John 1

John 1 is the canon's most concentrated statement of the nature of Yahuah (God) — the Formless source and his Formed expression in the Word who took on flesh. The chapter opens with eighteen verses of prologue laid against the opening lines of Genesis, walks the prologue out into the wilderness with John-the-Witness, lands at the *Lamb of Elohim* recognition at the Yarden, transfers the first disciples from the Witness to Yahusha (Jesus), and closes at the Bethel-Ladder identification that ties the Messiah's title back to Jacob's dream at Beit-El. The inherited Trinitarian reading flattens the prologue into three-co-equal-persons inside a hundred years of the Council of Nicaea (325 CE); the framework restores what the Greek of vv.1-18 actually says, with the Sinai self-disclosure of Exodus 34:6 carried as the load-bearing substance of v.14 made flesh.

---

## §1. *In the beginning was the Word* — the Formless and the Formed in three statements (vv.1-2)

John opens his gospel with a deliberate echo of the canon's first line.

*In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with Elohim (God), and the Word was Elohim (God). The same was in the beginning with Elohim (God).* (John 1:1-2)

*In the beginning Elohim (God) created the heaven and the earth.* (Genesis 1:1)

The Greek of John's opening — *en archē ēn ho Logos* — is built on the Septuagint's translation of the Hebrew *bereshit* in Genesis 1:1. John is signaling at the first syllable that what follows is a creation account, a *toledot* in the same literary register as Genesis 1. Matthew opened his gospel with *biblos genéseōs* — *the book of the generation* — echoing the next *toledot* of the Tanakh:

*This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that Elohim (God) created man, in the likeness of Elohim (God) made he him.* (Genesis 5:1)

The two gospels open at the two opening points of the Hebrew canon — the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the generations of Adam. John's opening is the eternal-side companion to Matthew's. Together they bracket the Messiah's identity at the deepest layer the Hebrew canon offers.

What was *in the beginning?* The Word. *Logos* in the Greek; *dabar* or *memra* in the Hebrew and the Aramaic of the targumic tradition the synagogue audience would have carried as background. The targums of the first century routinely rendered the divine action-name of Genesis 1 with the phrase *the memra of Yahuah (the LORD)* — the Word of Yahuah — as the active agent through whom Yahuah speaks the heavens and the earth into being.

*And Elohim (God) said, Let there be light: and there was light.* (Genesis 1:3)

The Aramaic targum reads this as *and the memra of Yahuah said.* John is not introducing a new philosophical concept at v.1. He is naming what the Hebrew canon and the targumic tradition already held: there is one who speaks the world, and that one is the Word.

The three statements of vv.1-2 are precise. Each one layers a distinct claim.

**The Word was in the beginning.** Pre-existent at the moment of creation. Not made within creation. The Greek imperfect *ēn* — *was* — is durative; the Word *was being.* The Word was already there.

**The Word was with Elohim.** Distinct from Elohim — *pros ton theon,* face-to-face with, alongside. Not collapsed into Elohim. The Word is its own subject. There is a relationship in view: the Word stands in relation to Elohim, and the relation precedes creation.

**The Word was Elohim.** The Word *was* — same predicate *ēn* — Elohim. Not *a god* (the Watchtower / Jehovah's Witnesses rendering), and not *one of three co-equal persons within a singular divine nature* (the Nicene Trinitarian rendering). The grammar is precise: the Word *was* Elohim, and the Word *was with* Elohim. Both, simultaneously. The Word is the Formed expression of the Formless source. Yahuah is one — that is the *Shema:*

*Hear, O Yashar'el (Israel): Yahuah Eloheinu (The LORD our God) is one Yahuah (LORD).* (Deuteronomy 6:4)

And within that oneness, the Formless source has expressed himself in a Formed Word — distinct enough to be *with* the Formless, yet itself *is* the divine being, because it is the self-expression of the same divine being. The Word is not a second god. The Word is the Formed expression of the Formless divine being who is one.

The redundancy of v.2 is rhetorical. John repeats his first two claims in a single restated sentence — *the same was in the beginning with Elohim* — to lock the reader. The Word is pre-existent. The Word is *with* Elohim. The Word *is* Elohim. And the Word is distinct from the Father. All four claims, simultaneously.

The inherited Trinitarian reading collapses these claims into the Nicene formulation of three-co-equal-persons-in-one-divine-substance (*homoousios*). The framework reads them differently: one Yahuah, Formless in source, Formed in expression. The Father is the Formless. The Word is the Formed. The Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) is the moving presence of Yahuah within both the Formless and the Formed and the creation he speaks into being. There are not three persons; there is one Yahuah revealing himself in Formless source and in Formed expression and in the moving Spirit.

The Tanakh already had this architecture. Every theophany names the Formed one — *the angel of Yahuah,* who *is* Yahuah and yet *speaks for* Yahuah. Abraham speaks with Yahuah at the oaks of Mamre. Jacob wrestles with Elohim at the Yabbok and calls the place Peniel — *I have seen Elohim face to face, and my life is preserved* (Genesis 32:30). Moses stands before the burning bush. The pattern is consistent: Yahuah appears, speaks, and acts — and the One who appears, speaks, and acts is the Formed. The Formless source is never seen.

*And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.* (Exodus 33:20)

This is the architecture John is naming. The Formed Word, pre-existent and *with* the Formless Father, is the One the Tanakh has been showing all along.

The Formless-Formed reading also clears what the Nicene reading muddies — the eternal submission of the Son to the Father. The Word is the *expression* of the Father, drawn *from* the Father. Self-submission to the source is the relation's own form. *And the Word was with Elohim* names the relation; the Formed eternally proceeds from and submits to the Formless. This is not a hierarchy of inferiority; it is the relation's own architecture. The Trinitarian reading flattens this submission because it must — three co-equal persons cannot stand in ordered relation — and so the Nicene formula has spent seventeen centuries trying to explain away passages like:

*Ye have heard how I said unto you, I go away, and come again unto you. If ye loved me, ye would rejoice, because I said, I go unto the Father: for my Father is greater than I.* (John 14:28)

*But of that day and that hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.* (Matthew 24:36)

The Formless-Formed reading does not require these passages to be explained away. The Son is the Formed expression of the Formless Father, eternally proceeding from and submitting to the source he is drawn from. The passages read at face value.

This is the chapter's load-bearing weight. The whole rest of John 1 — and arguably the whole rest of the gospel of John — is the working-out of what these four claims mean. The Word will be made flesh. The Word will tabernacle among us. The Word will be named the Lamb of Elohim. The Word will be confessed as the Son of Elohim and the King of Yashar'el. The Word will be identified as the Ladder of Jacob's Beit-El dream. Every framework anchor downstream rests on the prologue's Formless-Formed grid. Get vv.1-2 wrong and the chapter cannot land. Get them right and the chapter walks the reader straight into the substance of who Yahusha is.

## §2. *All things were made by him* — the Formed as the agent of creation (v.3)

*All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.* (John 1:3)

The Formed is the agent through whom the Formless speaks the heavens and the earth into being. The targumic tradition was already there: *bereshit bara Elohim* (Genesis 1:1) is read in the synagogue as *the memra of Yahuah* creating. John locks the same reading. The Word is not only pre-existent but creatively active at the moment of creation. Every creature, every star, every breath, every formed thing — by the Word.

The wider canon names the same architecture in multiple voices.

*By the word of Yahuah (LORD) were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth.* (Psalm 33:6)

*For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: and he is before all things, and by him all things consist.* (Colossians 1:16-17)

*Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds; who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power.* (Hebrews 1:2-3)

*But to us there is but one Elohim (God), the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him; and one Yahuah (Lord) Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ), by whom are all things, and we by him.* (1 Corinthians 8:6)

The Tanakh names *Yahuah* (the LORD) as the creator and John, Paul, and the writer of Hebrews all name the Formed Word as the agent through whom Yahuah creates. There is no contradiction. There is one Yahuah — Formless in source, Formed in agency. The Word is how Yahuah creates. The Word is how Yahuah is heard at the burning bush. The Word is how Yahuah descends on Sinai. The Word is what Isaiah saw:

*In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also Yahuah (the LORD) sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple.* (Isaiah 6:1)

The Word is what Jacob wrestled with at the Yabbok. The Word is what John saw on Patmos. The Word is the visible One. The Formless never appears; the Formed is everywhere the Tanakh records a theophany.

The doubled clause of v.3 — *and without him was not any thing made that was made* — closes the gap any Gnostic reading would try to open. There is no created thing that bypasses the Word. The Demiurge of Gnostic cosmology — a lesser creator-god who makes the material world while the true God remains far above — is ruled out by this verse. The Formed is the agent through whom every created thing is made. The material world is not a corruption from which the soul must escape; it is the creation of the Word, and the Word will himself enter that creation in flesh at v.14.

## §3. *In him was life; and the life was the light of men* — the Genesis echo and the cosmic conflict (vv.4-5)

*In him was life; and the life was the light of men. And the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not.* (John 1:4-5)

Two claims, with a contrast.

**The first claim.** *In him was life.* The Word is the source of life within the creation he made. The Greek *zōē* answers to the Hebrew *chayim* of Genesis 2:7, where Yahuah breathes the breath of lives into the dust of Adam. The Word is the channel through which the breath of lives flows into the creature. Adam is alive because the Word breathes; every man who breathes is breathing the breath of the Formed One who is in him.

**The second claim.** *And the life was the light of men.* The life-from-the-Word is the *light* — the *or* of Genesis 1:3, the light Yahuah speaks into being on day one of creation, before the sun and moon and stars are made on day four.

*And Elohim (God) said, Let there be light: and there was light.* (Genesis 1:3)

That light is what John is naming as the life-from-the-Word that is the light of men. It is the light that grants moral and rational seeing, the light that lets a creature recognize the Creator, the light that makes the *image of Elohim* in Adam (Genesis 1:26-27) operative.

**The contrast.** *And the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not.* The Greek *katalambanō* — *comprehended* — carries the double sense of *grasped* (understood) and *seized* (overpowered). The light shines, and the darkness neither understands it nor overpowers it. This is the cosmic conflict made visible in one verse. The darkness is the realm of the Watcher-rebellion (Genesis 6:1-4; 1 Enoch 6-16), the kingdoms-of-this-age that the canon names as currently under fallen administration, the seed of the serpent that runs alongside the seed of Adam through history.

*And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.* (Genesis 3:15)

The light shines into the darkness at Sinai, in the prophets, in the Messianic ministry that is about to open at v.14 — and the darkness cannot grasp it, cannot defeat it, but also cannot be reasoned into recognizing it. The conflict is older than Adam. The light's shining is what the John-the-Witness section is about to walk out — the voice in the wilderness preparing the way of Yahuah.

The inherited Christian commentary tends to read v.5 as a poetic flourish — *the light shines and the darkness cannot stop it* — and stop there. The framework reads the darkness as a specific agency. The light-in-darkness verse is naming the conflict the rest of John walks out: the Word's coming in flesh is the light entering the darkness — into the world the kingdoms-of-this-age now administer — and the darkness will respond with everything it has: the rejection of the people, the betrayal, the cross. The darkness will not comprehend the light. The darkness will also not overpower it. The Resurrection of chapter 20 is the same claim John locks at v.5: the darkness *katelaben ouk* — did not seize, did not overpower, did not understand.

## §4. *There was a man sent from Elohim, whose name was John* — the first witness, introduced before the Logos becomes flesh (vv.6-9)

*There was a man sent from Elohim (God), whose name was John. The same came for a witness, to bear witness of the Light, that all men through him might believe. He was not that Light, but was sent to bear witness of that Light. That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.* (John 1:6-9)

John-the-evangelist breaks the prologue's eternity-substance to introduce John-the-Witness — the prophet known to Christian readers as John the Baptist — before the prologue completes its arc into v.14's incarnation. The structural choice matters. The Witness is named at vv.6-9 *before* the Word is made flesh at v.14. The reader hears about the witness before the reader hears about what the witness will witness. This is deliberate. The prophetic preparation of the way precedes the arrival of the One whose way is prepared.

The Witness stands in the line of the prophets of the Tanakh — Elijah most prominently, per Malachi:

*Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and Yahuah (Lord), whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts).* (Malachi 3:1)

*Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of Yahuah (LORD): and he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.* (Malachi 4:5-6)

Yahusha will name the Witness as the fulfillment of this prophecy:

*And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come.* (Matthew 11:14)

The Witness's role is the bearing of witness. He is not the Light. He is the one who points to the Light. John-the-evangelist explicitly distinguishes the Witness from the Light at v.8 and will lock the distinction twice more at v.20 and v.30. The first-century reader needed this lock. The followers of John-the-Witness who remained loyal to him after his death — still a recognizable group at Ephesus when Paul met them years later (Acts 19:1-7) — needed the distinction. The Witness was a witness, not the One. The role of the Witness is the role of the prophet: to point.

The framework reads v.9 carefully. *That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.* The Christian universalist reading takes this as *every man without exception is enlightened by the Light* — a doctrine of universal salvation or universal access. The framework reads it differently: the Light is the *true* Light — *alēthinos,* genuine, real, as opposed to false lights. The Light *lights* every man who comes into the world — meaning the Light is the universal source of whatever light any man has, in the same sense that the sun lights every man who steps outside. This does not mean every man receives the Light's saving work, any more than the sun's existence means every man chooses to walk in its illumination. The Light shines on all; the response divides. The chapter itself walks this out in vv.10-13: the Light comes into the world, the world does not know him, and the receiving-or-not-receiving divides those who become the sons of Elohim from those who do not. The Light shines on all men; not all men receive him.

## §5. *He came unto his own, and his own received him not* — the reception failure and the elect revealing (vv.10-13)

*He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not. He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of Elohim (God), even to them that believe on his name: which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of Elohim (God).* (John 1:10-13)

These four verses carry the chapter's strongest statement of who the sons of Elohim are and how they become so. The framework's cause-and-effect reversal — *election precedes hearing; lineage precedes response; you did not choose to hear and become; you heard because you are these things, having been born of Elohim before your hearing* — is named here at clinical clarity. The Christian decisionism reading inverts the cause-and-effect; the framework reads vv.10-13 as the inversion being corrected at the front of the gospel.

*He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not* (v.10). The Word's pre-existence and creative agency from v.3 is recalled. The Word comes into the very world he made, and the world does not know him. *Know* — *ginōskō* — is the relational knowing of covenant, not abstract acquaintance. The world does not enter into covenant-knowing with its own Creator when he walks among it. This is the cosmic conflict of v.5 stated in narrative form: the darkness did not comprehend the light; the world did not know its Maker.

*He came unto his own, and his own received him not* (v.11). The Greek is precise: *eis ta idia ēlthen, kai hoi idioi auton ou parelabon.* *Eis ta idia* — into his own (things, place, country) — names the territory of the seed of promise. *Hoi idioi* — his own (people, ones) — names the seed of promise as a body. The Messiah came into the land of the covenant and to the people of the covenant, and that people did not receive him.

The framework reads this against the Lo-Ammi architecture of Hosea 1.

*Then said Elohim (God), Call his name Lo-ammi: for ye are not my people, and I will not be your Elohim (God).* (Hosea 1:9)

*Yet the number of the children of Yashar'el (Israel) shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living Elohim (God).* (Hosea 1:10)

The *not receiving* of v.11 is the Lo-Ammi condition operative at the Messiah's first coming — not by ethnic erasure but by covenant disqualification through unfaithfulness, until the gathering restores. The northern house was scattered through the Assyrian captivity centuries before; the southern house held the land at the time of the first coming but held it under the system Matthew 23's seven woes will diagnose — the Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership that had overlaid the Torah with traditions making the commandment of none effect. His own did not receive him because his own had been overlaid by a system that could not see him. The first-coming rejection of v.11 is the leadership-system's rejection — the same system that organizes the betrayal, the Sanhedrin trial, the cross. The seed of promise within the leadership-system did not receive him. The seed of promise scattered through the nations is still being gathered; the receiving of v.12 is the receiving by those whose hearing reveals them.

*But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of Elohim (God), even to them that believe on his name* (v.12). The Christian decisionism reading takes this verse as the proof-text for *receive Christ and become a child of God by your decision.* The framework reads it inside the architecture of v.13, which immediately follows.

*Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of Elohim (God)* (v.13). The Greek is unambiguous. The sons of Elohim of v.12 *were born* — passive — *not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of Elohim.* The new birth that v.12 names is the work of Elohim, not the work of the believing creature. The receiving of v.12 is the response that reveals what the begetting of v.13 has already done. The sons of Elohim are *born of Elohim* — the Father's begetting — and their receiving of the Light is the evidence of the begetting, not the cause of it.

The same cause-and-effect runs through John's gospel itself. Yahusha says it directly in chapter 10:

*But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.* (John 10:26-27)

They hear because they are sheep — not become sheep because they hear. The Christian decisionism reading inverts this and says *if you hear and confess, you become a child of Yahuah.* John 1:13 explicitly rules that reading out. The Light that came into the world did not make sons of Elohim out of nothing. The Light came and the sons of Elohim, born of Elohim, recognized their Father and received their Brother.

This is what the wild olive branches of Romans 11 are walking into. The natural branches of the olive tree are the covenant lineage of the patriarchs and the faithful prior generations, asleep awaiting the first resurrection; the wild olive branches are the descendants of the broken-off who hear when the Shepherd calls — they hear because they are sheep, not become sheep because they hear. The hearing reveals; it does not create. The sons of Elohim are written in the book from the foundation of the world.

*And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.* (Revelation 13:8)

*According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love.* (Ephesians 1:4)

The receiving of v.12 is the audible side of an inaudible work the Father did before the foundation of the world. The decisionism reading puts the creature's will at the cause of the new birth; v.13 explicitly rules that reading out. The Word coming into the world did not manufacture sons of Elohim from those who were not. The Word came and the sons of Elohim, already born of Elohim, recognized their Father and received their Brother.

## §6. *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us* — Sinai chen-v'emet incarnate (v.14)

*And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.* (John 1:14)

This is the chapter's central verse and one of the most concentrated framework moments in canon.

**The Word was made flesh.** *Sarx egeneto* — became flesh. The Formed One, the agent of creation, the source of life and the light of men, took on the same human substance the creature he made carries. The Christian docetism — the early heresy that the Word only *appeared* to be flesh but was not really flesh — is ruled out at this verse. The Word *became* flesh. John will lock the same claim in his first letter:

*Hereby know ye the Spirit of Elohim (God): Every spirit that confesseth that Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ) is come in the flesh is of Elohim (God): and every spirit that confesseth not that Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ) is come in the flesh is not of Elohim (God): and this is that spirit of antichrist, whereof ye have heard that it should come; and even now already is it in the world.* (1 John 4:2-3)

The flesh is real. The incarnation is real. The Word who was Elohim took on real human substance.

**And dwelt among us.** *Eskēnōsen en hēmin* — literally *tabernacled among us.* The Greek verb *skēnoō* is built on *skēnē,* the Septuagint's word for the *mishkan,* the tabernacle of the wilderness wandering. The Word did not *live* among us in the generic sense; the Word *tabernacled* among us — set up the mishkan in his own flesh and dwelt as Yahuah dwelt in the wilderness tabernacle.

*And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.* (Exodus 25:8)

*Then a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of Yahuah (LORD) filled the tabernacle. And Moses was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of Yahuah (LORD) filled the tabernacle.* (Exodus 40:34-35)

*Afterward he brought me to the gate, even the gate that looketh toward the east: and, behold, the glory of the Elohim (God) of Yashar'el (Israel) came from the way of the east: and his voice was like a noise of many waters: and the earth shined with his glory.* (Ezekiel 43:1-2)

The same architecture closes the canon at the new creation.

*And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of Elohim (God) is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and Elohim (God) himself shall be with them, and be their Elohim (God).* (Revelation 21:3)

The Word's tabernacling in flesh at v.14 is the same architecture the Tanakh walked out at Sinai, in the wilderness, in the temple, and the same architecture Revelation closes with at the new creation. The flesh of Yahusha is the mishkan of Yahuah dwelling among men. The Sukkot connection is also in view — the Feast of Tabernacles — when Yashar'el commemorates the wilderness tabernacling each year.

*Speak unto the children of Yashar'el (Israel), saying, The fifteenth day of this seventh month shall be the feast of tabernacles for seven days unto Yahuah (LORD).* (Leviticus 23:34)

*That your generations may know that I made the children of Yashar'el (Israel) to dwell in booths, when I brought them out of the land of Egypt: I am Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 23:43)

The Word's tabernacling at v.14 is the Tanakh-rooted Sukkot fulfillment substance. The chapter opens the gospel of John, and the gospel of John will walk Yahusha into the Sukkot feast in chapter 7 where the same tabernacling-flesh-of-the-Word will offer himself as the living water.

**(And we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father.)** The parenthetical clause names the witness John-the-evangelist himself is one of. *We beheld* — *etheasametha* — the same root as *theater,* the gazed-upon spectacle. They saw. They watched. They were there. The glory they beheld was *as of the only begotten of the Father* — *hōs monogenous para patros.* *Monogenēs* — only-begotten, only-of-its-kind, unique. There is no second such expression.

The Father has named the Son at the baptism, on the mountain of the Transfiguration, and at the entry of the passion week — three times.

*And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.* (Matthew 3:17)

*While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.* (Matthew 17:5)

*Then came there a voice from heaven, saying, I have both glorified it, and will glorify it again.* (John 12:28)

The glory of v.14 — *as of the only begotten of the Father* — is the glory the Father has been naming with his own voice over the Son. The Father-Son relation in the Formless-Formed architecture is what the parenthetical of v.14 carries.

**Full of grace and truth.** *Plērēs charitos kai alētheias.* This phrase carries the load. It echoes the Sinai self-disclosure of Exodus 34 — the moment when Yahuah passed before Moses on the mountain and proclaimed his own name.

*And Yahuah (LORD) descended in the cloud, and stood with him there, and proclaimed the name of Yahuah (LORD). And Yahuah (LORD) passed by before him, and proclaimed, Yahuah (LORD), Yahuah Elohim (The LORD God), merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty.* (Exodus 34:5-7)

The Hebrew is *rav-chesed v'emet* — abundant in *chesed* (covenant-loyal love, often translated grace) and *emet* (truth, faithfulness). John's *plērēs charitos kai alētheias* — *full of grace and truth* — names the Word made flesh in the same pair Yahuah proclaimed of himself on Sinai. The same Yahuah who spoke Exodus 34 to Moses on the mountain is now standing in flesh, full of the same chen-v'emet that the voice on the mountain proclaimed.

The same chen-v'emet pair runs through the Psalms.

*But thou, O Yahuah (Lord), art an Elohim (God) full of compassion, and gracious, longsuffering, and plenteous in mercy and truth.* (Psalm 86:15)

*Yahuah (LORD) is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.* (Psalm 103:8)

This is the Formless-Formed grid in flesh. The Voice of Sinai is the Word of v.14, and the chen-v'emet of the Voice is the chen-v'emet of the Word. The Word did not bring something new at v.14 that contradicted Sinai; the Word brought the Voice of Sinai *in flesh, in person, where his people could touch him.* This will become the framework's reading of v.17 — *the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Yahusha HaMashiach* — which the Reformation has weaponized against the Torah and which the framework restores to its proper meaning.

## §7. *He that cometh after me is preferred before me: for he was before me* — the Witness's testimony and the grace upon grace of v.16

*John bare witness of him, and cried, saying, This was he of whom I spake, He that cometh after me is preferred before me: for he was before me. And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace. For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ).* (John 1:15-17)

The Witness's testimony at v.15 makes three claims, each one a piece of the Formless-Formed architecture stated by the man who came to point at the Word.

**He that cometh after me.** Chronologically, in terms of public appearance, Yahusha appears after the Witness on the public stage. The Witness's wilderness baptizing precedes the opening of Yahusha's ministry.

**Is preferred before me.** *Emprosthen mou gegonen* — literally *has come to be ahead of me.* The Witness names the Messiah as ranked ahead of himself in honor and authority.

**For he was before me.** *Prōtos mou ēn* — literally *he was first of me,* meaning he was prior to me, before me in existence. The pre-existence claim of vv.1-2 is named by the Witness's own mouth. The man six months older than Yahusha in the flesh (Luke 1:36) calls Yahusha *before me* — locating Yahusha's existence before his own. The Witness names what the prologue already stated: the Word was in the beginning.

*And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace* (v.16). *Charin anti charitos* — *grace in exchange for grace,* or *grace upon grace.* The Greek *anti* can carry the sense of substitution-exchange or sequence. The framework reading hears this as the homecoming-grace architecture: the grace that the Word brings is not a different substance than the grace the Tanakh already carried; it is the same chen-v'emet of Sinai now flowing in an ongoing fullness, *grace upon grace,* the Word's chen replacing-as-it-fulfills the chen that was always already there. This is grace as the means of return — not the Reformation's sola-fide grace standing against the Torah, but the chen that brings the homecomer back to the Way.

*Of his fulness have all we received* — the fulness of the Word, made flesh, full of chen-v'emet, is the source from which we receive grace upon grace. The fulness flows; the receiver drinks; the grace is the means by which the receiver is brought back home to the One who is full.

The framework reading guards against the Reformation sola-fide reading that takes *grace upon grace* as proof of *grace alone, apart from any continuing standard.* That reading runs straight into v.17 and the centuries-old weaponization the next section addresses.

## §8. *The law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Yahusha HaMashiach* — the verse the Reformation cut in half (v.17)

*For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ).* (John 1:17)

This is the most weaponized verse in the chapter, and one of the most weaponized verses in the New Testament. The Reformation has used v.17 as a one-verse summary of the entire *grace-replaces-law* gospel: the Torah was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Yahusha; the Torah is therefore obsolete; what was given by Moses is replaced by what came by the Messiah; the Christian is *not under law but under grace.* The framework restores what the verse actually says.

**The framework reading of v.17 turns on three observations.**

**First, the conjunction is paratactic, not adversative.** The English *but* in the KJV (and most translations) renders the Greek *de.* The Greek *de* is a soft connective, often translated *and* or *now* or *moreover.* It is paratactic — placing two clauses alongside each other — not adversative — pitting them against each other. *Alla* is the Greek adversative *but* (against, in opposition to); *de* is the conjunction-of-continuation. The verse can be read with grammatical fidelity as *the law was given by Moses, and grace and truth came by Yahusha HaMashiach.* The two clauses stand alongside each other, not against. The Reformation reading requires *alla* — the verse does not have *alla.* The verse has *de.* The Reformation's adversative *but* is a translation choice, not a grammatical necessity.

**Second, the chen-v'emet of v.17 is the chen-v'emet of v.14, which is the chen-v'emet of Exodus 34:6.** The verse just before v.17 said *the Word was made flesh ... full of grace and truth.* The verse before that said *grace upon grace.* The chen-v'emet of v.17 is not introduced as a new substance arriving with the Messiah; it is the substance already named at v.14 as the chen-v'emet of Sinai *in flesh.*

*Yahuah (LORD), Yahuah Elohim (The LORD God), merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.* (Exodus 34:6)

The Sinai self-disclosure is the chen-v'emet the Messiah brings. The Messiah did not introduce chen-v'emet for the first time at the cross; the Messiah brought the chen-v'emet *that has been Yahuah's name since Sinai* in flesh to where his people could touch him. The Torah and the chen-v'emet are not two substances; they are the same Voice, the same Yahuah, named in two registers — Torah as the instruction the chen-v'emet gives to its covenant people, chen-v'emet as the relational fidelity Torah is given within.

**Third, the verse names the agent of each, not the opposition.** Moses was the agent through whom the Torah was *given* — *edothē,* passive aorist, *was given.* Moses did not author the Torah; he was the agent through whom Yahuah delivered it. Yahusha HaMashiach is the agent through whom chen-v'emet *came* — *egeneto,* aorist middle, *came to be.* The Messiah did not author chen-v'emet; he is the agent through whom the chen-v'emet of Sinai stepped into flesh. The verse names two agencies — Moses's role in the giving of Torah, Yahusha's role in the bringing of chen-v'emet in flesh — and the two agencies are coordinated, not opposed. The same Yahuah who spoke through Moses on Sinai is Yahuah who walked in flesh as Yahusha; the Torah and the chen-v'emet flow from the same source.

**What v.17 rules out, then, is the inverted reading that has dominated Christian preaching:** the reading that takes Moses's Torah as the bad thing the Messiah arrived to abolish, and the chen-v'emet of the Messiah as the better thing that replaces it. The Messiah himself rules out that reading at the opening of his public teaching.

*Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.* (Matthew 5:17-18)

The Messiah closes his Matthean teaching with the same anti-abolition substance at the Great Commission.

*Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.* (Matthew 28:20)

The Messiah loves the Torah his Father gave through Moses. He says so directly.

*If ye love me, keep my commandments.* (John 14:15)

*If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love.* (John 15:10)

His brother locks the same architecture in his own letter.

*And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.* (1 John 2:3-4)

*For this is the love of Elohim (God), that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.* (1 John 5:3)

The same Torah-affirming substance lives in Paul's letters where the Christian preacher most often denies it.

*Do we then make void the law through faith? Elohim (God) forbid: yea, we establish the law.* (Romans 3:31)

*Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.* (Romans 7:12)

The framework reads v.17 as naming the Tanakh-rooted continuity: the same Voice that gave Torah on Sinai is the Voice that came in flesh, and the chen-v'emet the Voice carries has been the same Voice's chen-v'emet since Exodus 34:6. The Torah and the chen-v'emet are not in competition. The Torah is the chen-v'emet's gift of instruction to its people; the chen-v'emet is the relational fidelity within which the Torah is given. The Messiah's coming in flesh did not abolish the Torah; his coming brought the Voice that gave the Torah into flesh where the Voice could now walk it out in person.

## §9. *No man hath seen Elohim at any time* — the Formless never visible, the Formed declares him (v.18)

*No man hath seen Elohim (God) at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.* (John 1:18)

The verse that closes the prologue is the Formless-Formed grid stated at clinical clarity.

*No man hath seen Elohim at any time* — *theon oudeis heōraken pōpote.* The Formless is never visible. The Hebrew canon has the same architecture.

*And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.* (Exodus 33:20)

Yahuah the Father, the Formless source, is never visible to creature-sight. Every theophany in the Tanakh — the angel of Yahuah wrestling Jacob at the Yabbok, Yahuah who stands at the door of Abraham's tent at the oaks of Mamre, Yahuah of the burning bush, Yahuah who walks in the garden in the cool of the day, Yahuah whose glory fills the tabernacle, Yahuah Isaiah sees high and lifted up, Yahuah whose chariot Ezekiel sees by the river of Chebar — every one of these is the Formed. The Formless is never the visible One. The Formed is.

*The only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.* The Formed *declares* — *exēgēsato,* the Greek root of *exegete,* the one who exposits, who reveals what is otherwise hidden. The Formed Son is the exegete of the Formless Father. The Son is *in the bosom of the Father* — *en tō kolpō tou Patros,* the position of intimate access. The same image returns in chapter 13.

*Now there was leaning on Yahusha's (Jesus's) bosom one of his disciples, whom Yahusha (Jesus) loved.* (John 13:23)

The Father has his Son in his bosom; the disciple has Yahusha in his bosom. The pattern is the relational intimacy of one drawn from the chest of the other. The Formed proceeds from the Formless, dwells in the bosom of the Formless, and exposits the Formless to creatures who cannot see the Formless directly.

This is the Formless-Formed grid at its clearest. The Tanakh has been saying the same thing all along. The John prologue lands it at v.18 as the closing thesis statement of the eighteen-verse argument: the Word was Elohim, was with Elohim, was made flesh, dwelt among us, full of chen-v'emet — and what he is *doing* is *declaring the Formless Father whom no creature can see.* The Messianic mission is the Formed declaring the Formless to the creature.

## §10. The interrogation in the wilderness — the Witness's threefold denial and the voice of Isaiah 40 (vv.19-28)

*And this is the record of John, when the Yahudim (Jews) sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, Who art thou? And he confessed, and denied not; but confessed, I am not the Messiah (Christ). And they asked him, What then? Art thou Elias? And he saith, I am not. Art thou that prophet? And he answered, No. Then said they unto him, Who art thou? that we may give an answer to them that sent us. What sayest thou of thyself? He said, I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of Yahuah (Lord), as said the prophet Esaias. And they which were sent were of the Pharisees. And they asked him, and said unto him, Why baptizest thou then, if thou be not that Messiah (Christ), nor Elias, neither that prophet? John answered them, saying, I baptize with water: but there standeth one among you, whom ye know not; he it is, who coming after me is preferred before me, whose shoe's latchet I am not worthy to unloose. These things were done in Bethabara beyond Jordan, where John was baptizing.* (John 1:19-28)

The prologue closes at v.18 and the narrative opens at v.19 with the interrogation of John-the-Witness by *the Yahudim — priests and Levites from Jerusalem.* The framework reads the interrogation carefully against the system-not-people posture. The *Yahudim* of v.19 is the leadership-system in Jerusalem — the same Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership Matthew 23's seven woes will diagnose — not the Yahudi (Jewish) people as a body. The framework is pro-Yahudah without qualification; the framework dismantles the leadership-system that has overlaid the Torah with traditions making the commandment of none effect. The interrogation is the system's institutional response to the Witness's wilderness baptism. They send their delegation to find out who he claims to be.

**The first denial.** *I am not the Messiah.* *Egō ouk eimi ho Christos.* The first-century reader needed this lock as much as the followers of the Witness at Ephesus did. The Witness himself denied the claim that would have made him the center of the gathering movement his wilderness ministry was producing.

**The second denial.** *Art thou Elias?* The reference is Malachi.

*Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of Yahuah (LORD).* (Malachi 4:5)

The Yahudim's leadership knew the Malachi prophecy and was asking whether this wilderness baptizer was Elijah-returned. The Witness says no. But Yahusha himself will name the Witness as *the Elijah who was to come.*

*And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come.* (Matthew 11:14)

The two statements are not contradictory. The Witness denies the identification because he is not the resurrected-prophet Elijah returned in his own person; Yahusha affirms the identification because the Witness comes *in the spirit and power of Elijah,* as Gabriel named at the Witness's annunciation (Luke 1:17). The Witness is the Malachi 4 Elijah-substance in operation, not the physical Elijah resurrected. The Witness's denial is precise; Yahusha's affirmation is also precise; both are right.

**The third denial.** *Art thou that prophet?* The reference is Deuteronomy 18.

*Yahuah Elohayka (The LORD thy God) will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken.* (Deuteronomy 18:15)

The prophet-like-Moses was a recognized first-century Messianic expectation. The Witness denies that he is that prophet. The prophet-like-Moses is Yahusha. Peter names this directly in Acts.

*For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall Yahuah (Lord) your Elohim (God) raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.* (Acts 3:22)

The Witness is the preparer of the way, not the Prophet himself.

**The self-identification.** *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of Yahuah (Lord).* The Witness names his own role from Isaiah 40, and the whole Isaiah 40 context is the comfort-of-the-gathering proclamation that opens the post-exile section of Isaiah.

*Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your Elohim (God). Speak ye comfortably to Jerusalem, and cry unto her, that her warfare is accomplished, that her iniquity is pardoned: for she hath received of the LORD'S hand double for all her sins. The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of Yahuah (LORD), make straight in the desert a highway for our Elohim (God).* (Isaiah 40:1-3)

The Witness situates himself in Isaiah 40's gathering substance. He is the voice of the wilderness-preparation that precedes the comforting of Jerusalem and the gathering of the scattered. Every subsequent move in John 1 — the Lamb of Elohim at v.29, the Spirit-descending at v.32, the first disciples called at vv.35-42, the Cephas-renaming at v.42, the Natan'el confession at v.49, the Bethel-Ladder identification at v.51 — is the gathering being inaugurated.

**The Pharisaic delegation.** *And they which were sent were of the Pharisees* (v.24). The verse names the delegation's affiliation. The Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership reads baptism as a Messianic ritual. The Witness's wilderness baptism does not fit their institutional Torah-tradition; they want to know by what authority he performs it. The Witness points to the One they cannot see.

*I baptize with water: but there standeth one among you, whom ye know not; he it is, who coming after me is preferred before me, whose shoe's latchet I am not worthy to unloose.* (John 1:26-27)

The Messiah is *among you* — already present in their midst — *whom ye know not.* The seeing-or-not-seeing-the-Light architecture from v.10 is operative here. The Light is shining in the darkness; the darkness does not comprehend it. The loosening of the latchet is the work of the lowest servant; the Witness names himself as not worthy of even that. The Messiah's rank is so far above his that the Witness positions himself below the servant who unties the sandal.

**The geography.** *These things were done in Bethabara beyond Jordan, where John was baptizing.* Bethabara — *beit-avarah* in Hebrew, *house of the crossing* — sits at the place where Yashar'el crossed the Yarden into the land under Yehoshua (Joshua).

*And it came to pass, when the people removed from their tents, to pass over Yarden (Jordan), and the priests bearing the ark of the covenant before the people; and as they that bare the ark were come unto Yarden (Jordan), and the feet of the priests that bare the ark were dipped in the brim of the water, (for Yarden (Jordan) overfloweth all his banks all the time of harvest,) that the waters which came down from above stood and rose up upon an heap very far from the city Adam, that is beside Zaretan: and those that came down toward the sea of the plain, even the salt sea, failed, and were cut off: and the people passed over right against Jericho.* (Joshua 3:14-16)

The Witness baptizes at the crossing-point of the original conquest. The Messianic ministry is inaugurated at the same crossing where the seed of promise first entered the land. The pattern of crossing-into-the-land is being relaunched in the wilderness of the Yarden. The gathering is being inaugurated at the geographic site of the original conquest.

## §11. *Behold the Lamb of Elohim, which taketh away the sin of the world* — Pesach, Akedah, Isaiah 53, daily tamid (vv.29 + 36)

*The next day John seeth Yahusha (Jesus) coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of Elohim (God), which taketh away the sin of the world.* (John 1:29)

*And looking upon Yahusha (Jesus) as he walked, he saith, Behold the Lamb of Elohim (God)!* (John 1:36)

The Witness's identification at v.29 — repeated in shortened form at v.36 — is one of the most concentrated Tanakh-fulfillment moments in the canon. The phrase *Lamb of Elohim* (*ho amnos tou theou*) loads four distinct Tanakh-lamb substances into one identification.

**The Pesach lamb of Exodus 12.** The lamb whose blood on the doorposts caused the destroyer to pass over the household of Yashar'el on the night of the tenth plague.

*In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house.* (Exodus 12:3)

*And ye shall keep it up until the fourteenth day of the same month: and the whole assembly of the congregation of Yashar'el (Israel) shall kill it in the evening.* (Exodus 12:6)

*And they shall take of the blood, and strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post of the houses, wherein they shall eat it.* (Exodus 12:7)

*And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.* (Exodus 12:13)

The Pesach substance carries the deliverance-from-judgment architecture: the lamb's blood marks the household; the destroyer passes by; the firstborn lives. Paul names the connection directly.

*For even Yahusha HaMashiach (Christ) our passover is sacrificed for us.* (1 Corinthians 5:7)

The framework reads the Lamb of Elohim of v.29 as the Pesach fulfillment locked at the Last Supper of Matthew 26 and at the timing of the crucifixion in John 19. The Lamb of Elohim is the Pesach lamb of the world-gathering.

**The Akedah ram of Genesis 22.** *Yahuah Yireh* (Genesis 22:14) — *Yahuah will provide.* The ram caught in the thicket that Abraham offered in the place of Isaac.

*And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering? And Abraham said, My son, Elohim (God) will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.* (Genesis 22:7-8)

*And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son.* (Genesis 22:13)

*And Abraham called the name of that place Yahuah Yireh (Jehovah-jireh): as it is said to this day, In the mount of Yahuah (LORD) it shall be seen.* (Genesis 22:14)

The Akedah is the Tanakh's foundational substitution pattern: the Father provides the lamb; the lamb dies in the place of the son. At the Akedah, the son was spared because the ram was provided. At the Cross, the Son is offered because the world is to be gathered. The Akedah substance and the Pesach substance both load into v.29's identification.

**The Isaiah 53 lamb led to the slaughter.** The suffering servant of Isaiah 53 is named as a lamb.

*He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not. Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of Elohim (God), and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and Yahuah (LORD) hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.* (Isaiah 53:3-7)

*He poured out his soul unto death: and he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.* (Isaiah 53:12)

The Lamb of Elohim of John 1:29 is the Isaiah 53 lamb who bears the sin and the sorrow and the iniquity. Peter names the same connection in his first letter.

*Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; but with the precious blood of HaMashiach (Christ), as of a lamb without blemish and without spot: who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you.* (1 Peter 1:18-20)

**The daily tamid lamb of Exodus 29 and Numbers 28.** Two lambs offered every day on the tabernacle altar — one in the morning, one between the evenings — as the continual offering.

*Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar; two lambs of the first year day by day continually. The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even.* (Exodus 29:38-39)

*This is a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before Yahuah (LORD): where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee.* (Exodus 29:42)

*And there I will meet with the children of Yashar'el (Israel), and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory.* (Exodus 29:43)

The tamid substance is the ongoing covenant-maintenance offering, the daily rhythm of atonement and access. The framework reads the Lamb of Elohim as the fulfillment of the tamid: the Lamb whose offering is *not* repeated daily because his one offering accomplishes what the tamid pointed toward.

*And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: but this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of Elohim (God).* (Hebrews 10:11-12)

**Takes away the sin of the world.** *Ho airōn tēn hamartian tou kosmou.* The Greek verb *airō* carries the sense of *lifting up and carrying off* — bearing the sin away as the scapegoat of Yom Kippur bore the iniquities of Yashar'el out into the wilderness.

*And when he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, he shall bring the live goat: and Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Yashar'el (Israel), and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness: and the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the goat in the wilderness.* (Leviticus 16:20-22)

The Lamb of Elohim bears and carries off. The *world* — *kosmos* — is the territory of the scattered seed. The framework reads *world* here as the substantial territory where the citizens of the kingdom are scattered, not as the inherited Christian universal-salvation reading. *Takes away the sin of the world* names the Lamb's accomplishment for the gathered remnant scattered through the *kosmos.* The Lamb's atonement reaches every place the scattered seed has been driven; the Lamb's blood is for the gathering.

The Witness repeats the identification the next day at v.36 — *Behold the Lamb of Elohim!* — in front of two of his own disciples, and the two disciples follow Yahusha as a result. The Lamb-of-Elohim identification is what produces the first transfer of disciples from the Witness's ministry to Yahusha's. The framework reads this as the structural function of the Witness's pointing: he points to the Lamb, and his own disciples leave him to follow the Lamb. The Witness's joy at this is locked at chapter 3:

*He must increase, but I must decrease.* (John 3:30)

The Witness's whole ministry was the pointing; the pointing is fulfilled when his own people follow the One he pointed to.

## §12. The Spirit descending and the baptizer with Ruach HaKodesh (vv.32-34)

*And John bare record, saying, I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, and it abode upon him. And I knew him not: but he that sent me to baptize with water, the same said unto me, Upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending, and remaining on him, the same is he which baptizeth with the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit). And I saw, and bare record that this is the Son of Elohim (God).* (John 1:32-34)

Between the two Lamb-of-Elohim identifications, the Witness names what he saw at Yahusha's baptism: the Spirit descending and abiding upon him. The descent of the Spirit is in two registers in the framework.

**The Genesis 1 register.** *And the Spirit of Elohim (God) moved upon the face of the waters.* (Genesis 1:2). The Ruach Elohim hovered over the waters of the deep before the creation began. At Yahusha's baptism in the waters of the Yarden, the same Ruach descends and abides upon the Son. The new-creation substance is being inaugurated. The Yahusha-ministry is the new creation arriving in flesh; the Ruach who hovered over the first creation hovers over the Son whose mission inaugurates the new.

**The Noach flood register.** The dove of Genesis 8 — the dove Noach sent out, who returned with the olive leaf and then did not return at all, signaling that the flood-judgment was over and the new earth was ready.

*And the dove came in to him in the evening; and, lo, in her mouth was an olive leaf pluckt off: so Noah knew that the waters were abated from off the earth.* (Genesis 8:11)

The Spirit *like a dove* descending upon Yahusha is the new-flood-ending substance: the judgment-flood has passed, the dove returns with the olive, the new earth is at hand. The dove signal is the gathering substance signaling its inauguration.

*And I knew him not: but he that sent me to baptize with water, the same said unto me, Upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending, and remaining on him, the same is he which baptizeth with the Ruach HaKodesh.* The Witness names the sign Yahuah gave him for identifying the Messiah: the descent *and remaining* of the Spirit. The remaining is critical — the Spirit had come *upon* prophets and judges and kings in the Tanakh, but only episodically. The Messiah carries the Spirit abidingly. The abidingly-Spirit-anointed Messiah is the one who will baptize *with the Ruach HaKodesh* — pouring out the Spirit upon his people, as Joel and Ezekiel foretold.

*And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh; and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions: and also upon the servants and upon the handmaids in those days will I pour out my spirit.* (Joel 2:28-29)

*Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:25-27)

The Pentecost of Acts 2 is in view at v.33; the connection between John 1 and Acts 2 runs through the Spirit-anointed Lamb baptizing his people with the Spirit he abidingly carries.

*And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting. And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Ghost), and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.* (Acts 2:1-4)

*And I saw, and bare record that this is the Son of Elohim (God)* (v.34). The Witness's final identification. The Witness has named Yahusha as: the One preferred before him (v.27), the Lamb of Elohim (v.29), the One on whom the Spirit descends and remains (v.32), the Baptizer with the Ruach HaKodesh (v.33), and the Son of Elohim (v.34). Five identifications. The Witness's witness is complete.

## §13. *Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone* — the first disciples and the Matthew 16 foreshadowing (vv.35-42)

*Again the next day after John stood, and two of his disciples; and looking upon Yahusha (Jesus) as he walked, he saith, Behold the Lamb of Elohim (God)! And the two disciples heard him speak, and they followed Yahusha (Jesus). Then Yahusha (Jesus) turned, and saw them following, and saith unto them, What seek ye? They said unto him, Rabbi, (which is to say, being interpreted, Master,) where dwellest thou? He saith unto them, Come and see. They came and saw where he dwelt, and abode with him that day: for it was about the tenth hour. One of the two which heard John speak, and followed him, was Andrew, Simon Peter's brother. He first findeth his own brother Simon, and saith unto him, We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Messiah (Christ). And he brought him to Yahusha (Jesus). And when Yahusha (Jesus) beheld him, he said, Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone.* (John 1:35-42)

The first transfer of disciples from the Witness to Yahusha happens at vv.35-37: the Witness points to Yahusha as the Lamb of Elohim, and *the two disciples heard him speak, and they followed Yahusha.* The two are Andrew — named at v.40 — and one other, traditionally identified as John-the-evangelist himself (the unnamed-disciple convention runs through this gospel). Andrew finds his brother Simon and brings him to Yahusha at vv.41-42.

The Aramaic-to-Greek-to-English convention at v.41 is worth naming. *We have found the Messias.* The Greek *Messias* is the transliteration of the Aramaic *meshicha,* which transliterates the Hebrew *mashiach* — the Anointed One. The Greek *Christos* — the translation — means the same thing. The verse names the Hebrew-Aramaic word and translates it for the Greek reader.

*Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone* (v.42). The Cephas-renaming is the first foreshadowing of Matthew 16. The Aramaic *kefa* — rock or stone — is what Yahusha names Simon at the moment of meeting him. Yahusha sees Simon and renames him before any work has been done. The renaming is prophetic; the rock-substance is what Simon will become.

*When Yahusha (Jesus) came into the coasts of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, saying, Whom do men say that I the Son of Adam am? ... He saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am? And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Messiah (Christ), the Son of the living Elohim (God). And Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said unto him, Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-jona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven. And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church.* (Matthew 16:13, 15-18)

The Caesarea Philippi confession is the moment the rock-substance breaks through. Cephas at the first meeting becomes Peter the confessor at Caesarea Philippi. The framework reads the apostolic-stone architecture as built on the confession-rock — the confession Peter makes is what the assembly is built upon, not Peter's person as a sole-succession authority. The Roman Catholic flattening of this passage into the papal succession from Peter to the bishops of Rome is rejected by the framework, but the apostolic-stone architecture is preserved: Simon is named *kefa* at John 1:42, named *the rock* at Matthew 16:18, restored as the shepherd of the sheep at John 21:15-19. The chapter we are reading opens the arc that runs through the whole gospel.

## §14. *We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write* — the Tanakh-fulfillment substance at the disciple-to-disciple proclamation (vv.43-46)

*The day following Yahusha (Jesus) would go forth into Galilee, and findeth Philip, and saith unto him, Follow me. Now Philip was of Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter. Philip findeth Nathanael, and saith unto him, We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write, Yahusha (Jesus) of Nazareth, the son of Joseph. And Nathanael said unto him, Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth? Philip saith unto him, Come and see.* (John 1:43-46)

Philip's identification of Yahusha at v.45 carries one of the chapter's most concentrated statements of the Messianic mission as the fulfillment of the Tanakh.

*Of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write* — Philip names the Messiah as the fulfillment of the entire Tanakh-prophetic architecture. The Torah of Moses and the prophets (the Nevi'im) both spoke of him; Philip identifies Yahusha as their referent. This is the same anti-abolition substance the Messiah himself will name at the Sermon on the Mount.

*Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.* (Matthew 5:17)

The Messianic mission is the fulfillment of the Tanakh's own architecture. Philip's identification of Yahusha at v.45 names this from the first disciple-to-disciple proclamation. The Torah and the prophets pointed to the Messiah; the Messiah arrived; Philip identifies him.

*Yahusha of Nazareth, the son of Joseph* — Philip's identification carries two markers. *Of Nazareth* names the geographic origin; *the son of Joseph* names the legal paternity. Both will be objected to by Nathanael at v.46. The framework reads Philip's identification at face value as the working knowledge of the disciple at the moment of finding the Messiah; the framework does not require Philip to have full Messianic theology at v.45. He has enough to bring his friend.

Nathanael's first response to Philip is skepticism: *Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth?* The framework hears the geographic prejudice that ran through first-century Yahudi communities — Nazareth was a small Galilean village without prophetic distinction. The Messiah from Beit Lechem (Bethlehem) was the expectation per Micah:

*But thou, Beth-lehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Yahudah (Judah), yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Yashar'el (Israel); whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.* (Micah 5:2)

The Messiah from Nazareth would have been disconcerting. Nathanael speaks the prejudice frankly. Philip answers without arguing: *Come and see.* The pattern is the gathering pattern. Yahusha himself used it at v.39 — *Come and see* — when the first two disciples asked where he dwelt. The framework's posture is the come-and-see, the proclamation that invites investigation rather than coercing assent. It is not the appendix posture — *go read the prophets and decide for yourself.* It is *come here, walk with me, look at what is right in front of you.*

## §15. *Behold an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile!* — the King recognizes his own at first sight (vv.47-49)

*Yahusha (Jesus) saw Nathanael coming to him, and saith of him, Behold an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile! Nathanael saith unto him, Whence knowest thou me? Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said unto him, Before that Philip called thee, when thou wast under the fig tree, I saw thee. Nathanael answered and saith unto him, Rabbi, thou art the Son of Elohim (God); thou art the King of Yashar'el (Israel).* (John 1:47-49)

This is one of the framework's most concentrated identity moments in the gospels. Yahusha sees Nathanael and identifies him at first sight as *a true Yashar'eli (Israelite) without guile.* The Greek *idou alēthōs Israēlitēs, en hō dolos ouk estin* — *behold, truly a Yashar'eli, in whom is no guile.*

The framework reads the *guile* word carefully. *Dolos* in the Greek is the Septuagint's word for the Hebrew *mirmah,* the deception/trickery word used at Genesis 27 to describe Jacob's deception of his father Isaac to steal Esau's blessing.

*And he said, Thy brother came with subtilty (mirmah), and hath taken away thy blessing.* (Genesis 27:35)

Jacob the supplanter, the guile-bearer, wrestled the angel at Peniel and emerged as Yashar'el.

*And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day. And when he saw that he prevailed not against him, he touched the hollow of his thigh; and the hollow of Jacob's thigh was out of joint, as he wrestled with him. And he said, Let me go, for the day breaketh. And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me. And he said unto him, What is thy name? And he said, Jacob. And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Yashar'el (Israel): for as a prince hast thou power with Elohim (God) and with men, and hast prevailed.* (Genesis 32:24-28)

The renaming from Jacob to Yashar'el is the renaming from guile to wrestling-with-Elohim-and-prevailing. The framework reads Yahusha's identification of Nathanael as *an Israelite without guile* as identification of Nathanael as *one who has come through the Jacob-to-Yashar'el journey* — the wrestling has been done, the guile has been put off, and what stands before Yahusha is the post-Peniel identity: the prince-with-El, the wrestler who prevailed, the seed of promise in the form Yahuah is gathering.

This is the moment the King recognizes one of his own at first sight. Nathanael has been seen by Yahusha *under the fig tree* (v.48) — the place of Torah-meditation in first-century Yahudi practice; the rabbinic tradition associates fig-tree sitting with intensive Torah study and prayer. Yahusha's seeing-under-the-fig-tree is the seeing-of-his-sheep before the call.

*But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.* (John 10:26-27)

Nathanael is one of his own. Yahusha sees him. Yahusha names him. The Yashar'el-without-guile identity is named by the King.

Nathanael's response is the dual confession of v.49: *Rabbi, thou art the Son of Elohim; thou art the King of Yashar'el.* Two titles, both locked at the first meeting.

**Son of Elohim** — the title for the Formed expression of the Formless Father. Nathanael names the Messiah with the same title the Witness used at v.34.

**King of Yashar'el** — the gathering-King, the Davidic kingship fulfillment, the One who comes to gather the scattered seed of Yashar'el. The Tanakh promised the gathering-King in Ezekiel 34, in Jeremiah 23, in the closing chapters of nearly every prophet. Nathanael names the kingship substance at first meeting.

The framework reads this dual confession as the gathering substance breaking through at the first encounter. Nathanael has come from the Tanakh-meditation under the fig tree to the recognition of the King of Yashar'el. The fig-tree Torah-study has been completed by the seeing of the Son of Elohim, King of Yashar'el. The gathering has begun.

## §16. *Ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of Elohim ascending and descending upon the Son of Adam* — the Bethel-Ladder fulfilled (vv.50-51)

*Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said unto him, Because I said unto thee, I saw thee under the fig tree, believest thou? thou shalt see greater things than these. And he saith unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of Elohim (God) ascending and descending upon the Son of Adam.* (John 1:50-51)

The chapter's closing identity-claim. Yahusha names the Son of Adam — *himself* — as the Ladder. The reference is the dream of Jacob at Beit-El (Bethel), when Jacob fled from Esau and lay down with the stone for his pillow.

*And Jacob went out from Beer-sheba, and went toward Haran. And he lighted upon a certain place, and tarried there all night, because the sun was set; and he took of the stones of that place, and put them for his pillows, and lay down in that place to sleep. And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of Elohim (God) ascending and descending on it. And, behold, Yahuah (LORD) stood above it, and said, I am Yahuah Elohim (the LORD God) of Abraham thy father, and the Elohim (God) of Isaac: the land whereon thou liest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed; and thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south: and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.* (Genesis 28:10-14)

*And Jacob awaked out of his sleep, and he said, Surely Yahuah (LORD) is in this place; and I knew it not. And he was afraid, and said, How dreadful is this place! this is none other but the house of Elohim (God), and this is the gate of heaven.* (Genesis 28:16-17)

The Bethel-Ladder is the connection-point between heaven and earth, with the angels traveling up and down upon it and Yahuah standing at the top. The Hebrew name *Beit-El* means *house of Elohim,* and Jacob calls the place *the gate of heaven.*

Yahusha names the Son of Adam — himself — as the Ladder. The angels of Elohim ascend and descend upon the Son of Adam. The Son of Adam *is* the ladder; the connection-point between the Formless realm (heaven) and the Formed realm (earth) is the Word made flesh; the angels travel up and down upon him. The Bethel-Ladder of Genesis 28 is fulfilled in the Son of Adam.

The Hebrew *ben adam* — son of Adam — names the lineage substance the framework rests on. The English translators flattened this to *son of man* and lost the connection to the seed of Adam the Tanakh keeps in view. The Greek of John 1:51 — *ton huion tou anthrōpou* — has no kaph-comparative, no *like;* this is the title-naming use. The figure who is the Ladder is named directly as the Son of Adam, the seed-bearing fulfillment of every Adamic-line promise the Tanakh has traced.

This is the chapter's closing identity-claim. The Formless-Formed grid of vv.1-2 has walked through the prologue into the incarnation, into the witness's identifications, into the first-disciples sequence, into the King's recognition of Nathanael, and now lands at the Bethel-Ladder identification. The Son of Adam is the Ladder. The angels move up and down upon him. He is the connection-point. The Formless above; the Formed below in flesh; the angels of Elohim traveling the Ladder that *is* the Son of Adam. The whole architecture of Yahuah's interaction with the seed of promise — every theophany of the Tanakh, every prophetic vision, every angelic visitation, every encounter between heaven and earth — moves through the Son of Adam. He is the Ladder Jacob saw at Beit-El. The Bethel-Ladder was a vision; the Son of Adam in flesh is the fulfillment.

The framework reads v.51 alongside the chapter's opening claim at v.1 as the chapter's bookend. The Word at v.1 is the Son of Adam at v.51. The pre-existent Logos at v.1 is the Bethel-Ladder at v.51. The chapter has named the Formless source and the Formed expression in eternal substance at vv.1-2, walked it into incarnation at v.14, and locked it at the closing identity-claim at v.51: *he is the Ladder.* The Son of Adam in flesh is the connection-point between the Formless source above and the Formed creation below. Every heavenly traffic, every angelic ministry, every divine intervention into the affairs of men — moves through him. He is what the Tanakh always pointed to. He is what the prophets saw. He is what the patriarchs glimpsed in vision and what the Witness pointed to at the Yarden. He is the Ladder. He is the Lamb. He is the King of Yashar'el. He is the Son of Elohim. He is the Son of Adam. He is the Word made flesh.

The chapter ends. The gospel of John has eighteen chapters of incarnate ministry, seven signs, seven *I am* statements, the Last Supper discourse of chapters 13-17, the crucifixion of chapter 19, the Resurrection of chapter 20, and the restoration of Peter at chapter 21 still to come. The whole gospel of John is the unfolding of John 1's prologue. The chapter is the seed; the gospel is the tree.
