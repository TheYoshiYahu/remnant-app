# Short-form commentary on Matthew 1

Matthew opens the canon of the New Testament with seventeen verses of names — what the Reformation's preaching tradition has skipped as *the begats,* the dull pre-chapter the reader endures before the story. The genealogy is doing far more than establishing first-century Yahudi (Jewish) parentage. It is a covenant-architecture argument so densely constructed that a single reading carries the entire shape of the gathering hope the prophets named.

The opening sentence sets the structure.

*The book of the generation of Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ), the son of David, the son of Avraham (Abraham).* (Matthew 1:1)

Two ancestors, not picked at random. David carries the kingship promise; Avraham (Abraham) carries the seed-promise. The Messiah holds both.

*And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom. He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.* (2 Samuel 7:12-13)

*And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.* (Genesis 22:18)

The phrasing *the book of the generation* — *biblos genéseōs* in the Greek — deliberately echoes the Septuagint of the second Tanakh *toledot.*

*This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that Elohim (God) created man, in the likeness of Elohim (God) made he him.* (Genesis 5:1)

Matthew is placing the canon-opening genealogy of Yahusha (Jesus) in the same literary register as the canon-opening *toledot* of Genesis. The Adamic-line continuity is in view from the first phrase. The genealogy is being placed in conversation with Genesis 5, not with first-century pedigree records.

Verse 17 declares the structure plainly.

*So all the generations from Avraham (Abraham) to David are fourteen generations; and from David until the carrying away into Babylon are fourteen generations; and from the carrying away into Babylon unto the Messiah (Christ) are fourteen generations.* (Matthew 1:17)

Fourteen, fourteen, fourteen. The number fourteen is the gematria of *David* in Hebrew — *dalet* (4) + *vav* (6) + *dalet* (4) = 14. Matthew is constructing the genealogy on a triple-Davidic signature. The first set climbs to David. The second set descends from the Davidic monarchy to its catastrophic collapse in the Babylonian deportation. The third set is the long quiet ascent through exile and partial restoration to the Messiah. Each set is labeled with David's number. Each is a different movement in the same Davidic argument. The construction is the argument.

Matthew names four women in the genealogy — Tamar, Rachab, Ruth, and *her that had been the wife of Uriah* (Bathsheba, named by reference to her dead husband). The inherited Christian reading takes the four as early signal of *Gentile inclusion in the line of Christ* — proof that the gospel was always going to expand beyond Yashar'el (Israel) to all peoples. The framework reads them differently. Each woman enters the line through a narrative that should have broken the line — Yahudah's (Judah's) failure to honor levirate, the herem of Yericho (Jericho) that would have erased Rachab's house, the famine that drove Naomi out and the dead husbands that should have closed her line, David's adultery and the murder of Uriah — and in every case the line is preserved by Yahuah's (God's) sovereign hand. The four women are markers of seed-preservation by sovereign decree, not a doctrinal pattern of inclusion-by-faith. Tamar's case is internal to the patriarchal family; Rachab and Ruth are Yah-ordained exception-grafts whose specific function is to carry the Davidic line forward; Bathsheba is Yashar'eli (Israelite) by birth, and Matthew's naming-by-Uriah refuses to whitewash the sin in the kingship line. The Davidic line stands because Yahuah (God) kept it standing — not because its kings were righteous.

The hinge between the second and third sets of fourteen is the Babylonian deportation, and Matthew names it four times in seventeen verses.

*And Josias begat Jechonias and his brethren, about the time they were carried away to Babylon: and after they were brought to Babylon, Jechonias begat Salathiel; and Salathiel begat Zorobabel.* (Matthew 1:11-12)

He will not let the reader miss it. The Babylonian captivity (the southern house of Yahudah, deported 605–586 BCE) closes a story that began with the Assyrian captivity (the northern house of Yashar'el, deported 722 BCE, with the ten tribes scattered into the nations). Both houses are scattered by the time the genealogy reaches its second hinge. The seventy years end with a partial return under Zerubbavel (Zerubbabel) and Ezra and Nechemyah (Nehemiah), but the prophetic ingathering of all twelve tribes — promised in every prophet — has not happened. The post-exilic community is a Yahudite-and-Levitical remnant in the land, not the restored twelve-tribe nation the prophets named. The northern house is still scattered. The full ingathering is still ahead.

*Then shall the children of Yahudah (Judah) and the children of Yashar'el (Israel) be gathered together, and appoint themselves one head, and they shall come up out of the land: for great shall be the day of Jezreel.* (Hosea 1:11)

*And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Yashar'el (Israel), and gather together the dispersed of Yahudah (Judah) from the four corners of the earth.* (Isaiah 11:12)

Matthew names the deportation as the central feature of the line his Messiah descends from because the deportation is the unfinished business his Messiah arrives to address. The Messianic mission is not to redeem-from-hell. It is to gather-the-scattered. The Babylonian-captivity hinge is the front-door announcement at the head of the New Testament that the Messiah descends from the line of the scattering, to address the scattering, in fulfillment of the prophetic promise of the gathering.

The conception narrative opens at verse 18. Yoseph (Joseph), the Torah-righteous man espoused to Miryam (Mary), discovers her pregnancy and prepares to put her away privily. The angel of Yahuah (the Lord) appears in a dream.

*But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of Yahuah (the Lord) appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Yoseph (Joseph), thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Miryam (Mary) thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit). And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name Yahusha (Jesus): for he shall save his people from their sins.* (Matthew 1:20-21)

Two framework anchors land in v.21.

First, the name. *Yahusha* is Hebrew for *Yah saves* — *Yahu* (the short divine name) plus *sha* (from *yasha,* to save). The reason given in the second clause of the verse — *for he shall save his people from their sins* — is the etymology of the name made visible. The angel is saying: name him what he is, because he is what he is named. The Reformation's preferred *Jesus* (the latinized transliteration through the Septuagint convention used for the Old Testament name Yahoshua/Joshua) softens the divine-name component to invisibility. The Hebrew form is restored on every mention because the name itself is the gospel: the *Yah* who scattered is the *Yah* who gathers. The Old Testament precedent is *Yahoshua,* the same root, with the same theological renaming-as-commissioning.

*And Mosheh (Moses) called Oshea the son of Nun Yahoshua (Jehoshua).* (Numbers 13:16)

Mosheh added the divine-name component to the name at the moment Yahoshua was commissioned to lead the entry into the land of promise. Yahusha at the head of Matthew is the same root made flesh, named at the moment of his conception, sent to lead the entry into the prophetic ingathering.

Second, *his people.* The Reformation has flattened this phrase to *everyone who would later believe,* projecting the post-resurrection commission backwards onto the first-century Messianic mission. The framework reads *his people* as the specific covenant people the prophets named — the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel), the scattered seed the Babylonian-captivity hinge has just made visible. Yahusha (Jesus) will lock the scope explicitly twice in this gospel.

*These twelve Yahusha (Jesus) sent forth, and commanded them, saying, Go not into the way of the Gentiles, and into any city of the Samaritans enter ye not: but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 10:5-6)

*But he answered and said, I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 15:24)

These are not pre-cross limitations later expanded. They are the lock on what *his people* means in 1:21. The Messianic mission of the first coming was to the scattered seed — to gather them back from the scattering judgment of the captivity hinge. The post-resurrection broadcast through the nations is the proclamation route to where the scattered citizens have been driven, not the abolition of the first-mission scope. The audience of the gospel is the citizens; the practice is broad proclamation, because the citizens are scattered and only the citizens hear.

The phrase *from their sins* is the prophetic phrase for the deliverance of the covenant people from the sin-condition that produced the scattering. The Tanakh's covenant cycle is sin → exile → repentance → restoration; the Messianic salvation in v.21 names the gathering-and-cleansing that closes the cycle.

*For I will take you from among the heathen, and gather you out of all countries, and will bring you into your own land. Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:24-27)

The gathering and the cleansing-from-sin are the same prophetic motion. Yahusha's (Jesus's) name in Matthew 1:21 is the announcement that Ezekiel 36 is the meaning of the Messianic salvation. The naming is also the lock on the name's-sake grammar — the gathering is by Yah's name, for Yah's name, not for the gathered people's righteousness.

*Therefore say unto the house of Yashar'el (Israel), Thus saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD); I do not this for your sakes, O house of Yashar'el (Israel), but for mine holy name's sake, which ye have profaned among the heathen, whither ye went.* (Ezekiel 36:22)

The chapter closes the conception narrative with the prophetic quotation from Isaiah.

*Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Yahuah (Lord) by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel, which being interpreted is, Elohim (God) with us.* (Matthew 1:22-23)

*Therefore the Yahuah (Lord) himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.* (Isaiah 7:14)

The deepest move in v.23 is the apposition: *they shall call his name Immanuel, which being interpreted is, Elohim (God) with us.* The Messiah is being identified as Elohim (God) in the flesh. The naming is theological, not merely descriptive of his role. The child in the manger IS Elohim (God) with his people. The Yahuah who walked with Adam in the cool of the day, who appeared to Avraham (Abraham) at the oaks of Mamre, who wrestled with Ya'aqov (Jacob) at the Yabbok, who spoke from the burning bush, who stood on Sinai — the Formed Yahuah of every theophany — has taken on the seed of Adam in flesh. The Father, the Formless source, remains where he always is, never seen by mortal eyes.

*And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.* (Exodus 33:20)

*No man hath seen Elohim (God) at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.* (John 1:18)

The Formless never appears; the Formed is everywhere the Tanakh records a theophany; and at Matthew 1:23 the Formed has come in flesh. The Trinity controversy of the fourth century reads three-co-equal-persons-in-one-divine-substance backwards into this verse — the verse itself does not carry that architecture. It carries the architecture the canon has been showing all along: one Yahuah (God), Formless in source, Formed in expression, and the Formed has tabernacled among us.

Matthew opens the gospel of the gathering before he narrates a single miracle. The genealogy is the scattering and the seed-preservation; the Babylonian-captivity hinge is the unfinished prophetic business the Messiah arrives to address; the name *Yahusha* is the announcement that *Yah saves*; the mission *to his people from their sins* is the gathering-and-cleansing of Ezekiel 36; and *Immanuel — Elohim (God) with us* is the Formed of every theophany now in flesh. The gospel is in the genealogy if the reader can hear what Matthew is naming. The rest of the book walks the announcement out into proclamation, conflict, cross, resurrection, and commission. Chapter 1 is the front door.
