# Free-tier commentary on Matthew 13

Matthew 13 is the parables-of-the-kingdom chapter. The same day that closed in Matthew 12 with the conspiracy-to-destroy hardened against the King opens here with the King teaching from a boat-pulpit on the Sea of Galilee — the Galilee-territory the prophet named as the place of the scattered northern tribes (Matthew 4:15-16 citing Isaiah 9:1-2). Seven parables structure the chapter across two settings — four from the boat to the multitudes (sower, wheat-and-tares, mustard seed, leaven); three in the house to the disciples (hidden treasure, pearl of great price, dragnet). Four moments anchor the chapter. (1) The Isaiah 6:9-10 hardening-citation at vv.13-15 locks the cause-and-effect-of-hearing architecture. The Greek *dedotai* (passive perfect, *it has been given*) at v.11 names a giving that precedes the hearing; election precedes response; hearing reveals what was always true (John 10:26-27). The *lest at any time they should ... be converted, and I should heal them* clause reads as the outcome-clause of the hardening-already-done (*their* heart waxed gross, *their* eyes *they have closed*), not as Yahuah (God) actively implanting unbelief — the Pharaoh-pattern at Exodus 8:15, 8:32, 9:34 (Pharaoh hardens his own heart) confirmed by Yahuah (the LORD) at Exodus 9:12, 10:1, 10:20, 11:10 (he confirms what Pharaoh chose) holds the architecture without collapsing into Reformed-monergistic-determinism or libertarian-self-determination. (2) The parable of the sower (vv.3-9) with its interpretation (vv.18-23) sorts hearers into four soils — way-side, stony, thorny, good ground — with the *who hath ears to hear, let him hear* close making the parable-form itself enact the diagnostic it teaches. The *understandeth it* of v.23 carries the *na'aseh v'nishma* substance of Exodus 24:7: heart-receiving with the disposition to walk in it, the new-heart of Ezekiel 36:26-27 internalized; the good-ground hundredfold echoes Yitschaq's (Isaac's) covenant-blessing at Genesis 26:12. (3) The wheat-and-tares parable (vv.24-30) with its framework-major interpretation at vv.36-43 is the most weight-bearing seed-architecture passage in the gospels. *The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil* (vv.38-39) names the Genesis 3:15 seed-war as structural — two seed-lines, two sowers, sown into the same field. The architecture is corroborated at 1 John 3:10-12 (*the children of Elohim (God) are manifest, and the children of the devil ... Qayin (Cain), who was of that wicked one*) and John 8:44 (*ye are of your father the devil*), with the names written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world at Revelation 13:8 and 17:8. The post-harvest sifting follows: the gathered wheat passes through a second sifting (Ezekiel 20:34-38 — the rod, the bond of the new covenant, the purging out of rebels; Matthew 25:32 — sheep from goats; Luke 3:17 — wheat from chaff); names get blotted out from the Book of Life at Exodus 32:33, Psalm 69:28, Revelation 3:5; the *I never knew you* of Matthew 7:22-23 is spoken to wheat that did not bear fruit, NOT to tares. *Let both grow together until the harvest* (v.30) is NOT a prohibition on the kingdom-citizens' discernment (Matthew 7:15-17, 1 John 4:1, Romans 16:17 mandate it); it is a refusal of the final judicial uprooting reserved for the angelic-reapers (v.39). The mustard-seed (vv.31-32) and leaven (v.33) parables are read inside the chapter's wheat-and-tares architecture, NOT as kingdom-triumphalism-throughout-the-age — the fowls-of-the-air lodging in the branches echo Daniel 4:11-12 and Ezekiel 31:10-11 (the proud system hewn down at the judgment), and leaven is consistently negative in Tanakh-substance (Exodus 12:15, Leviticus 2:11, Matthew 16:6, 1 Corinthians 5:6-7); both parables name the present-age mixed-growth pattern, not pure triumph. The Psalm 78:2 citation at v.35 names the substance *kept secret from the foundation of the world.* (4) The dragnet (vv.47-50) recapitulates the harvest-sifting in the disciples'-own-trade-language — two parables, two witnesses to one consummation. The hidden-treasure (v.44) and pearl-of-great-price (vv.45-46) name the kingdom's-worth-substance and the sell-all-to-buy disposition (Matthew 16:24-26); the scribe-instructed-unto-the-kingdom (v.52) brings forth *things new and old* out of one treasure-house, structurally precluding the Marcionite split. The chapter closes at Natsareth (Nazareth) with the *prophet-not-without-honour-save-in-his-own-country* rejection (vv.53-58) — the same not-receiving disposition the Pharisaic system has displayed nationally now operating at the family-and-village register (the brothers list at v.55: *Ya'aqov (James), and Yoseph (Joseph), and Shim'on (Simon), and Yahudah (Judah)*). John 1:10-13's prologue locks the architecture: *He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him ... were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of Elohim (God).* Matthew 14 opens with the rejection-disposition mirrored at the political-system level — the death of Yochanan (John) the Witness at Herod's hand.
