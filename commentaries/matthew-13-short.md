# Short-form commentary on Matthew 13

Matthew 13 is the parables-of-the-kingdom chapter. The same day that closed in Matthew 12 with the conspiracy-to-destroy hardened against the King and the kinship-redefinition naming who his family is opens here with the King leaving the house, sitting in a boat on the Sea of Galilee, and teaching the multitudes on the shore in parables. Seven parables structure the chapter, in two settings: four spoken from the boat to the multitudes — the sower, the wheat and the tares, the mustard seed, the leaven — and three spoken in the house to the disciples after the multitudes are sent away — the hidden treasure, the pearl of great price, the dragnet. Four moments anchor the chapter. The Isaiah 6:9-10 hardening-citation at vv.13-15 locks the cause-and-effect-of-hearing architecture the gospel has been displaying since the King's ministry began. The wheat-and-tares parable at vv.24-30 with its interpretation at vv.36-43 is the most weight-bearing seed-architecture passage in the gospels, where the King himself identifies the children of the kingdom and the children of the wicked one as two seed-lines sown across the field of the world by two different sowers, with the post-harvest sifting laying out the architecture the rest of scripture's witness corroborates. The dragnet at vv.47-50 recapitulates the harvest-sifting in a different image. The rejection at Natsareth (Nazareth) at vv.53-58 lands the *prophet-not-without-honour-save-in-his-own-country* pattern at the hometown level — the same not-receiving disposition the Pharisaic system has displayed nationally now playing at the family-and-village register. The chapter dismantles the Christian-commentary collapse of the mustard-seed and leaven into kingdom-triumphalism-throughout-the-age and restores the reading the verses themselves carry: the field is the world, the tares are sown in among the wheat across the age, and the burning of the tares precedes the gathered wheat's shining as the sun in the kingdom of their Father.

*The same day went Yahusha (Jesus) out of the house, and sat by the sea side. And great multitudes were gathered together unto him, so that he went into a ship, and sat; and the whole multitude stood on the shore. And he spake many things unto them in parables, saying, Behold, a sower went forth to sow.* (Matthew 13:1-3)

The chapter opens with the deliberate *the same day* — a chapter-of-conflicts-and-credentialing closes; a chapter of veiled-and-revealing teaching opens. The continuity is the King's response to the rejection-disposition Matthew 12 documented. The Pharisaic system has reached the conspiracy-to-destroy; the multitudes are still pressing toward recognition; the King now teaches in a form that veils-and-reveals according to the hearer's disposition. The boat-pulpit on the Sea of Galilee reaches the Galilee-territory that the prophet had named as the place where the scattered northern tribes would see the great light:

*The land of Zebulun, and the land of Naphtali, by the way of the sea, beyond Yarden (Jordan), Galilee of the Gentiles; The people which sat in darkness saw a great light; and to them which sat in the region and shadow of death light is sprung up.* (Matthew 4:15-16)

The Tanakh-source is Isaiah 9:1-2 — the prophet's promise to the territories the Assyrian exile had dispersed, the lost-sheep-territory of the scattered northern house. The lake-pulpit is reaching the gathering's foundational-audience. The multitudes who gather include the *am-haaretz* — the people-of-the-land whom the Pharisaic credentialing-system categorized as unlearned and cursed:

*But this people who knoweth not the law are cursed.* (John 7:49)

The multitudes the Pharisees disdain are the multitudes the King teaches. The parable-form will divide the multitudes the King is teaching from the system that has refused him without the King having to do the dividing — those-with-ears-to-hear will hear; those-without will not. The form itself enacts the cause-and-effect-of-hearing the chapter is about to make explicit.

*Behold, a sower went forth to sow; And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them up: Some fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up, because they had no deepness of earth: And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away. And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and choked them: But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit, some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some thirtyfold. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.* (Matthew 13:3-9)

The parable is given without interpretation in the King's first telling. The full unpacking comes at vv.18-23 when the disciples are alone with him. The structure is fourfold — way-side, stony places, thorns, good ground — and the closing line *Who hath ears to hear, let him hear* puts the receiving-disposition into the parable's very ending. The sower is the One who sows the kingdom-message; the seed is the word of the kingdom; the four soils are four kinds of hearers; the parable structures the response-pattern the gospel encounters wherever it goes.

The disciples ask the question the parable invites.

*And the disciples came, and said unto him, Why speakest thou unto them in parables? He answered and said unto them, Because it is given unto you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it is not given.* (Matthew 13:10-11)

Hold the verb. The Greek is *dedotai* — passive perfect tense, *it has been given.* A giving that precedes the receiving. The disciples are not understanding the parables because they figured them out by intellect; the multitudes are not failing to understand because they could not work it out by effort. The giving precedes the hearing. The hearing is the visible surface of a deeper architecture: those-given-to-hear hear; those-not-given-to-hear do not. The Tanakh has named this architecture from the beginning. Yahuah (the LORD) hardened Pharaoh's heart after Pharaoh had hardened his own; Yahuah (the LORD) chose Yitschaq (Isaac) and not Yishmael (Ishmael), Ya'aqov (Jacob) and not Esav (Esau), the seed of promise and not the seed of the flesh; Yahuah (the LORD) raised up Koresh (Cyrus) by name a hundred and fifty years before Koresh was born. The giving precedes; the response reveals. The King is telling the disciples what was always true: their hearing is gift, not credential.

*For whosoever hath, to him shall be given, and he shall have more abundance: but whosoever hath not, from him shall be taken away even that he hath.* (Matthew 13:12)

The principle of accumulation. Those who receive the word with disposition-to-hear receive more; those who receive the word with disposition-to-not-hear lose what they had superficially. The principle operates at the individual level and at the system level. The Pharisaic system had the Tanakh, had the temple, had the priestly architecture, had the rabbinic tradition; the system refused to hear what the King was credentialing; the system will lose what it had at the AD 70 destruction of the temple. The disposition-toward-the-word determines what is added and what is taken. The grace continues to be offered; the failure to receive results in loss-of-what-had-been-given.

*Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand. And in them is fulfilled the prophecy of Yeshayahu (Isaiah), which saith, By hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand; and seeing ye shall see, and shall not perceive: For this people's heart is waxed gross, and their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes they have closed; lest at any time they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and should understand with their heart, and should be converted, and I should heal them.* (Matthew 13:13-15)

The Tanakh-source is the prophet's commissioning-vision.

*And he said, Go, and tell this people, Hear ye indeed, but understand not; and see ye indeed, but perceive not. Make the heart of this people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes; lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed.* (Isaiah 6:9-10)

Read the citation carefully. The Christian-commentary tradition has sometimes taken the *lest at any time they should see ... and I should heal them* clause as Yahuah (God) actively preventing the people from hearing so they cannot be saved — a hard-determinist reading that makes the Father into the actor who shuts the eyes. The text does not say this. The text says *their* heart is waxed gross; *their* ears are dull of hearing; *their* eyes *they have closed.* The condition is past tense and actor-initiated. The people have closed their own eyes; the prophet's-mission is to proclaim into the condition the people have made. The *lest at any time* clause is the outcome-clause of the hardening-already-done: in the state of the already-hardened, the proclamation does not result in conversion-and-healing because the disposition to hear is gone. The proclamation continues to reveal the hardening rather than penetrate it.

The Pharaoh-hardening narrative in Exodus carries the same architecture. Pharaoh hardens his own heart at Exodus 8:15, 8:32, 9:34:

*But when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as Yahuah (the LORD) had said.* (Exodus 8:15)

Then Yahuah (the LORD) confirms the hardening Pharaoh has chosen at Exodus 9:12, 10:1, 10:20, 11:10:

*And Yahuah (the LORD) hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he hearkened not unto them; as Yahuah (the LORD) had spoken unto Mosheh (Moses).* (Exodus 9:12)

The actor initiates the disposition; the Father confirms and uses the disposition for the unfolding of his purposes. The Father does not implant the disposition the actor did not choose. The reading the framework holds against the Reformed-monergistic-determinism that makes the Father into the implanter of unbelief AND against the libertarian-self-determinative reading that makes the human-will sovereign over the Father's-knowing-from-the-foundation: the actor's-disposition is the actor's; the Father's-knowing is the Father's; the two operate in scripture's architecture without collapsing into either pole.

*But blessed are your eyes, for they see: and your ears, for they hear. For verily I say unto you, That many prophets and righteous men have desired to see those things which ye see, and have not seen them; and to hear those things which ye hear, and have not heard them.* (Matthew 13:16-17)

The disciples are positioned at the unique moment the prophets foresaw from afar. The apostolic letter names what they saw and did not see:

*Of which salvation the prophets have enquired and searched diligently, who prophesied of the grace that should come unto you: Searching what, or what manner of time the Ruach (Spirit) of Messiah (Christ) which was in them did signify, when it testified beforehand the sufferings of Messiah (Christ), and the glory that should follow. Unto whom it was revealed, that not unto themselves, but unto us they did minister the things, which are now reported unto you by them that have preached the gospel unto you with the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) sent down from heaven; which things the angels desire to look into.* (1 Peter 1:10-12)

The prophets ministered to a generation they would not see. The disciples in the boat-vicinity see what the prophets foretold. Their blessing is in the receiving — and the receiving is gift, because the giving-precedes-the-hearing.

*Hear ye therefore the parable of the sower. When any one heareth the word of the kingdom, and understandeth it not, then cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart. This is he which received seed by the way side. But he that received the seed into stony places, the same is he that heareth the word, and anon with joy receiveth it; Yet hath he not root in himself, but dureth for a while: for when tribulation or persecution ariseth because of the word, by and by he is offended. He also that received seed among the thorns is he that heareth the word; and the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, choke the word, and he becometh unfruitful. But he that received seed into the good ground is he that heareth the word, and understandeth it; which also beareth fruit, and bringeth forth, some an hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty.* (Matthew 13:18-23)

The King unpacks the parable. The first soil is the way-side hearer — the word arrives but is not understood, and the wicked-one catches it away before any rooting can begin. The fowls of the parable have a name now: the devil himself. The Tanakh-pattern of the unclean-birds-of-prey descending on the covenant-sacrifice operates here. Avraham (Abraham) drove them away from the covenant-cutting:

*And when the fowls came down upon the carcases, Avram (Abram) drove them away.* (Genesis 15:11)

The disposition of attentive-driving-away of the adversary's-snatching is the covenant-disposition the Tanakh has been training from the beginning. The way-side hearer has no driving-away because the word never landed deeply enough to be defended.

The second soil is the stony hearer — the word arrives with joy, but there is no root in himself, and when tribulation or persecution arises because of the word, he is offended. The disposition does not survive the first external test. The empty-house pattern Matthew 12:43-45 just described operates here at the entry-disposition level. The word reaches the surface; the new heart the prophet Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) promised does not internalize:

*A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:26-27)

Without the stony-heart replaced by the heart-of-flesh, the first external pressure reveals what was not internalized. The first-century disciples faced both kinds of pressure — persecution from the Pharisaic system that had just conspired to destroy the King at Matthew 12:14, and the broader tribulation the prophet had named as the day of Ya'aqov's (Jacob's) trouble:

*Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Ya'aqov's (Jacob's) trouble; but he shall be saved out of it.* (Jeremiah 30:7)

The stony hearer's-disposition is exposed when the cost of receiving the word is named. The good-ground hearer's-disposition is exposed when the cost is paid and the rooting holds.

The third soil is the thorny hearer — the word arrives and grows, but the care of this world and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word and he becomes unfruitful. The Mammon-or-Elohim (God) binary Matthew 6:24 named operates as the active-competing-allegiance. Hold the *deceitfulness of riches* phrase carefully. The Greek *apatē tou ploutou* — the deceit of riches. Riches in themselves are not condemned in the Tanakh-witness; Avraham (Abraham), Yitschaq (Isaac), Ya'aqov (Jacob), David, and Iyov (Job) were covenant-blessed with wealth and walked faithfully. The deceit-of-riches is the substance-that-promises-life-but-cannot-deliver — the lie that wealth makes the heart secure, that wealth makes the heart fulfilled, that wealth substitutes for the substance the heart was made to receive. The Mammon-system operates by this deception. The thorny hearer's-disposition is divided allegiance, and the divided allegiance is the strangling-substance.

The fourth soil is the good-ground hearer — the word is heard, is understood, bears fruit thirtyfold-sixtyfold-hundredfold. Hold the *understandeth it* substance. The understanding is not mere intellectual comprehension; the understanding is heart-receiving with the disposition to walk in it. The Tanakh-substance of *na'aseh v'nishma* names this:

*And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that Yahuah (the LORD) hath said will we do, and be obedient.* (Exodus 24:7)

The Hebrew is *na'aseh v'nishma* — *we will do, and we will hear.* The doing-and-hearing is one substance in the Tanakh's architecture; the understanding IS the heart's-disposition-to-walk-in-it. The good-ground hearer internalizes the word into the heart-Torah-walking life the prophet Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named. The variable fruit-yield — thirtyfold, sixtyfold, hundredfold — recognizes that the kingdom-citizens' fruit-yield varies by the empowering of the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) and the circumstances of life without the variable yield disqualifying any of the three from being good ground. The Tanakh-precedent for the hundredfold is in Yitschaq (Isaac):

*Then Yitschaq (Isaac) sowed in that land, and received in the same year an hundredfold: and Yahuah (the LORD) blessed him.* (Genesis 26:12)

The hundredfold is the covenant-blessing substance the Tanakh anchors.

The next parable shifts the imagery from the four-soils of hearing-disposition to the two-seed-lines of the field itself.

*Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field: But while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way. But when the blade was sprung up, and brought forth fruit, then appeared the tares also. So the servants of the householder came and said unto him, Sir, didst not thou sow good seed in thy field? from whence then hath it tares? He said unto them, An enemy hath done this. The servants said unto him, Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up? But he said, Nay; lest while ye gather up the tares, ye root up also the wheat with them. Let both grow together until the harvest: and in the time of harvest I will say to the reapers, Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn.* (Matthew 13:24-30)

The King will interpret this parable himself at vv.36-43, and the interpretation will lock the most load-bearing seed-architecture passage in the gospels. The reading here notes the parable's structure: the householder sows good seed in his field; the enemy sows tares while men sleep; the wheat and the tares grow together until the harvest; at harvest the reapers gather the tares first into bundles to be burned, and the wheat is gathered into the barn. Hold one phrase the parable lands carefully — *let both grow together until the harvest.* The Christian-commentary tradition has sometimes read this phrase as a prohibition on the kingdom-citizens' discernment in the present age, as if the kingdom-citizens must never identify the false-prophet or the false-disciple. The text does not say this. The kingdom-citizens are explicitly commissioned to discern by fruit:

*Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit.* (Matthew 7:15-17)

To test the spirits:

*Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of Elohim (God): because many false prophets are gone out into the world.* (1 John 4:1)

To mark and avoid those who cause divisions against the apostolic teaching:

*Now I beseech you, brethren, mark them which cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned; and avoid them.* (Romans 16:17)

The kingdom-citizens are mandated to discern. What the parable's *let both grow together until the harvest* prohibits is something else: the final judicial uprooting of persons from the field-of-the-world. The reapers at the harvest are identified at v.39 as the angels, not the kingdom-citizens. The kingdom-citizens are not authorized to determine the final-judgment of any person; the Father has reserved that determination for himself and for the angelic-reapers he appoints. Discern in the present age, yes — refuse to walk with the false-prophet, refuse to receive the false-doctrine, refuse to give place to the divisive person. Pull the tare out of the field-of-the-world before its time, no — that determination belongs to the Father.

The third and fourth parables of the boat-pulpit set follow.

*Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field: Which indeed is the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among herbs, and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof.* (Matthew 13:31-32)

*Another parable spake he unto them; The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.* (Matthew 13:33)

The Christian-commentary tradition has often read these two parables as kingdom-triumphalism-throughout-the-age — the kingdom as a small seed growing into a great tree that fills the world, the kingdom as leaven that pervades the entire mass of humanity until the whole is transformed. The reading does not survive the chapter's own context. The wheat-and-tares parable just landed before these two; the dragnet will land after; the chapter's whole-architecture is the present-age mixed-growth pattern where the enemy's-sowing operates alongside the King's-sowing across the field-of-the-world. The mustard-seed and the leaven sit within that architecture, not outside it.

Hold what the imagery itself does. The mustard-seed becomes a tree where the *birds of the air come and lodge in the branches.* The birds of the air in the King's-own-just-finished parable were the wicked-one snatching the seed from the way-side hearer. Are the birds the parable's bringing in here neutral wildlife, or are they the same fowls the King just named as the adversary's agents? The Tanakh-pattern of the fowls-of-the-air as adversary-figures has consistent witness. Avraham (Abraham) drove them away from the covenant-cutting at Genesis 15:11. Daniel sees the proud-and-corrupted-king reaching to heaven through the great tree whose branches bear the fowls of the air — and the tree is hewn down at the judgment:

*The tree grew, and was strong, and the height thereof reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to the end of all the earth: The leaves thereof were fair, and the fruit thereof much, and in it was meat for all: the beasts of the field had shadow under it, and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof, and all flesh was fed of it.* (Daniel 4:11-12)

The Babylonian-system's-tree harbours the fowls until it is cut down by the watcher's word. Yechezk'el's (Ezekiel's) Pharaoh's-Egypt is the cedar in Lebanon, lifted up in its height, whose boughs are nests for the fowls of the heaven — and the cedar is cast down:

*Therefore thus saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD); Because thou hast lifted up thyself in height, and he hath shot up his top among the thick boughs, and his heart is lifted up in his height; I have therefore delivered him into the hand of the mighty one of the heathen ... and have driven him out for his wickedness.* (Ezekiel 31:10-11)

The pattern is consistent. A growing-system that lifts itself up to heaven, that the fowls of the air come to lodge in, is a system the prophets diagnose as ripe for the judgment of being hewn down. Read the mustard-seed parable within the chapter that brackets it. The kingdom's-true-growth proceeds in the present age, yes — but the visible-form the growth takes is mixed, with the fowls finding lodging in the branches, with the wheat and tares growing together until the reapers are sent. The institutional-Christian-tree that has historically been celebrated as the kingdom's-triumph throughout the age has harboured the same fowls the King just identified as the adversary's-agents. The parable is naming the mixed-substance, not predicting pure triumph.

The leaven-parable is read the same way. Leaven is a consistently negative symbol in the Tanakh-witness. The Passover-substance is the removal of leaven from the house:

*Seven days shall ye eat unleavened bread; even the first day ye shall put away leaven out of your houses: for whosoever eateth leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off from Yashar'el (Israel).* (Exodus 12:15)

The grain-offerings to Yahuah (the LORD) are explicitly without leaven:

*No meat offering, which ye shall bring unto Yahuah (the LORD), shall be made with leaven: for ye shall burn no leaven, nor any honey, in any offering of Yahuah (the LORD) made by fire.* (Leviticus 2:11)

The King himself names leaven as the adversary's-substance in the immediately surrounding teaching of the gospel:

*Then said Yahusha (Jesus) unto them, Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.* (Matthew 16:6)

The apostolic letters name the same:

*Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump? Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened.* (1 Corinthians 5:6-7)

The leaven hidden in three measures of meal until the whole was leavened is not a parable of the kingdom's pure pervasion of the world. The leaven is hidden — *enkrupto,* the same root the King will use of the hidden-treasure parable at v.44 in a different register; here the leaven is concealed within the meal where it operates unseen. The leavening proceeds quietly through the substance until the whole is transformed. Read against the immediately surrounding architecture — the wheat-and-tares before, the dragnet after, the warnings against the leaven of the Pharisees in the gospel's own further teaching — the parable is naming the corrupting-substance the enemy works into the visible-form of the kingdom's growth in the present age. The kingdom's-true-substance grows; the visible-form is leavened from within; the harvest will separate.

*All these things spake Yahusha (Jesus) unto the multitude in parables; and without a parable spake he not unto them: That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world.* (Matthew 13:34-35)

The Tanakh-source is the psalmist:

*I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old: Which we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us.* (Psalm 78:2-3)

Hold the phrase *kept secret from the foundation of the world.* The substance the King is unfolding through the parables was kept secret from the foundation of the world. The architecture of the seed-war between the children of the kingdom and the children of the wicked one, the architecture of the names written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world, the architecture of the post-harvest sifting that will land both the gathering of the wheat into the barn and the sorting of the gathered wheat into the kingdom of the Father — all of it has been the substance from before the world began. The parables are not new doctrine the King is inventing on the lake-shore. They are the unveiling of substance that has been hidden in the architecture since the foundation. The prophet wrote the verse the King is fulfilling a millennium before the King stood in the boat to fulfil it.

The King then leaves the boat-pulpit, sends the multitudes away, and goes into the house with the disciples. The next teaching is for the inner circle.

*Then Yahusha (Jesus) sent the multitude away, and went into the house: and his disciples came unto him, saying, Declare unto us the parable of the tares of the field. He answered and said unto them, He that soweth the good seed is the Son of Adam; The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the world; and the reapers are the angels. As therefore the tares are gathered and burned in the fire; so shall it be in the end of this world. The Son of Adam shall send forth his angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom all things that offend, and them which do iniquity; And shall cast them into a furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth. Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.* (Matthew 13:36-43)

This is the load-bearing passage of the chapter, and one of the most architecturally weight-bearing passages the King speaks in the gospels. Read it slowly.

The sower of the good seed is the Son of Adam. The sower of the tares is the devil. The good seed are not the kingdom-message in this parable — they are the *children of the kingdom* themselves; the tares are the *children of the wicked one* themselves. The two are not merely two different responses to one seed. They are two different seed-lines, sown into the same field by two different sowers. The substance the King is naming is the seed-war that runs from Genesis 3:15 forward — the enmity Yahuah (the LORD) placed between the seed of the woman and the seed of the serpent:

*And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.* (Genesis 3:15)

The seed-war is structural. The two seed-lines are real, and they are sown by two different sowers. The apostolic letter names the architecture directly:

*In this the children of Elohim (God) are manifest, and the children of the devil: whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of Elohim (God), neither he that loveth not his brother. ... Not as Qayin (Cain), who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous.* (1 John 3:10, 12)

Qayin (Cain) is identified as *of that wicked one* — not as having behaved like the wicked one in a moral lapse, but as being *of* him in origin-substance. The King himself names the same architecture to the Pharisaic system in Yochanan (John):

*Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning, and abode not in the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it.* (John 8:44)

The King is not telling the Pharisees they have chosen the wrong father by behavior. He is identifying them by origin-substance. They are *of* the father whose lusts they do; they cannot hear his word because they are not of his sheep:

*But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.* (John 10:26-27)

The cause-and-effect here is the architectural-frame for the entire chapter. The Pharisaic system is not hearing because they are not sheep. The disciples are hearing because they are sheep. The multitudes are sifting in front of the King's-parables — those-with-ears-hear; those-without-ears-do-not — because the sheep-substance was either present or not present before the parable was ever spoken. The names written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world are the names of the sheep:

*And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.* (Revelation 13:8)

*And they that dwell on the earth shall wonder, whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world.* (Revelation 17:8)

The architecture is in place from before the world began. Hearing-reveals what was always true; hearing does not create what was not there. The Christian-inclusion-gospel reads the cause-and-effect backwards: *if you hear and confess, you become a child of Elohim (God).* The scripture says the opposite: *if you are a child of Elohim (God), you will hear when he calls.* Election precedes hearing. Lineage precedes response. The chapter that opened with the parable of the sower has been training this reading the whole way through.

The reapers are the angels. The harvest is the end of the world — the Greek is *synteleia tou aiōnos,* the consummation of the age. The angels gather out of the kingdom all things that offend and them which do iniquity, and cast them into a furnace of fire. The tares are bound and burned at the harvest because they were always tares — the burning does not change what they were; it consummates the architecture that was true from the field's first sowing.

Then the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father. The gathered wheat is the seed of Adam-substance the field has carried through the age. But hold what the King's-architecture in the chapter has not yet finished. The gathered wheat passes through a sifting before it shines as the sun. The prophet had named the architecture:

*And I will bring you out from the people, and will gather you out of the countries wherein ye are scattered, with a mighty hand, and with a stretched out arm, and with fury poured out. And I will bring you into the wilderness of the people, and there will I plead with you face to face. ... And I will cause you to pass under the rod, and I will bring you into the bond of the covenant: And I will purge out from among you the rebels, and them that transgress against me.* (Ezekiel 20:34-35, 37-38)

The gathered are brought to the wilderness; the wilderness is the place of the rod and the purging; the rebels are purged out from among the gathered. The judgment-of-separation between the sheep and the goats:

*And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as a shepherd divideth his sheep from the goats.* (Matthew 25:32)

The judgment of the wheat from the chaff:

*Whose fan is in his hand, and he will throughly purge his floor, and gather his wheat into the garner; but he will burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire.* (Luke 3:17)

And the King's-own-warning to those who say *Lord, Lord* and assume the credential is the substance:

*Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.* (Matthew 7:21-23)

The *I never knew you* of Matthew 7 is not spoken to tares. The tares were never gathered. The *I never knew you* is spoken to wheat that did not bear the fruit the good-ground hearer bears. The chapter's-architecture-of-the-fruit returns. The good-ground hearer bears fruit thirtyfold-sixtyfold-hundredfold. The wheat that bears no fruit is the wheat the post-harvest sifting purges out. The names that get blotted out are the names that were written. The Tanakh has named the architecture from the beginning:

*And Yahuah (the LORD) said unto Mosheh (Moses), Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book.* (Exodus 32:33)

*Let them be blotted out of the book of the living, and not be written with the righteous.* (Psalm 69:28)

The King's-promise to the faithful at Sardis carries the same architecture:

*He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.* (Revelation 3:5)

The promise to *not* blot out is given to the overcomer — which means the blotting-out is a real possibility for the non-overcomer. The warning is to the wheat, not to the tares. The tares cannot become wheat by trying; the wheat can lose its place by lawlessness. The harvest separates the wheat from the tares; the post-harvest sifting separates the fruitful wheat from the unfruitful. The two sortings are one consummation, and the consummation lands at the end of the age.

And so all Yashar'el (Israel) shall be saved — the gathered remnant of the seed of Adam, sifted through the rod, brought into the bond of the new covenant, grafted into the natural root of the fathers, shining forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father:

*And so all Yashar'el (Israel) shall be saved: as it is written, There shall come out of Tsion (Zion) the Deliverer, and shall turn away ungodliness from Ya'aqov (Jacob).* (Romans 11:26)

The King's-interpretation at vv.36-43 lays out the architecture the rest of scripture's witness corroborates. The chapter's-density-peak has landed.

Three more parables follow in the house.

*Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.* (Matthew 13:44)

*Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking goodly pearls: Who, when he had found one pearl of great price, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.* (Matthew 13:45-46)

The hidden-treasure parable and the pearl-of-great-price parable are paired. Both name the kingdom's-worth-substance. The kingdom is worth more than all-that-the-finder-has, and the disposition that enters the kingdom is the disposition of selling-all-to-buy. The treasure is found by someone who was not seeking it; the pearl is found by someone who was seeking pearls and finds the one. Both find; both sell-all; both buy. The King has named the same disposition in different language earlier in the gospel:

*Then said Yahusha (Jesus) unto his disciples, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: and whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall find it. For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?* (Matthew 16:24-26)

The kingdom-disposition is not a religious-addition to the rest of the life that was being lived; the kingdom-disposition is a reordering of the whole life around the substance that has been found. The finder of the treasure does not buy the treasure; he buys the field-in-which-the-treasure-is-hidden. The kingdom-substance is bound up with the field where it has been hidden — and the field, in the chapter's-own-architecture established at v.38, is the world. The kingdom-citizens take possession of the field where the kingdom-substance is hidden because the field's-restoration is part of what they are inheriting. The meek shall inherit the earth.

The last parable in the house recapitulates the wheat-and-tares.

*Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a net, that was cast into the sea, and gathered of every kind: Which, when it was full, they drew to shore, and sat down, and gathered the good into vessels, but cast the bad away. So shall it be at the end of the world: the angels shall come forth, and sever the wicked from among the just, and shall cast them into the furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth.* (Matthew 13:47-50)

The dragnet — the great sea-net the fishermen-disciples knew well from their trade — is cast into the sea and gathers of every kind. When full, it is drawn to shore; the good are gathered into vessels; the bad are cast away. The angels come forth; they sever the wicked from among the just; the wicked are cast into the furnace of fire. The same architecture as the wheat-and-tares, now in the disciples'-own-trade-language. The harvest-judgment is sealed by the recapitulation. Two parables land the same end. The Tanakh-pattern of two witnesses establishing the substance operates at the parable-level. The chapter has now twice taught what the consummation looks like, in two different images. The disciples cannot miss the architecture.

*Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto them, Have ye understood all these things? They say unto him, Yea, Lord. Then said he unto them, Therefore every scribe which is instructed unto the kingdom of heaven is like unto a man that is an householder, which bringeth forth out of his treasure things new and old.* (Matthew 13:51-52)

The disciples answer that they have understood. The King then names what kind of teacher the kingdom requires. A *scribe instructed unto the kingdom of heaven* is an householder who brings forth out of his treasure *things new and old.* The Tanakh — the things old — and the kingdom's-revelation in the Son — the things new — are brought forth together out of one treasure-house. The Marcionite-future-error that would centuries later try to split the testaments, throwing away the Tanakh as a different-god's-book, is structurally precluded by the King's-own-naming of the scribe's-treasure. The treasure is one; the things-new and the things-old come out of the same chest; the scribe-instructed-unto-the-kingdom brings them forth together. The kingdom-teaching that strips the Tanakh and keeps the King is not the kingdom-teaching the King himself names. The kingdom-teaching that strips the King and keeps the Tanakh is not it either. Both come out of the one treasure.

The chapter closes on the rejection at Natsareth (Nazareth).

*And it came to pass, that when Yahusha (Jesus) had finished these parables, he departed thence. And when he was come into his own country, he taught them in their synagogue, insomuch that they were astonished, and said, Whence hath this man this wisdom, and these mighty works? Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Miryam (Mary)? and his brethren, Ya'aqov (James), and Yoseph (Joseph), and Shim'on (Simon), and Yahudah (Judah)? And his sisters, are they not all with us? Whence then hath this man all these things? And they were offended in him. But Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country, and in his own house. And he did not many mighty works there because of their unbelief.* (Matthew 13:53-58)

The King returns to his own town. The villagers have seen him grow up; they have known his mother; they have worked beside his brothers; the carpenter's son is a known quantity to them. The wisdom and the mighty works land in front of them, and the response is *whence hath this man this wisdom* — not curiosity that opens to seeking, but offense that closes against receiving. The familiarity-breeds-rejection pattern. The King names what they cannot see — *a prophet is not without honour, save in his own country, and in his own house.* The same not-receiving disposition the Pharisaic system has displayed nationally is now playing at the family-and-village register. The cousins, the schoolmates, the neighbours who shared bread with the family for thirty years cannot receive what they are seeing because they have already-decided who he is.

The apostle Yochanan (John) names the same pattern in the gospel's prologue:

*He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not. He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of Elohim (God), even to them that believe on his name: Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of Elohim (God).* (John 1:10-13)

The not-receiving operates at every level — national, regional, familial, and hometown. The receiving operates by the same architecture the chapter has been training: those born of Elohim (God), the children of the kingdom from the foundation of the world, receive when they hear because they are sheep. The hearing reveals what was always true. The not-hearing at Natsareth (Nazareth) reveals what was always not.

*And he did not many mighty works there because of their unbelief.* The mighty-works are not withheld as punishment; the mighty-works require the disposition-to-receive in the recipient. The kingdom-substance does not impose itself on the closed-disposition. The disposition-to-receive is the doorway through which the mighty-works flow. The closed-village receives what their disposition has chosen to receive — the carpenter's son, no more.

Matthew 14 will open with the death of Yochanan (John) the Witness at Herod's hand — the rejection-disposition the gospel has been tracing in the religious-system mirrored now in the political-system, with the prophet's-voice silenced and the King withdrawing across the lake. The chapter that just closed with the not-receiving at Natsareth (Nazareth) will be followed by the chapter that opens with the not-receiving at the level of the kingdom of the world. The seed-war the parables-chapter has named is operating at every register, and the King's-response continues to be the gathering of the multitudes who do receive — the next chapter will land the feeding of the five thousand at the lake-territory the parables-chapter opened on.
