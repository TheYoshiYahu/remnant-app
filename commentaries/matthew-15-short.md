# Short-form commentary on Matthew 15

Matthew 15 is the chapter where the credentialing-system that has been displayed at the political-level in the silencing of Yochanan (John) at Matthew 14 now reaches at the religious-level to challenge the King directly, and where the King's response separates Tanakh-substance from rabbinic-fence-tradition with surgical clarity. Four movements anchor the chapter. The Pharisaic-scribal delegation from Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) challenges the disciples for not washing hands before bread, and the King answers with the Korban-evasion-of-the-fifth-commandment expose and the Isaiah 29:13 *their heart is far from me* citation (vv.1-9). The crowds-and-disciples teaching on heart-defilement-versus-mouth-defilement that follows establishes the architecture the Pharisaic-fence had inverted (vv.10-20). The withdrawal into Tsor (Tyre) and Tsidon (Sidon) and the healing of the Canaanite-woman's daughter, where the King speaks his most direct mission-scope statement in the gospel — *I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel)* — and the woman's faith is named great precisely because she submits to the architecture rather than challenging it (vv.21-28). And the healings on the mountain followed by the feeding of the four thousand, a distinct event from the feeding of the five thousand, in different territory with different numerology, with seven loaves and seven baskets remaining (vv.29-39). The chapter that opened with the Pharisaic delegation challenging the disciples closes with the King boarding a ship back across the lake to confront the same delegation again at Matthew 16.

*Then came to Yahusha (Jesus) scribes and Pharisees, which were of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), saying, Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? for they wash not their hands when they eat bread.* (Matthew 15:1-2)

Hold the level the challenge operates at. The scribes-and-Pharisees come from Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — the religious-system's headquarters. The challenge is not over the Torah; the Torah nowhere requires hand-washing before eating non-temple bread. The challenge is over *the tradition of the elders* — the rabbinic-oral-tradition that had been built up over centuries as the fence-around-the-Torah. The Pharisaic-system regarded the oral-tradition as having authority equal to (and in practice often above) the written Torah; they treated the tradition's-prescriptions as the marker of covenant-faithfulness. The disciples are not violating Torah by eating without ceremonial-hand-washing; they are violating the rabbinic-fence-around-Torah. The Pharisaic-system has equated the fence with the covenant. The King is about to separate them.

*But he answered and said unto them, Why do ye also transgress the commandment of Elohim (God) by your tradition? For Elohim (God) commanded, saying, Honour thy father and mother: and, He that curseth father or mother, let him die the death. But ye say, Whosoever shall say to his father or his mother, It is a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; And honour not his father or his mother, he shall be free. Thus have ye made the commandment of Elohim (God) of none effect by your tradition.* (Matthew 15:3-6)

The King counter-charges. The Pharisaic-tradition does not merely add to the Torah; it voids the Torah at specific points. The Korban-evasion the King names is the diagnostic instance. The Torah commands the honor of father and mother with the death-penalty backing the cursing of father or mother:

*Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which Yahuah (the LORD) thy Elohim (God) giveth thee.* (Exodus 20:12)

*And he that smiteth his father, or his mother, shall be surely put to death. ... And he that curseth his father, or his mother, shall surely be put to death.* (Exodus 21:15, 17)

The honor includes material-support of the aged parent — the obligation the Torah's covenant-economy assumes. The Pharisaic-tradition had developed a workaround: a man could declare assets *Korban* (the Hebrew word for an offering dedicated to Elohim (God)) and thereby block their use for any other purpose, including support of his parents. The asset-holder kept his use of the assets (Korban was a dedication-status, not a transfer-of-possession) while the parent was cut off from claim on them. The fifth commandment was structurally voided by the system that publicly claimed to honor it most strictly. Mark records the parallel saying with the *Corban* word explicit:

*For Mosheh (Moses) said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die the death: But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free. And ye suffer him no more to do ought for his father or his mother; Making the word of Elohim (God) of none effect through your tradition, which ye have received: and many such like things do ye.* (Mark 7:10-13)

The pattern the King names is not unique to Korban. *Many such like things do ye* — the Pharisaic-system had voided the Torah at multiple points by the same tradition-over-commandment mechanic. The fence-around-Torah had become the device by which Torah was nullified.

*Ye hypocrites, well did Yeshayahu (Isaiah) prophesy of you, saying, This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.* (Matthew 15:7-9)

The Tanakh-source is the prophet:

*Wherefore Adonai (the Lord) said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men.* (Isaiah 29:13)

The prophet had named the same diagnostic seven centuries before the Pharisaic-system arose. The form of worship — the mouth, the lips, the precept-of-men — is preserved; the substance of the heart-near-Yahuah (the LORD) is removed. *Teaching for doctrines the commandments of men* is what the prophet had said the system would do; the Pharisaic-tradition is the system the prophet had foreseen. The King names them with the prophet's own word: *hypocrites* — the Greek *hypokritēs,* a stage-actor wearing a mask. The mask is preserved; the face behind the mask is far from the Father.

*And he called the multitude, and said unto them, Hear, and understand: Not that which goeth into the mouth defileth a man; but that which cometh out of the mouth, this defileth a man.* (Matthew 15:10-11)

The King calls the multitude. He has separated the Korban-Pharisaic-tradition-from-Torah; now he separates Torah-substance-of-defilement from the rabbinic-fence's-inversion of it. Hold what this verse says and what it does not say. The verse is about the rabbinic hand-washing-defilement tradition the chapter opened on. It is not a statement abolishing the Torah's clean-and-unclean food architecture of Leviticus 11. The text does not mention dietary commandments at all. The thing the rabbinic-fence had been treating as the defiler of the man — un-ceremonially-washed-hands transferring uncleanness into the food — is what the King is dismantling. The substance the Torah names as a defiler of the man — the heart's outflow into thoughts, words, and deeds against the covenant — the King is restoring to its proper weight.

The Pharisees are offended. The disciples notice.

*Then came his disciples, and said unto him, Knowest thou that the Pharisees were offended, after they heard this saying? But he answered and said, Every plant, which my heavenly Father hath not planted, shall be rooted up. Let them alone: they be blind leaders of the blind. And if the blind lead the blind, both shall fall into the ditch.* (Matthew 15:12-14)

Hold the *every plant which my heavenly Father hath not planted* phrase. This is the wheat-and-tares-architecture of Matthew 13 surfacing again. The Pharisaic-system is the planting the Father did not plant. The tares-in-the-field-of-Yashar'el (Israel) are the religious-system that the enemy sowed alongside the genuine covenant-life the Father planted at Sinai. The King is naming the Pharisaic-system as the planting that the harvest will root up. The reapers-and-the-burning of Matthew 13:39-42 is in view. The disciples should not be distressed that the King's-words have offended the Pharisaic-leadership; the Pharisaic-leadership is precisely the planting the harvest is for.

*Let them alone* — leave them; their disposition is set. *Blind leaders of the blind* names what the system is and what its trajectory ends in. The ditch is the destination of the blind-led-by-the-blind. The institutional-religious-system that has built its credentialing-architecture on tradition-over-Torah is leading those who follow into the ditch. The King is not commissioning the disciples to fix the Pharisaic-system; he is commissioning them to recognize what it is and proceed.

*Then answered Kefa (Peter) and said unto him, Declare unto us this parable. And Yahusha (Jesus) said, Are ye also yet without understanding? Do not ye yet understand, that whatsoever entereth in at the mouth goeth into the belly, and is cast out into the draught? But those things which proceed out of the mouth come forth from the heart; and they defile the man. For out of the heart proceed evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false witness, blasphemies: These are the things which defile a man: but to eat with unwashen hands defileth not a man.* (Matthew 15:15-20)

Kefa (Peter) asks for the unpacking; the King provides it. The architecture is laid out plainly. Food enters the mouth, goes into the belly, is processed and cast out — it operates at the level of the digestion, not at the level of the heart-substance. What proceeds out of the mouth comes from the heart, and the heart's-substance is what defiles the man. The list the King gives names the catalogue of heart-defilement: *evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false witness, blasphemies.* The catalogue tracks the second-table of the Decalogue — murder, adultery, theft, false witness — extended to include the heart-substance behind those deeds. The Torah names these as defilers of the man; the rabbinic-fence had been treating un-ceremonially-washed-hands as the defiler in their place. The fence had inverted what the Torah taught; the King is restoring what the Torah had said.

The closing line locks the chapter's-architecture: *but to eat with unwashen hands defileth not a man.* The rabbinic-tradition is named explicitly as the source of the inversion, and the inversion is dismantled. The Torah-clean-and-unclean architecture is not touched. The heart-defilement architecture is restored to its place as the central diagnostic. The kingdom-citizens are taught the disposition: the heart's substance is what the Father weighs, and the heart's-substance manifests in the mouth's-outflow.

*Then Yahusha (Jesus) went thence, and departed into the coasts of Tsor (Tyre) and Tsidon (Sidon). And, behold, a woman of Canaan came out of the same coasts, and cried unto him, saying, Have mercy on me, O Lord, thou Son of David; my daughter is grievously vexed with a devil.* (Matthew 15:21-22)

The King withdraws northwest into the coastal region of Tsor (Tyre) and Tsidon (Sidon) — territory that lay outside the historical Yahudah (Judah) boundary, in territory once Phoenician, and territory through which the Assyrian-exile of the northern tribes had passed centuries before, leaving descendants of the scattered seed mixed into the population. The woman who comes out is identified as Canaanite — by ethnicity, descended from the original inhabitants of the land before the conquest under Yehoshua (Joshua). She addresses the King with the Davidic-Messianic title *Son of David,* which is the covenant-recognition language. Her daughter is *grievously vexed with a devil.* She is asking for what only the covenant-King can give.

*But he answered her not a word. And his disciples came and besought him, saying, Send her away; for she crieth after us. But he answered and said, I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 15:23-24)

Hold this scene carefully. The King ignores the woman. The disciples ask him to send her away. The King's answer, spoken to the disciples in the woman's hearing, is the most direct mission-scope statement the King makes in the entire gospel: *I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* The Greek is *ouk apestalēn ei mē eis ta probata ta apolōlota oikou Israēl* — *I was not sent except to the sheep, the lost ones, of the house of Yashar'el (Israel).* The negative construction is emphatic. There is no other audience for the King's-mission. The mission-scope-statement at Matthew 10:5-6, given to the twelve when he commissioned them to the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel), is now confirmed as the King's own scope as well. The lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) — the scattered seed of the twelve tribes, the descendants of the divorced-northern-house the prophets had promised to gather — is the audience. The Canaanite-woman is not one of them.

The Christian-commentary tradition has misread this scene in two directions. One reading treats v.24 as the King's pre-cross-mission-statement that gets later overridden post-cross to mean *the mission goes to all nations universally without distinction.* The text does not say this. The King never overrides v.24 in the gospel. The Great Commission at Matthew 28:19-20 does not abolish the lost-sheep-scope; it extends the proclamation to all nations because the lost sheep are scattered through all nations and the proclamation must reach them where they are. *His sheep hear his voice* (John 10:27) — the audience is the citizens; the practice is broad proclamation because we do not know them by sight. The lost-sheep-scope is the audience; the all-nations is the geography. The two are not contradictory.

The second misreading treats the Canaanite-woman's faith-confession as a paradigm-passage for nations-becoming-Yashar'el (Israel) by belief. The text gives the opposite of that. Watch what the King does next.

*Then came she and worshipped him, saying, Lord, help me. But he answered and said, It is not meet to take the children's bread, and to cast it to dogs.* (Matthew 15:25-26)

The woman comes and worships. The King's response is harder than the prior silence. *It is not meet to take the children's bread, and to cast it to dogs.* The children are the lost sheep of Yashar'el (Israel) — the covenant-children to whom the bread of the kingdom belongs. The dogs are the nations — and the woman is being placed inside that category by the King's own naming. The bread the King has been multiplying in the desert-place for the multitudes of the scattered-seed is not appropriately given to the non-covenant-recipient. The architecture the prophets had maintained — the covenant has a house; the nations stand outside the house — is the architecture the King is naming to her face.

The woman's response is the substance the chapter has been training the reader toward.

*And she said, Truth, Lord: yet the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters' table.* (Matthew 15:27)

Hold what she says. She does not contest the architecture. She does not say *I should be at the table.* She does not say *the children's bread is mine too by faith.* She does not say *the distinction is unjust.* She says *truth, Lord* — affirming the architecture exactly as the King has stated it — and then she names the place the architecture itself permits her to stand in: *the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters' table.* The crumbs from the table are not a demand for table-seating; they are a request for what falls under the table. She accepts the seed-priority architecture. Her plea is for mercy within it, not for revision of it. She submits.

*Then Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said unto her, O woman, great is thy faith: be it unto thee even as thou wilt. And her daughter was made whole from that very hour.* (Matthew 15:28)

*Great is thy faith.* The King names what is in her, and the named substance is the disposition that has just submitted to the architecture. The faith the King calls great is not a confession that overrode the lost-sheep-scope. The faith the King calls great is the disposition that received the architecture as true, did not protest the seed-distinction, asked only for the mercy that the architecture itself permits at the crumb-level — and trusted that the King would extend the mercy. Her healing is granted *as she will* — and what she has just willed is the position-as-recipient-of-crumbs-from-the-master's-table. The single grace-extension to a non-Yashar'el (Israel) woman in the territory where the scattered seed had been dispersed does not abolish the mission-scope-lock; it operates within it, as an exception that confirms the architecture rather than dissolving it.

The Tanakh has named the precedent. In the days of Eliyahu (Elijah), in famine across the land of Yashar'el (Israel), the prophet was sent not to the many widows of Yashar'el (Israel) but to a widow in this same Tsidon (Sidon) territory:

*And Eliyahu (Elijah) the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilad (Gilead), said unto Ahav (Ahab), As Yahuah (the LORD) Elohim (God) of Yashar'el (Israel) liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word. And the word of Yahuah (the LORD) came unto him, saying, Get thee hence, and turn thee eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, that is before Yarden (Jordan). ... And the word of Yahuah (the LORD) came unto him, saying, Arise, get thee to Tsarpat (Zarephath), which belongeth to Tsidon (Sidon), and dwell there: behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee.* (1 Kings 17:1-3, 8-9)

The widow of Tsarpat (Zarephath) is a non-Yashar'el (Israel) woman in the Tsidon (Sidon)-territory who sustains the prophet with the meal-and-oil that does not fail and whose dead son is restored to life by the prophet's prayer. The Tanakh records the precedent without explanation; the King himself unfolds the reading at Luke 4:25-26 in the Natsareth (Nazareth) synagogue:

*But I tell you of a truth, many widows were in Yashar'el (Israel) in the days of Eliyahu (Elijah), when the heaven was shut up three years and six months, when great famine was throughout all the land; But unto none of them was Eliyahu (Elijah) sent, save unto Tsarpat (Zarephath), a city of Tsidon (Sidon), unto a woman that was a widow.* (Luke 4:25-26)

The prophet's mission was to Yashar'el (Israel); the prophet's single grace-extension under that mission was to a non-Yashar'el (Israel) widow in the Tsidon (Sidon) territory; the architecture of the mission and the single-extension-within-it operated then exactly as it operates now with the Canaanite-woman. The King is enacting the Tanakh-pattern the prophet had laid down centuries before. The reading the framework holds is not a novelty; it is the architecture of the canon read consistently with itself.

The deeper substance the chapter has been teaching surfaces. The Pharisaic-system in Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), descended from the covenant-children by lineage, had built credentialing-systems on top of the lineage to defend the lineage's-privilege. The Canaanite-woman, lacking the lineage, did not build credentialing-systems against it; she accepted the architecture and stood in the position the architecture permitted her to stand in. The mercy flowed where the disposition-of-submission was, not where the credentialing-system claimed it should be. The first will be last and the last will be first the King had taught at Matthew 19:30 (which the gospel will reach later); the architecture operates here in real time. The Canaanite-woman's disposition is closer to the Father than the Yerushalayim (Jerusalem)-Pharisees', not because she has become Yashar'el (Israel) — she has not, and the text gives no indication that she has — but because mercy-from-the-King meets the submitted-disposition wherever it stands. The single-mercy-extension is not the architecture's-collapse; it is the architecture's-witness that even at the crumb-position the Father's-mercy is unbound.

*And Yahusha (Jesus) departed from thence, and came nigh unto the sea of Galilee; and went up into a mountain, and sat down there. And great multitudes came unto him, having with them those that were lame, blind, dumb, maimed, and many others, and cast them down at Yahusha's (Jesus's) feet; and he healed them: insomuch that the multitude wondered, when they saw the dumb to speak, the maimed to be whole, the lame to walk, and the blind to see: and they glorified the Elohim (God) of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Matthew 15:29-31)

The King returns to the Sea of Galilee, goes up into the mountain, sits down. The multitudes come bringing the diseased. The healings cascade. The closing line of the section names what the multitudes recognize: *they glorified the Elohim (God) of Yashar'el (Israel).* The covenant-name-substance the multitudes confess is not a generic *they glorified Elohim (God)*; it is the *Elohim of Yashar'el (Israel)* — the covenant-Father of the twelve-tribe-people. The recognition operates inside the covenant-architecture. The multitudes are the lost-sheep-of-Yashar'el (Israel) coming to recognize who the King is, in the territory the prophets had named as the place of their light.

*Then Yahusha (Jesus) called his disciples unto him, and said, I have compassion on the multitude, because they continue with me now three days, and have nothing to eat: and I will not send them away fasting, lest they faint in the way. And his disciples say unto him, Whence should we have so much bread in the wilderness, as to fill so great a multitude? And Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto them, How many loaves have ye? And they said, Seven, and a few little fishes. And he commanded the multitude to sit down on the ground. And he took the seven loaves and the fishes, and gave thanks, and brake them, and gave to his disciples, and the disciples to the multitude. And they did eat, and were filled: and they took up of the broken meat that was left seven baskets full. And they that did eat were four thousand men, beside women and children. And he sent away the multitude, and took ship, and came into the coasts of Magdala.* (Matthew 15:32-39)

The feeding of the four thousand. This is a distinct event from the feeding of the five thousand at Matthew 14:14-21 — different territory (the Sea of Galilee but east-side, in the Decapolis region per Mark 7:31 parallel), different number of loaves (seven), different number of baskets remaining (seven), different audience-size (four thousand men plus women and children). The disciples' question — *whence should we have so much bread in the wilderness?* — comes from disciples who have just witnessed the five-thousand-feeding, and yet they ask. The disciples are still being trained in the architecture of who the King is. The King will name this disposition explicitly in the next chapter at Matthew 16:9 — *do ye not yet understand, neither remember the five loaves of the five thousand ... neither the seven loaves of the four thousand?* The repeated feeding is the King's pedagogical-substance for disciples who need to see twice before the seeing settles.

Hold the seven-and-twelve numerology the two feedings carry. The five-thousand-feeding produced twelve baskets remaining — twelve tribes, the substance the prophets had named in the gathering of the scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel). The four-thousand-feeding produces seven baskets — the Tanakh's number of covenant-completion: seven days of creation with the Shabbat at the climax, the seven feasts of the covenant year, the seven nations of Canaan the conquest displaced, the seven sevens of the Yovel-cycle. The two numerologies together — twelve and seven — name the substance the King is enacting at two registers: the twelve-tribe gathering of the scattered seed, and the covenant-completion that the gathering will land at. The feedings are not duplicate-tellings of one story; they are two-pulled-up-shorts of the architecture, each carrying its own resonance.

The King boards a ship and crosses to Magdala (the western shore again). Matthew 16 will open with the Pharisees and Sadducees coming together to demand a sign from heaven — the credentialing-system regrouping for another confrontation — and the King's warning to the disciples against the *leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees* will be answered by the disciples' confusion over bread, the King's reminder of the two feedings, and then the weight-bearing confession-at-Caesarea-Philippi-of-Kefa (Peter): *Thou art the Messiah (Christ), the Son of the living Elohim (God).* The chapter that closed with the multitudes glorifying the Elohim (God) of Yashar'el (Israel) will be followed by the disciple-Kefa (Peter) confessing who the King is in the language the multitudes had reached for, and the King's first explicit announcement of the cross and the resurrection.
