# Short-form commentary on Matthew 2

Matthew 2 is the gathering-of-the-scattered walking out its first scenes. The chapter opens with the arrival of the Magi from the east, walks the infant Messiah down to Egypt and back, names the Beth-lehem slaughter against the chapter that promised the gathering and the new covenant, and lands at Natsareth (Nazareth) where the village name itself is the Branch-prophecy made geographic. The Christian Christmas-card reading flattens almost every beat of the chapter. The framework reads what Matthew actually carries.

*Now when Yahusha (Jesus) was born in Beth-lehem of Yahudah (Judaea) in the days of Herod the king, behold, there came wise men from the east to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), saying, Where is he that is born King of the Yahudim (Jews)? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him.* (Matthew 2:1-2)

The *wise men from the east* are not generic Gentile worshippers. The Greek *magoi* names a specific priestly caste — the Persian Magi, the Zoroastrian scholar-priests of the Persian and Parthian courts, the inheritors of the Babylonian astronomical-prophetic tradition. They are the same caste that appears in Daniel — the *Chaldeans, the magicians, the soothsayers, and the wise men* of the Babylonian and Medo-Persian courts — over whom Daniel was made master.

*Then the king made Daniel a great man, and gave him many great gifts, and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon.* (Daniel 2:48)

The Magi who arrive in Yerushalayim asking after the King of the Yahudim are not random Gentiles who happened to study astrology and stumble into Hebrew Messianic expectation. They are the institutional inheritors of the caste Daniel led — the prophetic tradition Daniel taught carried forward in the eastern dispersion through the centuries between Daniel and Yahusha (Jesus). The star they saw was the Numbers-24 sign Balaam had prophesied.

*I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh: there shall come a Star out of Ya'aqov (Jacob), and a Sceptre shall rise out of Yashar'el (Israel), and shall smite the corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth.* (Numbers 24:17)

The Christian read takes the Magi as proof that the gospel was always going to expand beyond Yashar'el to all peoples — the Jew-and-Gentile-inclusion thesis. The framework reads them differently. The eastern peoples among whom the Magi-caste operated were peoples in whose midst the scattered seed of Yashar'el lived — the northern ten tribes carried into Assyrian resettlement zones in Halah, Habor, the river of Gozan, the cities of the Medes; the Yahudite-Levitical exile to Babylon and Persia; the various subsequent waves into Media, Parthia, the East. The Magi's possession of Hebrew prophetic knowledge is plausible because the Hebrew prophetic tradition was alive in their territories. Their arrival is the first beat of the gathering — those who heard the proclamation responding when the moment arrived.

The chapter then turns to Herod. *When Herod the king had heard these things, he was troubled, and all Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) with him.* The framework hears the Edomite-on-the-Davidic-throne in panic. Herod the Great was an Idumean by ethnicity — his father Antipater Idumean, his mother Cypros Nabataean. The Edomites had been forced-converted to Yahudaism under the Hasmonean John Hyrcanus I's conquest of Edom around 125 BCE. Rome installed Herod as King of the Yahudim (Jews) in 37 BCE, displacing the Hasmonean line. He sat on the throne of David as an Edomite — not of the seed of David, not of the tribe of Yahudah (Judah), not even of Yashar'el (Israel) by paternal blood. He was the latest beat of the Esau-against-Ya'aqov hatred-pattern that runs through the Tanakh.

*And Esau hated Ya'aqov (Jacob) because of the blessing wherewith his father blessed him: and Esau said in his heart, The days of mourning for my father are at hand; then will I slay my brother Ya'aqov (Jacob).* (Genesis 27:41)

*Yet I loved Ya'aqov (Jacob), and I hated Esau, and laid his mountains and his heritage waste for the dragons of the wilderness.* (Malachi 1:3)

Herod's false-piety request that the Magi report back to him is the cover for the murder he is already planning. When the Magi do not return, the cover falls away and the murder becomes the slaughter of every infant in Beth-lehem and its coasts under two years of age. This is the Edomite-on-the-throne attempting to extinguish the Davidic-line claimant.

The flight to Egypt is the next scene, and Matthew names the prophetic substance with a single verse.

*When he arose, he took the young child and his mother by night, and departed into Egypt: and was there until the death of Herod: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of Yahuah (the Lord) by the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt have I called my son.* (Matthew 2:14-15)

*When Yashar'el (Israel) was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.* (Hosea 11:1)

The Hosea verse in its own context names Yashar'el (Israel) as Yahuah's (God's) firstborn son brought out of Egyptian bondage at the Exodus. The Christian reading of Matthew's quotation is that this is one more loose proof-text — Matthew finding an Old Testament verse that vaguely resembles the Egypt-sojourn of the infant Yahusha (Jesus). The framework reads the quotation at the depth it carries. Matthew is doing identification, not loose proof-texting. Yahusha (Jesus) IS Yashar'el (Israel). He carries the people's identity in his own person. He recapitulates their history. The exodus from Egypt becomes the Messiah's own infancy-exodus. The wilderness wandering will become his own forty-day wilderness temptation in Matthew 4. The mountain-instruction at Sinai will become his own mountain-instruction in the Sermon on the Mount of Matthew 5–7. The crossing into the land will become his own ministry in the land. The Messiah is the seed of Yashar'el who walks the path the people walked, and the recapitulation is the credential — the One who saves *his people from their sins* knows the journey from the inside because he walked it in his own body.

The Pharaoh-Mosheh (Moses) pattern is the deeper Tanakh-pattern Matthew has in view: Pharaoh ordered the killing of the Hebrew male infants in Exodus 1, and Mosheh was preserved in a basket on the Nile; Herod ordered the killing of the Beth-lehem infants, and Yahusha was preserved in Egypt. The pattern repeats, with Herod-Yahusha in the lead roles where Pharaoh-Mosheh once stood. Matthew is preparing the reader for the Sermon-on-the-Mount mountain and the new-Torah-from-the-mountain framing the gospel will carry.

The Beth-lehem slaughter Matthew narrates next, and the verse he quotes is one of the most concentrated gathering-prophecies in the prophetic corpus, in chapter context.

*Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Yiremyahu (Jeremiah) the prophet, saying, In Rama was there a voice heard, lamentation, and weeping, and great mourning, Rachel weeping for her children, and would not be comforted, because they are not.* (Matthew 2:17-18)

*Thus saith Yahuah (the LORD); A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping; Rahel (Rachel) weeping for her children refused to be comforted for her children, because they were not.* (Jeremiah 31:15)

The Christian read treats this as Matthew pulling a sad verse to color the slaughter. The framework reads the verse against the chapter it sits in. Jeremiah 31 opens with *the families of Yashar'el (Israel)* and the promise of the gathering.

*Behold, I will bring them from the north country, and gather them from the coasts of the earth, and with them the blind and the lame, the woman with child and her that travaileth with child together: a great company shall return thither. ... He that scattered Yashar'el (Israel) will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd doth his flock.* (Jeremiah 31:8, 10)

The verse Matthew quotes sits in the middle of this gathering-prophecy. Immediately after the Rachel-weeping verse, the chapter answers the weeping.

*Thus saith Yahuah (the LORD); Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears: for thy work shall be rewarded, saith Yahuah (the LORD); and they shall come again from the land of the enemy. And there is hope in thine end, saith Yahuah (the LORD), that thy children shall come again to their own border.* (Jeremiah 31:16-17)

And the chapter lands at the new covenant.

*Behold, the days come, saith Yahuah (the LORD), that I will make a new covenant with the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and with the house of Yahudah (Judah): Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt; which my covenant they brake, although I was an husband unto them, saith Yahuah (the LORD): But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Yashar'el (Israel); After those days, saith Yahuah (the LORD), I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people.* (Jeremiah 31:31-33)

Matthew is not quoting one sad verse. He is naming the whole chapter. The Beth-lehem slaughter is the latest beat of the long weeping; the protection of the Messianic child is the protection of the answer to the weeping; and the blood of the protected child will, twenty-six chapters later, cut the new covenant the chapter ends with. *For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins,* Yahusha (Jesus) will say at the Last Supper, naming Jeremiah 31:31 as he hands the cup. Matthew 2:18 and Matthew 26:28 are bracketing the same chapter of Jeremiah.

The chapter closes with the settling in Natsareth.

*And he came and dwelt in a city called Natsareth (Nazareth): that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophets, He shall be called a Nazarene.* (Matthew 2:23)

The interpretive crux: no Old Testament passage explicitly says *he shall be called a Nazarene.* Matthew attributes the saying to *the prophets* plural, signaling a theme drawn from across the prophetic corpus rather than a single verse-citation. The framework's reading is the *netser*-Branch one. The Hebrew *netser* means *branch* or *shoot,* and is one of the Branch-words in the Messianic-Branch prophecies.

*And there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Yishai (Jesse), and a Branch [netser] shall grow out of his roots.* (Isaiah 11:1)

*Behold, the days come, saith Yahuah (the LORD), that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth.* (Jeremiah 23:5)

*Hear now, O Yahoshua (Joshua) the high priest, thou, and thy fellows that sit before thee: for they are men wondered at: for, behold, I will bring forth my servant the BRANCH.* (Zechariah 3:8)

The Messiah settling in *Natsareth* — *Branch-town* — is the prophetic-name fulfillment of the Branch-prophecy made geographic. The Galilean residence also positions him in the territory Isaiah 9 named as the people walking in darkness who would see a great light. The chapter closes with the Messianic child in Branch-town in the Galilee, the despised northern territory where the scattered tribes of Naphtali, Zevulun, and Yissakhar (Issachar) had lived before the Assyrian deportation. The gospel-of-the-gathering has its King in the right place. Matthew 4 will pick the geography up explicitly when the public ministry opens.
