# Short-form commentary on Matthew 5

Matthew 5 opens the Sermon on the Mount — the King ascending the mountain, the disciples drawing close, the multitudes overhearing — and lays the heart-Torah down at the throne-place. The chapter carries the Beatitudes as the roster of the citizens of the kingdom already gathered, the salt-and-light naming of the disciples' covenant function, the load-bearing anti-antinomian declaration of verses 17 through 19, the contrast with the Pharisaic-Sadducean counterfeit-righteousness, and the six antitheses (*ye have heard ... but I say unto you*) which restore the Torah's heart-substance against the oral-tradition distortions. The Christian preaching tradition has flattened nearly every beat of this chapter; the framework recovers what the King actually said.

*And seeing the multitudes, he went up into a mountain: and when he was set, his disciples came unto him: And he opened his mouth, and taught them.* (Matthew 5:1-2)

The mountain is not incidental scenery. Mosheh (Moses) ascended a mountain to receive Torah from Yahuah and brought it DOWN to the people at the base. Matthew 5 inverts the spatial frame: the Messiah goes UP, and the people come up *to him.* The teacher does not descend with a tablet — the teacher SITS, the rabbinic posture of teaching with authority, and the disciples draw near. The Torah is now spoken in the King's own voice, from the mountain. Per the nature-of-God reading established at the *Immanuel — Elohim (God) with us* of Matthew 1 and confirmed at the immersion-voice of Matthew 3, the Yahuah who spoke at the burning bush, who descended on Sinai in fire, who spoke the ten words to Mosheh, is the Formed — the Son drawn from the Formless — who has now taken flesh and stands on the mountain. The same Voice. The same Torah. The intensification is in the unveiling of the heart-Torah the Sinai-Torah always carried in its substance.

*Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted. Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth. Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled. Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see Elohim (God). Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of Elohim (God). Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.* (Matthew 5:3-10)

The Christian commentary tradition has often read the Beatitudes as a how-to roster — a graduated ladder of dispositions one must achieve to attain the kingdom. The framework rejects that reading. The Beatitudes are a description of the citizens of the kingdom *already* — a roster of the postures by which the gathered remnant can be identified. Each Beatitude names a prophetic-remnant marker the Tanakh has already established. Yahusha (Jesus) is not telling the disciples *become these things to enter the kingdom;* he is naming them as those who *are* these things because the Father has drawn them.

The *poor in spirit* are the *anawim* of the Tanakh — the broken, the contrite, the destitute-in-self.

*For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones.* (Isaiah 57:15)

The *mourn* register goes through Ezekiel 9.

*And Yahuah (the LORD) said unto him, Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), and set a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof.* (Ezekiel 9:4)

The mourners are those whose hearts have been broken by the abominations in the holy city — those who carry the prophetic sorrow that the leadership-pulpit has voided the heart-Torah. They are MARKED for preservation through the day of judgment. The comfort they shall receive is Yeshayahu (Isaiah) 61's comfort to *all that mourn in Tsion (Zion).*

The *meek* shall inherit the earth — Yahusha quotes Psalm 37 directly.

*But the meek shall inherit the earth; and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace.* (Psalm 37:11)

The earth-inheritance is the prophetic land-inheritance of the gathered remnant at the consummation, not a vague heavenly-realm aspiration. The *peacemakers* are *called the children of Elohim (God)* — the lineage-identification surfacing in plain text. They are not made children by peacemaking; they are children who make peace because that is what the children do. The Beatitude reveals; it does not manufacture. The whole roster is the family-resemblance of the citizens already in the kingdom.

*Ye are the salt of the earth ... Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.* (Matthew 5:13-14)

The Greek *este* is indicative, not imperative: *you are.* The disciples are not commanded to become salt and light; they are named as the salt and the light they already are. Salt in the Tanakh carries the covenant-preservation register.

*And every oblation of thy meat offering shalt thou season with salt; neither shalt thou suffer the salt of the covenant of thy Elohim (God) to be lacking from thy meat offering: with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt.* (Leviticus 2:13)

The disciples are salt of the earth because they preserve the covenant — the heart-Torah, the Sabbath, the feasts, the names. The system that voids the heart-Torah is the salt that has lost its savour. Light in the Tanakh carries the revealing register, particularly in the gathering-of-Yashar'el frame of Yeshayahu.

*And he said, It is a light thing that thou shouldest be my servant to raise up the tribes of Ya'aqov (Jacob), and to restore the preserved of Yashar'el (Israel): I will also give thee for a light to the goyim (Gentiles), that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth.* (Isaiah 49:6)

The Yeshayahu 49 servant is the light to the lands of the scattering, where the seed of Yashar'el has been dispersed; the gathering of Ya'aqov is the FIRST mission; the light to the goyim is the SECOND mission — the light reaches the lost sheep in the lands of their scattering. The disciples on the second-Sinai mountain are the present-age witness of the gathered remnant carrying this light.

*Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled. Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven: but whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.* (Matthew 5:17-19)

These three verses are the load-bearing centerpiece of the chapter. The institutional-Christian system that voids the commandments has either ignored these verses entirely or developed elaborate exegesis to neutralize them. The framework reads them plainly.

Yahusha opens with a PREEMPTIVE denial. *Think not* — *me nomisēte* — *do not even suppose.* He knows the misreading is coming. He is teaching the heart-Torah at a depth no one before him has unfolded, and he knows that some will read his teaching as an attack on the written Torah. So he closes the door before it can be opened. *To destroy* — *kataluō* — means to dismantle, to take down stone by stone, to abrogate. Yahusha is NOT here to dismantle the Torah; he is NOT here to dismantle the prophets. *To fulfil* — *plēroō* — means to fill up, to bring to fullness, to complete in substance. The Christian commentary tradition has frequently read *plēroō* here as *to bring to a close, to satisfy and thereby end* — as if Yahusha saying *I have come to fulfill* means *I am closing the Torah's relevance by exhausting it.* This is the antichrist reading. The Greek *plēroō* throughout the New Testament regularly means *to fill up.* Yahusha fills the Torah with the heart-Torah; he does not empty it.

*One jot or one tittle.* The *jot* is the *yod* — the smallest letter of the Hebrew alphabet. The *tittle* is the *kotz* — the small ornamental stroke on certain Hebrew letters. Yahusha is binding himself to the consonantal Hebrew text at the SMALLEST-POSSIBLE-MARK level. Not one letter passes. Not one stroke of one letter passes. The eschatological terminus is the dissolution of the present heavens and earth at the consummation — not the cross. The *till all be fulfilled* clause parallels *till heaven and earth pass;* the two clauses bracket the same timeframe. Until the consummation of all things, the Torah stands.

The third verse — the diagnostic — has two components: *break* + *teach men so.* The personal breaking is one thing; the breaking-coupled-with-teaching-others-to-break is the diagnostic Yahusha names. The horn's pulpit teaches the congregation that the commandments are abolished — that the Sabbath is moved, that the dietary laws are voided, that the feasts are replaced, that the heart-Torah of obedience is contrary to grace. This is *break and teach men so.* Yahusha names them: *called the least in the kingdom of heaven.* Mercy is held open — they are not categorically out — but the diagnostic stands. The reverse direction is named in the same verse: *whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.* Walk the commandments, teach the commandments, and the kingdom-status is great. The Tanakh-prophets, Yochanan (John) the Immerser, the apostolic witnesses who did and taught, and the gathered remnant doing and teaching today are the kingdom's great ones.

Matthew 5:19 is the New Testament companion of John's first letter.

*And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.* (1 John 2:3-4)

Both verses say the same thing in different grammar: keeping the commandments — and teaching others to keep them — is the proof of knowing him.

*For I say unto you, That except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no wise enter into the kingdom of heaven.* (Matthew 5:20)

Yahusha is NOT saying *the Pharisaic righteousness is righteousness, but you need MORE of it.* That is the easy misread. The Pharisaic righteousness is a counterfeit-form — outside-only performance, oral-tradition expansion, void-of-heart compliance with display-value. To exceed it is not to do more of it; to exceed it is to walk the heart-Torah it counterfeits. The Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership-system was already named at Matthew 3:7 as the *generation of vipers* — the seed-war identification of the antichrist-front in its first-century beat. The pattern Paul will identify later as the *flesh-credential* gospel of the *circumcision party* — the legalistic-performance Judaizers — is the descendant of the same Pharisaic righteousness Yahusha names here. The heart-Torah Yahusha will now teach in verses 21 through 48 is the *exceeding* righteousness: the new-heart Torah Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) named and Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named.

*A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:26-27)

The six *ye have heard ... but I say unto you* exchanges of verses 21 through 48 are the most-frequently-misread block in the Sermon. The Christian commentary tradition has often read them as Yahusha ABROGATING the Torah by setting his own authority above Mosheh's. That reading collapses Yahusha into the *destroy the law* posture he just rejected at verse 17. The framework reads the antitheses on a different axis. Yahusha is not contrasting himself with the written Torah of Mosheh. He is contrasting himself with the ORAL TRADITION of the Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership-system — the *takkanot* and *gezerot,* the fence-around-the-Torah teachings, the truncations and additions that had been laid over the Torah by the rabbinic schools across the centuries. The *ye have heard* formula references what the people HEARD from the synagogue teachers, not what was WRITTEN in the scroll. Yahusha's *but I say unto you* is the corrective restoration of the original Torah's substance, set against the rabbinic-tradition distortion.

The pattern repeats across the six. The murder-commandment of antithesis 1 had been stopped at the bodily act; Yahusha restores the heart-substance the Torah always carried (*Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart,* Leviticus 19:17). The adultery-commandment of antithesis 2 had been stopped at the act; Yahusha restores the heart-substance the tenth commandment names (*Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife,* Exodus 20:17). The divorce-commandment of antithesis 3 had been inverted: the regulatory-concession of Deuteronomy 24:1 had been read as a Torah-mandate for permissive divorce; Yahusha restores the original Genesis-2 marriage-design and explicitly names what Deuteronomy 24:1 actually was at Matthew 19:8.

*He saith unto them, Mosheh (Moses) because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so.* (Matthew 19:8)

The oath-tradition of antithesis 4 had built an elaborate gradation system of binding-vs-non-binding formulas — *swear by the temple* is nothing, but *swear by the gold of the temple* is binding — that had become a tool for evading oaths through technical loopholes. Yahusha cuts the apparatus and restores the heart-substance: speak true, and your speech needs no fortification. The *lex talionis* of antithesis 5 — *eye for an eye* — was the Torah's judicial standard, a proportional-justice principle administered by the courts that LIMITED retaliation; the oral tradition had slipped it into the personal-revenge sphere; Yahusha restores it to its sphere by closing the personal-revenge misuse while leaving the judicial-Torah standing. The Christian-pacifist tradition has often misused this antithesis to claim Yahusha abolished the judicial-standard of the Torah; the framework rejects this. Paul confirms the magistrate's continuing-sword later.

*For rulers are not a terror to good works, but to the evil. ... For he is the minister of Elohim (God), a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil.* (Romans 13:3-4)

Antithesis 6 is the most explicit case of oral-tradition ADDITION. *Love thy neighbour* is Leviticus 19:18 — a real Torah-commandment. *And hate thine enemy* is NOT IN THE TORAH. There is no such commandment in the Tanakh. The oral tradition had ADDED *hate thine enemy* to the Torah's *love thy neighbor.* Yahusha is not contradicting Mosheh; he is correcting an oral-tradition INTERPOLATION. The Torah's actual command was love-of-neighbor AND love-of-stranger.

*But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 19:34)

*If thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him again. If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, and wouldest forbear to help him, thou shalt surely help with him.* (Exodus 23:4-5)

Yahusha extends the love-command explicitly to the enemy as well — restoring the full sweep of the Torah's heart-substance.

*Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.* (Matthew 5:48)

The chapter's closing word. *Perfect* — *teleios* — is the Greek translation of the Hebrew *tamim,* which in the Tanakh means *whole, complete, blameless.*

*And when Avram (Abram) was ninety years old and nine, Yahuah (the LORD) appeared to Avram (Abram), and said unto him, I am El Shaddai (the Almighty God); walk before me, and be thou perfect.* (Genesis 17:1)

*Thou shalt be perfect with Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God).* (Deuteronomy 18:13)

The Beatitudes' *pure in heart* finds its completion at the close of the chapter. The heart-Torah Yahusha has just unfolded across six antitheses calls the disciple to whole-hearted *tamim*-walk before the Father — the same standard the Torah has always named. The antitheses, taken together, do not abolish the Torah. They restore it. They strip the oral-tradition distortions off — the truncations of antitheses 1, 2, and 4, the inversion-of-concession of antithesis 3, the sphere-mistake of antithesis 5, and the outright addition of antithesis 6 — and they reveal the heart-Torah underneath. The Torah is intensified, not abolished. The contrary-to-Torah test of John's first letter stands at full force: the kingdom-righteousness keeps the commandments at heart-depth.
