# Commentary on Matthew 5

Matthew 5 opens the Sermon on the Mount — the King ascending the mountain, the disciples gathered close, the multitudes listening; the Beatitudes; the salt-and-light naming of the disciples' covenant function; the load-bearing anti-antinomian declaration of verses 17 through 19; the contrast with the Pharisaic-Sadducean righteousness; and the six antitheses (*ye have heard ... but I say unto you*) as oral-tradition correction, not Torah supersession. The Christian preaching tradition has flattened nearly every beat of this chapter; the framework recovers what the King actually said.

---

## §1. The mountain — the second-Sinai motif, the heart-Torah in the King's own voice

The framework reads the setting deliberately. The mountain is not incidental scenery. Mosheh (Moses) ascended a mountain to receive Torah from Yahuah (the LORD) and brought it DOWN to the people at the base. Matthew 5 inverts the spatial frame: the Messiah goes UP, and the people come up *to him.* The teacher does not descend with a tablet — the teacher SITS, the rabbinic posture of teaching with authority, and the disciples draw near. The Torah is now spoken in the King's own voice, from the mountain, to those gathering to hear.

The framework reading hears this as the second-Sinai motif Matthew has been preparing since chapter 2. Yahusha (Jesus) recapitulates Yashar'el (Israel) in his own body — the Egypt-sojourn of chapter 2, the Yarden (Jordan) immersion of chapter 3, the wilderness testing of chapter 4 — and now arrives at the mountain. But this is not a repetition of Sinai. This is its continuation by the same Voice. Per the Formless-Formed reading established at the *Immanuel — Elohim (God) with us* of Matthew 1:23 and the immersion-voice of Matthew 3:17, Yahuah who spoke at the burning bush, who descended on Sinai in fire, who spoke the ten words to Mosheh, is the Formed — the Son drawn from the Formless — who has now taken flesh and stands on the mountain teaching. The same Voice. The same Torah. The intensification is in the unveiling of the heart-Torah the Sinai-Torah always carried in its substance.

The audience-structure matters. The disciples *came unto him* — those who have left their nets, those who have stepped out of the vocational frame into the prophetic frame at Matthew 4:18-22. The multitudes follow and hear; the sermon closes with the multitudes astonished at the King's doctrine.

*And it came to pass, when Yahusha (Jesus) had ended these sayings, the people were astonished at his doctrine: For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.* (Matthew 7:28-29)

The teaching is FOR the disciples in primary scope and FOR the multitudes in overhearing scope. The Sermon's address is the gathered remnant — the lost sheep being drawn — with the multitudes pulled in toward the same gathering.

The framework reading lifts the mountain-and-multitude composition as deliberate. The King is on the mountain because the kingdom is at hand and he speaks from the throne-place. The disciples are close because they have answered the call. The multitudes are present because the Yeshayahu (Isaiah) 9 light has dawned in Galilee-of-the-goyim and they are being drawn. The Sermon on the Mount is the second-Sinai sermon of the kingdom-at-hand King.

## §2. The Beatitudes — the citizens-of-the-kingdom roster, not entry-conditions

*Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted. Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth. Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled. Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see Elohim (God). Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of Elohim (God). Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you, and persecute you, and shall say all manner of evil against you falsely, for my sake. Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets which were before you.* (Matthew 5:3-12)

This matters for the framework's audience-identification posture. The Beatitudes are how the lost sheep recognize themselves. They read the list and find their own posture named. They are gathered because they ARE these things, not made these things by gathering. The cause-and-effect reversal the canon teaches — *if you hear, you are sons of Avraham (Abraham); hearing reveals, hearing does not make* — applies to the Beatitudes too. The poor in spirit hear *theirs is the kingdom of heaven* and know themselves named.

**Poor in spirit (verse 3).** The *anawim* of the Tanakh — the broken, the contrite, the destitute-in-self.

*For all those things hath mine hand made, and all those things have been, saith Yahuah (the LORD): but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word.* (Isaiah 66:2)

The poor in spirit is the one whose self-trust has emptied; the kingdom belongs to such because such are the only ones who can receive the kingdom on the kingdom's terms. The Pharisaic-Sadducean righteousness-credential at Matthew 3:9 — *we have Avraham (Abraham) to our father* — is the OPPOSITE posture: full of credential, empty of contrition. The kingdom is for the empty.

**Mourn (verse 4).** The framework reads this through Ezekiel 9 directly.

The mourners are those whose hearts have been broken by the abominations in the holy city — those who weep over what the system has become, who carry the prophetic sorrow that the leadership-pulpit has voided the heart-Torah. They are MARKED for preservation through the day of judgment. The comfort they shall receive is Yeshayahu (Isaiah)'s comfort to all that mourn in Tsion (Zion).

*To proclaim the acceptable year of Yahuah (the LORD), and the day of vengeance of our Elohim (God); to comfort all that mourn; To appoint unto them that mourn in Tsion (Zion), to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of Yahuah (the LORD), that he might be glorified.* (Isaiah 61:2-3)

The mourners are the gathered remnant of the prophetic-cry posture. This is also the Beatitude that closes the door on Christian sentimentalism that reads *blessed are they that mourn* as a generic-grief-comfort. The mourning Yahusha names is the prophetic mourning over abominations — the same mourning that brings the Ezekiel-9 mark.

**Meek (verse 5).** Yahusha quotes Psalm 37 directly.

The Hebrew is *anavim* — close cousin of *anawim* — the humble, the bowed, the unviolent-in-strength. Psalm 37 is the framework Tanakh-context: the meek inherit the LAND (*ha-arets* in the Hebrew, *gēn* in the LXX), and the wicked are cut off from the land in the same psalm.

*For evildoers shall be cut off: but those that wait upon Yahuah (the LORD), they shall inherit the earth. For yet a little while, and the wicked shall not be: yea, thou shalt diligently consider his place, and it shall not be. But the meek shall inherit the earth; and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace.* (Psalm 37:9-11)

The Beatitude is a gathering-prophecy: the meek inherit the land at the end of the age. The framework reading hears this as direct continuation of the Tanakh's gathering-remnant promises — the gathered seed of promise enters the land at the consummation. The earth-inheritance is not a vague heavenly-realm aspiration; it is the prophetic land-inheritance of the gathered remnant in the millennial kingdom and beyond.

**Hunger and thirst after righteousness (verse 6).** *Tsedaqah* — covenant righteousness. The Pharisaic righteousness Yahusha will contrast at verse 20 is the counterfeit *tsedaqah* — outside-only performance for credential. The hunger Yahusha names is for the real thing.

*As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O Elohim (God). My soul thirsteth for Elohim (God), for the living Elohim (God): when shall I come and appear before Elohim (God)?* (Psalm 42:1-2)

The hungering and thirsting one shall be FILLED — the *plēroō* root that will return at verse 17 in *fulfil.* What fills is the heart-Torah, the new-heart promise of Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) 31 and Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) 36.

*Behold, the days come, saith Yahuah (the LORD), that I will make a new covenant with the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and with the house of Yahudah (Judah): ... But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Yashar'el (Israel); After those days, saith Yahuah (the LORD), I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people.* (Jeremiah 31:31, 33)

**Merciful (verse 7).** Covenant *chesed* — steadfast loyalty in love. Not Christian-sentimental softness. The merciful of the Tanakh are those who walk the prophetic *chesed* posture of Hoshea (Hosea) and Mikah (Micah).

*For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of Elohim (God) more than burnt offerings.* (Hosea 6:6)

*He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth Yahuah (the LORD) require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy Elohim (God)?* (Micah 6:8)

The merciful shall obtain mercy — the law of measure-for-measure the Tanakh repeatedly affirms. The Pharisaic-Sadducean system Yahusha will indict later at Matthew 23 is the antithesis: outside-only Torah-keeping without the *chesed*-substance.

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.* (Matthew 23:23)

**Pure in heart (verse 8).** This is the heart-Torah Beatitude — the direct echo of the new-heart promises of Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) 31:33 and Yechezk'el (Ezekiel).

The pure-in-heart shall SEE Elohim (God) — the unique covenantal vision-promise. *No man hath seen Elohim (God) at any time* (John 1:18) of the Father, the Formless; the pure-in-heart see the Formed — the Son who is the visible image of the Formless — and through him know the Father. This is the gathered-remnant promise lived out: the new-heart citizens of the kingdom see the King. Psalm 24 names the Tanakh-anchor of the pure-heart ascent.

*Who shall ascend into the hill of Yahuah (the LORD)? or who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully.* (Psalm 24:3-4)

**Peacemakers (verse 9).** *Shalom*-makers — those who carry the kingdom's wholeness-and-restoration ministry. *They shall be called the children of Elohim (God)* — the lineage-identification surfacing in plain text. *Children of Elohim* is the seed-of-Adam-line-of-promise identification, the called-out for whom names were written from the foundation of the world. The peacemakers are not made children by peacemaking; they are children who make peace because that is what the children do. The Beatitude reveals; it does not manufacture.

**Persecuted for righteousness' sake (verses 10-12).** The closing Beatitude lands on the cost of the gathered-remnant posture. The system that voided the heart-Torah persecutes those who walk it. *So persecuted they the prophets which were before you* — the prophetic-witness lineage from Hevel (Abel) through the Tanakh prophets through Yochanan (John) the Immerser stands as the testimony of the cost. The framework reads this in the seed-war terms Genesis 3:15 named — the persecution-pattern runs through the seed-of-the-serpent against the seed-of-the-woman across the ages. Yahusha will explicitly trace the lineage of prophetic-blood at Matthew 23.

*Wherefore, behold, I send unto you prophets, and wise men, and scribes: and some of them ye shall kill and crucify; and some of them shall ye scourge in your synagogues, and persecute them from city to city: That upon you may come all the righteous blood shed upon the earth, from the blood of righteous Hevel (Abel) unto the blood of Zechariah son of Berekhyah, whom ye slew between the temple and the altar.* (Matthew 23:34-35)

The disciples are joining a lineage of suffering witnesses. The reward is the same as the prophets'.

Eight Beatitudes — eight markers of the gathered citizens of the kingdom. The Christian commentary tradition has often listed them as virtues to cultivate. The framework reads them as identification-markers of those the Father has drawn — the roster by which the lost sheep find themselves named. The Beatitudes are not the ladder INTO the kingdom; they are the family-resemblance of the citizens already in it.

## §3. Salt of the earth, light of the world — the prophetic-witness function of the gathered

*Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men. Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid. Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth light unto all that are in the house. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.* (Matthew 5:13-16)

The framework reads the salt-and-light naming in two registers — the disciples already ARE the salt and the light (the Greek *este* is indicative not imperative), and the Tanakh-substance underneath each term carries the covenant-preservation register (salt) and the gathering-of-Yashar'el register (light) the Christian commentary tradition has flattened into generic personal-witness motifs. Salt is the preserving-of-covenant substance — the *covenant of salt* names a forever-covenant standard.

*All the heave offerings of the holy things, which the children of Yashar'el (Israel) offer unto Yahuah (the LORD), have I given thee, and thy sons and thy daughters with thee, by a statute for ever: it is a covenant of salt for ever before Yahuah (the LORD) unto thee and to thy seed with thee.* (Numbers 18:19)

The disciples are salt of the earth because they preserve the covenant the Father gave: the heart-Torah, the prophetic witness, the Sabbath, the feasts, the names. The system that voids the heart-Torah is the salt that has lost its savour and is cast out — the leadership-pulpit that voids the commandments has lost the preserving function.

Light in the Tanakh carries the revealing-and-instructing register, particularly in the gathering-of-Yashar'el frame of Yeshayahu. The Yeshayahu 49:6 sequence — gathering of Ya'aqov FIRST, light to the goyim SECOND — names the gathering-light the gathered carry into the lands of the scattering. Yeshayahu 60 widens the same scene at the consummation.

*Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) is risen upon thee. For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but Yahuah (the LORD) shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee. And the goyim (Gentiles) shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising.* (Isaiah 60:1-3)

*A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.* The framework reads this against Yeshayahu 2 and Mikah (Micah) 4.

*And it shall come to pass in the last days, that the mountain of Yahuah's (the LORD's) house shall be established in the top of the mountains, and shall be exalted above the hills; and all nations shall flow unto it. And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of Yahuah (the LORD), to the house of the Elohim (God) of Ya'aqov (Jacob); and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Tsion (Zion) shall go forth the law, and the word of Yahuah (the LORD) from Yerushalayim (Jerusalem).* (Isaiah 2:2-3)

The visible city is the eschatological gathering-place; the disciples on the second-Sinai mountain are the present-age foreshadowing of that city.

*Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.* The framework reads *good works* in the Tanakh-substance of *ma'asim tovim,* covenant-loyal action, heart-Torah lived out. The light is the lived life of the gathered citizens. Not preaching alone, not credential-claim, but the visible covenant-walk. The Christian commentary that has read *good works* against a *faith-not-works* framing has the grammar inverted; the framework reads it as the Beatitudes' postures lived out in visible covenant-faithful action, with the glory going to the Father whose Torah the action carries.

## §4. *Think not that I am come to destroy the law* — the load-bearing anti-antinomian declaration

These three verses are the load-bearing centerpiece of Matthew 5 for the framework. The institutional-Christian system that voids the commandments has either ignored these verses entirely or developed elaborate exegesis to neutralize them. The framework reads the verses plainly.

**Verse 17 — *Think not that I am come to destroy.*** Yahusha opens with a PREEMPTIVE denial. *Think not* — *me nomisēte* — *do not even suppose.* He knows the misreading is coming. He is teaching the heart-Torah at a depth no one before him has unfolded, and he knows that some will read his teaching as an attack on the written Torah. So he closes the door before the door can be opened.

*To destroy* — *kataluō* — to dismantle, to take down stone by stone, to abrogate. Yahusha is NOT here to dismantle the Torah. He is NOT here to dismantle the prophets. The Torah and the prophets stand. His ministry is not the demolition of the Tanakh; it is the FULFILLMENT of it.

*To fulfil* — *plēroō* — to fill up, to bring to fullness, to complete in substance. The Christian commentary tradition has frequently read *plēroō* here as *to bring to a close, to satisfy and thereby end* — as if Yahusha saying *I have come to fulfill* means *I am closing the Torah's relevance by exhausting it.* This is the antichrist reading. The Greek *plēroō* throughout the New Testament regularly means *to fill up* — to fill a net (Matthew 13:48), to fill a heart with sorrow (John 16:6), to fill a house with sound (Acts 2:2). When applied to the law and the prophets, *plēroō* means to bring the substance of the Torah and the prophets to its intended fullness — to fill the form with its substance. Yahusha fills the Torah with the heart-Torah; he does not empty it. The prophetic stream itself names the Messianic mission as Torah-magnification.

*Yahuah (the LORD) is well pleased for his righteousness' sake; he will magnify the law, and make it honourable.* (Isaiah 42:21)

The framework reading turns this verse on its plain meaning: the very Torah that the institutional-Christian leadership-pulpit has taught is *abolished, replaced, fulfilled-and-thereby-ended* is the Torah Yahusha said he came NOT to destroy. The horn's pulpit has spoken precisely the opposite of the King's opening declaration. The contrary-to-Torah test of John's first letter applies the moment the antinomian reading enters: the system that voids the commandments cannot know him.

**Verse 18 — *Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law.*** The eschatological terminus is the dissolution of the present heavens and earth — the consummation.

*But the day of Yahuah (the Lord) will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up. ... Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.* (2 Peter 3:10, 13)

*And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.* (Revelation 21:1)

Until then — *until heaven and earth pass* — every letter of the Torah stands.

*One jot or one tittle.* The *jot* is the *yod* — the smallest letter of the Hebrew alphabet. The *tittle* is the *kotz* — the small ornamental stroke on certain Hebrew letters, the smallest mark on the page. Yahusha is binding himself to the consonantal Hebrew text at the SMALLEST-POSSIBLE-MARK level. Not one letter passes. Not one stroke of one letter passes. The Torah stands in its entirety through this age until the consummation. The Lukan parallel locks the same reading.

*And it is easier for heaven and earth to pass, than one tittle of the law to fail.* (Luke 16:17)

*Till all be fulfilled* — *heōs an panta genētai* — *until all things come to pass.* Some Christian commentary has read this as *until I, on the cross, fulfill it.* That reading violates the verse on two counts. First, the parallel clause is *till heaven and earth pass* — the parallel terminus is the eschatological consummation, not the crucifixion. The two clauses bracket the same timeframe. Second, the immediate context (verse 19's *whosoever shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven*) presupposes the commandments are STILL operative in the kingdom of heaven Yahusha is establishing. If verse 18 meant *until the cross,* verse 19's binding force evaporates the moment verse 18's terminus is reached. The framework reads the two clauses as parallel: *until the consummation of all things,* the Torah stands.

**Verse 19 — the diagnostic for the institutional-Christian leadership-pulpit.** The framework reads this verse as the direct diagnostic for the modern Judaizer movement — institutional Christianity's leadership-pulpit. The diagnostic has TWO components: *break* + *teach men so.* The personal breaking is one thing; the breaking-coupled-with-teaching-others-to-break is the diagnostic Yahusha names. The horn's pulpit teaches the congregation that the commandments are abolished — that the Sabbath is moved, that the dietary laws are voided, that the feasts are replaced, that the heart-Torah of obedience is contrary to grace. This is *break and teach men so.* Yahusha names them: *called the least in the kingdom of heaven.*

Two framework observations on the *least* designation.

First — Yahusha does NOT say *not in the kingdom at all.* He says *least* (*elachistos*). Mercy is held open. The institutional-Christian congregation that has been *taught* the antinomian gospel by the pulpit is not categorically out; some hear the Shepherd's voice and turn. The system's teachers are the ones diagnosed first; the followers are victims of inherited interpretation. The system-not-people posture holds: the people in the pews were handed this in good faith; the deception is not their fault; they are also the lost sheep. The pulpit is *least;* the pew may be the gathered hearing through the noise.

Second — the *least* designation still indicts. The framework does not soften the verse into *all paths lead home.* The institutional-Christian leadership-pulpit that teaches the breaking of the commandments is identified by the King as *least in the kingdom of heaven.* This is not honorific. This is diagnostic. The dropping of the commandments from public teaching is named by Yahusha himself as the kingdom-status-diminishing act. The horn's seven centuries of voiding the Torah from the pulpit fall under this verse.

The contrast: *whosoever shall DO and TEACH them, the same shall be called GREAT in the kingdom of heaven.* The reverse direction. Walk the commandments, teach the commandments, and the kingdom-status is *great.* The framework reads this as the standard of the kingdom-citizens-teaching-roster: those who carry the heart-Torah in life and in proclamation are the kingdom's great ones. Mosheh, the prophets, Yochanan the Immerser, the apostolic witnesses who did and taught, the gathered remnant doing and teaching today. Paul himself denies any antinomian reading of his own ministry.

*Do we then make void the law through faith? Elohim (God) forbid: yea, we establish the law.* (Romans 3:31)

*Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.* (Romans 7:12)

Ya'aqov (James) names the same standing-of-the-Torah on the apostolic side.

*If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well: ... For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.* (James 2:8, 10)

Matthew 5:19 is the New Testament companion of John's first letter. Both verses say the same thing in different grammar: keeping the commandments — and teaching others to keep them — is the proof of knowing him.

## §5. *Except your righteousness exceed* — the Pharisaic-righteousness contrast

Verse 20 is the bridge-verse from the load-bearing anti-antinomian declaration of verses 17 through 19 to the six antitheses of verses 21 through 48. The framework reading hears the verse precisely.

Yahusha is NOT saying *the Pharisaic righteousness is righteousness, but you need MORE of it.* That would be the easy misread. The Pharisaic righteousness is named in this verse as a comparative baseline that must be EXCEEDED to enter the kingdom. The framework reading is sharper: the Pharisaic righteousness is a counterfeit-form of righteousness — outside-only performance, oral-tradition expansion, void-of-heart compliance with display-value. To exceed it is not to do more of it; to exceed it is to walk the heart-Torah it counterfeits.

The Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership-system has already been named at Matthew 3:7 as the *generation of vipers* — the seed-war identification of the antichrist-front in its first-century beat. The system Yahusha is now naming at 5:20 is the same system. He is not contrasting himself with a slightly-better-than-average righteousness; he is contrasting the kingdom-righteousness with the Judaizer-predecessor's counterfeit. The pattern Paul will identify in his letters as the *flesh-credential* gospel of the *circumcision party* — the legalistic-performance Judaizers — is the descendant pattern of the Pharisaic righteousness Yahusha names here. Both surface the same theology: do the outside, claim the credential, void the heart-Torah.

*Knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ), even we have believed in Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ), that we might be justified by the faith of HaMashiach (Christ), and not by the works of the law: for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified.* (Galatians 2:16)

Paul's *works of the law* (*erga nomou*) names the same flesh-credential performance Yahusha is naming as the Pharisaic righteousness. Both indict the system that uses outside-performance as the entry-credential while voiding the heart-Torah.

The heart-Torah Yahusha will now teach in verses 21 through 48 is the *exceeding* righteousness. It is the new-heart Torah of Yirmeyahu 31:33, the Spirit-empowered Torah of Yechezk'el 36:26-27. It walks the commandments not as performance-for-credential but as the natural expression of a new heart. The Beatitudes' *pure in heart* is the substance of the *exceeding.* The diagnostic of the modern Judaizer movement — institutional Christianity carrying the same flesh-credential gospel (be baptized, say the prayer, join the church, and you are saved) in a different costume — runs through this verse with full force. Both versions are the Pharisaic righteousness, and both fail to enter the kingdom on Yahusha's explicit standard at verse 20.

## §6. The six antitheses — oral-tradition correction, not Torah supersession

The six *ye have heard ... but I say unto you* exchanges of verses 21 through 48 are the bulk of Matthew 5 and the most-frequently-misread block in the Sermon on the Mount. The Christian commentary tradition has often read them as Yahusha ABROGATING the Torah by setting his own authority above Mosheh's. That reading collapses Yahusha into the *destroy the law* posture he just rejected at verse 17. The framework reads the antitheses on a different axis.

**The methodological key.** Yahusha (Jesus) is not contrasting himself with the written Torah of Mosheh (Moses); he is contrasting himself with the ORAL TRADITION of the Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership-system — the *takkanot* and *gezerot,* the truncations and additions laid over the Torah by the rabbinic schools across the centuries. Yahusha's *but I say unto you* is the corrective restoration of the original Torah's substance set against the rabbinic-tradition distortion of it. The framework reading recovers the pattern at each of the six instances — six cases of the same add-or-diminish violation the Torah itself names at Deuteronomy 4:2.

**Antithesis 1 — murder and anger (verses 21-26).**

*Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not kill; and whosoever shall kill shall be in danger of the judgment: But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment: and whosoever shall say to his brother, Raca, shall be in danger of the council: but whosoever shall say, Thou fool, shall be in danger of hell fire. Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.* (Matthew 5:21-24)

The oral tradition stopped the murder-commandment at the bodily act. Yahusha restores the heart-substance. The commandment against murder always carried the heart-substance — the Torah explicitly forbids the seed-of-murder at the heart level.

*Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him. Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:17-18)

The Torah has always taught what Yahusha is now teaching. He is not innovating; he is restoring what the oral-tradition's narrow surface-focus had left behind.

The escalating-curse pattern — anger, *Raca,* *thou fool* — names three intensities of the heart-violation, all subject to judgment. The altar-rule — *leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift* — names the practical priority: heart-reconciliation precedes worship. The framework reads this against the prophetic stream that has always taught that sacrifice without heart is rejected.

*For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of Elohim (God) more than burnt offerings.* (Hosea 6:6)

*Wherewith shall I come before Yahuah (the LORD), and bow myself before the high Elohim (God)? shall I come before him with burnt offerings, with calves of a year old? ... He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth Yahuah (the LORD) require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy Elohim (God)?* (Micah 6:6, 8)

Yahusha speaks the same prophetic stream.

**Antithesis 2 — adultery and lust (verses 27-30).**

*Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not commit adultery: But I say unto you, That whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.* (Matthew 5:27-28)

The oral tradition stopped at the act. Yahusha restores the heart-substance. The Torah already named the heart-violation in the tenth commandment.

*Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's.* (Exodus 20:17)

The tenth commandment names the heart-coveting that precedes the act. Yahusha is unfolding the tenth-commandment-substance into the seventh-commandment-frame. The Torah taught both; the oral tradition had separated them.

The verse 29-30 radical-renunciation rhetoric — *if thy right eye offend thee, pluck it out* — is hyperbolic. The framework rules out the literal-misuse reading; no one has ever taken Matthew 5:29-30 as a literal command to mutilate the body, and Yahusha does not contradict the Torah's prohibition on bodily mutilation at Leviticus 19:28. The rhetorical force is the gravity-naming: the heart-stake of the heart-violation is so high that radical-renunciation imagery is the proper register. Cut off the practice; cut off the access-pattern; the heart-Torah requires it.

**Antithesis 3 — divorce (verses 31-32).**

*It hath been said, Whosoever shall put away his wife, let him give her a writing of divorcement: But I say unto you, That whosoever shall put away his wife, saving for the cause of fornication, causeth her to commit adultery: and whosoever shall marry her that is divorced committeth adultery.* (Matthew 5:31-32)

The Pharisaic schools had developed a body of teaching on Deuteronomy 24:1's *because he hath found some uncleanness in her* — the Hillel school read *some uncleanness* expansively to permit divorce for *any cause* (the wife burns the dinner, for instance); the Shammai school read *some uncleanness* narrowly to permit divorce only for sexual immorality. Yahusha sides with the Shammai-restrictive reading and grounds it in the original Genesis-2 marriage-design, which he will state explicitly at Matthew 19.

*And he answered and said unto them, Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh? ... He saith unto them, Mosheh (Moses) because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so.* (Matthew 19:4-5, 8)

The framework reads this antithesis as oral-tradition correction by restoration of the original Torah. Deuteronomy 24:1's *writing of divorcement* was a REGULATORY-CONCESSION to the hardness of human hearts, not a TORAH-MANDATE for permissive divorce. The oral tradition had inverted the concession into a license. Yahusha restores the design — divorce is permitted only for *porneia* (sexual unfaithfulness), and otherwise the original *one flesh* of Genesis 2 stands.

*Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.* (Genesis 2:24)

The framework reading also rules out the inverse misuse — the prohibition is not a categorical no-divorce-ever stance; that misreading collapses the *porneia*-exception. The framework reads the verse as restoration of the Torah's covenantal-marriage design with the exception-clause Yahusha himself articulates.

**Antithesis 4 — oaths (verses 33-37).**

*Again, ye have heard that it hath been said by them of old time, Thou shalt not forswear thyself, but shalt perform unto Yahuah (the Lord) thine oaths: But I say unto you, Swear not at all; neither by heaven; for it is Elohim's (God's) throne: Nor by the earth; for it is his footstool: neither by Yerushalayim (Jerusalem); for it is the city of the great King. Neither shalt thou swear by thy head, because thou canst not make one hair white or black. But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.* (Matthew 5:33-37)

The oral tradition had developed elaborate oath-formulas with gradations of binding — swearing by heaven, by the earth, by Yerushalayim, by the temple, by the gold of the temple, by one's own head. Yahusha will indict the same gradation-system explicitly later in the gospel.

*Woe unto you, ye blind guides, which say, Whosoever shall swear by the temple, it is nothing; but whosoever shall swear by the gold of the temple, he is a debtor! Ye fools and blind: for whether is greater, the gold, or the temple that sanctifieth the gold?* (Matthew 23:16-17)

The whole apparatus had become a tool for evading the binding-force of oaths through technical loopholes — say the oath in the lower-binding formula and you can break it without penalty.

Yahusha cuts through the apparatus. *Swear not at all* — eliminate the gradation-system entirely. *Let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay.* The heart-Torah substance is the integrity of speech: a yes that means yes, a no that means no. The Torah commanded against false oaths.

*And ye shall not swear by my name falsely, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy Elohim (God): I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:12)

*If a man vow a vow unto Yahuah (the LORD), or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond; he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth.* (Numbers 30:2)

Yahusha does not abolish oath-taking categorically — Paul will swear oaths in his epistles, calling God to witness, which is the legitimate-oath form. He cuts the gradation-evasion-system and restores the heart-substance: speak true, and your speech needs no fortification.

**Antithesis 5 — eye for an eye (verses 38-42).**

*Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth: But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also. And if any man will sue thee at the law, and take away thy coat, let him have thy cloke also. And whosoever shall compel thee to go a mile, go with him twain. Give to him that asketh thee, and from him that would borrow of thee turn not thou away.* (Matthew 5:38-42)

The framework reading is critical here because the Christian-pacifist tradition has often misused this antithesis to claim Yahusha abolished the judicial-standard of the Torah. The framework rejects this misuse.

The *eye for an eye* was a JUDICIAL standard — the *lex talionis* — that LIMITED retaliation to proportional justice administered by the courts.

*And if any mischief follow, then thou shalt give life for life, Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.* (Exodus 21:23-24)

*And if a man cause a blemish in his neighbour; as he hath done, so shall it be done to him; Breach for breach, eye for eye, tooth for tooth: as he hath caused a blemish in a man, so shall it be done to him again.* (Leviticus 24:19-20)

*And thine eye shall not pity; but life shall go for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.* (Deuteronomy 19:21)

The *lex talionis* was a restraining-of-vengeance principle, not a license for personal-vengeance. The judicial system of Yashar'el was to administer proportional consequences for harm; the principle prevented escalating-cycle vengeance and prevented under-punishment as well.

The oral tradition had often slipped *lex talionis* out of the judicial sphere and into the personal-revenge sphere — applying it as a green-light for personal retaliation. Yahusha restores the principle to its sphere by cutting off the personal-revenge misuse: *resist not evil* on the personal level; *turn the other cheek* on the personal level; *let him have thy cloke also* on the personal level. The personal-disciple ethic is the higher path of non-retaliation, not because the judicial principle is wrong, but because the disciple does not personally administer *lex talionis* — the courts do. Paul confirms the magistrate's continuing-sword.

*For rulers are not a terror to good works, but to the evil. Wilt thou then not be afraid of the power? do that which is good, and thou shalt have praise of the same: For he is the minister of Elohim (God) to thee for good. But if thou do that which is evil, be afraid; for he beareth not the sword in vain: for he is the minister of Elohim (God), a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil.* (Romans 13:3-4)

The framework reads this antithesis as preserving the judicial-Torah while restoring the personal-disciple ethic. The *lex talionis* remains operative as the courts' standard. The disciple does not retaliate personally. Both stand together. The Christian-pacifist absolutization of the antithesis collapses one for the other.

The verse 41-42 extension — *whosoever shall compel thee to go a mile, go with him twain; give to him that asketh thee* — names the practical-living register of the heart-Torah generosity. The disciple's posture in the present age — under occupation by Rome, under oppression by the principalities — is to go beyond the demanded compliance and live generously toward even the enemy. This is the *chesed* of the merciful Beatitude lived out.

**Antithesis 6 — love your neighbor and hate your enemy (verses 43-48).**

*Ye have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy: But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you; That ye may be the children of your Father which is in heaven: for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.* (Matthew 5:43-45)

The framework reads this antithesis with special care because it surfaces the most-explicit oral-tradition-ADDITION case in the six. *Love thy neighbour* is from the Torah.

*Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:18)

*And hate thine enemy* is NOT IN THE TORAH. There is no such commandment in the Tanakh. The oral tradition — and the Qumran community's *War Scroll* explicitly taught a *hate-the-sons-of-darkness* doctrine; some rabbinic strands had similar exclusionary additions — had ADDED *hate thine enemy* to the Torah's *love thy neighbor.* Yahusha is not contradicting Mosheh; he is correcting an oral-tradition INTERPOLATION that had been spliced onto the Torah.

The Torah's actual command was love-of-neighbor AND love-of-stranger.

The Torah also commanded positive-action toward the enemy.

*If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink: For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and Yahuah (the LORD) shall reward thee.* (Proverbs 25:21-22)

The Torah's command was love-of-neighbor AND love-of-stranger AND positive-action toward the enemy. The oral tradition had inserted a hate-of-enemy clause; Yahusha (Jesus) strips the addition off and restores the Torah's actual command, extending the love-command to the enemy as the heart-completion of what the Torah had always carried. The *hate thine enemy* clause is the clearest *add*-violation; the truncations of antitheses 1, 2, and 4 are the *diminish*-violations; the six antitheses are six cases of the same Deuteronomy 4:2 diagnostic.

The framework reading also rules out the Christian-pacifist misuse of this antithesis, paralleling the same misuse on antithesis 5. The personal-disciple ethic is love-of-enemy; the judicial-Torah is preserved; the eschatological-judgment of Yahuah against the abominations of the counterfeit-system is preserved. He brings desolation on the false-system for her abominations at the day; he stands on the Mount of Olives to war with all the nations who gather against Yerushalayim. The disciple loves the personal enemy and does not personally administer judgment; Yahuah administers eschatological judgment on the systems and the principalities in his timing.

The six antitheses close with the heart-Torah climax. *Perfect* — *teleios* — is the Greek translation of the Hebrew *tamim,* which in the Tanakh means *whole, complete, blameless.*

The Beatitudes' *pure in heart* finds its completion at verse 48. The heart-Torah Yahusha has just unfolded across six antitheses calls the disciple to whole-hearted *tamim*-walk before the Father — the same standard the Torah has always named. Kefa (Peter) names the same Tanakh-pattern on the apostolic side.

*But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.* (1 Peter 1:15-16)

The antitheses, taken together, do not abolish the Torah. They restore it. They strip the oral-tradition distortions off — the truncations of antitheses 1, 2, and 4 (act-only readings), the inversion-of-concession of antithesis 3 (divorce permission), the sphere-mistake of antithesis 5 (personal vs judicial), and the outright addition of antithesis 6 — and they reveal the heart-Torah underneath. The Torah is intensified, not abolished. The contrary-to-Torah test of John's first letter stands: the kingdom-righteousness keeps the commandments at heart-depth.

## §7. The Sermon's interior posture — kingdom-citizens already, kingdom-walking now

The framework reading lifts the Sermon's interior architecture for the reader. The Beatitudes name who the citizens of the kingdom already ARE; the salt-and-light verses name what they ARE in the world's frame; the verses 17 through 19 declaration locks the Torah as the standard the citizens walk; verse 20 names the counterfeit-righteousness the citizens exceed; the antitheses unfold what walking the heart-Torah looks like in six specific cases; verse 48 names the *tamim*-completion the citizens grow toward.

This is the kingdom-citizens-living-as-kingdom-citizens architecture. The Sermon is not a how-to manual for salvation-earning. It is a description of the gathered remnant's life and the standards by which it walks. The Sermon dismantles the counterfeit-righteousness, names the heart-Torah, invites the gathered remnant to walk what they are. It does not prescribe practice as the program of salvation; it describes the practice the gathered already carry because the Father has drawn them.

The audience-identification posture of the framework's evangelism applies. The Sermon is heard by the disciples and the multitudes. The disciples — the gathered, the called, the lost-sheep-returning — hear themselves named and described. The multitudes — those still on the edge, still being drawn — hear the description and either find themselves recognized in it or do not.

*But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.* (John 10:26-27)

The Sermon is the Shepherd's voice articulated at length. The hearers who hear are the gathered; the hearers who do not hear are revealed by the same hearing-test. This is not a manipulation; this is the prophetic discrimination the kingdom-proclamation accomplishes by its own substance.

The contrary-to-Torah test stands as the master filter. Any reading of Matthew 5 that voids the commandments is wrong. The framework reads the chapter as the Torah-intensifying, Pharisaic-counterfeit-dismantling, heart-Torah-naming sermon of the second-Sinai King. The reading honors what the text says.

## §8. The eschatological filter — the kingdom of heaven inaugurated, not yet consummated

The Sermon's references to *the kingdom of heaven* at verses 3, 10, 19, and 20 sit on the same eschatological filter Matthew 3 carried forward. The kingdom-at-hand is INAUGURATED with Yahusha's ministry — it is at hand, available, being entered — but it is not YET CONSUMMATED. The consummation awaits the day of Yahuah, the cosmic signs, the Olivet-judgment, the gathering of the wheat, the burning of the tares, the millennial reign of the Messiah on the earth.

The framework reading of the Beatitudes' future-tense promises — *they shall be comforted; they shall inherit the earth; they shall be filled; they shall obtain mercy; they shall see Elohim (God); they shall be called the children of Elohim (God)* — lands in this eschatological frame. The future tenses are the consummation-tenses. The citizens of the kingdom ARE now (present indicative); they shall RECEIVE the consummation-blessings then (future passive). The earth-inheritance of the meek at verse 5 is the prophetic-land-inheritance at the consummation. The seeing of Elohim by the pure in heart at verse 8 is the eschatological-vision promise the prophets have named.

This eschatological filter rules out the Christian-amillennial collapse of the future-promises into present-spiritual-fulfillments. The meek do not currently *inherit the earth* in a spiritualized internal sense; they shall inherit the earth at the day of Yahuah — the same earth, restored, after the dreadful day has cleansed the abominations. The kingdom of heaven is not entirely realized in the present; the kingdom is at hand, the King is sitting on the mountain, and the consummation is ahead.
