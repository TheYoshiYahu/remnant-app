# Short-form commentary on Matthew 6

The chapter overview walks the main beats — the display-vs-secret diagnostic across alms, prayer, and fasting (vv.1-4, 5-8, 16-18); the Lord's Prayer (vv.9-13) as kingdom-citizens prayer-pattern with *hallowed be thy name* as Ezekiel 36 gathering-prayer and *thy kingdom come* as the Daniel 7 transfer to the Son of Adam; the forgiveness-economy of vv.14-15; the *single eye / evil eye* of vv.22-23 as the Tanakh-idiom for generosity / stinginess; the Mammon-or-Elohim binary as Eliyahu's (Elijah's) Carmel-confrontation in the disciple's economic life; the seek-first-the-kingdom manna-trust of vv.25-34. Two framework moves the overview does not surface tighten the Sermon's middle into the framework's central diagnostic — the *hypocrites* of vv.1-18 named as the flesh-credential gospel architecture in its Pharisaic costume (Isaiah 29:13 + Mark 7:6-7 the scripture-level anchors), and the conditional-forgiveness of vv.14-15 read against Matt 18:32-35 and Matt 7:21-23 as the canon-internal collapse of *once saved, always saved.*

**The *hypocrites* of vv.1-18 carry the flesh-credential gospel architecture in its Pharisaic costume.** The Greek *hupokritai* — stage-actors playing roles for an audience — names the Pharisaic-leadership-system Yahusha (Jesus) confronts at Matt 23 by name and at Mark 7 by Tanakh-citation. The framework reads the display-vs-secret diagnostic of vv.1-18 not as a generic motive-correction but as the SAME architecture the Sermon has been confronting throughout: religious-act-for-honor-from-men, surface-form-without-heart-substance, flesh-credential without the heart-Torah. Two scripture-level anchors lock the reading.

*Wherefore Adonai (the Lord) said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men.* (Isaiah 29:13)

*He answered and said unto them, Well hath Yeshayahu (Esaias) prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. For laying aside the commandment of Elohim (God), ye hold the tradition of men.* (Mark 7:6-8)

Yahusha (Jesus) himself quotes Isaiah 29:13 at the Pharisees in Mark 7, and the diagnostic he names there is identical to the architecture vv.1-18 walks at the practice-level: surface-act honoring with the mouth, heart far from the Father, doctrines-of-men replacing the heart-Torah. The Pharisaic-counterfeit-righteousness Yahusha (Jesus) named at Matt 5:20 is one costume of the architecture; the Galatian agitators carry it in another costume (*be circumcised, keep the boundary-marker, you are in the covenant* — flesh-credential without the heart-Torah); institutional Christianity carries it in another (*say the prayer, be baptized, join the church, you are saved* — flesh-credential without the heart-Torah); modern Hebrew Roots' false-inclusion gospel carries it in another (*Torah-keep and confess, you are Yashar'el (Israel)* — flesh-credential without the paternal-blood covenant the prophets named); Islam carries it in another. The contrary-to-Torah test of 1 John 2:3-4 names the lie in every costume. The *they have their reward* verdict at Matt 6:2, 5, 16 lands on the same architecture wherever it appears.

**The conditional-forgiveness of vv.14-15 collapses *once saved, always saved* at canon-internal architecture — Matt 18:32-35 and Matt 7:21-23 are the dramatized forms.** The Greek of vv.14-15 is conditional: *if ye forgive ... your heavenly Father will also forgive. If ye forgive NOT ... neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.* The receiving of forgiveness from the Father is coupled to the giving of forgiveness to the brother. The institutional-Christian *once saved, always saved* doctrine cannot stand on this verse — and the same Matthew locks the point twice more in narrative.

*Then his lord, after that he had called him, said unto him, O thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all that debt, because thou desiredst me: Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee? And his lord was wroth, and delivered him to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due unto him. So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.* (Matthew 18:32-35)

The king in the parable forgives the great debt, then REVERSES the forgiveness the moment the forgiven servant refuses to extend it. The lord's word is plain: *so likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you.* The parable is the dramatized form of vv.14-15 — the proposition of Matt 6 walked as a story in Matt 18, with the king's reversal confirming what vv.14-15 stated in proposition.

*Not every one that saith unto me, Adonai (Lord), Adonai (Lord), shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Adonai (Lord), Adonai (Lord), have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.* (Matthew 7:21-23)

The *I never knew you* at the Sermon's own close is spoken to wheat that did not bear fruit — the prophets-in-his-name and the demon-casters-in-his-name and the workers-of-wonders-in-his-name are not random pagans; they invoked the right name and walked a counterfeit walk. The same conditional-grammar at vv.14-15 governs the *I never knew you* at Matt 7. Both stand inside the canon-wide pattern: the names of the wheat can be blotted out of the book of the living for lawlessness (Exodus 32:33; Psalm 69:28); the warning is to the wheat, not to the tares; the tares were never written. The grace is real, freely offered, freely received, and the gathering is the Father's own work — and the same grace is forfeited by the citizens who refuse to walk its economy through the door of forgiving the brother. The Reformation's *saved by grace through faith* sentence is true; the Reformation cut the second half of the sentence off. The conditional-grammar of vv.14-15 puts the second half back. 1 John 2:3-4 stands at full force: *He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.*
