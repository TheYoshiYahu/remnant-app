# Short-form commentary on Matthew 6

Matthew 6 is the middle of the Sermon on the Mount, and it walks one diagnostic across three practical cases — alms, prayer, and fasting — then turns to the heart-orientation of the kingdom-citizens against the economic-orientation of the age. The chapter is the practical specification of the Pharisaic-counterfeit-righteousness Yahusha (Jesus) named at Matthew 5:20.

*For I say unto you, That except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no case enter into the kingdom of heaven.* (Matthew 5:20)

Where the antitheses of Matthew 5:21-48 corrected the oral tradition at six commandment cruxes, Matthew 6 walks three practices the covenant had always commanded — alms, prayer, fasting — and contrasts the Pharisaic display-form with the kingdom-citizens secret-form. The structure is identical in each unit: the *to be seen of men* refrain identifies the counterfeit, the *thy Father which seeth in secret* refrain names the kingdom-citizens form, and the Father's reward-economy is the same in each.

*Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them: otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven.* (Matthew 6:1)

The opening verse frames the next seventeen. Yahusha (Jesus) does not abolish alms-giving; the Torah commands it.

*If there be among you a poor man of one of thy brethren within any of thy gates in thy land which Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God) giveth thee, thou shalt not harden thine heart, nor shut thine hand from thy poor brother: But thou shalt open thine hand wide unto him, and shalt surely lend him sufficient for his need, in that which he wanteth.* (Deuteronomy 15:7-8)

What is being corrected is the motive. The same act flows from two different hearts; the Pharisaic-counterfeit form practices alms *to be seen of them* — performance for honor-from-men — and *they have their reward* in the seeing. The kingdom-citizens form practices alms so quietly that *the left hand* does not know what the right does, and the Father who *seeth in secret* rewards openly. This is not a contradiction of Matthew 5:16, where the disciples' visible good works glorify the Father; that verse names the visible-life-of-covenant-faithfulness flowing from the heart, while Matthew 6:1 names the engineered-display engineered for self-glory. The same act, two different sources. The motive separates them.

The prayer-unit (vv.5-8) repeats the diagnostic.

*And when thou prayest, thou shalt not be as the hypocrites are: for they love to pray standing in the synagogues and in the corners of the streets, that they may be seen of men. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward. But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.* (Matthew 6:5-6)

The closet — *tameion,* the inner room, the storeroom, the private space — is the posture of unperformed prayer. The verse is sometimes weaponized against gathered-community prayer, which is a misuse. The Tanakh teaches public prayer extensively; Shlomo (Solomon) dedicated the temple with a public prayer; the apostolic assemblies prayed together; Yahusha (Jesus) prayed publicly at the raising of El'azar (Lazarus) and named the public posture explicitly:

*Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me. And I knew that thou hearest me always: but because of the people which stand by I said it, that they may believe that thou hast sent me.* (John 11:41-42)

The Matthew 6:5-6 prohibition is against the prayer *engineered* for display, not against the gathered-community prayer the Tanakh and the apostolic assemblies practiced. Then v.8 lands the relational-frame the Lord's Prayer will articulate:

*Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.* (Matthew 6:8)

Prayer is not the conveying of information the Father lacks. It is the kingdom-citizens orientation of the heart toward the Father who already knows, the alignment of the citizens' wills with the Father's kingdom, the practice of trust in the Father's foreknowledge and provision.

*After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.* (Matthew 6:9-13)

The Lord's Prayer is given as a pattern — *after this manner* — not as a magic-formula. Each phrase is Tanakh-rooted, and the second phrase carries the framework's whole gathering hope in five words. *Hallowed be thy name* is heard against the Tanakh passage it draws on:

*And I will sanctify my great name, which was profaned among the heathen, which ye have profaned in the midst of them; and the heathen shall know that I am Yahuah (the LORD), saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD), when I shall be sanctified in you before their eyes. For I will take you from among the heathen, and gather you out of all countries, and will bring you into your own land.* (Ezekiel 36:23-24)

The sanctifying of the Name happens THROUGH the gathering of the scattered house. The Name has been profaned in the lands of the scattering; the gathering is the means of its sanctification. The kingdom-citizens prayer for the sanctification of the Name is the prayer for the gathering. *Hallowed be thy name* is a gathering-prayer.

*Thy kingdom come, thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven* extends the same arc — the prayer for the consummation Daniel saw transferred to *like the Son of Adam* with the clouds of heaven:

*I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of Adam came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.* (Daniel 7:13-14)

The kaph at *like the Son of Adam* honors his incarnation — he resembled mortal-man because he took on flesh, while remaining the Formed cloud-rider. The everlasting kingdom v.14 names belongs to Yahusha (Jesus) alone; the counterfeit's dominion is never everlasting. The kingdom-citizens at Matthew 6:10 pray for the consummation of this kingdom — the same kingdom Yahusha (Jesus) refused to short-circuit when the devil offered the kingdoms of the world from a mountain in the wilderness narrative just behind this Sermon.

The daily-bread petition (v.11) carries the manna-pattern of the wilderness — *that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no* (Exodus 16:4) — the Father's covenant-provision economy in which the citizens trust him one day at a time. The forgiveness-petition (v.12) couples the citizens' receiving of forgiveness to their extending of it, which v.14 immediately locks:

*For if ye forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you: But if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.* (Matthew 6:14-15)

This is the chapter's clearest collapse of the institutional-Christian *once saved, always saved* doctrine. The receiving of forgiveness from the Father is conditioned on the giving of forgiveness to the brother. This is not earning; it is the relational-economy of the kingdom. The kingdom-citizens carry the forgiveness-substance in both directions — receiving from the Father, extending to the brother — or the substance is broken at the citizens' end and the receiving-side closes. Yahusha (Jesus) will return to this exact pattern at the parable of the unforgiving servant, where the king reverses the forgiveness the moment the forgiven servant refuses to extend it. The Tanakh has been teaching the same architecture for centuries — that grace can be forfeited:

*Woe unto them! for they have fled from me: destruction unto them! because they have transgressed against me: though I have redeemed them, yet they have spoken lies against me.* (Hosea 7:13)

*But when the righteous turneth away from his righteousness, and committeth iniquity, and doeth according to all the abominations that the wicked man doeth, shall he live? All his righteousness that he hath done shall not be mentioned: in his trespass that he hath trespassed, and in his sin that he hath sinned, in them shall he die.* (Ezekiel 18:24)

*Yahuah (The LORD) said unto Mosheh (Moses), Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book.* (Exodus 32:33)

The names of the wheat can be blotted out for lawlessness. The warning is to the redeemed, not to the tares; the tares were never written in the book. The conditional-forgiveness of Matthew 6:14-15 sits inside this whole canonical pattern. Grace is real, freely offered, freely received — and grace can be forfeited by the citizens who refuse to walk its economy.

The fasting-unit (vv.16-18) closes the three-part diagnostic and reaches back into the substance of Isaiah 58:

*Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke? Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to thy house? when thou seest the naked, that thou cover him; and that thou hide not thyself from thine own flesh?* (Isaiah 58:6-7)

Isaiah's fast-substance is the heart-Torah expressed in *chesed*-action toward the oppressed. The Matthew 6:16-18 form-correction sits inside that substance-correction; the kingdom-citizens form of the fast is not the Pharisaic-display-pallor but the Isaiah 58 heart-fast.

The chapter then turns from the three Tanakh-substantial practices to the broader economic-orientation of the kingdom-citizens. The treasures-in-heaven teaching is straightforward:

*Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.* (Matthew 6:19-21)

The diagnostic-verse is v.21: the disciple discovers what the heart actually serves by tracing where the treasure has been laid up. The Tanakh-wisdom-stream has named the earth-treasure economy as futile in the day of wrath since the Proverbs were collected:

*Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.* (Proverbs 11:4)

*Labour not to be rich: cease from thine own wisdom. Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not? for riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven.* (Proverbs 23:4-5)

Then the chapter turns to a single verse-pair that Christian commentary has chronically spiritualized. The framework recovers it.

*The light of the body is the eye: if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light. But if thine eye be evil, thy whole body shall be full of darkness. If therefore the light that is in thee be darkness, how great is that darkness!* (Matthew 6:22-23)

The Christian commentary tradition reads *single eye* as singleness-of-spiritual-focus — a generic spiritual-vision metaphor about looking at the right thing. The framework reads it as the Hebrew idiom the Tanakh has been using all along. *Good eye* (*tov-ayin*) is the Hebrew term for GENEROSITY; *evil eye* (*ra-ayin*) is the Hebrew term for STINGINESS:

*He that hath a bountiful eye shall be blessed; for he giveth of his bread to the poor.* (Proverbs 22:9)

*Eat thou not the bread of him that hath an evil eye, neither desire thou his dainty meats: For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he: Eat and drink, saith he to thee; but his heart is not with thee.* (Proverbs 23:6-7)

*Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart, saying, The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand; and thine eye be evil against thy poor brother, and thou givest him nought; and he cry unto Yahuah (the LORD) against thee, and it be sin unto thee.* (Deuteronomy 15:9)

*He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye, and considereth not that poverty shall come upon him.* (Proverbs 28:22)

The Tanakh-idiom is consistent across four witnesses, including the Torah itself at Deuteronomy 15:9. *Good of eye* is the open-handed, generous heart that gives to the poor. *Evil of eye* is the grasping, stingy heart that withholds. Matthew 6:22-23 sits BETWEEN the treasures-in-heaven teaching (vv.19-21) and the Mammon-or-Elohim binary (v.24). The literary context locks the reading; the *single eye* whose body is *full of light* is the generous person who lays up treasure in heaven through generous-giving, and the *evil eye* whose body is *full of darkness* is the stingy person who lays up treasure on earth through grasping. The Christian-spiritualization loses the framework substance entirely. The framework recovers what the Hebrew has always said.

Then comes the binary that closes the economic block.

*No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve Elohim (God) and mammon.* (Matthew 6:24)

Matthew leaves *mammon* untranslated. The Aramaic *mamona* names not just wealth but the PERSONIFIED POWER of wealth — a master, a competing lord. The kingdom-citizens face a forced-choice; Mammon is one of the principalities-of-this-age the apostolic writings identify as the structural-powers contesting the kingdoms in the present age. Paul names the architecture:

*For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.* (Ephesians 6:12)

The first commandment had already ruled out the dual-allegiance:

*Thou shalt have no other gods before me.* (Exodus 20:3)

And the Tanakh's prophetic confrontation had named the same forced-choice the disciples now face:

*And Eliyahu (Elijah) came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if Yahuah (the LORD) be Elohim (God), follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.* (1 Kings 18:21)

Matthew 6:24 is the Carmel-confrontation in the disciple's own economic life. The kingdom-citizens cannot serve both Yahuah (God) and the principality the Aramaic names *Mammon.*

The chapter closes on the trust-economy that flows from the right choice.

*Therefore I say unto you, Take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink; nor yet for your body, what ye shall put on. Is not the life more than meat, and the body than raiment? Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns; yet your heavenly Father feedeth them. Are ye not much better than they?* (Matthew 6:25-26)

*Take no thought* — *do not be torn-apart-with-care.* The verb does not abolish prudent planning; it abolishes anxious-grasping. The fowls and the lilies illustrate the Father's covenant-provision-economy; the disciples called from their fishing nets are now being taught to trust the Father for the daily-provision they used to secure by their own labor. The closing verse locks the kingdom-citizens' priority:

*But seek ye first the kingdom of Elohim (God), and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you. Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof.* (Matthew 6:33-34)

The *first* is not chronological-first-and-then-the-other; it is priority-first-as-organizing-principle. The kingdom-citizens orient first toward the kingdom — the gathering, the consummation, the Father's will done in earth — and toward *his righteousness* — the heart-Torah Yahusha (Jesus) has been unfolding throughout the Sermon. The daily-provision is *added unto you* in the framework of that priority. The hungering-and-thirsting Beatitude of Matthew 5:6 finds its present-age practice here: seek the Father's righteousness, walk the heart-Torah, trust the Father one day at a time, and let the morrow take its own thought. The manna-pattern of the wilderness, walked out by the kingdom-citizens called from their nets.

Matthew 6 is the middle of the Sermon and the practical specification of the kingdom-citizens-righteousness against the Pharisaic-counterfeit form. Alms, prayer, and fasting in secret. The Lord's Prayer as the kingdom-citizens prayer-pattern in seven Tanakh-rooted phrases. The forgiveness-economy that conditions the receiving on the giving. Treasures laid up in heaven by the open hand of the *good eye.* The forced-choice between Yahuah (God) and Mammon-the-principality. The seek-first-the-kingdom priority and the daily-trust in the Father's covenant-provision. The chapter does not abolish a single Torah practice; it names what the practice looks like when the heart that walks it has been brought home.
