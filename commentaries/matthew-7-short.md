# Short-form commentary on Matthew 7

The chapter overview walks the main beats — the just-judgment / mote-and-beam / pearls-before-swine discrimination corrected at the standard-of-judgment level (vv.1-6); the ask-seek-knock prayer-confidence with Luke 11:13 naming the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) as *the good gift* (vv.7-11); the Golden Rule named by Yahusha (Jesus) himself as *the law and the prophets* (v.12); the narrow gate and the few who find it (vv.13-14); the false-prophet diagnostic with Deuteronomy 13:1-3's signs-real-prophet-still-false test (vv.15-20); the load-bearing *Lord, Lord* dismissal at vv.21-23 with *anomia* (torahlessness) exposing the institutional-Christian antinomian-gospel community (the canon-internal collapse of which is walked in S135's matthew-6-short.md against vv.14-15); the two builders with the *na'aseh v'nishma* hearing-AND-doing standard (vv.24-27). Two framework moves the overview does not surface lock the Sermon's close as restoration-of-the-Hebrew-library teaching rather than gospel-innovation — the Golden Rule at v.12 anchored in Tobit 4:15 and Hillel's centuries-old negative form, and the narrow-gate / wide-gate at vv.13-14 anchored in the Hebrew library's own Two-Ways architecture from Mosheh (Moses) at Sinai through 1 Enoch 94.

**The Golden Rule at v.12 is restoration, not innovation — Tobit 4:15 carries the same teaching in the Hebrew library centuries before, and Hillel taught it in negative form a generation before Yahusha (Jesus).** The Christian commentary tradition has often flattened v.12 into a Yahusha-invented ethical simplification of an alleged Tanakh complexity — *the Torah was complicated but Yahusha (Jesus) reduced it to one simple rule.* The framework rules out the read. The teaching was already in the Hebrew library, articulated explicitly in the wisdom-stream.

*Do that to no man which thou hatest: drink not wine to make thee drunken: neither let drunkenness go with thee in thy journey.* (Tobit 4:15)

The angel Raphael's instruction to Tobias names the Golden Rule in negative form at the close of Tobit's narrative, centuries before the Sermon on the Mount. Hillel — the most-prominent rabbi of the generation before Yahusha (Jesus) — taught the same rule in the same negative form (Talmud Shabbat 31a): *what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow; this is the whole Torah, the rest is commentary — go and learn.* The Hebrew library carried the teaching; the Pharisaic-tradition at its best carried the teaching; the rule was articulated and taught for centuries before the King restored it. Yahusha (Jesus) at v.12 walks the teaching in positive direction (*do unto others* rather than *do not do*) and binds it to the Sermon's whole architecture by the closing words *for this is the law and the prophets.* The King has just spent Matthew 5:17-19 binding himself and the kingdom-citizens to every jot and tittle, and Matthew 5:21-48 restoring the heart-Torah at six commandment cruxes; v.12 names the heart-summary of what those commandments express, not a substitute for them. The Christian-supersessionism reading that treats Yahusha (Jesus) as the innovator of a new ethic replacing the *complicated* Torah collapses against Tobit 4:15. The teaching was already there; the King restores it.

**The narrow gate / wide gate at vv.13-14 walks the Hebrew library's own Two-Ways architecture, not a gospel-innovation of remnant-dualism.** Mosheh (Moses) at the close of Deuteronomy named the two-ways covenant-decision before the people went into the land:

*See, I have set before thee this day life and good, and death and evil... I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live.* (Deuteronomy 30:15, 19)

The Psalter opened on the same architecture — *blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly... for Yahuah (the LORD) knoweth the way of the righteous: but the way of the ungodly shall perish* (Psalm 1:1, 6) — and Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) carried the architecture into the prophets in the same explicit framing:

*Thus saith Yahuah (the LORD); Behold, I set before you the way of life, and the way of death.* (Jeremiah 21:8)

The apocalyptic-Hebrew-library brought the same two-ways forward into the wisdom of Enoch the righteous in extras-library witness:

*And now I say unto you, my sons, love righteousness and walk therein; For the paths of righteousness are worthy of acceptance, But the paths of unrighteousness shall suddenly be destroyed and vanish. And to certain men of a generation shall the paths of violence and death be revealed, And they shall hold themselves aloof from the paths of righteousness, And they shall not find mercy.* (1 Enoch 94:1-2)

Mosheh (Moses), the Psalter, Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah), and Enoch all stand on the same two-ways covenant-architecture. The teaching had been operative in the Hebrew library for centuries before the Sermon on the Mount. Yahusha (Jesus) at Matthew 7:13-14 restores the architecture at the kingdom-citizens level — wide gate to destruction with many entering, narrow gate to life with few finding — and binds the same architecture to the King's own naming of the kingdom-entry-criterion at vv.21-23 and the two-builders' summary at vv.24-27. The Christian commentary that treats the narrow gate as a gospel-invented dualism — *Old Testament was inclusivist, Jesus introduced the narrow way* — collapses against the Hebrew library's centuries-old architecture. The teaching was already in the library; the King restores it to the kingdom-citizens grammar.
