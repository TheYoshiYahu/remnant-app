# Short-form commentary on Matthew 7

Matthew 7 closes the Sermon on the Mount. The chapter walks the kingdom-citizens through the practical-discrimination standards — how the disciples judge, what they ask, what they take with them through the narrow gate, how they recognize false teachers, what the King requires for entry — and lands at the two builders as the Sermon-summary. The chapter contains one of the most load-bearing passages in the entire New Testament for what the framework names as the contrary-to-Torah test: Yahusha (Jesus) at vv.21-23 names in advance the community that will confess him as Lord, prophesy and cast out demons in his name, and hear in return *I never knew you.*

*Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again.* (Matthew 7:1-2)

The opening verse is constantly weaponized as a categorical prohibition on all moral discernment — *Jesus said do not judge, so the disciple may not assess any behavior, doctrine, or teacher.* The reading collapses on the next sentence. *With what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged* presupposes that the disciple WILL judge; the variable is the STANDARD. The mote-and-beam parable that follows specifies the same: the disciple removes the beam from his own eye FIRST, and *then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye* (v.5) — the brother's-mote-removal is restored, not abolished. The Torah itself commanded the rebuke:

*Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.* (Leviticus 19:17)

Then v.6 demands active discrimination outright:

*Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you.* (Matthew 7:6)

The disciple is commanded to KNOW the difference between *the holy* and *the dogs,* between *pearls* and *swine.* The verse requires the same discernment the Christian-tolerance reading of v.1 rules out. The framework reads vv.1-6 as a unified teaching: judge the brother with the same standard you apply to yourself; deal with your own faults first; then help the brother with his; and recognize the difference between brothers receiving correction and dogs and swine who reject the holy. The discernment is intensified, not abolished.

The chapter then turns to prayer-confidence — the Father's covenant-care for the gathered.

*Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you ... If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?* (Matthew 7:7, 11)

Luke's parallel specifies what the *good things* most centrally are:

*If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?* (Luke 11:13)

THE good gift is the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) — the indwelling Spirit who writes the heart-Torah on the citizens, fulfilling Ezekiel's promise:

*A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:26-27)

The Father's covenant-care for the kingdom-citizens is most fundamentally the gift of the Spirit who enables the kingdom-walking. The prosperity-gospel misuse of *ask and ye shall receive* misses what the *good things* are.

*Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets.* (Matthew 7:12)

Yahusha (Jesus) names what is often called the Golden Rule as the summary of *the law and the prophets* — the whole Tanakh-revelation. The framework reads this as a load-bearing Torah-affirmation, not a Torah-replacement. The King has just spent Matthew 5:17-19 binding himself and the kingdom-citizens to every jot and tittle; he has just specified the heart-Torah at six commandment cruxes in Matthew 5:21-48 and at three core practices in Matthew 6:1-18. The Golden Rule at Matthew 7:12 is the HEART-SUBSTANCE of the Torah and the prophets in summary-form, not a substitute for them. The Christian commentary tradition that flattens the Rule into a generic ethical principle replacing the Torah's specifics — *the Torah was complicated but Jesus simplified it to just love your neighbor* — collapses against the Sermon's whole architecture. Yahusha (Jesus) is naming the heart of what the Torah and the prophets express; he is not voiding what they express. The Tanakh's commandments toward the neighbor — *thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself* (Leviticus 19:18); *the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself* (Leviticus 19:34); the Tanakh's social-commandments throughout — are summed in the Rule's substance. The two are mutually-coextensive at the heart-substance level.

*Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.* (Matthew 7:13-14)

The narrow-gate teaching is remnant-language. The framework refuses two readings the Christian commentary tradition has offered. The first is the universalist softening that reads *many enter the wide way* as *many believers go astray on side-issues* while preserving an implicit-universal-salvation in the background. *Destruction* (*apōleia*) is the framework's destruction — exclusion from the kingdom, the second death, the post-harvest disposal of the tares and the unfruitful wheat. *Many* are there. The verse does not soften. The second is the institutional-Christian-mass-salvation reading that assumes the church-pew is automatically on the narrow way. The framework rejects this too. The Sermon has just named who walks the wide way in Matthew 5:19 — those who break the commandments and teach others to break — and will name them again at vv.21-23 as the *Lord, Lord* community whose ministry is supernatural and whose walking is torahless. The mere claim-of-Yahusha (Jesus) is not the proof of the narrow way; the heart-Torah-walking is. The Tanakh-stream is the remnant-pattern the prophets had named for centuries:

*For though thy people Yashar'el (Israel) be as the sand of the sea, yet a remnant of them shall return: the consumption decreed shall overflow with righteousness.* (Isaiah 10:22)

*And it shall come to pass, that in all the land, saith Yahuah (the LORD), two parts therein shall be cut off and die; but the third shall be left therein. And I will bring the third part through the fire, and will refine them as silver is refined, and will try them as gold is tried: they shall call on my name, and I will hear them: I will say, It is my people: and they shall say, Yahuah (The LORD) is my Elohim (God).* (Zechariah 13:8-9)

The remnant is consistently a minority. The third-part. The few who find. Yahusha (Jesus) walks the same prophetic stream.

*Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits.* (Matthew 7:15-16)

The false prophets *come to you in sheep's clothing* — they LOOK like the gathered remnant, they wear the sheep-costume — but inwardly they are wolves. The Tanakh's false-prophet diagnostic was already explicit:

*If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder, And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them; Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams: for Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) proveth you, to know whether ye love Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) with all your heart and with all your soul.* (Deuteronomy 13:1-3)

The signs are real; the prophet is false. Signs-and-wonders are not the proof; the Torah-fidelity is. The false prophet is identified by his teaching against the commandments, regardless of the apparent success of his ministry. This sets up the most load-bearing passage in the chapter.

*Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.* (Matthew 7:21-23)

These three verses dismantle the institutional-Christian gospel at the kingdom-entry level. Yahusha (Jesus) names the community he is describing precisely. They confess him as Lord — twice over, in emphatic acclamation. They prophesy IN HIS NAME — they are active in supernatural-prophetic ministry under the name of the Messiah. They cast out devils IN HIS NAME — they are active in deliverance-ministry. They do wonderful works IN HIS NAME — the Greek *dunameis pollas,* many works of power. By every credential the modern charismatic and evangelical communities track, these people pass. The verbal-confession is explicit. The supernatural-ministry is active. And Yahusha (Jesus) will say to *many* of them — not some, not a few, MANY — *I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.*

The Greek for *iniquity* is *anomia.* The English translation flattens it. *Anomia* literally means *without-Torah* — the *a-* (without) plus *nomos* (law) — TORAHLESSNESS. The same word stands behind Yochanan's (John's) definition of sin in his first letter:

*Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.* (1 John 3:4)

The *transgression of the law* there is *anomia* — sin defined as Torah-violation. Paul names the same architecture as the operative principle of the present age:

*For the mystery of iniquity doth already work.* (2 Thessalonians 2:7)

The *mystery of iniquity* in Paul's Greek is *to mystērion tēs anomias* — the mystery of TORAHLESSNESS, already working in the apostolic age. The system Paul named there is the system Yahusha (Jesus) is identifying at Matthew 7:23. The people the King dismisses are people who confessed him verbally, ministered supernaturally in his name, AND LIVED WITHOUT THE TORAH. Their ministry was active; their Torah-walking was absent.

This is the diagnostic for the institutional-Christian antinomian-gospel community in advance. The horn-pulpit teaches the church-congregation that the commandments are abolished, that the Sabbath is moved to Sunday, that the dietary laws are voided, that the feasts are replaced — that *grace* means freedom-from-the-Torah. The community formed under that teaching confesses Yahusha (Jesus) as Lord, often manifests supernatural-gifts in his name, and lives without the Torah. Yahusha (Jesus) calls them workers of *anomia.* He says *I never knew you.* He says *depart from me.* The contrary-to-Torah test the apostle Yochanan (John) names in his first letter walks the same diagnostic:

*And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.* (1 John 2:3-4)

The 1 John 2:3-4 test and the Matthew 7:21-23 dismissal are the same diagnostic in two registers. The kingdom-citizens are doers of the Father's will — the heart-Torah-walking the King has been unfolding through the Sermon. The *Lord-Lord* sayers who do not do are not in. The credentials of supernatural-ministry do not change the verdict; the post-harvest sifting separates the wheat-that-bore-fruit from the wheat-that-did-not, and the unfruitful wheat hears *I never knew you* regardless of how active its religious ministry was. The grace was real; the gift was active; the entry-condition was not met. This is the grace that can be forfeited at full New Testament voltage.

The Sermon closes on the two builders.

*Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock. And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them not, shall be likened unto a foolish man, which built his house upon the sand: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it.* (Matthew 7:24-27)

Both the wise and the foolish HEAR the sayings; the wise hears AND does, the foolish hears and does not. The hearing alone is not the foundation; the hearing-AND-doing is. This is the Sinai-pattern the Tanakh has been carrying since the Yashar'el (Israel) of the Exodus stood at the foot of the mountain:

*And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that Yahuah (the LORD) hath said will we do, and be obedient.* (Exodus 24:7)

The Hebrew of *will we do, and be obedient* is *na'aseh v'nishma* — we will do, and we will hear. The DOING comes first. The kingdom-citizens at Matthew 7:24-27 walk the same na'aseh v'nishma pattern: hearer-and-doer. The Reformation hierarchy that elevates hearing over doing, faith over works, grace over obedience runs straight into the Sermon's closing parable. The doing is not a downstream-fruit-that-may-or-may-not-follow; the doing IS the foundation-establishing act.

The chapter ends on the multitudes' response:

*And it came to pass, when Yahusha (Jesus) had ended these sayings, the people were astonished at his doctrine: For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.* (Matthew 7:28-29)

The scribes taught via tradition-chain authority — *Rabbi X said in the name of Rabbi Y who said.* Yahusha (Jesus) taught DIRECTLY — *but I say unto you* — in the King's own voice. The authority is the Torah's authority, but spoken in the voice of the One whose voice gave the Torah at Sinai. The Formed who stood on the mountain and thundered the ten words is now standing on a Galilean hillside and unfolding the heart-Torah in the same voice. The multitudes recognize the authority. What they do with it now reveals what they are. The Sermon has been delivered; the wide gate and the narrow gate have been named; the post-harvest sifting has been previewed; the entry-criterion has been locked; the two builders have been contrasted. The chapter closes on the recognition, not on the response. The response is the rest of the gospel.
