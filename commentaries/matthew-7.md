# Commentary on Matthew 7

Matthew 7 is the close of the Sermon on the Mount. The chapter walks the kingdom-citizens through the practical-discrimination standards — how the disciples judge (vv.1-6), what they ask and what the Father gives (vv.7-11), the Golden Rule named by Yahusha (Jesus) himself as *the law and the prophets* (v.12), the narrow gate and the few who find it (vv.13-14), the false prophets and the fruit-test (vv.15-20), the load-bearing entry-criterion declaration at vv.21-23 where the King names a future community that will confess him as Lord and minister supernaturally in his name and hear in return *I never knew you,* and the two-builders summary that lands the whole Sermon on the foundation-of-hearing-and-doing (vv.24-27). The multitudes' astonishment at v.29 — *for he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes* — closes the chapter on the recognition that the voice teaching the heart-Torah is the same voice that thundered the ten words from Sinai.

## §1. *Judge not* — the framework reading against the modern tolerance-absolutization (vv.1-6)

*Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again. And why beholdest thou the mote that is in thy brother's eye, but considerest not the beam that is in thine own eye? Or how wilt thou say to thy brother, Let me pull out the mote out of thine eye; and, behold, a beam is in thine own eye? Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye. Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you.* (Matthew 7:1-6)

The framework reads *Judge not* with particular care because the modern Christian commentary tradition has often weaponized v.1 as a categorical prohibition on moral-discernment of any kind. *Jesus said do not judge, therefore the believer must extend non-judgmental acceptance to every behavior, every doctrine, every teacher, every claim.* That reading collapses the verse against its own context and against the rest of Yahusha's (Jesus's) ministry.

The verse is followed IMMEDIATELY by *for with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again.* The next sentence presupposes that the disciple WILL judge — the question is the STANDARD of the judgment. The Greek *en hō krimati* — *with what KIND of judgment* — names the variable. The disciple is being instructed on HOW to judge, not commanded to abolish all judgment.

The mote-and-beam parable then specifies the form. The hypocritical-judgment pattern: the disciple sees the *mote* (the Greek *karphos,* a small piece of dry matter, a splinter) in the brother's eye while harboring a *beam* (the Greek *dokos,* a large load-bearing wooden beam) in his own. The corrective is NOT *therefore stop seeing the brother's mote.* The corrective is *cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye.* The judgment of the brother's fault is RESTORED, after the disciple has dealt with his own. The standard is consistent self-examination FIRST, then helpful correction of the brother. The Torah itself commanded the rebuke — not the abolition of the rebuke — and joined the rebuke to the love-of-neighbor in the same passage:

*Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him. Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:17-18)

The rebuke is commanded as part of the love-of-neighbor; the loving-neighbor includes the not-suffering-sin-upon-him. The Matthew 7:1-5 teaching is the Tanakh's love-and-rebuke pattern carried forward — with the self-examination discipline as the prerequisite for the helpful rebuke. Yahusha (Jesus) himself made the same clarification explicitly in the gospel of Yochanan (John):

*Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.* (John 7:24)

The judgment is restored at v.24; what is forbidden is the according-to-appearance judgment. The framework reads Matthew 7:1 in the same register: appearance-judgment is forbidden; righteous-judgment (which requires self-examination first) is required.

The pearls-before-swine verse at v.6 then specifies the OUTWARD-direction discrimination. *Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine.* The disciple is commanded to KNOW the difference between *the holy* and *the dogs,* between *pearls* and *swine.* This requires active moral-discernment — exactly what the modern tolerance-absolutization rules out. The disciple who cannot tell the holy from the dogs cannot obey v.6. The framework rules out any reading of vv.1-5 that contradicts v.6 in the same paragraph. The wisdom-tradition had said the same about discrimination of audience:

*Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words.* (Proverbs 23:9)

The framework reading of vv.1-6 as a unified teaching: judge the brother with the same standard you apply to yourself; deal with your own faults first; then help the brother with his; and recognize the difference between the brother-receiving-correction and the dogs-and-swine who reject the holy. The discernment is INTENSIFIED, not abolished. The framework's posture toward those still inside the antichrist systems — the people still in the institutional-Christian pew, the people still in the synagogue, the people still in the Hebrew-roots false-inclusion community, the people still in the Islamic-tawhid framework — is the patient-toward-the-deceived posture of the kingdom-citizens, not the tolerance-of-the-systems that would prohibit naming the lie. The framework attacks systems, never people; the systems remain named; the dogs and swine who finally reject the pearls are recognized and the pearls are not cast.

## §2. Ask, seek, knock — the Father's covenant-care and the gift of the Spirit (vv.7-11)

*Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you: For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened. Or what man is there of you, whom if his son ask bread, will he give him a stone? Or if he ask a fish, will he give him a serpent? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?* (Matthew 7:7-11)

The framework reads vv.7-11 as continuation of the Lord's Prayer's relational-trust frame. The kingdom-citizens approach the Father in confidence because the Father's covenant-care is sure. The threefold *ask / seek / knock* is the progressively-deeper kingdom-citizens prayer-posture, and the threefold *given / find / opened* names the corresponding Father's-response.

The earthly-father / heavenly-Father comparison at vv.9-11 walks the rabbinic *kal va'chomer* argument-form — the *light and weighty* — *if a lesser case holds, how much more the greater.* If earthly-fathers — who are *evil* — know how to give good gifts, *how much more* shall the heavenly Father give good things to the citizens who ask. The framework reads the *being evil* not as a Calvinist total-depravity claim but as the post-Eden condition of the seed of Adam living under the watcher-rebellion administration; even fallen-and-flawed earthly fathers give good gifts to their children. The argument-shape locks the kingdom-citizens prayer-confidence: the heavenly Father is greater than the earthly father, and the lesser case holds.

Luke's parallel teaching specifies what the *good things* most centrally are:

*If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent? Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?* (Luke 11:11-13)

THE good gift is the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit). The kingdom-citizens prayer-petition is most centrally for the indwelling Spirit who writes the heart-Torah on the citizens. This is the prophetic promise the Tanakh has been holding out since Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) and Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) — the new-heart promise that the Spirit will accomplish in the gathered:

*But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Yashar'el (Israel); After those days, saith Yahuah (the LORD), I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people.* (Jeremiah 31:33)

The Father's covenant-care for the gathered is most fundamentally the gift of the Spirit who enables the kingdom-walking. The Matthew 7:11 *good things* — the Greek *agatha* — name the kingdom-substance the Father gives, with the Spirit at the center. The Spirit gift, the heart-Torah-writing, the cause-to-walk-in-the-statutes — all of these the Father has been promising since Jeremiah and Ezekiel, and Yahusha (Jesus) at Matthew 7:11 is saying *this is the substance the Father has been waiting to give to those who ask.*

The framework rules out the prosperity-gospel misuse of vv.7-11. The *ask / seek / knock* is not a wealth-and-blessing-claim formula. The Father gives *good things,* and the framework definition of *good* is the kingdom-substance — the Spirit, the heart-Torah, the gathering-promise, the daily-bread of Matthew 6:11, the deliverance from the evil one of Matthew 6:13. The vv.7-11 verses sit inside the Sermon's broader teaching on the kingdom-citizens orientation; they do not turn into a wealth-promise template apart from that teaching.

## §3. The Golden Rule named by Yahusha (Jesus) as *the law and the prophets* (v.12)

The framework reads v.12 as a load-bearing Torah-affirmation (the restoration-not-innovation framing — Tobit 4:15, Hillel's negative-form rule, and the heart-substance of the Tanakh-revelation behind the Rule — is walked in the chapter's short-form commentary). The long-form develops one further architectural move: the Rule is the heart-substance of the Torah, NOT a replacement of the Torah's specific commandments. Yahusha (Jesus) has just spent Matthew 5:17-19 binding himself and the kingdom-citizens to every jot and tittle of the Torah; he has spent Matthew 5:21-48 restoring the heart-Torah at six commandment cruxes; he has spent Matthew 6:1-18 specifying the kingdom-citizens form of three core practices. The Golden Rule at Matthew 7:12 cannot abolish what the King has just affirmed. The Christian commentary tradition that flattens the Rule into a generic ethical principle replacing the Torah's specifics — *the Torah was complicated but Yahusha (Jesus) simplified it to just love your neighbor* — collapses the verse against the Sermon's whole architecture. Yahusha (Jesus) is naming the heart of what the Torah and the prophets EXPRESS; he is not voiding what they express.

The Tanakh's commandments toward the neighbor and the stranger are summed in the Rule's substance:

*Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:18)

*But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Mitsrayim (Egypt): I am Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 19:34)

The Tanakh's prohibitions on theft and false witness and covetousness, the prophets' demands for justice-and-mercy-and-humble-walk — these are summed in the Golden Rule's substance. The kingdom-citizens who walk the Torah's commandments are walking what the Rule names; the kingdom-citizens who walk the Rule are walking the Torah's commandments. The two are mutually-coextensive at the heart-substance level. The Sermon's earlier articulation of the same principle at Matthew 5:17-19 — *whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven* — and the Sermon's later articulation at Matthew 7:21 — *but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven* — both lock the Golden Rule inside the Torah-affirmation context. The Rule is not a replacement; it is the heart-substance of what the Torah and the prophets express.

## §4. The narrow gate and the few — remnant-language at full voltage (vv.13-14)

The framework reads the narrow-gate teaching as remnant-language and rules out the Christian-universalist softening that has often diluted it.

The structure is binary and quantitatively-asymmetric: TWO gates, TWO ways, TWO destinations. The wide gate / broad way leads to destruction; *many there be which go in thereat.* The strait gate / narrow way leads to life; *few there be that find it.* The numerical-asymmetry is explicit. The framework will not soften it.

The Tanakh-stream behind the teaching is the remnant-of-Yahuah pattern the prophets had named for centuries:

*And it shall come to pass in that day, that the remnant of Yashar'el (Israel), and such as are escaped of the house of Ya'aqov (Jacob), shall no more again stay upon him that smote them; but shall stay upon Yahuah (the LORD), the Holy One of Yashar'el (Israel), in truth. The remnant shall return, even the remnant of Ya'aqov (Jacob), unto the mighty El (God). For though thy people Yashar'el (Israel) be as the sand of the sea, yet a remnant of them shall return: the consumption decreed shall overflow with righteousness.* (Isaiah 10:20-22)

The remnant is by definition a subset; the *consumption decreed* names the larger consequence. The same prophetic stream runs through Tzefanyah (Zephaniah):

*I will also leave in the midst of thee an afflicted and poor people, and they shall trust in the name of Yahuah (the LORD). The remnant of Yashar'el (Israel) shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies; neither shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth: for they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid.* (Zephaniah 3:12-13)

And through Zecharyah (Zechariah), at the end of the prophetic canon, in the most explicit numerical-asymmetric form:

The same prophet had named the third-part survivors with even more precision — those who look on the pierced one:

*And I will pour upon the house of David, and upon the inhabitants of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), the spirit of grace and of supplications: and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son, and shall be in bitterness for him, as one that is in bitterness for his firstborn.* (Zechariah 12:10)

The remnant is consistently named as a minority — the third-part, the marked, the few who find. Yahusha (Jesus) at Matthew 7:13-14 walks the same prophetic stream.

This rules out two readings the framework refuses.

First — the Christian-universalist softening that reads *many enter the wide way* as *many believers go astray on side-issues* while preserving an implicit-universal-salvation in the background. The framework rejects this. *Destruction* (the Greek *apōleia*) is the framework's destruction — exclusion from the kingdom, the second death, the post-harvest disposal of the tares and the unfruitful wheat. *Many* are there. The verse does not soften.

Second — the institutional-Christian-mass-salvation reading that assumes the church-pew is automatically on the narrow way. The framework rejects this too. The Sermon on the Mount has just specified at Matthew 5:19 the diagnostic of breaking-and-teaching-others-to-break (which the modern Judaizer movement's pulpit fits) and will specify at Matthew 7:21-23 the *Lord-Lord* community walking the wide way. The mere claim-of-Yahusha (Jesus) is not the proof-of-the-narrow-way; the heart-Torah-walking is.

The *few there be that find it* lock at v.14 names the actual finding-pattern. *Find* — the Greek *heuriskō* — they are FOUND in the narrow way; the way does not just open for any who claim it. The framework's hearing-reveals-not-makes principle is operative here. Those who find are those whose names were written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world AND who walk the heart-Torah through the post-harvest sifting:

*And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.* (Revelation 13:8)

The wheat hear the Shepherd because they are sheep — *my sheep hear my voice* (John 10:27) — and the tares cannot hear because they are not. Among the gathered wheat, the post-harvest sifting then separates the wheat-that-bore-fruit from the wheat-that-did-not, and names get blotted out among the unfruitful. The narrow gate corresponds to the remnant who pass both the foundation-of-the-world identification (the sheep who hear) and the post-harvest sifting (the wheat that bore fruit). Yahusha (Jesus) himself locks the intensification of the *few* in the Lukan parallel:

*Then said one unto him, Adonai (Lord), are there few that be saved? And he said unto them, Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able.* (Luke 13:23-24)

The strive — the Greek *agōnizomai,* to contend, to wrestle — is the kingdom-citizens posture toward the narrow gate. Many will seek and not be able. The few who find — by hearing and by walking — enter the life the gate leads to.

## §5. False prophets and the fruit-test — the seed-war diagnostic at the leadership level (vv.15-20)

*Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit. A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit. Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire. Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them.* (Matthew 7:15-20)

The framework reads vv.15-20 as the seed-war diagnostic specified at the false-prophet-identification level. The false prophets *come to you in sheep's clothing* — they LOOK like the gathered remnant, they wear the sheep-costume — *but inwardly they are ravening wolves.* The framework's seed-war architecture from Genesis 3:15 is direct here: the seed of the serpent walks among the seed of the woman in disguise, and the disciple's task is to discern.

The *good tree / corrupt tree* dichotomy at vv.16-18 names the lineage-identification register. *A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit.* The fruit reveals the nature. This is consistent with the framework's hearing-reveals-not-makes principle: the fruit reveals what the tree IS, the fruit does not transform what the tree is. The wolf-in-sheep's-clothing is a wolf; the costume does not make the wolf into a sheep. The fruit-test exposes the underlying nature.

*Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) which I command you.* (Deuteronomy 4:2)

## §6. *Lord, Lord* — the load-bearing anti-antinomian declaration of the Sermon (vv.21-23)

These three verses are the most load-bearing passage in Matthew 7 for the framework — and one of the most load-bearing passages in the whole New Testament. The institutional-Christian system has either ignored this passage or developed elaborate exegesis to neutralize it. The framework reads it directly.

**Verse 21.** *Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.*

Yahusha (Jesus) opens with a direct denial of the verbal-confession-as-entry-criterion gospel. *Saying Lord, Lord* — the verbal-confession of Yahusha (Jesus) as Lord — is NOT THE ENTRY CRITERION to the kingdom. The Pauline citation often used to argue otherwise stands inside a longer passage:

*That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Adonai (Lord) Yahusha (Jesus), and shalt believe in thine heart that Elohim (God) hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.* (Romans 10:9-10)

The framework reads Paul as harmonizing with the King, not contradicting him. Romans 10:9-10 names the initial confession-and-belief that enters the journey; the King at Matthew 7:21 names the doing-of-the-Father's-will as the kingdom-entry criterion at the judgment-day. The two are not in conflict. The journey begins with confession; the entry happens through the walking. The institutional-Christian collapse of Romans 10:9-10 into the entire gospel — *say the prayer, you're in forever* — is the antinomian flattening of Paul into the antichrist reading. The same Paul who wrote Romans 10:9-10 wrote at the same letter that grace does not abolish the Torah:

*Do we then make void the law through faith? Elohim (God) forbid: yea, we establish the law.* (Romans 3:31)

And the same Paul wrote, against the wresting of his own letters that was happening even in his own lifetime:

*As also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction. Ye therefore, beloved, seeing ye know these things before, knowing them, be ye on your guard lest ye also, being led away with the error of the wicked, fall from your own stedfastness.* (2 Peter 3:16-17)

Peter — writing about Paul — names the wresting of Paul's letters as a known phenomenon that destroys the unstable. The institutional-Christian collapse of Paul into an anti-Torah gospel is the wresting Peter warned about, two thousand years downstream. No Christian doctrine standing on the wrested Paul can stand as load-bearing against the King's own teaching at Matthew 7:21. The actual entry-criterion is *doing the will of the Father.* The framework reads the *will of the Father* as the heart-Torah-walking — the same Father's will the Lord's Prayer petitions for in the previous chapter when it asks *thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.* The doer-of-the-Father's-will enters the kingdom; the verbal-confessor without the doing does not.

**Verse 22.** *Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works?*

The framework reads this verse with particular care because of who Yahusha (Jesus) is describing. These are not the obvious-pagan unbelievers. These are people who confess Yahusha (Jesus) as Lord — twice, in emphatic acclamation. They prophesied IN HIS NAME — they were active in supernatural-prophetic ministry under his name. They cast out devils IN HIS NAME — they were active in deliverance-ministry under his name. They did many wonderful works — the Greek *dunameis pollas,* many works of power — IN HIS NAME.

This is the IMPRESSIVE religious-ministry-performance community. They are not just church-attenders; they are active in supernatural-gifted-ministry under Yahusha's (Jesus's) name. By every visible-credential the modern Christian-charismatic and Christian-evangelical communities track, these people pass the test. **Verse 23 — the load-bearing diagnostic.** *And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.*

The framework reads every word of this verse.

*I never knew you* — the Greek *oudepote egnōn humas.* Yahusha (Jesus) names them as never-known. This is not *I knew you once and now reject you;* this is *I never knew you at all.* The relational-knowing — the *yada* of the Tanakh covenant-relationship — never existed between Yahusha (Jesus) and these supernatural-ministry-performers. Their entire ministry under his name was performed without his actual knowing of them. The credentials were real; the supernatural-acts apparently produced their effects; but the underlying knowing-relationship was absent.

*Depart from me* — the Greek *apochōreite ap' emou.* The framework reads this as the post-harvest sifting of the wheat. The wheat is gathered from all nations into the wilderness for sifting; the unfruitful are purged; only the fruitful pass through to the kingdom-citizenship in the millennial reign. Ezekiel named the architecture explicitly:

*And I will cause you to pass under the rod, and I will bring you into the bond of the covenant: And I will purge out from among you the rebels, and them that transgress against me: I will bring them forth out of the country where they sojourn, and they shall not enter into the land of Yashar'el (Israel): and ye shall know that I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Ezekiel 20:37-38)

The *purge out from among you the rebels, and them that transgress against me* is the post-harvest sifting language Matthew 7:23's *depart from me* echoes. The people at Matthew 7:22 had the appearance of being wheat — they were gathered, they were active in supernatural-ministry under Yahusha's (Jesus's) name — but the rod-of-the-covenant sifting reveals them as rebels-among-the-wheat. They are not tares (tares never entered the threshing-floor; tares were burned in the field at the harvest); they are unfruitful wheat dismissed at the second sifting. David's psalm had named the same departure-from-me formula that Yahusha (Jesus) quotes:

*Depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity; for Yahuah (the LORD) hath heard the voice of my weeping.* (Psalm 6:8)

Yahusha (Jesus) is quoting David's psalm against the supernatural-ministry community he is dismissing. The Tanakh-anchor of the *depart from me, workers of iniquity* formula is the very psalm of David that names the same diagnostic for the same kind of community.

*Ye that work iniquity* — the Greek *hoi ergazomenoi tēn anomian* — *the WORKERS OF ANOMIA.* This is the diagnostic phrase, and the English translation flattens it. *Anomia* — the *a-* (without) plus *nomos* (law) — literally means *WITHOUT-TORAH.* The Greek names the without-Torah condition. The people Yahusha (Jesus) is dismissing are the workers of TORAHLESSNESS. They prophesied, cast out demons, and did wonderful works WHILE LIVING WITHOUT THE TORAH. Their ministry was active; their Torah-walking was absent. They confessed Yahusha (Jesus) verbally; they violated the commandments practically.

The apostle Yochanan (John) defined sin itself with the same Greek word:

The *transgression of the law* is *anomia* in the Greek. Sin is Torah-violation, defined directly. Sin is what the workers of *anomia* DO; the workers of *anomia* are sinners by Yochanan's (John's) own definition. And Paul names the operative system of the present age in the same vocabulary:

*For the mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way. And then shall that Wicked be revealed, whom the Adonai (Lord) shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming.* (2 Thessalonians 2:7-8)

The *mystery of iniquity* in Paul's Greek is *to mystērion tēs anomias* — the mystery of TORAHLESSNESS, already working in the apostolic age. The system Paul named there is the system Yahusha (Jesus) is identifying at Matthew 7:23. The mystery of torahlessness was already at work in the first century; the institutional-Christian system that rose out of it has been the mystery's full surface manifestation for seventeen centuries.

The framework reads v.23 as the DIRECT diagnostic for the institutional-Christian antinomian-gospel community. The framework's identification of institutional Christianity as the modern Judaizer movement — same flesh-credential gospel as the Pharisees and the Galatian agitators, in a different costume; same voiding of the Torah (by subtraction rather than by addition); the contrary-to-Torah test of 1 John 2:3-4 identifying the lie — applies at full force here. The modern Judaizer-pulpit has taught the church-congregation that the commandments are abolished, that the Sabbath is moved to Sunday, that the dietary laws are voided, that the feasts are replaced — that *grace* means freedom-from-the-Torah. The community formed under that teaching confesses Yahusha (Jesus) as Lord, often manifests supernatural-gifts in his name, and lives WITHOUT the Torah. Yahusha (Jesus) calls them *workers of anomia.* He says *I never knew you.* He says *depart from me.*

This is the most-frequently-evaded verse in the institutional-Christian commentary tradition. The Christian commentaries have variously: read *iniquity* as some-other-sins-not-Torah-violation (the very translation flattening of *anomia* into *iniquity* obscures the without-Torah substance); read *I never knew you* as applying to some-fringe-community-of-extreme-cases (preserving the assumption that the institutional-mainstream is safe); read the verse as a warning to apply to ourselves while extending mass-comfort to the church-pew (collapsing the warning's force). The framework reads the verse plainly. *Anomia* is *without-Torah.* The *many* who will be dismissed are *many.* The credential-list — prophesying, casting out devils, wonderful works — is the institutional-Christian-charismatic credential-list. The diagnostic is exact.

The framework reads vv.21-23 as the load-bearing New Testament anchor for the architecture that the Tanakh has been teaching since Moses: grace freely given, grace that can be forfeited, the redeemed who walked away losing what they had. Hosea named it:

*Woe unto them! for they have fled from me: destruction unto them! because they have transgressed against me: though I have redeemed them, yet they have spoken lies against me.* (Hosea 7:13)

Ezekiel named it for the righteous-turned-to-iniquity:

*When I shall say to the righteous, that he shall surely live; if he trust to his own righteousness, and commit iniquity, all his righteousnesses shall not be remembered; but for his iniquity that he hath committed, he shall die for it.* (Ezekiel 33:13)

The Hebrews-letter author named it for the redeemed who tasted the Spirit:

*For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit), And have tasted the good word of Elohim (God), and the powers of the world to come, If they shall fall away, to renew them again unto repentance.* (Hebrews 6:4-6)

These were people who had received the gift — they could prophesy, they could cast out demons, they could do wonderful works, they had tasted the powers of the world to come — and the gift was real, and the opportunity was real, and they LOST it through *anomia.* The grace was offered; the gift was active; the entry-condition (doing-the-Father's-will = heart-Torah-walking) was not met; the dismissal at the post-harvest sifting is the result. Matthew 7:21-23 is the load-bearing New Testament voltage of the whole canonical pattern. The grace can be forfeited. The institutional-Christian *once saved, always saved* doctrine collapses on this passage and is wrong at the foundation.

## §7. The two builders — *na'aseh v'nishma* hearing-AND-doing as the kingdom-citizens foundation (vv.24-27)

The framework reads the closing parable as the Sermon-on-the-Mount summary at the foundation-level. The DIFFERENTIATOR between the wise and the foolish is *heareth ... AND doeth them.* Both the wise and the foolish HEAR the sayings of Yahusha (Jesus); the wise hears AND does; the foolish hears AND does not. The hearing alone is not the foundation; the hearing-AND-doing is.

The framework reads this against the Tanakh-pattern of *na'aseh v'nishma* — Exodus 24:7's *we will do, and we will hear:*

*And Mosheh (Moses) took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that Yahuah (the LORD) hath said will we do, and be obedient.* (Exodus 24:7)

The Hebrew of *will we do, and be obedient* is *na'aseh v'nishma* — *we will do, and we will hear.* The Yashar'el (Israel) at Sinai responded to the Torah-presentation with the we-will-do-and-hear commitment — putting the DOING first. The framework reads Matthew 7:24-27 as the same pattern in the Sermon-on-the-Mount frame. The kingdom-citizens hear-AND-do; the *Lord-Lord* community of v.22 hears-without-doing; the difference is the kingdom-entry-criterion.

The rock-foundation imagery walks the Tanakh-substance of Yahuah (the LORD) as the Rock:

*He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a El (God) of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he.* (Deuteronomy 32:4)

*Yahuah (The LORD) is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my Elohi (God), my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower.* (Psalm 18:2)

The foundation-rock the wise man builds upon is Yahuah (the LORD) himself — and the access to that foundation is through hearing-and-doing the heart-Torah Yahusha (Jesus) has unfolded in the Sermon. The doing-of-the-sayings is what locks the citizen into the rock-foundation. The hearer-without-the-doing has heard the sayings of the King — has perhaps even spoken them, taught them, sung them, preached them — but has not built on them; the house stands on sand because the foundation-establishing act is the doing.

The sand-foundation is the hearing-without-doing pattern. The framework reads this as the institutional-Christian-pulpit-and-pew arrangement specifically. The pew HEARS the sayings every Sunday — *love your enemies, judge not, lay up treasure in heaven, seek first the kingdom, do unto others* — and the pulpit teaches the congregation that the heart-Torah's specific commandments are abolished by grace. The hearing happens; the doing does not. The foundation is sand. *And great was the fall of it* — the framework reads the fall against the post-harvest sifting, the day of Yahuah (the LORD), the eschatological-test that exposes what each builder built upon.

The apostle Ya'aqov (James) carried the same diagnostic forward into the apostolic letters:

*But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves. For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was. But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.* (James 1:22-25)

Ya'aqov names the same hearer-AND-doer standard, names the Torah as the *perfect law of liberty,* and locks the *doer of the work* as the one who is blessed. The Tanakh's *na'aseh v'nishma* pattern, Yahusha's (Jesus's) two-builders parable, and Ya'aqov's (James's) doer-of-the-word standard are the same teaching in three registers. The Reformation hierarchy that elevates hearing over doing, faith over works, grace over obedience runs straight into the Sermon's closing parable and into Ya'aqov's first chapter. The doing is not a downstream-fruit-that-may-or-may-not-follow; the doing IS the foundation-establishing act.

## §8. *Authority, not as the scribes* — the second-Sinai voice locked at chapter-close (vv.28-29)

The Sermon closes on the multitudes' response. *Astonished* — the Greek *ekplēssomai* — struck out of themselves, knocked-loose-from-the-default. The teaching landed at a register the listeners had not heard before.

The framework reads the *authority, and not as the scribes* contrast as the second-Sinai motif locking back at chapter-end. The scribes taught via tradition-chain authority — *Rabbi-X said in the name of Rabbi-Y who said in the name of...* The chain-of-tradition was the authority-source. Yahusha (Jesus) taught DIRECTLY — *but I say unto you* — in the King's own voice. The authority IS the Torah's authority (the King has bound himself to every jot and tittle at Matthew 5:18), but spoken in the voice of the One whose Voice gave the Torah at Sinai.

The framework's nature-of-God reading from earlier chapters surfaces again here. The Voice that spoke the Torah at Sinai was the Formed — the Son drawn from the Formless — who has now taken flesh and speaks the heart-Torah directly. The *Immanuel — Elohim (God) with us* of Matthew 1:23 is the same voice as the *but I say unto you* of the Sermon. The *authority* the multitudes recognize at v.29 is the same authority that thundered at Sinai. The scribes lacked it because the scribes lacked the relationship-with-the-Voice the Sinai-Speaker is now demonstrating in person. Mark's parallel records the same astonishment:

*And they were astonished at his doctrine: for he taught them as one that had authority, and not as the scribes.* (Mark 1:22)

The chapter closes on the recognition-of-authority by the multitudes — not on their committed-discipleship. The multitudes are astonished; some will follow; many will not. The Sermon has been delivered. The wide gate / narrow gate distinction has been named. The post-harvest sifting has been previewed. The two builders have been contrasted. The Father's-will-doing as the kingdom-entry-criterion has been locked. The multitudes hear the authority; what they do with it now reveals what they are.
