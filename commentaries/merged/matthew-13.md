## Two sowers, two seeds, one field

Matthew 13 is the parables-of-the-kingdom chapter, and the lens it turns on is the oldest one in scripture: two seed-lines sown into the same field. The same day that closed in Matthew 12 with the conspiracy to destroy the King hardened against him opens here with the King teaching from a boat-pulpit on the Sea of Galilee — the Galilee-territory the prophet named as the land of the scattered northern tribes, the light-in-darkness territory of Isaiah 9:1-2 (Matthew 4:13-16). Seven parables structure the chapter across two settings: four from the boat to the multitudes — the sower, the wheat and the tares, the mustard seed, the leaven — and three in the house to the disciples — the hidden treasure, the pearl of great price, the dragnet. At the center of the seven stands the wheat-and-tares interpretation, the King's own most direct articulation of the seed-war that runs from Genesis 3:15: two seed-lines, two sowers, sown into one field, grown together until the harvest, sorted at the end. The chapter opens and closes on the same diagnostic — *who hath ears to hear, let him hear* — because hearing is the thing the chapter is about.

## The setting and the sower

*The same day went Yahusha (Jesus) out of the house, and sat by the sea side. And great multitudes were gathered together unto him, so that he went into a ship, and sat; and the whole multitude stood on the shore.* (Matthew 13:1-2)

The chapter opens *the same day* — deliberate architectural juxtaposition. The chapter that landed the Shabbat-controversies, the Beelzebul-attribution, the blasphemy-against-the-Ruach warning, the sign-of-Yonah refusal, and the empty-house parable closes with the rejection-disposition of the Pharisaic system locked. The chapter that opens the same day explains in parable-form WHY the King now teaches in parable-form rather than plain discourse — the division between hearing and not-hearing has reached the point where the teaching is veiled to the system and revealed only to the hearing disciples.

The setting itself carries framework-substance. The King teaches from a boat-pulpit; the multitudes stand on the shore. The Galilee-territory is the Isaiah 9 light-in-darkness territory of the scattered northern house. The lake-pulpit reaches the scattered-seed-territories, and the multitudes who gather include the *am-haaretz* — the people-of-the-land the Pharisaic system categorized as unlearned and cursed (*this people who knoweth not the law are cursed,* John 7:49) — the marginalized whom the credentialing-system had excluded.

*Behold, a sower went forth to sow; And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them up: Some fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up, because they had no deepness of earth: And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away. And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and choked them: But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit, some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some thirtyfold. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.* (Matthew 13:3-9)

The sower is unnamed but implied — the One who has been sowing the kingdom-message from Matthew 3 forward (Yochanan (John), Yahusha (Jesus), the twelve sent at Matthew 10). The seed is the word of the kingdom. The four soils are four kinds of hearers. And the *who hath ears to hear, let him hear* close makes the parable-form itself enact the diagnostic it teaches: those with ears hear; those without ears do not. The King is sowing in parable-form precisely because the division between hearing and not-hearing must now be visible in the form of the teaching.

## Why parables — the hearing already given, the hardening already chosen

*And the disciples came, and said unto him, Why speakest thou unto them in parables? He answered and said unto them, Because it is given unto you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it is not given.* (Matthew 13:10-11)

This is the chapter's first framework-major moment. The Greek *dedotai* — *it has been given,* passive perfect — names a giving that precedes the hearing. The disciples are not hearing because they figured out the parables, and the multitudes are not failing because they could not work them out. The giving precedes the receiving; the receiving makes the hearing possible. This is the cause-and-effect the whole framework rests on: *if you hear, you are sons of Avraham (Abraham); you are sons of Adam; you are the seed of promise.* You did not choose to hear and become these things. You heard because you are these things. Hearing reveals what was always true. The King names it from the inside elsewhere:

*Ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.* (John 10:26-27)

**The principle of accumulation** (v.12). *For whosoever hath, to him shall be given, and he shall have more abundance: but whosoever hath not, from him shall be taken away even that he hath.* The having and not-having operate at the level of disposition toward the Word. Those who receive it with the disposition to hear receive more; those who receive it with the disposition not to hear lose even what they held superficially. The principle operates at the system-level too: the Pharisaic system had the Tanakh, the temple, the priestly architecture, the rabbinic tradition; it refused to hear the King's credentialing-substance; it will lose even what it had at the destruction of the temple.

**The hardening-citation** (vv.13-15). *Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand. And in them is fulfilled the prophecy of Esaias (Isaiah), which saith, By hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand; and seeing ye shall see, and shall not perceive: For this people's heart is waxed gross, and their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes they have closed; lest at any time they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and should understand with their heart, and should be converted, and I should heal them.*

Isaiah 6 is the prophet's commissioning vision; Yahuah (the LORD) sends Isaiah to a people whose hearing and seeing have already closed. The prophet's mission is not to make them hear — they have already closed — but to proclaim the word and let the word itself reveal the hearers from the non-hearers. The Christian commentary tradition has sometimes read the *lest at any time they should ... be converted, and I should heal them* clause as Yahuah (God) actively preventing the people from hearing so they cannot be saved. The framework rules that reading out. The condition is past-tense and actor-initiated — *their* heart waxed gross, *their* ears dulled, *their* eyes *they have closed.* The clause is the outcome of the hardening already done: in the state of the already-hardened, the proclamation does not result in conversion and healing because the disposition to hear is gone. The Father does not implant the unbelief; the people chose it; the proclamation continues to reveal the hardening rather than penetrate it.

This is the Pharaoh-pattern of Exodus. Pharaoh hardened his own heart (Exodus 8:15, 8:32, 9:34); the Father confirmed the hardening (Exodus 9:12, 10:1, 10:20, 11:10). The actor initiates the disposition; the Father confirms and uses it for the unfolding of his purposes. The disposition is the actor's; the consequence is the Father's judicial handing-over. The framework holds the cause-and-effect of hearing without collapsing into the Reformed determinism that denies the actor's responsibility, and without collapsing into the libertarian self-determination that makes the actor the source of his own election. The Father knows; the actor disposes; the hearing reveals.

**The disciples' blessing** (vv.16-17). *But blessed are your eyes, for they see: and your ears, for they hear. For verily I say unto you, That many prophets and righteous men have desired to see those things which ye see, and have not seen them; and to hear those things which ye hear, and have not heard them.* The disciples see and hear because they have been given to see and hear; the giving precedes the seeing; the blessing names what they received as gift. The prophets inquired into the salvation that would come and saw it from afar; the disciples are positioned at the unique moment of the King's presence in the flesh, and their seeing and hearing is the realized substance the prophets foretold.

## The four soils

*Hear ye therefore the parable of the sower. When any one heareth the word of the kingdom, and understandeth it not, then cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart. This is he which received seed by the way side. But he that received the seed into stony places, the same is he that heareth the word, and anon with joy receiveth it; Yet hath he not root in himself, but dureth for a while: for when tribulation or persecution ariseth because of the word, by and by he is offended. He also that received seed among the thorns is he that heareth the word; and the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, choke the word, and he becometh unfruitful. But he that received seed into the good ground is he that heareth the word, and understandeth it; which also beareth fruit, and bringeth forth, some an hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty.* (Matthew 13:18-23)

The four soils are four kinds of hearers, the fourfold pattern enacting the cause-and-effect of hearing at the response-level.

**The way side** (v.19). The Word arrives but is not understood; the wicked one catches it away. The not-understanding precedes the snatching; the snatching exploits it. The snatcher is the same actor identified at verse 39 — the devil. The fowls that devoured the seed at verse 4 carry the same register as the unclean birds of prey that descended on Avraham's (Abraham's) covenant-sacrifice, which he drove away (Genesis 15:11): fowls as adversaries to the covenant.

**The stony places** (vv.20-21). The Word arrives with joy but does not take root; the disposition does not survive tribulation or persecution. This is the partial-deliverance-without-internal-replacement pattern the empty-house parable just described at Matthew 12:43-45. The Word reaches the surface; the new heart of Ezekiel 36:26-27 does not internalize; the first test from outside reveals the shallow rooting. The Word given is grace; the failure to internalize is the path to losing what was given. The first-century disciples faced both — persecution from the system that conspired against the King (Matthew 12:14) and would extend to the apostolic band, and the broader tribulation of the day of Ya'aqov's (Jacob's) trouble (Jeremiah 30:7). The disposition under tribulation is one of the key diagnostics of root-substance.

**Among thorns** (v.22). The Word arrives and grows but is choked by the care of this world and the deceitfulness of riches. The Mammon-or-Elohim binary of Matthew 6:24 operates here. Riches are not themselves condemned — the Tanakh shows Avraham (Abraham), Yitschaq (Isaac), Ya'aqov (Jacob), David, and Iyov (Job) covenant-blessed with wealth. The deceit of riches is the substance that promises life but cannot deliver — the promise that wealth secures or fulfills the heart. The thorns are the active disposition of competing allegiance that strangles the Word's fruit.

**The good ground** (v.23). The Word arrives, is heard, is understood, and bears fruit thirtyfold, sixtyfold, hundredfold. The *understandeth it* is not mere intellectual comprehension; it is heart-receiving with the disposition to walk in it — the *na'aseh v'nishma,* do-and-hear, of Exodus 24:7, where the hearing-and-doing IS the understanding. The Word is internalized into the heart-Torah-walking life; the new heart of Ezekiel 36:26-27 takes residence; the fruit follows. The variable yield does not disqualify any of the three from being good ground — the hundredfold echoes the covenant-blessing-substance the Tanakh anchors: *Then Yitschaq (Isaac) sowed in that land, and received in the same year an hundredfold: and Yahuah (the LORD) blessed him* (Genesis 26:12).

## The wheat and the tares

*Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field: But while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way. But when the blade was sprung up, and brought forth fruit, then appeared the tares also. So the servants of the householder came and said unto him, Sir, didst not thou sow good seed in thy field? from whence then hath it tares? He said unto them, An enemy hath done this. The servants said unto him, Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up? But he said, Nay; lest while ye gather up the tares, ye root up also the wheat with them. Let both grow together until the harvest: and in the time of harvest I will say to the reapers, Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn.* (Matthew 13:24-30)

This is the chapter's most weight-bearing seed-architecture passage, and the King himself will interpret it in full at verses 36-43. Note now the *let both grow together until the harvest* instruction, which has produced enormous controversy and is easily misread.

The *let both grow together* is NOT a license for the kingdom-citizens to refuse to discern in the present age. The citizens are taught to discern false prophets by fruit (Matthew 7:15-20), to test the spirits (1 John 4:1), to mark and avoid those who cause divisions against the teaching (Romans 16:17). The discernment-disposition is mandated. What the instruction refuses is the final judicial uprooting that the citizens are NOT authorized to perform — they are not the reapers (verse 39 will identify the reapers as the angels). The citizens are not authorized to determine final judgment on individuals; the Father has reserved the harvest-judgment for himself and the reapers he appoints. The Yahudi (Jewish) history of forced conversions and the institutional-Christian history of inquisitions, forced baptisms, and crusades against the supposed tares all violate the *let both grow together* instruction. This is the *sit out, speak up* posture: the citizens speak truth and cry out against abominations as the prophets did; they do not raise arms against the system to root out the counterfeit. The Father brings the desolation; the citizens do not. This is not the Christian-pacifist absolutization — the Torah's judicial architecture (the cities of refuge, the courts of elders, the death penalty for capital offenses) operates in its own frame. The *let both grow together* governs the citizens' disposition toward the eschatological final judgment, which is the Father's prerogative alone.

## The mustard seed and the leaven — mixed growth, not pure triumph

*Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field: Which indeed is the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among herbs, and becometh a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in the branches thereof.* (Matthew 13:31-32)

The inherited Christian commentary tradition reads the mustard-seed parable as the kingdom's growth from small beginnings to global dominance throughout the age. The framework reads with caution, inside the wheat-and-tares architecture the chapter has just established. The image of a tree with the birds of the air lodging in its branches is not categorically positive. It is the imagery of kingdoms providing shelter — and in the Tanakh the kingdoms that carry it are judged. Babylon is the great tree that the birds lodge in:

*The tree grew, and was strong, and the height thereof reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to the end of all the earth ... and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof.* (Daniel 4:11-12)

The cedar of Assyria carries the same image and the same fall:

*All the fowls of heaven made their nests in his boughs ... Therefore thus saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD); Because thou hast lifted up thyself in height ... I have therefore delivered him into the hand of the mighty one of the heathen.* (Ezekiel 31:6, 31:10-11)

And the birds in this very chapter's parable of the sower are identified as the wicked one (vv.4, 19). The kingdom's growth is real, but the present-age growth is not uncomplicated progress toward global dominance — the wheat-and-tares parable just given establishes that both wheat and tares grow together until the harvest. The mustard-seed parable continues the same architecture: the kingdom grows; the growth itself produces structures that birds of different substance can lodge in; the post-harvest sifting will sort it.

*Another parable spake he unto them; The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.* (Matthew 13:33)

The inherited reading takes this as the kingdom's positive permeation of the whole world. But scripture's leaven-witness across the Tanakh and the apostolic letters is consistently corruption-substance. The Pesach removes leaven from the house:

*Seven days shall ye eat unleavened bread; even the first day ye shall put away leaven out of your houses: for whosoever eateth leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off from Yashar'el (Israel).* (Exodus 12:15)

The meal-offerings to Yahuah (the LORD) are explicitly without leaven:

*No meat offering, which ye shall bring unto Yahuah (the LORD), shall be made with leaven: for ye shall burn no leaven, nor any honey, in any offering of Yahuah (the LORD) made by fire.* (Leviticus 2:11)

The King himself identifies leaven as corruption-substance:

*Then said Yahusha (Jesus) unto them, Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees ... Then understood they how that he bade them not beware of the leaven of bread, but of the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.* (Matthew 16:6, 16:12)

And the apostle extends the same diagnostic to corruption in the assembly:

*Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump? Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened, for even Messiah (Christ) our passover is sacrificed for us.* (1 Corinthians 5:6-7)

The leaven is *hidden* in three measures of meal until the whole is leavened — proceeding quietly through the substance until the whole is transformed. Read against the wheat-and-tares parable before it and the dragnet after it, the leaven names the corrupting-substance the enemy works into the visible form of the kingdom-movement across the present age. The kingdom's true substance grows; the visible form is leavened from within. The institutional-Christian Sunday-Law leaven, the Pharisaic flesh-credential leaven, the Judaizer inclusion-gospel leaven, the antinomian grace-against-Torah leaven — all operate by the same hidden-and-spreading mechanism the parable names. The harvest sorts. Both parables continue the wheat-and-tares architecture; they do not contradict it.

## Why the parables — what was kept secret from the foundation

*All these things spake Yahusha (Jesus) unto the multitude in parables; and without a parable spake he not unto them: That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world.* (Matthew 13:34-35)

Matthew anchors the King's method to the psalmist's, citing Psalm 78:2. Psalm 78 is Asaph's recounting of Yashar'el's (Israel's) history from the Egypt-deliverance through the wilderness-rebellion to the Davidic establishment, structured as the *mashal* — the parable — of the nation's history that surfaces the meaning of the Father's dealings. The *things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world* are not new doctrines invented at the incarnation. The mysteries the parables reveal are the substance kept secret from the foundation that the prophets foretold and the King now reveals — the gathering of the scattered seed and the inauguration of the kingdom. The apostle names the same architecture:

*Now to him that is of power to stablish you according to my gospel ... according to the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began, But now is made manifest, and by the scriptures of the prophets ... made known to all nations for the obedience of faith.* (Romans 16:25-26)

The prophets foretold it; the King fulfills and reveals it; the disciples hear and understand it; the division between hearing and not-hearing blocks the others.

## The interpretation of the wheat and the tares — the seed-war named

*Then Yahusha (Jesus) sent the multitude away, and went into the house: and his disciples came unto him, saying, Declare unto us the parable of the tares of the field. He answered and said unto them, He that soweth the good seed is the Son of Adam; The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the world; and the reapers are the angels. As therefore the tares are gathered and burned in the fire; so shall it be in the end of this world. The Son of Adam shall send forth his angels, and they shall gather out of his kingdom all things that offend, and them which do iniquity; And shall cast them into a furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth. Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.* (Matthew 13:36-43)

This is the chapter's framework-major moment — the King's own interpretation, the load-bearing seed-war anchor in the gospels. He sends the multitude away and goes into the house with the disciples: the multitudes received the parables without interpretation per the cause-and-effect of hearing; the disciples receive the interpretation in the house. The two settings of the chapter enact the diagnostic the chapter teaches.

He names each element directly. **The sower is the Son of Adam** — the restoration of *son of man* preserving the lineage-weight of the seed-of-Adam line of promise; the Sower IS the King. **The field is the world** — not, against the inherited collapse, the church. The King says THE WORLD. The wheat-and-tares architecture operates across the world-territory where the kingdom is proclaimed, not narrowly within the visible assembly. **The good seed are the children of the kingdom** — the scattered seed coming home, the sheep who hear the shepherd's voice, the sons of Adam whose names were written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8; 17:8). They are wheat by lineage and by hearing — the seed of promise that Genesis traces forward. **The tares are the children of the wicked one** — the *zizania,* the bearded darnel that resembles wheat in early growth and only becomes distinguishable as the blade comes out (v.26). They are not wheat by substance; they were sown by the enemy from outside the original sowing.

This is the seed-war architecture scripture has carried from the beginning. From the start, two groups — the peoples of the earth (Genesis 1) and the seed of Adam (Genesis 2). The fallen angels' incursion brought a third — the giants and the corrupted line through their union with the daughters of the peoples of the earth. The dragon's seduction of Chava (Eve) brought a fourth — the corruption of the line of Adam from within. The sons of Belial appear inside the covenant community precisely because the seed-war runs within the line, not only outside it: Eli's sons Hophni and Phinehas were of the Levitical priestly line, physical seed of promise, yet sons of Belial (1 Samuel 2:12). Two of Yahuah's (God's) people can produce a son of Belial, as two brunettes can produce a red-headed child. The architecture is corroborated from the inside:

*In this the children of Elohim (God) are manifest, and the children of the devil: whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of Elohim (God), neither he that loveth not his brother ... Not as Qayin (Cain), who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother.* (1 John 3:10, 3:12)

*Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning, and abode not in the truth, because there is no truth in him.* (John 8:44)

When the King said *your father the devil* to specific individuals, it was not a rejection of the Yahudim (Jews) as a whole — it was the identification of sons of Belial operating within the covenant community, the same pattern scripture shows from the beginning. There is nothing new in the New Testament. The King says they are the children OF the wicked one — paternal source identified. There is a seed-distinction at the level of paternal source within the world-as-field; the tares were never wheat; the wheat was never tares.

This is not the racial determinism various systems have produced. The seed-distinction is real but operates within every people, not categorically along ethnic lines. The sons of Adam are scattered through all nations; the tares are also scattered through all nations. The harvest is the sorting; the present age cannot finally determine the sort by visible criteria.

**The enemy is the devil** — the wicked one of verse 19 and the enemy of verse 39, the serpent of Genesis 3, the satan of Job, the *murderer from the beginning* of John 8:44. **The harvest is the end of the world** — *synteleia tou aiōnos,* the end of the age, the dreadful day of Yahuah and the harvest-judgment that follows. It has not yet happened; the harvest is ahead, not behind. **The reapers are the angels** — the angels with the great sound of a trumpet gathering the elect from the four winds (Matthew 24:31).

The order is preserved from verse 30: the tares are gathered first and burned, then the wheat is gathered into the barn. The dreadful day burns the tares first, in the land and abroad; then the wheat is gathered into the wilderness of the people for the post-harvest sifting under the rod; then brought into the new covenant and grafted into the natural root of the fathers; then into the land as a kingdom of priests in the millennial reign. The angels gather *out of his kingdom* all things that offend and them which do iniquity — the same *anomia,* the lawlessness, that the *I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity* of Matthew 7:23 names. The furnace-of-fire imagery the framework reads in the eschatological judgment-architecture of Isaiah 66:24, Joel 2:3, and Malachi 4:1 — the specific eschatological structure is the load-bearing layer; the translation of the imagery across the two ages is held carefully, not pressed into the Christian eternal-conscious-torment doctrine at every syllable. And the righteous shine forth as the sun:

*And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament; and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.* (Daniel 12:3)

The *kingdom of their Father* is the eschatological destination toward which the gathered remnant is prepared — the Father, the Formless source; the kingdom prepared from the foundation of the world for the elect named in the Book of Life from the same foundation:

*Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world.* (Matthew 25:34)

The closing call — *who hath ears to hear, let him hear* — brackets the chapter. It opened at verse 9 with the cause-and-effect of hearing; it closes here with the same diagnostic, positioning the disciple and the reader to hear or not hear by the disposition already given.

## The hidden treasure and the pearl — the Buyer who pays the price

*Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field. Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking goodly pearls: Who, when he had found one pearl of great price, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.* (Matthew 13:44-46)

The inherited Christian reading collapses both parables into believer-finds-the-kingdom-and-pays-the-price: the kingdom is the treasure and the pearl; the believer is the man and the merchant; the believer's decision and cost of discipleship is the parable's substance. That reading inverts the cause-and-effect the chapter has just established at verses 10-17. Ask who the buyer is in the parable's own economy. The Sower of the wheat-and-tares parable, the One who interprets at verses 36-43, is the Son of Adam; the field is the world; the treasure hidden in the field is the substance hidden in the field-of-the-world the Sower has been sowing across the age — the children of the kingdom scattered through every territory of the dispersion (Ezekiel 37:21). The buyer who finds the treasure, rejoices, and pays the full price to obtain it is more naturally the same Sower. The Father, the Son, finds the elect-treasure, rejoices, and pays the full price. The Tanakh names the architecture:

*Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honourable, and I have loved thee: therefore will I give men for thee, and people for thy life.* (Isaiah 43:4)

The Father's paying the price for the elect-treasure is direct Tanakh-substance. The apostle names the operative moment the price was paid:

*Who, being in the form of Elohim (God), thought it not robbery to be equal with Elohim (God): But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.* (Philippians 2:6-8)

This preserves the cause-and-effect the chapter established with the *dedotai* — the giving precedes the hearing — and with the names written in the Book of Life from the foundation. The Father finds the elect; the elect did not find the Father by their own initiative; the hearing reveals what was always true. The inherited believer-buyer reading collapses into the inverted causation the framework rules out: *if you find the kingdom and pay the price, you obtain it.* The chapter's architecture says the opposite — the kingdom-citizens' supreme prioritization of the kingdom is the response flowing from a belonging already conferred, not the mechanism of the belonging. The treasure and the pearl name the Buyer's finding and paying first; the citizens' response-disposition is named in different vocabulary downstream of it:

*If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.* (Matthew 16:24)

## The dragnet — the sorting at the shore

*Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a net, that was cast into the sea, and gathered of every kind: Which, when it was full, they drew to shore, and sat down, and gathered the good into vessels, but cast the bad away. So shall it be at the end of the world: the angels shall come forth, and sever the wicked from among the just, And shall cast them into the furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth.* (Matthew 13:47-50)

The dragnet recapitulates the wheat-and-tares architecture from a different angle — two parables, two witnesses to one consummation. The wheat-and-tares used a field-and-harvest image; the dragnet uses a sea-and-net image; both end at the same post-harvest sifting. The net-image is broader: it includes the citizens' apostolic mission of casting the net widely, the *fishers of men* commissioning of Matthew 4:19. The disciples are the net-casters; the gathering of every kind happens through the apostolic proclamation in the present age. The sorting on the shore is the same sifting — *sever the wicked from among the just* — continuous with the sheep-and-goats judgment of Matthew 25:31-46 and the two-fold resurrection of Daniel 12:2-3.

The net is cast broadly because we do not know by sight which of the gathered are the citizens of the hearing-disposition and which are not. The proclamation is broad; the sorting is the Father's. And the *every kind* gathered does not erase the citizens' identity-distinction. The net gathers broadly; the sorting confirms the distinction. Both branches of the olive tree are Yashar'el (Israel); no non-Israelite is grafted into the tree by faith-confession alone. The net reaches into every territory of the dispersion; the sorting happens at the shore.

## The householder — things new and old

*Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto them, Have ye understood all these things? They say unto him, Yea, Lord. Then said he unto them, Therefore every scribe which is instructed unto the kingdom of heaven is like unto a man that is an householder, which bringeth forth out of his treasure things new and old.* (Matthew 13:51-52)

The disciples affirm they have understood, and the King names their vocation. The scribe instructed unto the kingdom — the disciple given to know the mysteries per verse 11 — brings forth out of his treasure things new AND old. The treasure is the kingdom-substance: the things new are the King's fresh revelation, the gospel of the kingdom now inaugurated; the things old are the Tanakh and the prophets, the covenant-substance from Avraham (Abraham) through Mosheh (Moses) through David through the prophets. The bringing-forth is the heart-Torah-walking life — the Word internalized through the new heart of Ezekiel 36:26-27, lived out in the present age.

This stands against the antinomian-Christianity collapse that has discarded the old — the Tanakh, the Torah, the prophets, the feasts, the Shabbat, the dietary substance, the sacred names, the seed-of-promise distinction — and kept only a flattened grace-gospel detached from its Tanakh-substance. The citizens' vocation is the keeping of BOTH new AND old. The new IS new precisely because it grows from and fulfills the old, not because it abolishes it. The structure is the same the King named in the Sermon on the Mount:

*Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.* (Matthew 5:17)

The scribe instructed into the kingdom holds both. The institutional pulpit's pattern of bringing forth the new without the old is the antinomian collapse the framework dismantles.

## The rejection at Natsareth — a prophet not without honour

*And when he was come into his own country, he taught them in their synagogue, insomuch that they were astonished, and said, Whence hath this man this wisdom, and these mighty works? Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Mariam (Mary)? and his brethren, Ya'aqov (James), and Yoses, and Shim'on (Simon), and Yahudah (Jude)? And his sisters, are they not all with us? Whence then hath this man all these things? And they were offended in him.* (Matthew 13:54-57)

The chapter closes with the rejection-disposition mirrored at the family-and-village register. The King returns to Natsareth (Nazareth) and teaches in the synagogue; the response is astonishment at the wisdom and mighty works combined with rejection at the source. The hometown knows the family background — the carpenter, the mother Mariam (Mary), the brethren named, the sisters present. The knowledge is real; the conclusion drawn from it — he cannot be the King because we know his background — is the seed-war pattern operating at the level where familiarity blocks recognition. The hometown is scandalized; the knowledge of the family background becomes the stone of stumbling, the same *skandalon* Isaiah named (the *stone of stumbling* and *rock of offence,* Isaiah 8:14) and the apostle named (*we preach the Messiah crucified, unto the Yahudim (Jews) a stumblingblock,* 1 Corinthians 1:23).

*But Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country, and in his own house. And he did not many mighty works there because of their unbelief.* (Matthew 13:57-58)

This is the Tanakh prophetic pattern — the prophets faced rejection at home: Jeremiah at his hometown of Anatot (Anathoth) (Jeremiah 11:21-23), the broader pattern of the rejected prophet (*Yahuah Elohim (the LORD God) of their fathers sent to them by his messengers ... but they mocked the messengers of Elohim (God),* 2 Chronicles 36:15-16). And the *he did not many mighty works there because of their unbelief* is not a limitation on his power; it is the cause-and-effect of hearing operating at the territorial level. The mighty works are credentialing-substance; the credentialing operates where the disposition to hear is present. The text says *because of their unbelief* — the unbelief is theirs, named as theirs, against any reading that would deny the people's agency in the not-receiving. The King's credentialing meets the disposition already set; the disposition determines the encounter; the encounter manifests the disposition.

The chapter that opened with the boat-pulpit teaching to the multitudes closes with the hometown-synagogue rejection. The division between hearing and not-hearing that opened the chapter closes it at the same level. The kingdom is sown; the field is the world; the wheat and the tares grow together until the harvest; the post-harvest sifting sorts; the hometown's rejection foreshadows the broader rejection at Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) the gospel will land in the chapters ahead. And the prologue of the gospel of John locks the architecture the chapter has been tracing the whole way:

*He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of Elohim (God), even to them that believe on his name: Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of Elohim (God).* (John 1:11-13)

Matthew 14 opens with the rejection-disposition mirrored at the political-system level — the death of Yochanan (John) the Witness at Herod's hand.
