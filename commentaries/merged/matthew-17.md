## The Formed unveiled on the mountain

Matthew 17 opens with the gospel's deepest window into the nature of the One who came in the flesh, and the lens the whole chapter turns on is the Formless and the Formed. Hold the architecture before the first verse. The Father is the formless infinite — the Source, the unseen, the One whom *no man hath seen at any time* (John 1:18). The Son is the Formed expression of the Formless drawn into form — the One who appeared to Avraham (Abraham) at Mamre, who wrestled Ya'aqov (Jacob) at Penuel, who spoke from the burning bush, who led Yashar'el (Israel) through the wilderness in the pillar of cloud and fire, who came down on Sinai and made the mountain shake. The God of every Tanakh theophany is the Son — the Formed Voice through whom all things were made.

*In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with Elohim (God), and the Word was Elohim (God). The same was in the beginning with Elohim (God). All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.* (John 1:1-3)

That Formed One came in the flesh and took the form of a servant. He did not begin at the mountain, and he did not begin at Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem) — he came *in* the flesh; he was not formed *at* it. The transfiguration is the moment the servant-form goes momentarily transparent and the disciples see the form of Elohim (God) it had been clothing all along.

## After six days — the Sinai pattern

*And after six days Yahusha (Jesus) taketh Kefa (Peter), Ya'aqov (James), and Yochanan (John) his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart, and was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light. And, behold, there appeared unto them Mosheh (Moses) and Eliyahu (Elijah) talking with him. Then answered Kefa (Peter), and said unto Yahusha (Jesus), Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Mosheh (Moses), and one for Eliyahu (Elijah). While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him. And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid. And Yahusha (Jesus) came and touched them, and said, Arise, and be not afraid. And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Yahusha (Jesus) only.* (Matthew 17:1-8)

The narrative-link is precise. Matthew 16:28 closed with the King's promise that *there be some standing here, which shall not taste of death, till they see the Son of Adam coming in his kingdom.* Six days later — the seventh day, in the count that includes the day of speaking — the King takes three of those standing-there up the mountain. The foretaste of the coming is what the three are about to see. The full coming in his kingdom remains future, carried forward to the second-coming-in-glory the prophets named at the dreadful day of Yahuah (the LORD); the transfiguration does not exhaust the saying. The transfiguration is the foretaste the saying promised.

The six-days-and-the-seventh resonance carries Tanakh substance. The Sinai pattern is the closest:

*And the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) abode upon mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days: and the seventh day he called unto Mosheh (Moses) out of the midst of the cloud. And the sight of the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Yashar'el (Israel). And Mosheh (Moses) went into the midst of the cloud, and gat him up into the mount.* (Exodus 24:16-18)

The cloud on Sinai for six days, the voice on the seventh, Mosheh (Moses) going up into the cloud — the architecture is the same architecture the disciples are about to walk into, now made visible at a deeper level. The One who spoke from the cloud at Sinai is the One standing on this mountain in flesh, with the Father's-voice from the cloud declaring his identity.

**The three witnesses.** Kefa (Peter), Ya'aqov (James), and Yochanan (John) — the inner circle. The same three are present at the raising of Yair's (Jairus's) daughter (Mark 5:37) and at Gethsemane (Matthew 26:37). The Tanakh standard for testimony is *at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established* (Deuteronomy 19:15). Three witnesses to what the eye cannot otherwise have seen and the mouth alone cannot otherwise have established. The Catholic-Petrine-papacy reading that elevates Kefa (Peter) alone to a primacy over the others does not stand against the text — the three witness together, and each of the three will bear apostolic testimony to what was seen. Yochanan (John) will write the gospel that opens *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth* (John 1:14). Kefa (Peter) will write *we were eyewitnesses of his majesty* (2 Peter 1:16). Ya'aqov (James) will die under Herod-Agrippa at Acts 12:2 carrying what he had seen on the mountain.

The mountain is unnamed. The traditional identification with Mount Tabor is conjectural; the textual emphasis is on the *high mountain apart* the Tanakh repeatedly names as the place of theophany — Sinai for Mosheh (Moses), Horeb for Eliyahu (Elijah) at *the still small voice* (1 Kings 19:8-13), Tsion (Zion) for the prophets — not on a particular geographic point. The disciples are taken to the high place set apart for the same reason the prophets were: the substance about to be displayed requires it.

## The veil pulled back

*And was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light.* The Greek is *metemorphōthē* — his form was changed in their sight. The veil of the flesh is pulled back. The Formed-essence the King had laid aside in the incarnation becomes visible on the mountain:

*Who, being in the form of Elohim (God), thought it not robbery to be equal with Elohim (God): But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men.* (Philippians 2:6-7)

The everyday-walking disciples have walked with the King for substantial time. They have seen him preach, heal, exorcise, walk on water, feed multitudes. They have not seen him in his glorified form. On the mountain, the veil is momentarily pulled aside, and they see what has been there all along — the Formed-essence the King has always been.

The face-shining substance is the Tanakh glory pattern, but with a decisive difference. When Mosheh (Moses) came down from Sinai, his face shone:

*And it came to pass, when Mosheh (Moses) came down from mount Sinai with the two tables of testimony in Mosheh's (Moses') hand, when he came down from the mount, that Mosheh (Moses) wist not that the skin of his face shone while he talked with him.* (Exodus 34:29)

Mosheh's (Moses') face shone by reflection. The glory had been near him, and the face caught it as a face catches sunlight. The shining faded, and the apostle Paul names the fading at 2 Corinthians 3:13. The King's face on the mountain does not shine by reflection. The King's face shines because the King is the source. The light comes from inside the King outward, not from outside the King reflected. The same kind of difference the prophet Daniel sees in his visions of the One who stands among the lampstands:

*I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire.* (Daniel 7:9)

*Then I lifted up mine eyes, and looked, and behold a certain man clothed in linen, whose loins were girded with fine gold of Uphaz: His body also was like the beryl, and his face as the appearance of lightning, and his eyes as lamps of fire, and his arms and his feet like in colour to polished brass, and the voice of his words like the voice of a multitude.* (Daniel 10:5-6)

And the vision the apostle Yochanan (John) will have on Patmos decades after he stood on this mountain:

*And in the midst of the seven candlesticks one like unto the Son of Adam, clothed with a garment down to the foot, and girt about the paps with a golden girdle. His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire; And his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace; and his voice as the sound of many waters. And he had in his right hand seven stars: and out of his mouth went a sharp twoedged sword: and his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength.* (Revelation 1:13-16)

The kaph-preserved *one like unto the Son of Adam* names the figure Yochanan (John) sees on Patmos — the same Son of Adam the disciple had seen transfigured on the mountain, now glorified beyond the foretaste, his countenance shining as the sun the way the King's face shone on the high mountain. The kaph honors the incarnation: he resembled mortal-man because he took on flesh, while remaining the Formed cloud-rider. The disciple Yochanan (John) is in both visions. The substance is the same substance. The transfiguration is the gospel's foretaste of what Yochanan (John) will see again in full on Patmos.

## Mosheh and Eliyahu — Torah and Prophets testifying

*And, behold, there appeared unto them Mosheh (Moses) and Eliyahu (Elijah) talking with him.* The Torah-witness and the Prophets-witness, standing together with the King. Mosheh (Moses) is the Torah-giver, the mediator of the first Sinai covenant, the prophet-pattern the King fulfills at Deuteronomy 18:15. Eliyahu (Elijah) is the Tishbite prophet, the restorer of the faith during Achav (Ahab) and Izevel's (Jezebel's) apostasy, the pattern-prophet whose return the close of the Tanakh foretells. The two whom the law and the prophets had pointed toward stand with the One they pointed at. This is the Tanakh itself testifying to the King's identity at the maximum-visible moment.

The Luke gospel adds what they were speaking of:

*Who appeared in glory, and spake of his decease which he should accomplish at Yerushalayim (Jerusalem).* (Luke 9:31)

The Greek word for *decease* is *exodos* — his exodus. The Torah-witness and the Prophets-witness speak with the King about the exodus he is about to accomplish at Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — his death, his resurrection, the leading-out of the gathered into their inheritance the way Mosheh (Moses) led the first generation out of Mitsrayim (Egypt). The two who stand with him understand what is coming. The disciples do not yet.

Hold this carefully. The forefathers who walked the covenant faithfully in their lifetimes — Avraham (Abraham), Yitschaq (Isaac), Ya'aqov (Jacob), Mosheh (Moses), David, the prophets, the faithful unnamed of every generation back — are asleep, awaiting the resurrection. The apostle names it directly:

*And these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise: Elohim (God) having provided some better thing for us, that they without us should not be made perfect.* (Hebrews 11:39-40)

The forefathers have not yet been made perfect; they await the first resurrection, when the dead in the Messiah rise and the gathered are made perfect together. Mosheh (Moses) died and was buried by Yahuah (the LORD) himself at Deuteronomy 34:5-6; his resting-place is the one Yahudah (Jude) 9 references in the dispute between Michael and the adversary over Mosheh's (Moses') body. Eliyahu (Elijah) was taken up alive in the whirlwind at 2 Kings 2:11 — the Chanok (Enoch) parallel of Genesis 5:24 (*Chanok (Enoch) walked with Elohim (God): and he was not; for Elohim (God) took him*). The two have different exit-patterns; both await the resurrection. The transfiguration is not the first resurrection. It is the vision in which Mosheh (Moses) and Eliyahu (Elijah) appear in glorified form for the testimony their appearance bears. Yahuah (the LORD) is able to show the forefathers in glorified form in a vision without that vision being the resurrection itself.

## Three tabernacles — the right instinct, the wrong architecture

*Then answered Kefa (Peter), and said unto Yahusha (Jesus), Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Mosheh (Moses), and one for Eliyahu (Elijah).* Kefa's (Peter's) instinct is right and his proposal is wrong, and the wrongness is in the architecture. The Mark gospel adds the diagnostic: *for he wist not what to say; for they were sore afraid* (Mark 9:6). Kefa (Peter) is afraid, and the afraid disciple reaches for the cultic pattern he knows. What he reaches for is Sukkot (Tabernacles) — the feast of Yahuah (the LORD) dwelling with his people in booths, the wilderness substance carried forward as the annual remembrance at Leviticus 23:33-43, and the eschatological substance the prophet Zechariah named for the Messianic age:

*And it shall come to pass, that every one that is left of all the nations which came against Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) shall even go up from year to year to worship the King, Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts), and to keep the feast of tabernacles.* (Zechariah 14:16)

Sukkot (Tabernacles) is the Messianic-age feast. Kefa (Peter) recognizes the substance of what he is seeing — the glory of the King displayed, the prophets standing with him, the cloud about to come — and reaches for the appropriate cultic form. The instinct is sound. The proposal is wrong in one respect: three tabernacles. The three figures are not co-equal. Mosheh (Moses) and Eliyahu (Elijah) are witnesses; the King is the substance the witnesses witness to. Three equal tabernacles would flatten the architectural distinction. The Father will correct Kefa (Peter) immediately.

## The bright cloud and the Father's voice

*While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.* The bright cloud is the *kavod* (glory) cloud of the Tanakh:

*Then a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) filled the tabernacle. And Mosheh (Moses) was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) filled the tabernacle.* (Exodus 40:34-35)

*And it came to pass, when the priests were come out of the holy place, that the cloud filled the house of Yahuah (the LORD), So that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud: for the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) had filled the house of Yahuah (the LORD).* (1 Kings 8:10-11)

The glory-cloud at Sinai, at the Tabernacle's dedication, at the Temple's dedication — and now at the transfiguration. The same substance. The Formless-Father's presence marked by the bright cloud, the way the presence of Yahuah (the LORD) has always been marked when it comes near. And the voice that speaks out of the cloud is the Father's voice — the same voice that spoke from the cloudy pillar at Numbers 11:25 and Psalm 99:7. The Formless speaks the Formless-Father's acknowledgment of the Formed-Son. This is the gospel's deepest window into the canon's older and simpler structure: the Father unseen in the cloud, the Son seen in glorified form on the mountain, the same Formed One of every theophany now standing in flesh. The fourth-century formula of three co-equal persons was built to guard the Son's divinity, and in reaching for that guard it stepped past the simpler thing the scene is showing — one Yahuah (God), Formless in source, Formed in expression, the Formless acknowledging the Formed before three witnesses. The divinity the formula wanted to protect is already here, and older than the formula.

The voice that spoke now is the same voice that spoke at the immersion at the Yarden (Jordan) at Matthew 3:17 — the same Voice, the same Witness. The Yarden-voice launched the public ministry; the transfiguration-voice reinforces it at the moment of the disciples' deepest confusion about the passion-trajectory.

**The combined citation.** The voice carries three Tanakh texts in one utterance.

*This is my beloved Son* — the substance of the second Psalm:

*Yahuah (the LORD) hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee. Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.* (Psalm 2:7-8)

The Davidic-royal-Son enthronement substance. The King is the Son of the second Psalm, the begotten-Son whose inheritance is the nations.

*In whom I am well pleased* — the substance of the first servant-song of the prophet:

*Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him: he shall bring forth judgment to the goyim (Gentiles). He shall not cry, nor lift up, nor cause his voice to be heard in the street. A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench: he shall bring forth judgment unto truth.* (Isaiah 42:1-3)

The Suffering-Servant substance. Matthew 12:18-21 had quoted this passage as the longest Matthean Tanakh-citation, applying it to the King's meek-and-not-striving disposition. The Father's voice now combines the Davidic-royal-Son enthronement substance with the Suffering-Servant election — the same King is both. The two strands of Tanakh-Messianic-prophecy that the rabbinic tradition often split into two figures of deliverance, a triumphal-Messiah and a suffering-Messiah, are revealed at the transfiguration to be the same Messiah. The royal-Son IS the chosen-Servant; the chosen-Servant IS the royal-Son.

*Hear ye him* — the substance of the prophet-like-unto-Mosheh promise:

*Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God) will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken... And it shall come to pass, that whosoever will not hearken unto my words which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him.* (Deuteronomy 18:15, 18-19)

The prophet whose words must be heard the way Mosheh's (Moses') words were heard. The apostle Kefa (Peter) himself will cite this verse, naming the King as the Prophet:

*For Mosheh (Moses) truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall Yahuah your Elohim (the LORD your God) raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you. And it shall come to pass, that every soul, which will not hear that prophet, shall be destroyed from among the people.* (Acts 3:22-23)

And Stephan (Stephen) will cite the same passage at Acts 7:37 before the council that stones him. The Father's *hear ye him* is the explicit corrective to Kefa's (Peter's) three-tabernacles proposal. Three equal tabernacles is wrong; one Son must be heard. Mosheh (Moses) and Eliyahu (Elijah) are witnesses; the King is the prophet to whom the witnesses point. The architecture the apostle to the Hebrews will state directly is being established on the mountain:

*Elohim (God), who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds.* (Hebrews 1:1-2)

## The fall, the touch, the Son only

*And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid.* This is the universal Tanakh-theophany response, not a failure of nerve. Yeshayahu (Isaiah) before the throne — *woe is me! for I am undone* (Isaiah 6:5). Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) at the river Chebar — *I fell upon my face* (Ezekiel 1:28). Daniel before the angel at the river Hiddekel — *I fell into a deep sleep on my face* (Daniel 10:9). Yochanan (John) on Patmos — *I fell at his feet as dead* (Revelation 1:17). The kingdom-citizen at the appearance of the holy substance falls on his face, undone.

*And Yahusha (Jesus) came and touched them, and said, Arise, and be not afraid.* The King's touch is the Tanakh pattern of the heavenly-messenger's touch after the prostration. *Then there came again and touched me one like the appearance of a man, and he strengthened me* (Daniel 10:18). *And he laid his right hand upon me, saying unto me, Fear not* (Revelation 1:17). The King occupies the position of the heavenly messenger in the vision-pattern because the King IS the One whom the heavenly messengers served. The pattern that prophets walked across the Tanakh — the vision, the prostration, the touch, the *fear not* — is the King's pattern in the flesh now reaching the disciples.

*And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Yahusha (Jesus) only.* The closing scene is the final correction to the three-tabernacles proposal. The witnesses have testified; the witnesses depart. The Son remains. *Yahusha (Jesus) only.* The substance the witnesses witness to is enough. The King is the tabernacle the Father has set among his people — the *eskēnōsen* of John 1:14, *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt (tabernacled) among us.* No second tabernacle for Mosheh (Moses) is needed; no third tabernacle for Eliyahu (Elijah) is needed. The King is the tabernacle. The disciples come down from the mountain with the substance compressed into one image they will not be able to articulate until after the resurrection.

## Eliyahu is come already

*And as they came down from the mountain, Yahusha (Jesus) charged them, saying, Tell the vision to no man, until the Son of Adam be risen again from the dead. And his disciples asked him, saying, Why then say the scribes that Eliyahu (Elijah) must first come? And Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said unto them, Eliyahu (Elijah) truly shall first come, and restore all things. But I say unto you, That Eliyahu (Elijah) is come already, and they knew him not, but have done unto him whatsoever they listed. Likewise shall also the Son of Adam suffer of them. Then the disciples understood that he spake unto them of Yochanan (John) the Immerser.* (Matthew 17:9-13)

*Tell the vision to no man, until the Son of Adam be risen again from the dead.* The Messianic-secret pattern continues from Matthew 16:20. The transfiguration cannot be told yet because the resurrection has not yet happened, and the vision's substance lands properly only when the resurrection-credential has authenticated the King. The vision is reserved for post-resurrection disclosure. Decades later, when Kefa (Peter) writes his second letter knowing his death is near, he tells it, and it lands as the foundation of the prophetic witness:

*For we have not followed cunningly devised fables, when we made known unto you the power and coming of our Lord Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ), but were eyewitnesses of his majesty. For he received from Elohim (God) the Father honour and glory, when there came such a voice to him from the excellent glory, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. And this voice which came from heaven we heard, when we were with him in the holy mount. We have also a more sure word of prophecy.* (2 Peter 1:16-19)

The order matters. The resurrection happens. The vision becomes proclaimable. The proclamation establishes the *more sure word of prophecy.*

The disciples' question — *why then say the scribes that Eliyahu (Elijah) must first come?* — comes from the eschatology of the last prophet at the close of the Tanakh:

*Behold, I will send you Eliyahu (Elijah) the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of Yahuah (the LORD): And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.* (Malachi 4:5-6)

The scribes had read this and concluded that an Eliyahu-figure would precede the Messiah's coming. The disciples have just seen the actual Eliyahu (Elijah) in glorified form, and they wonder how the scribal expectation works if the King is already here.

The King's answer carries both fulfillments. *Eliyahu (Elijah) truly shall first come, and restore all things.* The future-tense is the second-fulfillment substance — the Eliyahu (Elijah) of Revelation 11, one of the two witnesses who prophesy in the days of the great and dreadful day of Yahuah (the LORD), who shut up the heavens that it rain not (the Eliyahu pattern of 1 Kings 17-18), and are martyred in the streets of the great city. The restoration-of-all-things is the prophesied gathering of the scattered seed, the re-engrafting of the natural branches at the first resurrection, the kingdom of priests to the nations left alive after the day. Acts 3:21 names it *the times of restitution of all things, which Elohim (God) hath spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began.*

*But I say unto you, That Eliyahu (Elijah) is come already, and they knew him not, but have done unto him whatsoever they listed.* The past-tense is the first-fulfillment substance. Yochanan (John) the Immerser was the Eliyahu-pattern prophet at the first coming. The Matthew 11:14 statement — *and if ye will receive it, this is Eliyahu (Elijah), which was for to come* — is now restated explicitly. The Eliyahu pattern that ran in the Tanakh — the prophet who stood against the kings and the apostate priesthood, who confronted Achav (Ahab) and Izevel (Jezebel) at Mount Carmel and in the vineyard of Navot (Naboth) — recurred in Yochanan (John) the Immerser standing against Herod-Antipas and Herodias. The Herod and Herodias of Matthew 14 are the Achav (Ahab) and Izevel (Jezebel) of the first century, and the Eliyahu-figure who confronted them was killed for it the way the Tanakh Eliyahu (Elijah) was hunted for it. His beheading at Matthew 14:1-12 is the Eliyahu pattern reaching its first-coming fulfillment.

*Likewise shall also the Son of Adam suffer of them.* The King now ties his own coming passion to the Eliyahu-pattern prophet's martyrdom. The system that kills the prophets will kill the One the prophets pointed toward. The chapter just opened with the glory; it immediately re-grounds the disciples in the passion. The two are one motion. The glory the disciples just saw is the glory the cross opens onto.

*Then the disciples understood that he spake unto them of Yochanan (John) the Immerser.* The hearing-and-understanding the King had named at Matthew 13:13-15 operates here. The disciples hear and understand; the scribes hear and do not. The cause-and-effect of hearing is working at the disciple-level now.

## The demoniac boy and the mustard-seed faith

The three come down from the mountain to find the rest of the disciples surrounded by a multitude. While the three were seeing the foretaste of the coming, the nine were failing to heal a demon-possessed boy.

*And when they were come to the multitude, there came to him a certain man, kneeling down to him, and saying, Lord, have mercy on my son: for he is lunatick, and sore vexed: for ofttimes he falleth into the fire, and oft into the water. And I brought him to thy disciples, and they could not cure him. Then Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said, O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you? bring him hither to me. And Yahusha (Jesus) rebuked the devil; and he departed out of him: and the child was cured from that very hour. Then came the disciples to Yahusha (Jesus) apart, and said, Why could not we cast him out? And Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, Because of your unbelief: for verily I say unto you, If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you. Howbeit this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting.* (Matthew 17:14-21)

The father's petition is the Tanakh-petitioner pattern. *Lord, have mercy on my son.* The same opening the Canaanite woman of Matthew 15 used, the same opening the two blind men of Matthew 9 used. The Greek for *lunatick* is *selēniazomai* — moon-struck, the affliction associated in the Second-Temple period with the moon's cycles. The symptoms — *ofttimes he falleth into the fire, and oft into the water* — are the grand-mal-style seizures the Mark and Luke parallels expand at Mark 9:18 and Luke 9:39: the boy thrown down, foaming, gnashing the teeth, the demon attempting to destroy the child by fire and water. The affliction is demonic, operating through the natural faculties.

*And I brought him to thy disciples, and they could not cure him.* The disciples had been commissioned for exactly this. *And when he had called unto him his twelve disciples, he gave them power against unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all manner of sickness and all manner of disease* (Matthew 10:1). They had cast out demons before — the Mark and Luke parallels at Mark 6:13 and Luke 9:6 record the previous successes. The failure at this case is not categorical; it is at this particular case, for a particular reason.

*O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you?* The rebuke is wider than the disciples. The Greek is *genea apistos kai diestrammenē* — *faithless and twisted generation.* The phrase echoes Mosheh's (Moses') song against the wilderness generation:

*They have corrupted themselves, their spot is not the spot of his children: they are a perverse and crooked generation.* (Deuteronomy 32:5)

*And he said, I will hide my face from them, I will see what their end shall be: for they are a very froward generation, children in whom is no faith.* (Deuteronomy 32:20)

The wilderness generation's faithlessness reaches the King's day in the religious-system of the leadership and the broader disposition of the people. The rebuke is aimed at the *generation* in which the disciples are forming, not narrowly at the disciples alone. The disciples are inheriting the faithlessness; they must come out of it. The exodus the King is leading at Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) is the exodus from the unbelieving generation as well as from death itself.

*Bring him hither to me. And Yahusha (Jesus) rebuked the devil; and he departed out of him: and the child was cured from that very hour.* The King exorcises without difficulty. The substance the disciples could not move flows through the King without obstruction. The kingdom-power is the King's; it flows where the King wills it; the disciples' failure was not the failure of the King's power.

*Why could not we cast him out? ... Because of your unbelief.* The Greek word the King uses is *oligopistia* — little-faith. The same word he used to Kefa (Peter) on the water at Matthew 14:31, the same word he used about the disciples at the loaves at Matthew 16:8. The little-faith pattern is the disciples' chronic disposition — they have seen, but the seeing and the faith-substance do not always track together.

*If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you.* The mustard-seed substance was the parable of Matthew 13:31-32 — the smallest of seeds with explosive potential. The substance is qualitative, not quantitative. Faith of the right substance, even at small quantity, moves what cannot otherwise be moved; faith of the wrong substance, regardless of quantity, moves nothing. The disciples' little-faith here is small in quantity AND off in substance — they have been measuring the case against their own previous successes rather than against the King's authority that was the source of every success they ever had. The faith that operates in the kingdom-mission is faith in the King and in the Father who sent him, not faith in the disciples' own spiritual credentialing.

The mountain-moving is the Tanakh pattern of the visible-impossible becoming possible at the speaking of the kingdom substance. *Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea* (Psalm 46:2). *Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low* (Isaiah 40:4). *Oh that thou wouldest rend the heavens, that thou wouldest come down, that the mountains might flow down at thy presence* (Isaiah 64:1). The same Yahuah (the LORD) who moves the mountains at his presence is the One whose Spirit moves through the disciples' prayer and action when the substance is right. *Nothing shall be impossible unto you* — the kingdom-impossibilities have a different floor than the world's, because the kingdom is the present-incursion of the One who created all things and holds them by the Word of his power.

Hold what this is not. The inherited-Christian prosperity-gospel reading detaches *nothing shall be impossible* from the kingdom-mission and turns it into a name-it-and-claim-it formula for commanding material blessings into existence by sufficient faith. That is not what the King is saying. The failure that occasions this teaching was a failure in the kingdom-mission — the casting out of a demon to free an afflicted child. The faith-substance flows in the kingdom-mission, not in private prosperity acquisition. The apostle holds the architecture firmly: *and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not love, I am nothing* (1 Corinthians 13:2). Faith without love-substance has missed the kingdom's orientation.

*Howbeit this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting.* The qualifier teaches that some classes of demonic affliction require the deeper disposition of prayer and fasting for the kingdom-substance to flow, and that fasting remains a kingdom-substance discipline. The inherited-Christian collapse of fasting into optional-or-abolished does not stand against the text. The King fasted forty days at the beginning of his ministry. He taught the kingdom-citizens how to fast at Matthew 6:16-18 — *but thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; that thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret.* When the disciples of Yochanan (John) asked about fasting at Matthew 9:14, the King's answer was not that fasting was abolished but that his disciples would fast after the Bridegroom was taken from them. The apostolic assembly fasted at Acts 13:2-3 before sending Sha'ul (Paul) and Bar-Naba (Barnabas), and at Acts 14:23 before ordaining elders. Fasting continues; the King teaches its weight here.

## The second passion-prediction

*And while they abode in Galilee, Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, The Son of Adam shall be betrayed into the hands of men: and they shall kill him, and the third day he shall be raised again. And they were exceeding sorry.* (Matthew 17:22-23)

The second of three predictions runs through the gospel — Matthew 16:21, Matthew 17:22-23, Matthew 20:17-19. The threefold naming is deliberate, the way the prophets named coming events more than once so the witnesses could not later claim they had not been warned. The first prediction at Matthew 16:21 produced Kefa's (Peter's) active rebuke and the King's *get thee behind me, Satan.* The second produces *exceeding sorry* — passive grief now, not active rebuke. The third at Matthew 20:17-19 will produce the request of the sons of Zavdai (Zebedee) for thrones — the disciples will not yet have grasped the passion even when the King is about to walk into it. The disciples' progression across the three predictions is the long arc of learning the *must* of the passion.

*The Son of Adam shall be betrayed into the hands of men.* The Greek verb is *paradidotai* — *delivered-up,* the same word the Greek of Isaiah 53 uses through the Suffering-Servant passage. *He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter.* The Servant of Isaiah 53 is delivered-up through the entire passage, and the King names himself as the One who will be delivered-up in the same way. The Tanakh prophetic architecture is again the source of the *must.*

*And the third day he shall be raised again.* The resurrection-credential renamed. The sign of Yonah (Jonah) the King had announced at Matthew 12:40 (*for as Yonah (Jonah) was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of Adam be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth*) is the substance the prediction holds in front of the disciples. The exodus Mosheh (Moses) and Eliyahu (Elijah) were speaking with him about on the mountain has the third-day resurrection as its decisive credential. The disciples' *exceeding sorry* is the natural response of those who love him; the resurrection they will only be able to receive when they have walked through what the prediction announces.

## The children are free — and the King pays

*And when they were come to Kfar-Nachum (Capernaum), they that received tribute money came to Kefa (Peter), and said, Doth not your master pay tribute? He saith, Yes. And when he was come into the house, Yahusha (Jesus) prevented him, saying, What thinkest thou, Shim'on (Simon)? of whom do the kings of the earth take custom or tribute? of their own children, or of strangers? Kefa (Peter) saith unto him, Of strangers. Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto him, Then are the children free. Notwithstanding, lest we should offend them, go thou to the sea, and cast an hook, and take up the fish that first cometh up; and when thou hast opened his mouth, thou shalt find a piece of money: that take, and give unto them for me and thee.* (Matthew 17:24-27)

The chapter that opened with the glory closes with a half-shekel coin from a fish's mouth. The architecture is deliberate. The same King who stood on the high mountain with his face shining as the sun also pays the temple-tax for himself and Kefa (Peter) with a coin pulled out of the Galilee.

The tribute-money is the half-shekel temple-tax of the Torah:

*This they shall give, every one that passeth among them that are numbered, half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary... The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel, when they give an offering unto Yahuah (the LORD), to make an atonement for your souls.* (Exodus 30:13, 15)

The half-shekel is atonement-money. The Torah names it explicitly — *to make an atonement for your souls.* Every male over twenty gives it; the rich do not give more, the poor do not give less; the substance is the same atonement-substance for every covenant member. In Mosheh's (Moses') day, the half-shekel funded the Tabernacle's construction (Exodus 38:25-28). In Yoash's (Joash's) day, it funded the temple-repair (2 Kings 12:4-16). In Nechemyah's (Nehemiah's) post-exile reconstruction, the rate was adjusted to a third of a shekel (Nehemiah 10:32-33). By the first century the half-shekel had been re-established as the annual temple-tax, collected from every adult over twenty for the temple sacrifices, maintenance, and priestly service — the people's corporate atonement-substance. (After AD 70, Vespasian would redirect this collection to the *fiscus Iudaicus*, the Roman tax funding the Capitoline Jupiter temple in Rome — the atonement-money turned to the pagan system that destroyed the temple. But that lies in the future; at the time of Matthew 17 the tax still goes to the temple-service.)

The collectors approach Kefa (Peter), not the King directly. Kefa (Peter) answers *yes* on the King's behalf — perhaps because the King had paid in previous years, perhaps because Kefa (Peter) does not yet see the architecture the King is about to unfold. The King anticipates him at the house: *what thinkest thou, Shim'on (Simon)? of whom do the kings of the earth take custom or tribute? of their own children, or of strangers?*

The Tanakh precedent is clear. Kings do not tax their own sons. Custom and tribute flow from foreign peoples and subject populations into the king's house; the king's sons receive from the house, not pay into it. David's sons did not pay tribute to David. The architecture of monarchic tax-collection is universal across the ancient Near East — the children of the king are free.

*Kefa (Peter) saith unto him, Of strangers. Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto him, Then are the children free.* The King is the Son of the One whose temple this is. The half-shekel is the temple-tax. The Father's-Son does not pay the Father's-tax to the Father's-house — the Father's-Son is free of the obligation by the architecture of who the Son is. The King has just stood on the mountain hearing the Father's voice declare him the beloved Son; the same Son is the one the temple-tax is being demanded from. This is the same self-disclosure of categorical identity as the *Lord of the Shabbat* of Matthew 12:8 — the King is the Owner-Son of the temple, not a subject of the temple-tax.

The same architecture extends to the King's disciples — those whom the King has named brothers and to whom the Father is also Father by the Son's adoption. *I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my Elohim (God), and your Elohim (God)* (John 20:17). *For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren* (Hebrews 2:11). *If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed* (John 8:36). The children of the Father are categorically free of the temple-tax.

But the King does not claim the exemption. *Notwithstanding, lest we should offend them.* The Greek for *offend* is *skandalisōmen* — that we cause a stumbling-block, the same root the King had used at Matthew 16:23 of Kefa's (Peter's) rebuke. The kingdom-citizen does not throw the kingdom-exemption in the face of the still-unawakened system in a way that creates a stumbling-block to those who could otherwise come home. The disposition is the one the apostle Paul would later spell out across his letters:

*Wherefore, if meat make my brother to offend, I will eat no flesh while the world standeth, lest I make my brother to offend.* (1 Corinthians 8:13)

*Even as I please all men in all things, not seeking mine own profit, but the profit of many, that they may be saved.* (1 Corinthians 10:33)

*Let us not therefore judge one another any more: but judge this rather, that no man put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in his brother's way.* (Romans 14:13)

The King models the substance at the half-shekel: categorically exempt, voluntarily paying, lest we offend. The kingdom-citizens will inherit the disposition.

*Go thou to the sea, and cast an hook, and take up the fish that first cometh up; and when thou hast opened his mouth, thou shalt find a piece of money.* The miraculous substance is the Owner-of-the-sea's sovereign direction. The same King who walked on the water now directs a fish to bring up the precise coin needed for the precise tax for the precise two men. The Greek for *piece of money* is *statēr* — a four-drachma coin, exactly two half-shekels, the tax for the King and for Kefa (Peter) with no remainder. The substance is the Adamic dominion of the eighth Psalm:

*Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet: All sheep and oxen, yea, and the beasts of the field; The fowl of the air, and the fish of the sea, and whatsoever passeth through the paths of the seas.* (Psalm 8:6-8)

The Yonah (Jonah) pattern is also in view — *now Yahuah (the LORD) had prepared a great fish to swallow up Yonah (Jonah)* (Jonah 1:17). The Owner of the sea appoints fish to his purposes. At the great scale, a great fish swallows a fleeing prophet and delivers him to the city he was sent to. At the small domestic scale, a fish brings up a coin for the tax-payment of the King and his disciple. The same sovereign Owner of the sea.

Hold what the half-shekel atonement-substance pre-figures. *To make an atonement for your souls.* The King who is categorically exempt from the atonement-tax pays it for himself-as-categorically-free and for Kefa (Peter)-as-categorically-free. The substance models, at the small scale, what the King is about to walk at the cross-scale: the categorically-exempt One pays the atonement-substance that the categorically-bound owe. The half-shekel from the fish's mouth is the gospel's foreshadowing in miniature of the cross-payment in full. The disciples will not see the architecture clearly until after the resurrection; but the King is laying it down here for them to find later. *He needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself* (Hebrews 7:27). The half-shekel from the fish's mouth is the architectural rehearsal.

The chapter that opened with the King in glorified form on the mountain closes with him paying a half-shekel as if he were any other man in Kfar-Nachum (Capernaum). The two scenes are one motion. The glory is what the cross opens onto, and the cross is what the categorically-exempt One walks into for the sake of the categorically-bound. The next chapter will open with the disciples asking *who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?* — and the King will set a little child in their midst.
