## The kingdom inverts greatness — a child set in the midst

Matthew 18 is the King's fourth major discourse, and the lens it turns on is the kingdom's inversion of greatness. The world the disciples came out of measures greatness by position — who stands highest, who commands most, who is owed deference by whom. The King is building an assembly that measures it the other way: by the depth of the humility-substance, not the height of the position-substance. The chapter lays out the internal architecture of that assembly from one end to the other — disposition, protection, seeking, discipline, forgiveness — and it opens with the King answering a question about rank by setting a child in the center of grown men.

*At the same time came the disciples unto Yahusha (Jesus), saying, Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven? And Yahusha (Jesus) called a little child unto him, and set him in the midst of them, and said, Verily I say unto you, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven. Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, the same is greatest in the kingdom of heaven. And whoso shall receive one such little child in my name receiveth me.* (Matthew 18:1-5)

*Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?* The disciples come with the question that has been moving among them. Mark's gospel preserves what Matthew omits: *and he came to Kfar-Nachum (Capernaum): and being in the house he asked them, What was it that ye disputed among yourselves by the way? But they held their peace: for by the way they had disputed among themselves, who should be the greatest* (Mark 9:33-34). They have been disputing it on the road. They have just come down from the high mountain of the transfiguration where three of them saw the foretaste of the coming and the other nine failed to cast out the demon from the boy. The inner-circle three who were on the mountain have a thing to defend; the nine who stayed below have a thing to recover from. Both reach for hierarchical position. The disciples have imbibed substantial kingdom-substance over months of ministry; they have not yet shed the natural kingdoms' hierarchy-substance. The King will address it directly at Matthew 20:25-28 — *ye know that the princes of the goyim (Gentiles) exercise dominion over them, and they that are great exercise authority upon them. But it shall not be so among you.* Here at Matthew 18, he addresses it through a child set in the middle of them.

*And Yahusha (Jesus) called a little child unto him, and set him in the midst of them.* The King could have answered with words alone; instead he draws a particular child into the conversation and stands the child in the center of the gathered men. This acted-parable belongs to the Tanakh's prophetic vocabulary. Yeshayahu (Isaiah) walked naked and barefoot for three years as a sign against Mitsrayim (Egypt) and Kush (Ethiopia) (Isaiah 20:2-4). Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) was sent down to the potter's house to watch the vessel break and be reformed (Jeremiah 18:1-6). Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) was made to lie on his side for the iniquity of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) (Ezekiel 4:4-6), to eat his bread by weight, to bring forth his stuff at evening as one going into captivity (Ezekiel 12:1-7). Hoshea (Hosea) was commanded to take a wife of whoredoms and name his children Lo-Ammi and Lo-Ruhamah as the sign of the divorce-judgment against the northern house (Hosea 1:2-9). The acted-parable runs through the prophets. The King takes the child and sets the child in the middle as the visible substance of his answer.

*Verily I say unto you, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.* The Greek for *converted* is *straphēte* — *turned around.* The Tanakh substance is *shuv* — the turning back, the repentance the prophets named at the heart of the covenant-renewal. *And shalt return unto Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God), and shalt obey his voice* (Deuteronomy 30:2). *Turn thou me, and I shall be turned; for thou art Yahuah my Elohim (the LORD my God). Surely after that I was turned, I repented* (Jeremiah 31:18-19). *O Yashar'el (Israel), return unto Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God); for thou hast fallen by thine iniquity* (Hosea 14:1). The turning is the kingdom-entry condition. And the turning lands the disciple in the disposition of a little child.

Hold what the child-substance is and what it is not. The institutional-Christian spiritualization of childhood takes the verse as a sentimental gesture toward childlike innocence as an aesthetic — a soft, vaguely-Romantic image of children as morally pure beings teaching their elders by their unsullied presence. That is not what the King is naming. The Hebrew-Roots false-inclusion reading takes it as universal — anyone can become as a little child by simple willingness, and the kingdom is therefore open to anyone who adopts the disposition — and again the substance is collapsed. The King is naming four specific dispositions the child carries that the adult disciple is being called back to.

**The child's humility.** The child does not claim hierarchical position. The child has not yet learned to compete with siblings for primacy at the family table; the child has not yet learned to lobby the parent for preferment over peers. The child receives what the parent gives. The disciples who have been disputing on the road over who is the greatest have not yet shed the hierarchical substance. *Before honour is humility* (Proverbs 15:33). *For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones* (Isaiah 57:15). *To this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word* (Isaiah 66:2). The *anawim* — the humble-poor of the Tanakh — are the substance the Father dwells with. Greatness in the kingdom is measured by the depth of the humility, not the height of the position.

**The child's receptiveness.** The child trusts the parent's instruction. The child does not adjudicate the parent's teaching against the child's own prior frameworks, does not weigh the parent's words against the consensus of a peer-group and decide whether the parent is credible. The child hears and receives. The Pharisaic and Sadducean leaven the King had warned of at Matthew 16:6 was the leaven of the adult-system that adjudicates the King's teaching against its own prior architecture and rejects what does not fit. The child-disciple does not do that. *Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings hast thou ordained strength because of thine enemies, that thou mightest still the enemy and the avenger* (Psalm 8:2) — the King will quote this against the chief priests and scribes at Matthew 21:16 when they complain about the children crying *Hosanna to the son of David* in the temple. The child's receptive substance is the substance the Father has ordained to silence the systems that have closed against the kingdom.

**The child's dependence.** The child has no illusion of self-sufficiency. The child cannot feed himself, clothe himself, defend himself, or even direct himself; the child knows it. The disciple must come to the same recognition — that he is in the kingdom because the Father called him and the Son set him in the assembly, not because his own resources earned him the place. *My soul is even as a weaned child. Let Yashar'el (Israel) hope in Yahuah (the LORD) from henceforth and for ever* (Psalm 131:2-3). The weaned child has stopped grasping for what the breast cannot continue to provide; the weaned child rests in the mother's lap and trusts her for the next provision. The disciple is to be the weaned child resting in the Father's lap.

**The child's honesty.** The child has not yet learned to mask internal state with external performance. The Pharisaic counterfeit-righteousness pattern of Matthew 6:1-18 — the alms trumpeted, the prayer publicly displayed, the fasting with the disfigured face so as to appear unto men — has not yet set in. The child says what is so. The kingdom-citizen's *yea* is *yea* and *nay* is *nay* per Matthew 5:37; the child still operates that way naturally. The adult-system the disciples have absorbed teaches the opposite — say what gets results, posture for the position, manage the appearance. The kingdom-substance reverses the system.

*Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, the same is greatest in the kingdom of heaven.* The inversion lands. The disciples ask *who is the greatest?* and the King answers in terms of humility, not position. The teaching will be restated at Matthew 23:11-12 against the scribes and the Pharisees, and it runs into the apostolic witness: *Elohim (God) resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble* (1 Peter 5:5, citing Proverbs 3:34). *Humble yourselves in the sight of Yahuah (the LORD), and he shall lift you up* (James 4:10).

*And whoso shall receive one such little child in my name receiveth me.* The disciple is not only to become as a child; the disciple is to receive the children the Father sends. The substance is the same the King will name at the sheep-and-goats judgment of Matthew 25:31-46 — *inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.* The kingdom-citizen's disposition is tested at the level of how he treats those who cannot reciprocate, who have no power-substance to confer, who are easily overlooked. *In my name* — *epi tō onomati mou,* upon-my-name — names receiving on the King's authority, not on the disciple's preference. The disciple receives the child because the King stands behind the child, not because the child has earned the receiving.

## The millstone, and the warning to the systems

*But whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and that he were drowned in the depth of the sea. Woe unto the world because of offences! for it must needs be that offences come; but woe to that man by whom the offence cometh! Wherefore if thy hand or thy foot offend thee, cut them off, and cast them from thee: it is better for thee to enter into life halt or maimed, rather than having two hands or two feet to be cast into everlasting fire. And if thine eye offend thee, pluck it out, and cast it from thee: it is better for thee to enter into life with one eye, rather than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire.* (Matthew 18:6-9)

The King turns from the disciple's disposition toward the child to the responsibility for not causing the child to stumble. This is one of the heaviest warnings he issues in the gospel.

*Whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me.* The Greek for *offend* is *skandalisē* — to cause to stumble. The Tanakh *mikshol* — the stumbling-block — runs through the prophets as the act of placing a thing in the way of a walker that causes the walker to fall. *Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind, but shalt fear thy Elohim (God): I am Yahuah (the LORD)* (Leviticus 19:14). *Cast ye up, cast ye up, prepare the way, take up the stumblingblock out of the way of my people* (Isaiah 57:14). The kingdom-citizen who becomes the substance that causes the stumble has reversed the kingdom-disposition into its opposite.

The *little ones which believe in me* are not exclusively children-in-age. The substance extends to all believers in the vulnerable position — the new disciple who has just begun to hear, the awakening believer still coming out of the system they inherited, the believer without the credentials of the religious establishment who can be easily steered by the credentialed class. The Tanakh's pattern of the vulnerable protected runs through the Torah and the prophets. *Thou shalt neither vex a stranger, nor oppress him: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt. Ye shall not afflict any widow, or fatherless child. If thou afflict them in any wise, and they cry at all unto me, I will surely hear their cry; And my wrath shall wax hot* (Exodus 22:21-24). *Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow* (Isaiah 1:17). *A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is Elohim (God) in his holy habitation* (Psalm 68:5). The vulnerable have a particular standing with the Father; the wrath against those who exploit them is named directly in the Torah. The same architecture now operates around the little ones who believe in him.

*It were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and that he were drowned in the depth of the sea.* The Greek *mylos onikos* names the upper-stone of the grinding-mill — the heavy stone-wheel turned by donkey-power, several hundred pounds, by no human strength removable once tied. The picture is grotesque on purpose. The one who causes the believing little one to stumble is in a position where the millstone and the sea would have been the mercy. The substance is consistent across the gospels (Mark 9:42; Luke 17:1-2). The King is naming the seriousness with which the Father holds the offense against the vulnerable believer.

And the offense-substance is not narrowly the individual disciple's personal sin against an individual believer. It is also the structural, doctrinal substance the systems perpetuate against the believers in their care. Institutional Christianity's antinomian doctrine is the offense at the doctrinal level — the inherited pulpit teaches the little ones who believe that the Torah is bondage, that the law is the curse, that the believer is *not under law but under grace* in a register where grace cancels the standard rather than restores the walker. The little ones stumble in their kingdom-walk because they have been taught to reject the very substance the Spirit of Yahuah (the LORD) enables them to keep — the Spirit *will cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them* (Ezekiel 36:27). The Hebrew-Roots false-inclusion gospel is the offense at the lineage-collapse level — the doctrine teaches the little ones that any believer who Torah-keeps is thereby Yashar'el (Israel), regardless of bloodline, regardless of the prophetic-restoration architecture, and the little ones stumble at the seed-distinction the prophets maintain. The Pharisaic traditions-of-the-elders were the offense at the law-overriding level the King had dismantled at Matthew 15:1-9 — the Korban-evasion that voided the fifth commandment under the cloak of religious zeal. The Galatian agitators' circumcision-party was the offense at the inversion of the covenant order — telling the scattered seed they had to become Yahudim (Jews) by ritual flesh before they could come into a covenant that was already theirs by the Father's promise to Avraham (Abraham). The millstone-warning lands on the systems and on the leadership who perpetuate them. The little ones themselves are victims, not enemies. The architecture of the warning is system-aimed; the patience toward the deceived holds throughout.

*Woe unto the world because of offences! for it must needs be that offences come; but woe to that man by whom the offence cometh.* The King names the inevitability and maintains the responsibility together. The present age operates under the watcher-rebellion administration — *we know that the whole world lieth in wickedness* (1 John 5:19); the seed-war continues; the offenses come because the architecture of the fallen age guarantees they will. The inevitability does not excuse the man by whom the offense comes. The architecture is the one the prophet had named — *the earth also is defiled under the inhabitants thereof; because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant. Therefore hath the curse devoured the earth* (Isaiah 24:5-6). The earth is defiled by its inhabitants; the responsibility stays with those who do the defiling.

*Wherefore if thy hand or thy foot offend thee, cut them off, and cast them from thee.* The King now turns the warning inward. The same stumbling-block substance that operates between disciples can operate inside the single disciple. The hand and foot and eye that occasion the disciple's own stumble must be cut off, cast away, plucked out. The teaching parallels Matthew 5:29-30; the substance is hyperbolic. The King is not commanding literal self-mutilation; the substance is the kingdom-citizen's radical disposition against his own stumbling-substance, to be amputated rather than managed.

Hold what the institutional-Christian once-saved-always-saved doctrine cannot account for here. The cross does not cancel the disciple's responsibility for his walk. The cross opens the door home; the door remains real; the walker walks through the door and into the way the door opens onto. The walker who tolerates the hand or foot or eye that causes his own stumbling is walking against the door the cross opens. The substance is in continuity with what the apostle writes the Corinthians — *I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway* (1 Corinthians 9:27). The apostle who preached the grace of the cross also disciplined his own body for fear of becoming a castaway. The two are not in tension; they are one motion of one obedience.

*Cast into everlasting fire ... cast into hell fire.* The institutional-Christian doctrine of an eternal-conscious-torment hell has been read back into these phrases for centuries, and that read-back is what the Tanakh substance does not carry — the same read-back the King's *gates of hell* statement at Matthew 16:18 suffered, where *hadēs* names the grave-realm, not a medieval hell of unending torture. The word *gehenna* in verse 9 is the Greek transliteration of *gei ben-Hinnom* — the Valley of the sons of Hinnom, a specific valley running along the southern edge of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem).

*And he defiled Topheth, which is in the valley of the children of Hinnom, that no man might make his son or his daughter to pass through the fire to Molech.* (2 Kings 23:10)

The valley had been the place where the apostate kings of Yahudah (Judah) passed their sons and daughters through the fire to Molech — the abomination the prophets named as the substance the curse of Deuteronomy 28 came down upon. Yoshiyah (Josiah) cleansed the valley in his reformation, and from his reign forward it became the dump where the refuse of the city was burned; the fires burned continuously, the worms consumed what the fire did not. The prophets named the valley as the place where the judgment would land at the day of the LORD:

*Therefore, behold, the days come, saith Yahuah (the LORD), that it shall no more be called Topheth, nor the valley of the son of Hinnom, but the valley of slaughter: for they shall bury in Topheth, till there be no place.* (Jeremiah 7:32)

*And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh.* (Isaiah 66:24)

The gehenna the King names is the judgment-substance of the Tanakh's valley of Hinnom carried forward to the day of the LORD. The substance is real; the judgment is real; the warning is severe. What it is not is the medieval-Christian eternal-conscious-torment hell read back into the term — the doctrine that pictures the wicked maintained in conscious existence under torture without end as the Father's perpetual active judgment. The Tanakh's gehenna is a place of destruction, not of maintenance. The wicked perish in it; the worm consumes the carcases of the men that have transgressed; the substance is destruction, not perpetually-tormented souls maintained for the purpose. The inherited doctrine built on a Greek-philosophical soul-immortality assumption the Tanakh does not carry. The kingdom-citizen takes the warning seriously because the destruction is real — *it is better for thee to enter into life halt or maimed.* Life is the kingdom's substance, the resurrection-substance, the inheritance the prophets named. The maimed disciple entering the kingdom is incomparably better off than the whole disciple cast into the gehenna. The teaching lands as deliberate extreme to drive the disciple's disposition against his own stumbling.

## Despise not the little ones — the shepherd seeks the one

*Take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones; for I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven. For the Son of Adam is come to save that which was lost. How think ye? if a man have an hundred sheep, and one of them be gone astray, doth he not leave the ninety and nine, and goeth into the mountains, and seeketh that which is gone astray? And if so be that he find it, verily I say unto you, he rejoiceth more of that sheep, than of the ninety and nine which went not astray. Even so it is not the will of your Father which is in heaven, that one of these little ones should perish.* (Matthew 18:10-14)

The chapter pivots from the warning against causing the stumble to the protection extended over the little ones and the pursuit the shepherd undertakes. It is one motion. The same little ones the King warned the disciples not to offend are the ones whose angels stand in the Father's presence and who are sought when they stray.

*Take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones.* The Greek for *despise* is *kataphronēsēte* — to think-down-on, to hold in contempt. The disciples' natural disposition from verse 1 — the hierarchical question — was a disposition that despises what is lower on the hierarchy. The King names the kingdom-disposition as the opposite. Do not write off the new believer who cannot yet articulate what they know; do not write off the awakening disciple still coming out; do not write off the believer without the credentials. The disposition the King is forming pays attention to the very members the system overlooks.

*For I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven.* The Tanakh and Second-Temple guardian-messenger pattern. The Torah names the messenger walking before the people through the wilderness — *behold, I send a messenger before thee, to keep thee in the way* (Exodus 23:20). The Psalms name the charge over the kingdom-walker — *for he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways* (Psalm 91:11). Daniel names the angelic substances operating around the nations (Daniel 10:13). The apostolic assembly recognized it: when Kefa (Peter) was released from prison and came to the door, the gathered said *it is his angel* (Acts 12:15). The letter to the Hebrews summarizes it: *are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?* (Hebrews 1:14). The little ones have messengers; the messengers stand before the Father with continuous access; the Father's attention is upon the little ones. The disciple who despises the little one is despising a substance the Father attends to directly.

*For the Son of Adam is come to save that which was lost.* The kingdom's mission-statement in compressed form, restated in full at Luke 19:10 — *for the Son of Adam is come to seek and to save that which was lost.* The Greek *to apolōlos* — the perished, the cut-off. The substance reaches back to the King's own mission-scope at the sending of the twelve: *go not into the way of the goyim (Gentiles)... But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel)* (Matthew 10:5-6), and forward to the Canaanite-woman pericope: *I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel)* (Matthew 15:24). The lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) are the scattered seed of the prophets' restoration the King has come to gather. The Son of Adam — the seed of Adam, the seed of promise, the One in whom the lineage of Avraham (Abraham), Yitschaq (Isaac), and Ya'aqov (Jacob) reaches its fullness — has come to save the lost of his own.

*How think ye? if a man have an hundred sheep, and one of them be gone astray, doth he not leave the ninety and nine, and goeth into the mountains, and seeketh that which is gone astray?* The shepherd cannot leave the one. The ninety-and-nine are safe; the one is at risk; the shepherd goes after the one. The substance lands at two levels at once.

The first is the assembly's internal little ones in the chapter-context. The assembly-discipline procedure of verses 15-17 follows directly; the disposition the King is teaching is what drives the discipline. The brother who has trespassed is the strayed sheep; the procedure is the going-after-the-one to bring the one home. The discipline flows from the seeking, not the reverse.

The second is the canon-wide lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) mission-scope. The shepherd is the Tanakh's Yahuah (the LORD) who seeks the scattered flock:

*For thus saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD); Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out. As a shepherd seeketh out his flock in the day that he is among his sheep that are scattered; so will I seek out my sheep, and will deliver them out of all places where they have been scattered in the cloudy and dark day. And I will bring them out from the people, and gather them from the countries, and will bring them to their own land.* (Ezekiel 34:11-13)

*I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away* (Ezekiel 34:16). The prophet Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) names the same — *my people hath been lost sheep: their shepherds have caused them to go astray* (Jeremiah 50:6). And Yeshayahu (Isaiah) — *all we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and Yahuah (the LORD) hath laid on him the iniquity of us all* (Isaiah 53:6). The King is the Yechezk'el-shepherd in flesh, gathering what was scattered, seeking what was driven away. The kingdom-citizens' assembly-disposition is the same shepherd-disposition at the assembly-internal level; the King's mission-scope is the same shepherd-disposition at the canon-wide level. The Lukan parallel at Luke 15:1-7 applies the same shepherd-substance to the King's eating with publicans and sinners. Both applications are legitimate extensions; the King-as-Shepherd is the foundational substance, and the application extends to both scopes.

*Even so it is not the will of your Father which is in heaven, that one of these little ones should perish.* The Father's will is the seeking-will — *not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance* (2 Peter 3:9). Hold this against any reading that flattens it into a universalist determinism that overrides the cause-and-effect of hearing the King has taught since Matthew 13. The Father's will is not the Father overriding all into salvation regardless. The Father's will is the disposition that drives the shepherd into the mountains after the one, and the seeking gathers what was already the lost-sheep substance. The post-harvest sifting of Ezekiel 20:35-38 still operates; the wheat and tares of Matthew 13:24-30 still operate. The Father's will toward the little ones is the seeking, and the disciples are commanded to walk in the same disposition.

## The four steps, and the binding-and-loosing extended

*Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother. But if he will not hear thee, then take with thee one or two more, that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established. And if he shall neglect to hear them, tell it unto the assembly: but if he neglect to hear the assembly, let him be unto thee as an heathen man and a publican. Verily I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven. Again I say unto you, That if two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven. For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.* (Matthew 18:15-20)

The chapter's framework-major moment lands here. The King lays down the assembly's internal procedure for restoration when a brother has trespassed, and the binding-and-loosing authority first given to Kefa (Peter) at Matthew 16:19 is now extended explicitly to the assembly as a whole. The architecture of apostolic authority moves from the individual confessor to the corporate body.

**Step one:** *go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone.* The private confrontation. The Tanakh substance is the direct command of Leviticus 19:17 — *thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.* The Torah commands the direct rebuke as the alternative to harboring the grievance in the heart or gossiping it through the community. *If he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother.* The desired outcome is restoration. The procedure exists for the gaining-back of the brother, not for cataloging his offenses or bypassing to public shame. The substance is restoration-aimed at every step.

**Step two:** *take with thee one or two more, that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established.* The Tanakh evidentiary standard from Deuteronomy 19:15 — *at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.* The witness-standard the Torah named for capital and civil cases the King applies to the assembly's internal pastoral discipline. The witnesses establish the substance of the trespass and provide supplementary witness to the brother who would not hear the private confrontation; they also restrain the offended party, who cannot inflate or fabricate the charge without their participation. The Torah substance protects both the brother accused and the brother accusing. The apostle Paul applies the same standard to leadership discipline — *against an elder receive not an accusation, but before two or three witnesses* (1 Timothy 5:19).

**Step three:** *if he shall neglect to hear them, tell it unto the assembly.* The Greek for *assembly* is *ekklēsia* — the second explicit use of the word in the gospel after Matthew 16:18. The *ekklēsia* is the called-out-ones of the gathering of the scattered seed, in continuity with the Tanakh *qahal* — the assembly of Yashar'el (Israel) gathered at Sinai (Exodus 19:5-6), hearing the Torah at the renewal of the covenant (Deuteronomy 31:30, Joshua 8:35), praised in the Psalms (Psalm 22:22, cited Messianically at Hebrews 2:12). The *ekklēsia* the King is building is the present-age form of the prophetic gathering. The matter is brought before the gathered assembly so the assembly can speak its corporate witness to the brother.

**Step four:** *if he neglect to hear the assembly, let him be unto thee as an heathen man and a publican.* The Greek for *heathen man* is *ethnikos* — the man of the nations, outside the covenant-membership the assembly represents. *Publican* is *telōnēs* — the tax-collector. Hold what this step is and is not. The substance is not condemnation to permanent exclusion. It is the disposition-shift from internal assembly-member to mission-target. The unrepentant brother is positioned as one to whom the kingdom-message must be re-proclaimed from outside the assembly's internal fellowship — the very disposition the King had walked toward the publicans and sinners throughout his ministry (Matthew 9:9-13; *a friend of publicans and sinners*, Matthew 11:19). The internal fellowship is temporarily withdrawn pending restoration; the kingdom-witness toward the brother continues. The institutional-Christian collapse of this step into permanent shunning and condemnation is what the King's own mission-disposition rules out. Restoration remains the goal at every step, including this one.

*Whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven.* The authority first given to Kefa (Peter) alone at Matthew 16:19 is now extended to the assembly. Binding and loosing in rabbinic vocabulary was the decision-making about what the Torah forbids and permits — *asar* (bind) and *hattir* (loose). The Pharisaic system had used this authority to construct the fence-around-Torah that voided the Torah at points; the King's extension of the authority to the apostolic assembly is the explicit transfer of the steward-substance from the corrupt stewards to the apostolic stewards. The keys derive from Isaiah 22:22 — *and the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder; so he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open* — now operating through the apostolic assembly as the stewards of the kingdom-substance.

The locus-classicus of the apostolic binding-and-loosing in action is the Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) Council of Acts 15. The question before the assembly was the scattered seed living as Gentiles among the nations who were returning to the King — and the circumcision-party's demand that these returning seed be circumcised after the manner of Mosheh (Moses) before they could be saved. The council met, and the decision was rendered in the apostolic letter: *for it seemed good to the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit), and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things; That ye abstain from meats offered to idols, and from blood, and from things strangled, and from fornication: from which if ye keep yourselves, ye shall do well* (Acts 15:28-29). The assembly bound four substances and loosed the rest of the Pharisaic tradition the circumcision-party had been adding. The binding and loosing was the assembly speaking the Torah as the King restored it against the corruption being added. The apostolic assembly exercises the same authority again across the letters — Sha'ul (Paul) through 1 Corinthians 5 against the man with his father's wife and again at 2 Corinthians 2:5-11 when repentance comes; Sha'ul (Paul) across Galatians against the agitators; Yochanan (John) through the seven letters of Revelation 2-3.

Hold what the binding-and-loosing-extended is and is not. The Roman-Catholic magisterial-authority reading takes it as the church's authority to define doctrine binding on the conscience independent of and even against scripture — the assembly elevated above the Torah-substance it stewards. The substance rules this out. The apostolic binding and loosing operates in continuity with the Torah-substance, not against it. The apostles are stewards of what the King the master of the house has given; they administer the kingdom-substance, not innovate it. The papal-primacy concentration of binding and loosing in Kefa (Peter) as the first pope is what verse 18 makes impossible — the very same authority was extended to the assembly as a whole, not concentrated in Kefa (Peter) alone and his successors.

*If two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven.* The two-or-three-witnesses standard of the discipline procedure carries over into the corporate-prayer substance. Hold what this is not. The institutional-Christian prayer-meeting reading turns it into a generic formula for prayer-power — gather two or three, agree on the request, the request is granted regardless of its substance. The verse cannot bear that. The substance is bounded by what the binding and loosing stewards: the Torah-substance the King has restored. The two or three agreeing on what the King's substance commends is what the Father's will accomplishes; the substance is in continuity with the apostolic restatement — *and this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us* (1 John 5:14).

*For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.* The King's presence with the corporate substance. The Tanakh precedent is Malachi 3:16 — *then they that feared Yahuah (the LORD) spake often one to another: and Yahuah (the LORD) hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared Yahuah (the LORD), and that thought upon his name.* The kingdom-citizens speaking to one another in the fear of Yahuah (the LORD) was already a substance the Father attended to; the King now names his own personal presence with the gathered two or three. *In my name* — into-my-name — is the substance the gathering is around, not merely the formula recited at the beginning. Where that substance is, the King is in the midst. Hold this against the institutional-Christian individualistic reading that treats verse 20 as a generic promise of the King's presence with the lone believer who prays alone. The substance is the corporate gathering in the King's name; the two or three gathered is the kingdom-assembly in its smallest faithful form, and the King's presence sanctifies the smallest faithful form as fully as the largest.

## Seventy times seven — Lamekh inverted

*Then came Kefa (Peter) to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.* (Matthew 18:21-22)

Kefa (Peter) has been listening through the discipline-procedure, which assumed a brother who sins, a confrontation, a possible restoration. He presses it on the personal level: *how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him?* And, already accustomed to the kingdom-substance exceeding the system-substance, he proposes a number he thinks generous — *till seven times?* The rabbinic tradition of the day taught that forgiveness was due three times; on the fourth offense the offended party was free to refuse. Kefa (Peter) more than doubles the rabbinic standard. He thinks he is being generous.

*I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.* The substance does not turn on the arithmetic. The Tanakh substance the King is citing is what the architecture lands on — Lamekh's (Lamech's) song five generations from Adam in the line of Cain:

*And Lamekh (Lamech) said unto his wives, Adah and Tsillah, Hear my voice; ye wives of Lamekh (Lamech), hearken unto my speech: for I have slain a man to my wounding, and a young man to my hurt. If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamekh (Lamech) seventy and sevenfold.* (Genesis 4:23-24)

Lamekh's (Lamech's) boast is escalated vengeance. Cain had killed Hevel (Abel), and Yahuah (the LORD) had set a mark on Cain promising sevenfold vengeance on anyone who killed him (Genesis 4:15). Lamekh (Lamech) hears about that protection-clause and announces his own version — *truly Lamekh (Lamech) seventy and sevenfold.* The Cainite line that began with the murder of a brother has now escalated the vengeance through five generations to the point where Lamekh (Lamech) is killing young men over wounds and hurts and announcing unlimited retaliation as his glory. This is the seed-war line operating in full substance — the same line that produced the inventions of the arts and metallurgy the Tanakh names as the Cainite civilization (Genesis 4:21-22) produces Lamekh's (Lamech's) escalating-vengeance song in the same generation.

The King's substance inverts Lamekh's (Lamech's) exactly. *If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamekh (Lamech) seventy and sevenfold* becomes *until seventy times seven* — the same numerical form, the opposite substance. Lamekh's (Lamech's) seventy-and-sevenfold vengeance becomes the King's seventy-times-seven forgiveness. The seed-war line's escalation is inverted into the kingdom line's forgiveness. The disciple does not count. The kingdom-citizen who counts the offenses forgiven against him as a budget that can run out has reverted to the Lamekh-substance under a cosmetic overlay of forgiveness-language. The kingdom-citizen who does not count operates in the disposition the King is forming.

The substance is in continuity with the Tanakh's pattern of the Father's forgiveness. *Yahuah (the LORD) is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy... As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us* (Psalm 103:8, 12). *Who is an El (God) like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity, and passeth by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage?... thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea* (Micah 7:18-19). *I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins* (Isaiah 43:25). The disciple who has received the seventy-times-seven from the Father extends the seventy-times-seven to the brother. The King is about to make the architecture explicit.

## The unforgiving servant — the grace that can be lost

*Therefore is the kingdom of heaven likened unto a certain king, which would take account of his servants. And when he had begun to reckon, one was brought unto him, which owed him ten thousand talents. But forasmuch as he had not to pay, his lord commanded him to be sold, and his wife, and children, and all that he had, and payment to be made. The servant therefore fell down, and worshipped him, saying, Lord, have patience with me, and I will pay thee all. Then the lord of that servant was moved with compassion, and loosed him, and forgave him the debt. But the same servant went out, and found one of his fellowservants, which owed him an hundred pence: and he laid hands on him, and took him by the throat, saying, Pay me that thou owest. And his fellowservant fell down at his feet, and besought him, saying, Have patience with me, and I will pay thee all. And he would not: but went and cast him into prison, till he should pay the debt. So when his fellowservants saw what was done, they were very sorry, and came and told unto their lord all that was done. Then his lord, after that he had called him, said unto him, O thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all that debt, because thou desiredst me: shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee? And his lord was wroth, and delivered him to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due unto him. So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.* (Matthew 18:23-35)

The parable lays the architecture under Kefa's (Peter's) question and the seventy-times-seven answer.

*Ten thousand talents.* The magnitude is staggering. A single talent in the first century is roughly 6,000 denarii; a denarius is roughly a day's wage; ten thousand talents is approximately sixty million denarii — roughly two hundred thousand years of a laborer's wages. The deliberate hyperbole is on purpose. The substance is impossibility of repayment, not literal accounting. The disciple's sin-debt before the Father is what the ten thousand talents represents — a debt the disciple cannot pay, not by labor, not by penance, not by the fullness of his life. The architecture of impossibility is the architecture of grace; the substance the cross will pay is the substance no human labor can.

*Lord, have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.* Hold the servant's petition carefully. He does not ask for cancellation; he asks for time to pay. The petition assumes his ability to discharge the debt given enough patience. It is the petition of the man who does not yet understand the magnitude of his own debt. The lord could have granted it strictly — given the time, watched him fail, returned the debt to its original status. The lord does not.

*Then the lord of that servant was moved with compassion, and loosed him, and forgave him the debt.* The lord grants more than the petition. The Greek for *moved with compassion* is *splanchnistheis* — moved in the bowels, the deep visceral substance the Tanakh-Father exercises toward his people. *And Elohim (God) heard their groaning, and Elohim (God) remembered his covenant with Avraham (Abraham), with Yitschaq (Isaac), and with Ya'aqov (Jacob)* (Exodus 2:24). The compassion moves the lord beyond the bounds of the petition into the bounds of his own mercy. He forgives the debt the servant had only asked time to pay. The grace exceeds the petitioner's request. This is the kingdom's grace.

The Tanakh anchor under the lord's act is the Jubilee. The Torah commands the release of debts every seventh year and the great release in the fiftieth year, when debts are canceled, slaves freed, and inheritances returned to the family-lines from which they had been alienated:

*And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof: it shall be a jubile unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family.* (Leviticus 25:10)

The Jubilee is debt-release at the appointed time. What the lord performs is the Jubilee compressed into a single transaction — the unpayable debt forgiven in the moment of the petitioner's appeal. The King is teaching the kingdom-Jubilee, the Father's act of releasing the unpayable debt of the disciples through the cross-substance the King is about to walk into. The disciples will not see the architecture clearly until after the resurrection; the King lays it down here as the parable they will return to.

*But the same servant went out, and found one of his fellowservants, which owed him an hundred pence.* A hundred denarii — roughly a hundred days' wages. The debt is real and not trivial. But it is roughly six-hundred-thousand-times less than the debt the lord had just forgiven the first servant. The architecture depends on the ratio. The first servant has been forgiven what he could not pay; he immediately demands what his fellow servant could potentially pay.

*And he laid hands on him, and took him by the throat, saying, Pay me that thou owest.* The choking grip is the violence-substance of the Cainite line operating now between two who have both been set in the king's house. The first servant has been positioned by the lord's grace; he operates against the fellow servant in the disposition of the unforgiving creditor.

*And his fellowservant fell down at his feet, and besought him, saying, Have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.* The petition mirrors the first servant's petition word for word — the same words, the same plea for time to pay. The parable presses the parallel hard. The first servant is hearing in the fellow servant's mouth the very petition he himself had just spoken to the lord — and refusing the petition the lord had honored. *And he would not: but went and cast him into prison, till he should pay the debt.* The kingdom-substance the first servant received does not flow through him to the fellow servant. It stops at his own substance.

*Then his lord, after that he had called him, said unto him, O thou wicked servant.* The lord's charge is direct. The first servant is *wicked* — Greek *ponēre,* the same word the King used for the wicked one who sows the tares (Matthew 13:38-39). The substance the first servant has shown is the seed-war substance the lord's grace had momentarily suspended; the lord now names it for what it is. *And his lord was wroth, and delivered him to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due unto him.* The lord revokes. The debt once forgiven is restored to debt-status; the first servant is now required to pay what he had been forgiven. The remission comes back upon him because the remission did not actually transform him. The substance the grace was given to form did not form.

Hold what this is against the institutional-Christian once-saved-always-saved doctrine. The doctrine teaches that the substance the cross paid cannot be revoked under any subsequent disciple-walk — once received, the disciple is permanently secured regardless of how he walks. The substance does not stand against the text. The unforgiving-servant parable is the most direct articulation in the gospels of the architecture that the substance the grace has given can be lost. The lord forgave; the lord was wroth; the lord delivered the same servant who had been forgiven to the tormentors. The forgiveness given is reversed when the disposition the forgiveness was given to form does not form. The cross opened the door home; the door is real; the walker walks through the door and into the way it opens onto. The disciple who does not walk the substance is the disciple whose grace has not formed him. The substance is in continuity with the King's own teaching elsewhere — *but if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses* (Matthew 6:15) — and with the apostolic witness: *he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy* (James 2:13); *forbearing one another, and forgiving one another... even as Messiah (Christ) forgave you, so also do ye* (Colossians 3:13); *be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as Elohim (God) for Messiah's (Christ's) sake hath forgiven you* (Ephesians 4:32).

*So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.* The chapter's closing word. The conditional is direct — *if ye from your hearts forgive not.* The Greek *apo tōn kardiōn hymōn* — from your hearts. The substance must be of the heart, not of the mouth or the public display. This is the heart-Torah of the new covenant the prophets named:

*But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Yashar'el (Israel); After those days, saith Yahuah (the LORD), I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people.* (Jeremiah 31:33)

*A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:26-27)

The Torah is to be in the disciples' inward parts, written on the hearts, with the new heart of flesh given by the Father and the Spirit causing the walking. Forgiveness from the heart is the new-heart-substance operating; forgiveness of the mouth without the heart is the non-substance the Father responds to with the revocation of the forgiveness given. The chapter closes on the heart-architecture of the new covenant the King is bringing.

The chapter as one motion: the kingdom-citizens' greatness as child-substance, the millstone-warning against offending the little ones with the sin-amputation under the gehenna-judgment, the despise-not-the-little-ones warning and the lost-sheep parable applied at both the assembly-internal level and the canon-wide lost-sheep mission-scope, the four-step discipline procedure with the binding and loosing extended from Kefa (Peter) alone to the assembly, and the seventy-times-seven inversion of Lamekh's (Lamech's) vengeance with the unforgiving-servant parable closing on the heart-Torah of the new covenant. The internal architecture of the King's assembly is laid out from disposition through protection through seeking through discipline through forgiveness. The next chapter will open with the King setting out from Galil (Galilee) toward the coasts of Yahudah (Judea) beyond the Yarden (Jordan) — the long approach to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) the gospel has been pointing toward since Matthew 16:21 — and the Pharisees will meet him with the divorce-question.
