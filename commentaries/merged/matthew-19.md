## Reading the Torah architecturally — the road to Yerushalayim

Matthew 19 opens the King's long approach to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), and the lens the chapter turns on is how the kingdom-citizen reads the Torah. When the Pharisees come at him with the divorce-question, the King does not pick a side in their case-law debate; he reaches behind the case-law to the Father's will from the beginning, and in doing so he teaches the assembly to read the Torah in layers — the creation-ordinance that is the Father's will from the beginning, the covenant-instruction Sinai delivers, and the concessions the covenant-instruction makes to the hardness of heart the community carries. That same architectural reading governs the whole chapter as it lays out the kingdom-citizens' substance toward four things the inherited systems have most thoroughly confused: marriage, children, possessions, and reward.

*And it came to pass, that when Yahusha (Jesus) had finished these sayings, he departed from Galil (Galilee), and came into the coasts of Yahudah (Judaea) beyond the Yarden (Jordan); and great multitudes followed him; and he healed them there.* (Matthew 19:1-2)

*When Yahusha (Jesus) had finished these sayings.* The Matthean discourse-closing formula. The fourth of the King's five major discourses has closed at the end of Matthew 18; the same formula closes the first discourse at Matthew 7:28, the second at Matthew 11:1, the third at Matthew 13:53, and the fifth at Matthew 26:1 — the deliberate structuring of the gospel as a five-discourse architecture under the new-Mosheh (Moses) pattern. Mosheh (Moses) gave the people five books; the King delivers five major discourses as the substance the kingdom-citizens carry into the assembly. The fourth-discourse close sets the King in motion toward the final discourse that will be delivered in the precincts of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) itself across Matthew 23-25.

*He departed from Galil (Galilee), and came into the coasts of Yahudah (Judaea) beyond the Yarden (Jordan).* The geographic shift is the long-foretold motion toward Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) the King has been pointing toward since the first passion-prediction at Matthew 16:21. The Galil (Galilee) mission is now closing; the trajectory toward the cross is opening. *Beyond the Yarden (Jordan)* names the Trans-Yarden Peraean territory, the eastern-bank region. The customary pilgrimage-route for Galilean believers heading to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) for the feasts ran the eastern bank to avoid the Samaritan territory along the western-bank's direct route. The King takes the customary route, and the route is itself a Tanakh-substance.

The entry from beyond the Yarden (Jordan) frames the motion. The original entry into the inheritance ran from beyond the Yarden (Jordan) across the river into the land — Mosheh (Moses) saw the land from Mount Nebo on the eastern bank and died there; Yahoshua (Joshua) led the people across the Yarden (Jordan) on dry ground (Joshua 3:14-17), set up the twelve stones at Gilgal (Joshua 4:20), and began the inheritance. The King now traverses the same territory in the long final pilgrimage. The substance the prophet pointed toward at Hosea 2:14-15 — *behold, I will allure her, and bring her into the wilderness, and speak comfortably unto her. And I will give her her vineyards from thence, and the valley of Achor for a door of hope* — is the substance the gathering of the scattered seed will pass through. The King is the true Yahoshua (Joshua); the name *Yahusha* is the same name as *Yahoshua* — Yahuah saves. He is gathering, and the geographic substance bears witness to the architecture.

*And great multitudes followed him; and he healed them there.* The Peraean multitudes gather; the healing continues the Isaiah-35 Messianic credential — *then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped. Then shall the lame man leap as an hart* (Isaiah 35:5-6). The Peraean territory, like the Galil of the goyim (Gentiles) of Matthew 4:15-16, was a mixed-population region with substantial scattered-tribes' representation. The kingdom-mission continues to land where the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) are gathering home.

## The divorce-question — the King reaches behind Mosheh

*The Pharisees also came unto him, tempting him, and saying unto him, Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife for every cause? And he answered and said unto them, Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh? Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore Elohim (God) hath joined together, let not man put asunder. They say unto him, Why did Mosheh (Moses) then command to give a writing of divorcement, and to put her away? He saith unto them, Mosheh (Moses), because of the hardness of your hearts, suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so. And I say unto you, Whosoever shall put away his wife, except it be for fornication, and shall marry another, committeth adultery: and whoso marrieth her which is put away doth commit adultery.* (Matthew 19:3-9)

The Pharisees meet the King with a question that is, on its surface, about Mosaic case-law and, beneath the surface, a trap with two prongs. The trap fails because the King answers neither side of it and moves the substance behind both sides to the place it actually lives.

*Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife for every cause?* The Greek *kata pasan aitian* — *according to every cause* — names the rabbinic-school debate of the day over Deuteronomy 24:1's *some uncleanness* (Hebrew *ervat davar* — *nakedness of thing*). The school of Shammai read it strictly: *some uncleanness* meant substantial sexual impurity, and divorce was permitted only on that ground. The school of Hillel read it permissively: *some uncleanness* was anything the husband found displeasing, and the famous example was *even if she burnt his food.* Hillel produced an effective regime of any-cause divorce; Shammai restricted it. The Pharisees hope the King will land on one side and alienate the followers of the other, and hope to land him against Mosheh (Moses) himself if he condemns the divorce-substance Deuteronomy 24:1 permits. Two prongs.

*Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female.* The King refuses the Hillel-Shammai frame and appeals behind Mosheh (Moses) to Bereshit (Genesis) 1-2:

*So Elohim (God) created man in his own image, in the image of Elohim (God) created he him; male and female created he them.* (Genesis 1:27)

*Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.* (Genesis 2:24)

The two substances the King names are pre-Sinai, pre-Mosheh (Moses), pre-the-hardness-of-heart-permission of Deuteronomy 24:1 — they are the Father's will from the beginning, before the covenant-architecture had to engage with human hardness. *Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore Elohim (God) hath joined together, let not man put asunder.* The one-flesh substance the Father instituted at the beginning is a substance the Father himself joined; the Father's joining is not a thing human authority can undo at preference. The substance is creation-ordinance, not case-law.

The King's method here is the architectural reading the kingdom-citizen carries. The Tanakh substance is layered, and the layers are not all the same kind. The creation-ordinance of Bereshit (Genesis) 1-2 is the Father's will from the beginning; the Sinai Torah carries the covenant-instruction; and within the Sinai Torah are particular substances the Torah itself names as concessions to the hardness of heart the community carries. The kingdom-citizen distinguishes these layers — and that is precisely what the King does next.

*Why did Mosheh (Moses) then command to give a writing of divorcement, and to put her away?* The Pharisees push back, detecting the architectural move, and try to force the conflict with Mosheh (Moses). They re-frame Deuteronomy 24:1 from *permission* to *command* — Mosheh (Moses) *commanded* the bill of divorcement, they say, so the King's appeal to the creation-ordinance must come against Mosheh's (Moses') command. But Deuteronomy 24:1 was never a command to divorce. The verse is grammatically a protasis-apodosis — *when a man hath taken a wife... and it come to pass that she find no favour in his eyes... then let him write her a bill of divorcement* — regulating what must happen *if* a man divorces, not commanding that any man divorce. The Pharisees push the Torah harder than the Torah says.

*Mosheh (Moses), because of the hardness of your hearts, suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so.* The Greek *epetrepsen* — *permitted, suffered* — names what Mosheh (Moses) actually did. The bill of divorcement was a permission, not a command, given *because of the hardness of your hearts.* The community at Sinai and across the wilderness carried a stiff-necked hardness the Torah named directly (Exodus 32:9; Deuteronomy 9:6; Deuteronomy 10:16; Deuteronomy 31:27). The legislation preserved regulated divorce because the alternative under hardness-of-heart conditions would have been worse — wives cast out without papers, with no legal protection, no possibility of remarriage, vulnerable to the very things the Torah elsewhere protects against. The bill of divorcement was the Torah protecting the woman who was being put away in a system where the husband's hardness of heart was operative. The Torah was not endorsing what was happening; the Torah was regulating it and limiting the damage.

Hold this carefully against the inherited-Christian collapse of the Tanakh. The institutional-Christian reading has too often treated the King's words here as an abrogation of Deuteronomy 24:1 — as if he were saying *Mosheh (Moses) gave you a corrupt law and I am now replacing it.* The substance does not stand. The Torah permission is not abolished by the King's statement; the King names the permission for what it always was — a concession to hardness of heart, never the Father's will from the beginning. The Torah is never the curse, and it is not corrupt here; the curse is the hardness of heart it was given to limit. The Torah is preserved; the Father's will from the beginning is restored to view; the kingdom-citizen reads both substances together and walks toward the Father's will from the beginning while acknowledging the concession the Torah preserved for the hardness-of-heart condition.

The same architectural pattern runs elsewhere. Deuteronomy 17:14-20 preserves the substance of the human king the people would eventually demand — *I will set a king over me, like as all the goyim (nations) that are about me* — and that was concession to hardness of heart, not the Father's will from the beginning (the Father's rule was the substance from the beginning). It ran exactly as Mosheh (Moses) foresaw: at 1 Samuel 8 the people demanded a king to judge them like all the nations, and Yahuah (the LORD) said to Shmuel (Samuel), *they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them* (1 Samuel 8:7). The Torah preserved the regulated form of what the community would do under hardness of heart; the prophet named the substance for what it was. The same pattern operates at the divorce-substance.

The Tanakh prophetic substance against unfaithful putting-away is named directly by the prophet Malachi:

*Yahuah (the LORD) hath been witness between thee and the wife of thy youth, against whom thou hast dealt treacherously: yet is she thy companion, and the wife of thy covenant... For Yahuah Elohim (the LORD God) of Yashar'el (Israel) saith that he hateth putting away.* (Malachi 2:14, 16)

The Father has never preferred divorce; he has permitted the regulated form for the hardness of heart; his will from the beginning is the one-flesh substance the kingdom-citizen walks toward.

*And I say unto you, Whosoever shall put away his wife, except it be for fornication, and shall marry another, committeth adultery.* The *except it be for fornication* exception preserves substantial sexual impurity as a substance that genuinely breaks the one-flesh bond. The Greek *porneia* is broader than narrowly adultery — it includes the range of substantial sexual impurity the Torah names at Deuteronomy 22:13-21 and Leviticus 20:10-21. The exception is not the Hillelite any-cause divorce the Pharisees were trying to lock in; it is the recognition that some substances genuinely break the one-flesh bond from the inside, and the kingdom-citizen whose bond has been broken from the inside is not bound to maintain a fiction against a substance that has already broken it.

Hold this against the Roman-Catholic reading of no-divorce-no-remarriage as absolute. The Catholic tradition reads *what Elohim (God) hath joined together, let not man put asunder* as an absolute prohibition under any circumstances, with the exception-clause read out of the substance through the annulment-procedure. The substance does not stand against the King's own mouth, which preserved the exception directly. The substance is in continuity with the Father's own act — at Jeremiah 3:8 the Father names what he had done with the northern house: *and I saw, when for all the causes whereby backsliding Yashar'el (Israel) committed adultery I had put her away, and given her a bill of divorce.* The Father himself put away the northern house on the ground of substantial spiritual adultery. The exception the King preserves at Matthew 19:9 stands on the same architecture the Father himself walked at the divine marriage broken from the inside. (The Markan parallel at Mark 10:2-12 and the Lukan parallel at Luke 16:18 carry the same teaching without the *porneia* exception preserved — the King's correction against the Hillelite any-cause substance at its most direct; the Matthean account preserves the exception the Mosaic Torah already recognized. The apostle Paul applies the same architecture at 1 Corinthians 7:10-16, adding the case of the kingdom-citizen whose unbelieving spouse departs, who is not under bondage in such cases — the breaking the kingdom-citizen did not cause.)

## Eunuchs for the kingdom

*His disciples say unto him, If the case of the man be so with his wife, it is not good to marry. But he said unto them, All men cannot receive this saying, save they to whom it is given. For there are some eunuchs, which were so born from their mother's womb: and there are some eunuchs, which were made eunuchs of men: and there be eunuchs, which have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake. He that is able to receive it, let him receive it.* (Matthew 19:10-12)

The disciples' reaction reveals how thoroughly the Hillelite any-cause substance had set into the assumptions of the day. *If the case of the man be so with his wife, it is not good to marry* — if the marriage bond is what the King has just named, with no any-cause escape, then marriage itself becomes a thing the disciples are not sure they want. The reaction is honest. The kingdom-substance of marriage demands more than the inherited system had been requiring.

*All men cannot receive this saying, save they to whom it is given.* Not everyone is given the substance of marriage. Some are given the substance of celibacy in some form, and the King names the three categories. *There are some eunuchs, which were so born from their mother's womb: and there are some eunuchs, which were made eunuchs of men: and there be eunuchs, which have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake.* Those born so from the womb; those made so by men; and those who have made themselves so for the kingdom's sake. The first two are conditions imposed. The third is voluntary — the kingdom-citizen who chooses celibacy as the substance of undistracted kingdom-service.

The Tanakh anchor for the eunuch-substance is Isaiah 56:

*Neither let the son of the stranger, that hath joined himself to Yahuah (the LORD), speak, saying, Yahuah (the LORD) hath utterly separated me from his people: neither let the eunuch say, Behold, I am a dry tree. For thus saith Yahuah (the LORD) unto the eunuchs that keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that please me, and take hold of my covenant; Even unto them will I give in mine house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters: I will give them an everlasting name, that shall not be cut off.* (Isaiah 56:3-5)

The Isaiah 56 substance is the scattered seed coming home. The strangers and eunuchs of Isaiah 56 are not random pagan converts who have decided to join. The strangers are the sons of the stranger — the scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel) living in the territories of their exile, made strangers from their own covenant identity by the scattering, now being gathered home. The *Yahuah (the LORD) hath utterly separated me from his people* fear they speak is the Lo-Ammi fear of Hosea 1 — *I will say to them which were not my people, Thou art my people* (Hosea 2:23). The eunuchs are those who cannot produce physical descendants — by birth, by men, or, as becomes clear here, by voluntary substance for the kingdom's sake. The prophet promises them *a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters.* The substance is not the negation of the physical seed-line; it is the Father's place and name for those who, in the architecture of the gathering, do not add to the physical seed-line at the level of their own descendants. The substance is honored, not negated.

The King's saying extends the Tanakh substance forward. The voluntary eunuch for the kingdom's sake takes the substance into the kingdom-mission. The King himself walked it — Yahusha (Jesus) lived celibately as undistracted kingdom-mission. The apostle Paul walked it and wrote to the Corinthians: *for I would that all men were even as I myself. But every man hath his proper gift of Elohim (God), one after this manner, and another after that... He that is unmarried careth for the things that belong to the Lord* (1 Corinthians 7:7, 32). It is gift-substance — not every kingdom-citizen receives it. *He that is able to receive it, let him receive it,* the King says. It is offered as a substance some receive, not imposed on all.

Hold this against two inherited collapses. The Roman-Catholic mandatory clerical celibacy imposes the gift on the priesthood as a categorical requirement regardless of whether the gift is given — exactly what the King ruled out with *save they to whom it is given.* The gift is not a mandate, and the systems that mandate it operate against the substance the King named. On the other side, the Reformation's over-correction has too often produced a marriage-mandate instead — a default in which the unmarried adult is treated as incomplete and the celibate kingdom-citizen as defective. That, too, is ruled out at the King's mouth. Voluntary celibacy for the kingdom's sake is a substance the King honors; the kingdom-citizen who receives it walks in a substance the King himself walked.

## The children and the disciples' rebuke

*Then were there brought unto him little children, that he should put his hands on them, and pray: and the disciples rebuked them. But Yahusha (Jesus) said, Suffer little children, and forbid them not, to come unto me: for of such is the kingdom of heaven. And he laid his hands on them, and departed thence.* (Matthew 19:13-15)

The substance is parents and family-members bringing children to the King for blessing. The Tanakh substance under the act runs through Bereshit (Genesis) 48, where Ya'aqov (Jacob) lays his hands on Ephrayim (Ephraim) and Menashsheh (Manasseh) — *the messenger which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads* (Genesis 48:16) — and through the priestly blessing of Numbers 6:24-26 — *Yahuah (the LORD) bless thee, and keep thee.* The parents are bringing the children to the King as the Tanakh-Father of blessing in flesh.

*And the disciples rebuked them.* The disciples have not yet internalized what the King taught in the previous chapter. The Greek *epetimēsan* — *rebuked* — is the same word the disciples operated in when Kefa (Peter) rebuked the King at Matthew 16:22 over the first passion-prediction. One chapter earlier the King had taught *converted and become as little children*, the millstone-warning against offending the little ones, the *despise not one of these little ones* — and here the disciples are immediately operating against it, placing themselves as a substance between the children and the King, exactly the offense-substance the millstone-warning named. The King rebukes the rebuke.

*Suffer little children, and forbid them not, to come unto me: for of such is the kingdom of heaven.* The Greek *aphete* — *suffer, allow* — names the disciples' need to stop blocking the children's approach. The King re-opens the way. *For of such is the kingdom of heaven.* The kingdom belongs to those who bear what the children carry — not because the children have not yet sinned (the original-innocence reading the institutional-Christian tradition has read into the verse is not what the Tanakh or the King names) but because the four-fold child-substance of humility, receptiveness, dependence, and honesty the King unpacked at Matthew 18:1-5 is the disposition the kingdom-citizens walk in. The kingdom belongs to those who bear the substance. The Markan and Lukan parallels (Mark 10:13-16; Luke 18:15-17) add the categorical word — *whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of Elohim (God) as a little child, he shall not enter therein.* *And he laid his hands on them, and departed thence.* The King performs the paternal-blessing substance the parents had brought their children for, enacted by the King himself.

## The rich young ruler — keep the commandments

*And, behold, one came and said unto him, Good Master, what good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life? And he said unto him, Why callest thou me good? there is none good but one, that is, Elohim (God): but if thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments. He saith unto him, Which? Yahusha (Jesus) said, Thou shalt do no murder, Thou shalt not commit adultery, Thou shalt not steal, Thou shalt not bear false witness, Honour thy father and thy mother: and, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. The young man saith unto him, All these things have I kept from my youth up: what lack I yet? Yahusha (Jesus) said unto him, If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven: and come and follow me. But when the young man heard that saying, he went away sorrowful: for he had great possessions.* (Matthew 19:16-22)

The rich-young-ruler pericope lands one of the most load-bearing substances in the entire gospel.

*Good Master, what good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life?* The young man's question is substantial. He recognizes eternal life as a thing worth pursuing, addresses the King with respect, and asks what the pursuit requires. This is not the Pharisees tempting; it is the man who believes the substance can be pursued and asks the One he recognizes can answer.

*Why callest thou me good? there is none good but one, that is, Elohim (God).* The King's first move is a probe. Hold what it is and is not. He is not denying his own divinity. The substance is not *I am not Elohim (God), so do not call me good.* It is *think about what you are saying when you call me good — only Elohim (God) is good; do you mean to identify me as such?* The young man has spoken better than he knows; the King presses him to consider whether he means what his own words imply. Under the question is the simpler, older structure the canon shows — the Father is the Formless source of all good, never seen; the Son is the Formed expression of that same good, who walked with Avraham (Abraham), spoke from the fire, and now stands in flesh asking the question. The King is not separating himself from the Father's goodness; he is inviting the young man to recognize what his own address entails. The fourth-century formula reached for three co-equal persons to guard the Son's divinity; the verse shows something older — one good, Formless in source, Formed in expression, now in flesh before the young man's eyes.

*But if thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments.* The King's direct answer at the kingdom-entry-criterion level. The young man asks what gets him into eternal life; the King answers *keep the commandments.* Direct, unambiguous, and the King's own mouth. This is the load-bearing substance against the antinomian-Paul reading the institutional-Christian tradition has built. The substance is in continuity with the King's Sermon: *think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law* (Matthew 5:17-18); and *not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven... And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity* (Matthew 7:21-23). The Greek for *iniquity* at Matthew 7:23 is *anomia* — *lawlessness, without-Torah.* The substance the King dismisses at that judgment-day is confession without Torah-walking. The substance he affirms at Matthew 19:17 is commandment-keeping at the kingdom-entry level. Two sides of one substance.

The Tanakh under the answer runs deep. Mosheh (Moses) set it before the people at the end of his life:

*See, I have set before thee this day life and good, and death and evil; In that I command thee this day to love Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God), to walk in his ways, and to keep his commandments and his statutes and his judgments, that thou mayest live and multiply... I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live.* (Deuteronomy 30:15-16, 19)

Life is on the side of commandment-keeping; death is on the side of turning away. *Ye shall therefore keep my statutes, and my judgments: which if a man do, he shall live in them: I am Yahuah (the LORD)* (Leviticus 18:5). *My son, attend to my words... For they are life unto those that find them, and health to all their flesh* (Proverbs 4:20, 22). And the apostolic restatement carries the same substance forward — this is the 1234 of Truth at the apostle Yochanan's (John's) own mouth:

*And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.* (1 John 2:3-4)

*For this is the love of Elohim (God), that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous* (1 John 5:3). *Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of Elohim (God), and the faith of Yahusha (Jesus)* (Revelation 14:12). *Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life* (Revelation 22:14). The Tanakh, the King, and the apostles all carry the same substance.

The institutional-Christian collapse is the antinomian-Paul reading that treats commandment-keeping as the substance of an old administration the cross supposedly abolished, deploying isolated phrases — *not under law but under grace,* *Christ is the end of the law* — torn from the apostle's own writings as proof-texts against what the King himself answered here. The substance does not stand. The same apostle wrote *wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good* (Romans 7:12) and *for not the hearers of the law are just before Elohim (God), but the doers of the law shall be justified* (Romans 2:13); he took Torah-vows and went into the temple to pay for four men's Nazarite-vow completions at the assembly's request (Acts 21:23-26). The apostle who wrote the verses the antinomian reading collapses was a Torah-observant disciple who never abolished the Torah at any point of his life. *Not under law* names *not under Torah as a condemning system* — the curse-system the cross removed — not *not under Torah as the way of life the Father gave,* which the Spirit causes the disciple to walk per Ezekiel 36:27. Justification is by faith; covenant life is Spirit-empowered Torah-walking; the two never compete. The substance at the King's own mouth is what the assembly is being called back to.

*He saith unto him, Which? Yahusha (Jesus) said, Thou shalt do no murder, Thou shalt not commit adultery, Thou shalt not steal, Thou shalt not bear false witness, Honour thy father and thy mother: and, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.* The King cites five of the Ten Commandments plus Leviticus 19:18 as the summary. The selection is the second table of the Decalogue — the commandments toward the neighbor — with *love thy neighbour as thyself* as the summary. The first-table commandments toward the Father are not named here. The King is testing the young man at the neighbor-substance, and the young man will discover shortly that the neighbor includes the poor whose substance his possessions could be feeding.

*All these things have I kept from my youth up: what lack I yet?* The young man asserts letter-keeping. He can truly say of the letter — I have not murdered, not committed adultery, not stolen, not borne false witness, honored my parents, loved my neighbor as my heart understands it. What he has not walked is the heart-Torah behind the letter — the *law in the inward parts* of Jeremiah 31:33, the *new heart of flesh* of Ezekiel 36:26-27, the Sermon's *whosoever is angry with his brother* (Matthew 5:22) and *whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her* (Matthew 5:28). The Pharisaic counterfeit-righteousness the King named at Matthew 5:20 — *except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no case enter into the kingdom of heaven* — is what the young man is operating at: letter-keeping without heart-walking. *What lack I yet?* he asks, sensing something missing. The honest question is what the King will honor with the test.

*If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven: and come and follow me.* The Greek *teleios* — *perfect* — is the Tanakh *tamim,* the whole-hearted substance: *walk before me, and be thou perfect (tamim)* to Avraham (Abraham) at Genesis 17:1; *thou shalt be perfect (tamim) with Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God)* at Deuteronomy 18:13; *be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect* at Matthew 5:48. Whole-hearted, not partial.

The test is diagnostic, not categorical. The King is not commanding every kingdom-citizen to sell all possessions as a categorical kingdom-entry requirement; the institutional-Christian reading of the verse as a universal poverty-mandate — or the Marxist reading as redistribution-program — is not what the substance says. Different kingdom-citizens face different obstacles, and the King's diagnostic addresses the obstacle this particular man carries. But hold what the diagnostic reveals about his own claim. He said *all these things have I kept,* naming among them *thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.* If love of neighbor had been kept from the heart, what the King now asks would not have been an obstacle. His love of neighbor stops at the substance of his own possessions — the love that does not extend to the poor who could be fed by what he keeps back. The Tanakh-substance of giving to the poor was law he should have been walking: *thou shalt open thine hand wide unto him* (Deuteronomy 15:8); *he that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto Yahuah (the LORD)* (Proverbs 19:17); *is not this the fast that I have chosen?... Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry?* (Isaiah 58:6-7). The diagnostic reveals where the heart-Torah had not yet formed. And *come and follow me* — the substance the test was for — is the discipleship the kingdom-entry ultimately lands on.

*But when the young man heard that saying, he went away sorrowful: for he had great possessions.* The Greek *lypoumenos* names it — he recognizes what the King has named and knows he cannot walk it. His possessions hold his heart at a place the kingdom-entry cannot be walked from. The Markan account adds *then Yahusha (Jesus) beholding him loved him* (Mark 10:21) — the King's disposition is love, not condemnation. The substance is open for the man's return; his departure with sorrow may yet turn to a return with the possessions loosed.

## The camel and the needle's eye

*Then said Yahusha (Jesus) unto his disciples, Verily I say unto you, That a rich man shall hardly enter into the kingdom of heaven. And again I say unto you, It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of Elohim (God). When his disciples heard it, they were exceedingly amazed, saying, Who then can be saved? But Yahusha (Jesus) beheld them, and said unto them, With men this is impossible; but with Elohim (God) all things are possible.* (Matthew 19:23-26)

*A rich man shall hardly enter into the kingdom of heaven.* The Greek *dyskolōs* — *with difficulty.* The King moves from the particular man to what he illustrated. The rich man as such operates against the kingdom-substance at a place the poor man does not have to overcome — the place of the heart the possessions hold. *Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also* (Matthew 6:21). The treasure shapes the heart; the rich man whose treasure is on earth carries his heart on earth and does not easily receive the kingdom.

*It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of Elohim (God).* This is straight Tanakh hyperbole. The popular-Christian explanation that *the Needle's Eye* was a small Yerushalayim gate the camel could just barely squeeze through, burdens removed and on its knees, is a story with no historical evidence whatsoever — no such gate existed at any documented period. The substance is impossibility, deliberately stated. The largest land-animal of the region cannot fit through the smallest practical aperture.

The Tanakh prophetic substance against trusting in riches stands under it. *Give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me: Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is Yahuah (the LORD)?* (Proverbs 30:8-9). *Let not the rich man glory in his riches: But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me* (Jeremiah 9:23-24). *Beware that thou forget not Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God)... Lest when thou hast eaten and art full... Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God)* (Deuteronomy 8:11-14). *If riches increase, set not your heart upon them* (Psalm 62:10). The heart lifted up and forgetting Yahuah (the LORD) when the multiplication comes is exactly what the rich young ruler had just walked out. The apostle Ya'aqov (James) will restate it with equal force: *go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries that shall come upon you* (James 5:1).

*When his disciples heard it, they were exceedingly amazed, saying, Who then can be saved?* The reaction reveals the inherited substance of the day. The Tanakh had at points associated material prosperity with the Father's blessing — Avraham (Abraham) rich in cattle, silver, and gold (Genesis 13:2); Yitschaq (Isaac) receiving a hundredfold the same year (Genesis 26:12); the blessings of Deuteronomy 28 including prosperity for the covenant-faithful. The inherited reading collapsed this into a straight equation — the rich man is rich because the Father has blessed him, therefore the rich man is by default on the side of the Father's substance. The disciples' amazement reveals what they had absorbed: if even what the Tanakh treats as blessing is an obstacle, *who then can be saved?* The inherited substance had to be deconstructed to make room for the kingdom-substance.

*With men this is impossible; but with Elohim (God) all things are possible.* The Tanakh anchor is the word to Avraham (Abraham) when Sarah laughed: *is any thing too hard for Yahuah (the LORD)?* (Genesis 18:14). The prophet Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) restates it — *there is nothing too hard for thee* (Jeremiah 32:17) — and the Father himself answers in the same chapter: *behold, I am Yahuah (the LORD), the Elohim (God) of all flesh: is there any thing too hard for me?* (Jeremiah 32:27). Yov (Job) confesses it at the end of his trial: *I know that thou canst do every thing* (Job 42:2). And the messenger Gavri'el (Gabriel) speaks it to Miryam (Mary): *for with Elohim (God) nothing shall be impossible* (Luke 1:37).

Hold what this is and is not. The substance is not that all rich men will be saved by the Father's power regardless of how they walk — the King has just said the rich man shall hardly enter. The Father's substance making the impossible possible operates at the new-heart substance the prophets named: *a new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you... And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them* (Ezekiel 36:26-27). The Father's substance softens the heart the possessions hold and enables the substance the natural man cannot walk. The rich young ruler who walked away may yet turn and return; the Father is able to soften what the man could not soften in the moment. This is grace — but grace as the means of return to what the Father commands, not the cancellation of what the Father commands. The Spirit *will cause you to walk in my statutes.* The walking in the statutes is what the Father's substance enables. It is one motion with the King's answer at verse 17 — *if thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments* — made possible with Elohim (God) where it is impossible with men.

## Twelve thrones, twelve tribes

*Then answered Kefa (Peter) and said unto him, Behold, we have forsaken all, and followed thee; what shall we have therefore? And Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, Verily I say unto you, That ye which have followed me, in the regeneration when the Son of Adam shall sit in the throne of his glory, ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel). And every one that hath forsaken houses, or brethren, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my name's sake, shall receive an hundredfold, and shall inherit everlasting life. But many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first.* (Matthew 19:27-30)

Kefa (Peter) speaks the question the disciples have been carrying. They have done what the young man refused — left the nets (Matthew 4:18-22), left the customs-table (Matthew 9:9), walked the substance the discipleship requires. Kefa (Peter) names what they have done and asks what the reward is. The substance is not prosperity-now; it is the apostolic question about the substance of the following.

*Verily I say unto you, That ye which have followed me, in the regeneration when the Son of Adam shall sit in the throne of his glory, ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel).* The King's answer is the framework-major substance of the chapter — the most direct statement in the gospels of the architecture the prophets pointed to all along. The consummated kingdom features the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel) with twelve apostles ruling as judges over the twelve tribes. The Father's promise through the prophets of the gathering of the twelve tribes is stated here at the King's own mouth.

*In the regeneration.* The Greek *en tē palingenesia* — *in the re-genesis, the re-creation.* The substance is the new heavens and new earth the prophet Yeshayahu (Isaiah) named — *for, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth* (Isaiah 65:17); *for as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith Yahuah (the LORD), so shall your seed and your name remain* (Isaiah 66:22). It is the consummated kingdom the apostle Kefa (Peter) will name to the assembly at Acts 3:21 as the *times of restitution of all things, which Elohim (God) hath spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began.* The Greek for *restitution* there is *apokatastasis* — restoration back to the original. The bringing-back of all things to the Father's will from the beginning that the divorce-controversy of verses 3-9 had just pointed to. The apostle Sha'ul (Paul) carries the same substance to the Romans — *the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of Elohim (God)* (Romans 8:21). The whole creation will be brought back to the Father's will from the beginning.

*When the Son of Adam shall sit in the throne of his glory.* The substance the prophet Daniel named:

*I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of Adam came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.* (Daniel 7:13-14)

The everlasting kingdom transferred from the Ancient of Days to the One who resembles mortal-man — the kaph honoring the incarnation: he resembled mortal-man because he took on flesh, while remaining the Formed cloud-rider, and the everlasting indestructible kingdom of verse 14 belongs to him alone. The substance is in continuity with the King's own word at Matthew 25:31 — *when the Son of Adam shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory* — the sheep-and-goats judgment of the fifth discourse.

*Ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel).* The twelve thrones are the twelve apostolic thrones — the twelve disciples commissioned at Matthew 10:2-4 seated, in the consummated kingdom, as judges over the twelve tribes. The Tanakh under the architecture runs through the prophets. Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named the two sticks becoming one — *I will take the children of Yashar'el (Israel) from among the goyim (heathen)... and will make them one nation in the land upon the mountains of Yashar'el (Israel); and one king shall be king to them all: and they shall be no more two nations* (Ezekiel 37:21-22) — the northern house and the southern house gathered into one under the one king. Yeshayahu (Isaiah) named it — *he shall set up an ensign for the goyim (nations), and shall assemble the outcasts of Yashar'el (Israel), and gather together the dispersed of Yahudah (Judah) from the four corners of the earth* (Isaiah 11:12). Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) named the new covenant with both houses — *I will make a new covenant with the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and with the house of Yahudah (Judah)* (Jeremiah 31:31). Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named the twelve-tribes land-allotment in the consummated kingdom across Ezekiel 47-48. And the apostle Yochanan (John) sees it at the close of the canon — *twelve gates... names written thereon, which are the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Yashar'el (Israel)... And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb* (Revelation 21:12, 14).

Hold what this is against the inherited collapses. The replacement-theology substance that collapses the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel) into a spiritualized church-as-the-new-Israel is what the King's own mouth at Matthew 19:28 rules out. The twelve tribes at the regeneration are not the church-as-spiritual-Israel; they are the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel) — the gathered paternal-blood remnant of all twelve tribes the prophets promised throughout the Tanakh. The substance is concrete, not metaphorical; paternal blood, not spiritualized self-selection. It is the gathering from the north and the south and the east and the west the prophets named, the gathering the King restricted his earthly mission to (*I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel)*, Matthew 15:24), the gathering the apostolic mission to the uncircumcision continued among the scattered seed living as goyim (Gentiles) among the nations.

The Hebrew-Roots false-inclusion gospel that collapses the twelve tribes into anyone-who-believes-and-Torah-keeps-is-therefore-Yashar'el (Israel) is also ruled out at the King's mouth. The twelve tribes are paternal-blood remnant — named by the Father, traced through the prophets, gathered into the consummated kingdom — not a spiritual category open to self-selection through Torah-practice. The wild-olive branches the apostle Sha'ul (Paul) names at Romans 11 are the scattered seed living as goyim (Gentiles) among the nations now being gathered home; they are not non-Yashar'el branches grafted in. The olive tree is the covenant tree of Avraham (Abraham), Yitschaq (Isaac), and Ya'aqov (Jacob), and both branches on it are Yashar'el (Israel) — the natural branches the faithful patriarchs and prophets of every generation, the wild-olive branches the descendants of the broken-off coming home through the journey the prophets named. Matthew 19:28 lands at the destination of that journey.

*Judging the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel).* The Greek *krinontes* — *judging, ruling, governing.* The substance is not narrowly condemnatory judgment; it is the governance the Shofetim (Judges) of Yashar'el (Israel) walked in. *And Yahuah (the LORD) raised up judges, which delivered them out of the hand of those that spoiled them* (Judges 2:16). The twelve thrones are the apostolic governance over the twelve tribes at the consummated kingdom — the apostolic substance carrying forward what the Shofetim (Judges) carried in their generation, now under the King on the throne of glory.

*And every one that hath forsaken houses, or brethren, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my name's sake, shall receive an hundredfold, and shall inherit everlasting life.* The King extends the substance from the apostolic twelve to all kingdom-citizens. The discipleship is *for my name's sake* — what the kingdom-citizen forsakes natural substance for. The reward is not natural prosperity multiplied; the Markan parallel makes it explicit — *he shall receive an hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life* (Mark 10:29-30). The hundredfold is the corporate substance the kingdom-citizens enter — the assembly-family the King named at Matthew 12:50, *whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother* — multiplied now in the corporate fellowship, with persecutions (the cost), and everlasting life in the coming age. The substance is in continuity with the early assembly walking it directly at Acts 2:44-45 and Acts 4:32-37.

*But many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first.* The chapter's closing word and the bridge to Matthew 20:1-16. The substance is the kingdom-economy's inversion of the natural economy. What the rich young ruler illustrated — counted first by natural substance, by possessions, by lineage, by letter-keeping — is counted last in the kingdom; and what is counted last by natural substance — the publican, the sinner, the disciple who left everything to follow — is counted first. The kingdom does not rank by natural substance, by when one arrived or how much time was served or how much was given; it ranks by the Father's substance, the substance his grace distributes and rewards. The next chapter will open with the laborers in the vineyard — the householder hiring at the first hour and the eleventh hour and paying all the same penny — unpacking *many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first* directly, then moving to the third passion-prediction, the mother of Zavdai's (Zebedee's) sons asking for thrones on the King's right and left, and the healing of the two blind men at Yericho (Jericho) on the way up to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem).
