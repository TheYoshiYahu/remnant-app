## The kingdom-economy is grace, not wage

Matthew 20 continues the King's long approach to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), and the lens it turns on is the kingdom-economy against the natural economy. The previous chapter closed on *many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first* (Matthew 19:30), and this chapter opens with the parable that unpacks it — the householder who pays the eleventh-hour laborer the same penny as the first-hour laborer, distributing by his own will rather than by hours served. That inversion governs the whole chapter as it moves from the laborers to the disciples' grasping for thrones to the marginalized blind men who see the King the system cannot. At the chapter's center the King names himself as the Suffering Servant who gives his life a ransom for many — the deepest substance in the chapter and the one the whole inversion was pointing toward.

*For the kingdom of heaven is like unto a man that is an householder, which went out early in the morning to hire labourers into his vineyard. And when he had agreed with the labourers for a penny a day, he sent them into his vineyard. And he went out about the third hour, and saw others standing idle in the marketplace, And said unto them; Go ye also into the vineyard, and whatsoever is right I will give you. And they went their way. Again he went out about the sixth and ninth hour, and did likewise. And about the eleventh hour he went out, and found others standing idle, and saith unto them, Why stand ye here all the day idle? They say unto him, Because no man hath hired us. He saith unto them, Go ye also into the vineyard; and whatsoever is right, that shall ye receive.* (Matthew 20:1-7)

The householder is the Father; the vineyard is Yashar'el (Israel)'s covenant-body the prophets named; the laborers are those the Father calls into the vineyard across the day's hours. The Tanakh vineyard-substance lands at the prophet Yeshayahu's (Isaiah's) great vineyard-song:

*Now will I sing to my wellbeloved a song of my beloved touching his vineyard. My wellbeloved hath a vineyard in a very fruitful hill... For the vineyard of Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts) is the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and the men of Yahudah (Judah) his pleasant plant.* (Isaiah 5:1, 7)

The vineyard is Yashar'el's covenant-body the Father has planted and fenced and tended for fruit. The prophet Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) carries the same substance — *many pastors have destroyed my vineyard* (Jeremiah 12:10). The parable's householder is the Father standing over that vineyard, hiring laborers into the substance he is still cultivating.

The first-hour hiring lands at the day's opening with the agreed wage named explicitly: *a penny a day.* The Greek for *penny* is *dēnarion* — the Roman silver denarius the day-laborer received as the standard full-day wage. The wage is fair by the natural economy; the laborer agrees and goes in with the agreement in hand. But from the third hour onward the wording shifts — the householder does not agree to a fixed wage with the later-hired; he says *whatsoever is right I will give you.* The early-hired have an agreed wage; the later-hired have the householder's promise of rightness without a fixed amount. The substance the parable will turn on is laid in that shift.

*So when even was come, the lord of the vineyard saith unto his steward, Call the labourers, and give them their hire, beginning from the last unto the first. And when they came that were hired about the eleventh hour, they received every man a penny. But when the first came, they supposed that they should have received more; and they likewise received every man a penny.* (Matthew 20:8-10)

*Beginning from the last unto the first.* The payment-order is the reversal the closing line will name. The eleventh-hour laborers receive their penny first; the first-hour laborers watch the late-comers receive what was agreed for a full day, and *supposed that they should have received more.* The natural economy assumes more hours mean more pay. But the householder pays each the penny agreed. The first-hired have not been cheated — they received what they agreed to. What they complain about is not a wage-injustice; it is the householder's grace toward the eleventh-hour laborers exceeding the natural economy's expectation. The kingdom-economy has entered the natural-economy's frame and broken it open at the substance of the householder's will.

*And when they had received it, they murmured against the goodman of the house, Saying, These last have wrought but one hour, and thou hast made them equal unto us, which have borne the burden and heat of the day.* (Matthew 20:11-12)

The Greek for *murmured* is *egongyzon* — the same root the Septuagint uses across the wilderness narratives where Yashar'el (Israel) murmurs against Yahuah (the LORD). *The whole congregation of the children of Yashar'el (Israel) murmured against Mosheh (Moses) and Aharon (Aaron) in the wilderness* (Exodus 16:2). *How long shall I bear with this evil congregation, which murmur against me?* (Numbers 14:27). The wilderness pattern is the one the first-hired laborers recapitulate: the substance refuses what the Father gives by asserting the natural substance against it. The wilderness murmurers refused the manna by comparing it to the fleshpots of Mitsrayim (Egypt); the laborers refuse the penny by comparing it to the natural economy's expectation.

*But he answered one of them, and said, Friend, I do thee no wrong: didst not thou agree with me for a penny? Take that thine is, and go thy way: I will give unto this last, even as unto thee. Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own? Is thine eye evil, because I am good?* (Matthew 20:13-15)

*Friend.* The Greek is *hetaire* — a polite address of acquaintance, not the warm *philos* of the King's friends at John 15:14-15. The address lands in three places in Matthew, and each carries a correction following: here to the murmuring first-hired; at Matthew 22:12 to the man without the wedding-garment thrown out of the feast; at Matthew 26:50 to Yahudah (Judas) at the betrayal-kiss. The householder uses the polite form of substantial correction, and the correction follows. *I do thee no wrong... Didst not thou agree with me for a penny?* The justice of the agreement holds. The first-hired agreed to the penny and received the penny. *Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own?* The householder's prerogative over his own substance is the parable's point — the Father's grace is the Father's grace to distribute as he wills. The Tanakh substance under it: *who hath prevented me, that I should repay him? whatsoever is under the whole heaven is mine* (Job 41:11).

*Is thine eye evil, because I am good?* The closing question is a Hebrew idiom — the *good-eye* and *evil-eye* the King already named at the heart of the Sermon: *if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light. But if thine eye be evil, thy whole body shall be full of darkness* (Matthew 6:22-23). The idiom is not optical physiology; the *good-eye* (*ayin tovah*) is the disposition of generosity toward the other, and the *evil-eye* (*ayin ra'ah*) is the disposition of stinginess and envy. The Torah and the wisdom literature carry it — *thine eye be evil against thy poor brother, and thou givest him nought; and he cry unto Yahuah (the LORD) against thee, and it be sin unto thee* (Deuteronomy 15:9); *he that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye* (Proverbs 28:22). The first-hired's evil eye is the begrudging of the householder for his grace toward the eleventh-hour laborer. The kingdom-citizen's substance is the good-eye that rejoices when the grace falls on the fellow laborer.

*So the last shall be first, and the first last: for many be called, but few chosen.* (Matthew 20:16)

The closing line restates Matthew 19:30, the bridge the parable was sent to unpack. The kingdom-economy does not rank by when one arrived or how much time was served; it ranks by the Father's grace distributed by his will — the same inversion that runs through *blessed are the poor in spirit* (Matthew 5:3), *whosoever shall humble himself as this little child* (Matthew 18:4), and the servant-greatness the King is about to deliver in this very chapter.

*For many be called, but few chosen.* The closing clause lands the parable at the cause-and-effect of hearing the King has unfolded through every kingdom-parable since Matthew 13 — the substance the prophet Yeshayahu (Isaiah) was sent into at his commissioning, *hear ye indeed, but understand not; and see ye indeed, but perceive not* (Isaiah 6:9), which the King applied to the parables at Matthew 13:13-14. The *many* are called because the citizens of the kingdom are scattered among the nations and the proclaimers do not know them by sight; the proclamation goes broadly because the sheep are scattered. The *few chosen* — Greek *eklektoi* — are those whose names are written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8; 17:8), and the sheep hear because they are sheep. *Ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me* (John 10:26-27). Hearing does not make the sheep; the sheep hear because they are the sheep. *Yahuah (the LORD) knoweth them that are his* (2 Timothy 2:19).

The substance is BOTH-AND, not EITHER-OR. The inherited-Reformed monergistic determinism that collapses it into the-Father-unilaterally-chooses-without-disposition-walked is read out by the cause-and-effect architecture the King's own mouth names; the substance walked is the substance walked by the citizen who is. The inherited-Arminian self-determinative freedom that collapses it into the-human-chooses-without-the-Father's-foreknowledge is also read out; the hearing reveals what was already true before the foundation, and does not create it. Matthew 22:14 will restate it at the wedding-feast — *for many are called, but few are chosen.*

The parable also carries a historic-redemptive substance at the band-edge. The first-hour laborers and the eleventh-hour laborers stand in the vineyard at the same payment-hour despite their different hours of entry. The first-hour laborers stand for the house of Yahudah (Judah), the southern house that retained the Torah and the temple and the priesthood and the tribal identity through the exile and return; the eleventh-hour laborers stand for the scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel), the ten northern tribes divorced through the Assyrian captivity, now being gathered home through the King's proclamation. The Father distributes the same penny of inheritance to both, by his own will. The parable rules out the murmuring against the late-coming of the scattered seed — whether from the southern house against the Father's grace to the gathered remnant, or from Christian replacement theology against the paternal-blood remnant of the twelve tribes, or from a Hebrew-Roots Torah-keeping substance against the scattered seed still on the road home. The kingdom-citizen's substance is the good-eye that rejoices when the eleventh-hour laborer receives the same penny — when the scattered seed comes home.

## The third passion-prediction

*And Yahusha (Jesus) going up to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) took the twelve disciples apart in the way, and said unto them, Behold, we go up to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem); and the Son of Adam shall be betrayed unto the chief priests and unto the scribes, and they shall condemn him to death, And shall deliver him to the goyim (Gentiles) to mock, and to scourge, and to crucify him: and the third day he shall rise again.* (Matthew 20:17-19)

The third and most specific of the passion-predictions. The first at Matthew 16:21 laid the substance generally — the *must* (Greek *dei*) of divine necessity, the suffering at the hands of the elders and chief priests and scribes, the killing, the third-day raising. The second at Matthew 17:22-23 added the betrayal (Greek *paradidotai* — *delivered up*). The third here adds the chief-priests-and-scribes condemnation, the delivery to the goyim (Gentiles) for the execution, the specific mocking and scourging and crucifying, and the third-day raising sealed at the close.

*The Son of Adam shall be betrayed unto the chief priests and unto the scribes.* The Adamic seed of promise is about to suffer the substance the Tanakh Suffering-Servant prophesied — *he is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief* (Isaiah 53:3). The chief priests and scribes are the Pharisaic-Sadducean system the gospel has confronted since the wilderness immersion, where the King named them *generation of vipers* (Matthew 3:7) — the same system that will deliver the King to the cross. *They shall condemn him to death, and shall deliver him to the goyim (Gentiles).* The condemnation lands at the system; the execution lands at the Roman administration the system cannot carry the death-sentence without. The Tanakh pattern the delivery recapitulates is the goyim-administration as instrument of Yahuah's (the LORD's) judgment — *O Ashshur (Assyrian), the rod of mine anger... Howbeit he meaneth not so* (Isaiah 10:5-7); *Nevukhadnetstsar (Nebuchadrezzar) the king of Babel (Babylon), my servant* (Jeremiah 25:9). The administration acts as instrument while meaning it not so. Rome will mock and scourge and crucify the King without knowing the substance of what they walk in.

*To mock, and to scourge, and to crucify him.* The three-fold specification lands point by point against the Tanakh prophetic witness. The mocking: *all they that see me laugh me to scorn: they shoot out the lip, they shake the head* (Psalm 22:7). The scourging: *I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair* (Isaiah 50:6). The crucifying: *but he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed* (Isaiah 53:5). *And the third day he shall rise again.* The resurrection-credential the King named as the only sign the generation would receive — the sign of Yonah (Jonah), *three days and three nights in the heart of the earth* (Matthew 12:40) — and the prophet Hosea's revival-substance: *after two days will he revive us: in the third day he will raise us up* (Hosea 6:2). The cross is not a defeat imposed by circumstances beyond the King's control; it is the substance the Son walks into knowingly and willingly under the Father's determination from before the foundation of the world.

## The cup, and greatness as service

*Then came to him the mother of Zavdai (Zebedee)'s children with her sons, worshipping him, and desiring a certain thing of him. And he said unto her, What wilt thou? She saith unto him, Grant that these my two sons may sit, the one on thy right hand, and the other on the left, in thy kingdom.* (Matthew 20:20-21)

The third passion-prediction has just closed. The King has placed the betrayal, the condemnation, the mocking and scourging and crucifying in the disciples' hearing — and the very next scene is the mother of Zavdai (Zebedee)'s sons coming to petition for thrones on the King's right and left in the kingdom as the disciples imagine it. The juxtaposition is the substance: the King names the cross, and the disciples ask for the highest-honor positions. The natural kingdoms' hierarchy still operates beneath their hearing.

The Markan parallel names the sons themselves as the petitioners — *Ya'aqov (James) and Yochanan (John)... Grant unto us that we may sit, one on thy right hand, and the other on thy left hand, in thy glory* (Mark 10:35-37). Both tellings are true: the family of Zavdai (Zebedee) walks the petition together, the mother walking it at the sons' request, and the King's answer addresses the sons directly. The right and left hands are the positions of highest honor in the king's court — *upon thy right hand did stand the queen in gold of Ophir* (Psalm 45:9); *Yahuah (the LORD) said unto Adoni (my Lord), Sit thou at my right hand* (Psalm 110:1). The petition reveals what the disciples have not internalized despite the three passion-predictions and the twelve-thrones statement at Matthew 19:28. That statement laid the apostolic thrones broadly, undifferentiated, corporate under the King's throne of glory; the petition is the natural kingdoms' hierarchy entering the twelve-thrones substance and ranking it — top of the hierarchy is right and left hand, everyone else below.

*But Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said, Ye know not what ye ask. Are ye able to drink of the cup that I shall drink of, and to be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with? They say unto him, We are able.* (Matthew 20:22)

*Ye know not what ye ask.* The thrones on the King's right and left attend the cup of suffering the King is about to drink. The disciples ask for the honor without the suffering that attends it. *Are ye able to drink of the cup that I shall drink of?* The cup reads at the Tanakh cup-of-wrath the prophets have carried throughout the prophetic corpus. *For in the hand of Yahuah (the LORD) there is a cup, and the wine is red... the wicked of the earth shall wring them out, and drink them* (Psalm 75:8). *Awake, awake, stand up, O Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), which hast drunk at the hand of Yahuah (the LORD) the cup of his fury... Behold, I have taken out of thine hand the cup of trembling* (Isaiah 51:17, 22). *Take the wine cup of this fury at my hand, and cause all the goyim (nations), to whom I send thee, to drink it* (Jeremiah 25:15). *The cup of Yahuah's (the LORD's) right hand shall be turned unto thee* (Habakkuk 2:16).

The cup the King is about to drink is the substance of Yahuah's (the LORD's) judgment on the substance of sin, poured on the Son of Adam on the cross. The cup he will pray to have pass at Gethsemane confirms it — *O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt* (Matthew 26:39). The King drinks the cup of wrath in the place of those the Father has known from the foundation of the world. And the cross opens the door home through the new-heart substance the prophet Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named — *a new heart also will I give you... And I will put my Ruach (Spirit) within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them* (Ezekiel 36:26-27). But the cross does not cancel the curse-system. Walking off the Way still bears the consequence; the curse still operates against the substantial rejection of the Father's ways. The cup the King drinks opens the door to the new heart the Spirit causes the disciple to walk in; it does not abolish the Torah the Spirit causes the disciple to keep.

*And to be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with.* The baptism reads in continuity with the cup — the substance of being overwhelmed by suffering, the Tanakh psalms of distress: *all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me* (Psalm 42:7); *the waters are come in unto my soul* (Psalm 69:1). This is not the literal immersion of Matthew 3; it is the metaphorical immersion into the cup of suffering. *We are able.* The sons claim what they do not yet understand — the same confidence Kefa (Peter) will walk in when he says *though all men shall be offended because of thee, yet will I never be offended* (Matthew 26:33) before the threefold denial that very night.

*And he saith unto them, Ye shall drink indeed of my cup, and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with: but to sit on my right hand, and on my left, is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared of my Father.* (Matthew 20:23)

*Ye shall drink indeed of my cup.* The King confirms the sons will drink — at the apostolic-mission-cost level, not at the substitutionary level. Ya'aqov (James) the son of Zavdai (Zebedee) is the first of the twelve to drink it: *Herodes (Herod) the king... killed Ya'aqov (James) the brother of Yochanan (John) with the sword* (Acts 12:1-2), about AD 44. And Yochanan (John) walks the long apostolic suffering — *I Yochanan (John), who also am your brother, and companion in tribulation... was in the isle that is called Patmos, for the word of Elohim (God)* (Revelation 1:9). Both sons drink the cup in the apostolic substance; neither drinks it in the substitutionary substance, which only the Son of Adam can drink in the once-for-all offering.

*But to sit on my right hand, and on my left, is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared of my Father.* The King preserves the Father's prerogative over the assignment of the positions. This reads at the canon's own Father-Son architecture. The Father is the Formless infinite, the source of all; the Son is the Formed, drawn out of the Formless, the One present in every pre-incarnate Tanakh encounter from the prophets onward. The Son's submission to the Father is the eternal substance of the Formed yielded to the Formless — the submission the Son walks in the flesh and walked before the flesh. *The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do* (John 5:19); *I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me* (John 6:38); *I and my Father are one* (John 10:30); *the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works* (John 14:10). The Son and the Father are one in the substance of the Formed drawn from the Formless, and the Son submits to the Father in that same architecture.

The substance is not the denial of the Son's divinity; it is the eternal submission of the Formed to the Formless the canon names. The inherited Trinity formula of three co-equal persons without the eternal submission flattens a submission the King himself walks in the gospel record — read the verse plainly and the co-equal reading has nowhere to put it. The modalist collapse of Father and Son into one undifferentiated being is also read out — the Son addresses the Father as another and speaks of the Father having prepared the thrones as a distinct substance the Son does not personally assign. The divinity the formula wanted to guard is already here, older than the formula: one Yahuah (God), Formless in source, Formed in expression, the Formed yielding to the Formless from the foundation through the cross and into the consummated kingdom. The thrones will be given to those for whom the Father has prepared them — the same Father-prerogative the householder walked in the vineyard. The kingdom-citizen's substance is not grasping after the positions but yielding to the Father's will.

*And when the ten heard it, they were moved with indignation against the two brethren.* (Matthew 20:24)

The ten are not indignant at the wrongness of the petition but at the priority of it — the two brothers asked first. All twelve walk the same natural kingdoms' hierarchy; the two sons of Zavdai (Zebedee) simply articulated what the other ten had been walking in without articulating. The natural substance still operates among the twelve despite the three passion-predictions, despite the twelve-thrones statement, despite *whosoever shall humble himself as this little child* (Matthew 18:4) and *of such is the kingdom of heaven* (Matthew 19:14).

*But Yahusha (Jesus) called them unto him, and said, Ye know that the princes of the goyim (Gentiles) exercise dominion over them, and they that are great exercise authority upon them. But it shall not be so among you: but whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister; And whosoever will be chief among you, let him be your servant: even as the Son of Adam came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.* (Matthew 20:25-28)

*Ye know that the princes of the goyim (Gentiles) exercise dominion over them.* The Greek for *exercise dominion* is *katakyrieuousin* — *lord-it-down-upon*; for *exercise authority,* *katexousiazousin* — *authoritate-down-upon.* Both carry the *kata-* prefix of dominating down from above by force. The King names the natural kingdoms' administration, and the Tanakh prophetic witness names what operates behind it — the watcher-rebellion administration. The prophet Daniel encountered the princes behind the human kingdoms — *the prince of the kingdom of Paras (Persia) withstood me one and twenty days: but, lo, Mika'el (Michael), one of the chief princes, came to help me* (Daniel 10:13); *the prince of Yavan (Grecia) shall come* (Daniel 10:20). The apostle Sha'ul (Paul) carries it forward — *we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places* (Ephesians 6:12). And the apostle Yochanan (John) closes it — *the whole world lieth in wickedness* (1 John 5:19). The natural kingdoms of the present age operate under the watcher-rebellion administration the Father has given them over to until the day of Yahuah (the LORD) comes and the substance is burned and the consummated kingdom of the Son stands without rival.

*But it shall not be so among you.* The categorical inversion. The kingdom-citizen's substance is not power flowing down by force but greatness flowing up as service. *Whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister* — Greek *diakonos,* the table-server who attends the meal at the others' need — *and whosoever will be chief among you, let him be your servant* — Greek *doulos,* the bond-servant yielded to the other. The substance runs through the whole gospel and will be restated at Matthew 23:11-12, modeled at the foot-washing — *I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you* (John 13:15) — and laid by the apostle Sha'ul (Paul) in the Philippian Christology: *let this mind be in you, which was also in Messiah Yahusha (Christ Jesus): Who, being in the form of Elohim (God)... made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant... and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross* (Philippians 2:5-8). The apostle Kefa (Peter) lays it on the elders — *neither as being lords over Elohim (God)'s heritage, but being ensamples to the flock* (1 Peter 5:3).

## The ransom for many

*Even as the Son of Adam came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.* The chapter's framework-major moment lands at verse 28 — the King's own mouth identifying himself as the Tanakh Suffering-Servant of Isaiah 53 and the *kofer*-ransom of the Torah.

*The Son of Adam.* The title carries the Adamic seed of promise the Father has carried through the prophets to the substantial ransom paid by the Son of Adam himself. *Came not to be ministered unto, but to minister* — the substance of the coming-not-to-be-served-but-to-serve the King now commands the kingdom-citizens to walk.

*And to give his life a ransom for many.* The Greek is *dounai tēn psychēn autou lytron anti pollōn* — *to give his soul as ransom in-place-of many.* The *lytron* reads at the Tanakh *kofer*, the substantial ransom paid in place of the life of the one redeemed. *If there be laid on him a sum of money, then he shall give for the ransom of his life whatsoever is laid upon him* (Exodus 21:30). The Torah's half-shekel atonement-money is *kofer* substance — *every man a ransom for his soul unto Yahuah (the LORD)... to make an atonement for your souls* (Exodus 30:12, 16) — the same half-shekel the King referenced at the temple-tax of Matthew 17:24-27. And the Torah names what the *kofer* cannot cover: *ye shall take no satisfaction (kofer) for the life of a murderer... the land cannot be cleansed of the blood that is shed therein, but by the blood of him that shed it* (Numbers 35:31, 33). The substance no natural *kofer* could cover is the very substance the King fulfills — the substantial blood the Son of Adam alone could give. The prophet-king David named what no natural man can do: *none of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to Elohim (God) a ransom for him: (for the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceaseth for ever)* (Psalm 49:7-8). No natural man can give it; only the Father's Son can.

The prophet Yeshayahu's (Isaiah's) Servant-passage is the central Tanakh substance the King draws from:

*Yet it pleased Yahuah (the LORD) to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin... by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities... and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.* (Isaiah 53:10-12)

The *many* (Hebrew *rabbim*) the Servant justifies, whose iniquities he bears, is the *many* (Greek *pollōn*) the King names at Matthew 20:28. The substance is a direct quotation by substance of the Suffering-Servant the prophet named seven centuries before the incarnation. *He was wounded for our transgressions... and Yahuah (the LORD) hath laid on him the iniquity of us all* (Isaiah 53:5-6) is the substance the King explicitly claims as his own coming. This is not the inherited-Christian reading grafted onto the passage after the fact; it is the King's own mouth identifying himself with the Servant the prophet named. The prophet Daniel's seventy-weeks substance names what the atonement accomplishes — *to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness... Messiah shall be cut off, but not for himself* (Daniel 9:24, 26).

The ransom opens the door home the canon's own architecture names. It does not cancel the curse-system; it is the substantial opening of the door — the new heart of Ezekiel 36:26-27 and the new covenant of Jeremiah 31:31-34: *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts... for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more.* The ransom opens the new heart and the new covenant — the Torah written on the inward parts and the Spirit causing the disciple to walk in the Father's statutes. What the ransom does not do is cancel the consequence for the substantial rejection of the Way. The sola-fide cancellation of the Torah is the substance the canon itself reads out — the cross is the door home and the new heart, and the Torah the Spirit causes the disciple to walk is what the door opens onto.

The *for many* (*anti pollōn*) reads at the architecture the prophets and the King have laid throughout the canon. The *many* is the *rabbim* of the Servant-passage, the substantial multitude of citizens the Father has known from the foundation of the world — the citizens of the house of Yahudah (Judah) that retained the tribal identity, the citizens of the scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel) now being gathered home through the King's proclamation, and the Father's foreknown citizens through the whole gathering-arc. The *for many* is not the *for all* the universalist substance would collapse it into without disposition; the *many* is not every soul in every condition regardless of the substance walked. And it is not the *for the few* the limited-atonement substance would close it off into; the *many* is not a restricted substance shut off from the broad multitude the Father has known. It is BOTH-AND, not EITHER-OR — the substance the Father has known from the foundation, and the substance walked that the citizen's walking reveals.

The apostolic witness carries the ransom-substance forward as one voice. *Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption (apolytrōsin) that is in Messiah Yahusha (Christ Jesus): Whom Elohim (God) hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood* (Romans 3:24-25). *One mediator between Elohim (God) and men... Who gave himself a ransom (antilytron) for all* (1 Timothy 2:5-6). *By his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us* (Hebrews 9:12). *Ye were redeemed... with the precious blood of Messiah (Christ), as of a lamb without blemish and without spot* (1 Peter 1:18-19). *Thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to Elohim (God) by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation* (Revelation 5:9). The whole apostolic witness carries forward what the King named at Matthew 20:28 — the King's own mouth, Tanakh Suffering-Servant self-identification at the chapter's pivot.

## The blind men of Yericho — Son of David

*And as they departed from Yericho (Jericho), a great multitude followed him. And, behold, two blind men sitting by the way side, when they heard that Yahusha (Jesus) passed by, cried out, saying, Have mercy on us, O Lord, thou Son of David. And the multitude rebuked them, because they should hold their peace: but they cried the more, saying, Have mercy on us, O Lord, thou Son of David.* (Matthew 20:29-31)

The chapter closes at Yericho (Jericho), the last Tanakh-locked city before the ascent to Yerushalayim (Jerusalem). Yericho is the city of the original entry into the land under Yahoshua (Joshua) son of Nun — the seven days of circling, the seventh-day shout, *the wall fell down flat, so that the people went up into the city* (Joshua 6:20) — the Father acting substantially on behalf of the people at the entry. And it carries the curse on the rebuilders: *cursed be the man before Yahuah (the LORD), that riseth up and buildeth this city Yericho (Jericho)* (Joshua 6:26), fulfilled in the days of Ach'av (Ahab) when *Chi'el (Hiel) the Bethelite build Yericho (Jericho): he laid the foundation thereof in Aviram (Abiram) his firstborn* (1 Kings 16:34). The King passes through Yericho on the final approach to Yerushalayim — the substance the original entry into the land walked through, now walked through into the new entry the cross and resurrection will open.

*Two blind men sitting by the way side.* The marginalized substance lands at the roadside — outside the walking multitude, dependent on what the multitude passes them by with. The Markan parallel names one of them, *blind Bar-Timai (Bartimaeus), the son of Timai (Timaeus)* (Mark 10:46); both tellings are true — the Matthean account preserves the corporate cry of the two, the Markan the primary voice within it. *When they heard that Yahusha (Jesus) passed by, cried out, saying, Have mercy on us, O Lord, thou Son of David.* The *Son of David* title is the Tanakh Davidic-Messianic substance the prophets carried throughout the canon — the Davidic covenant at *I will set up thy seed after thee... I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever* (2 Samuel 7:12-13); the rod of Yishai (Jesse) at *there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Yishai (Jesse)* (Isaiah 11:1); the righteous Branch at *I will raise unto David a righteous Branch... and this is his name whereby he shall be called, Yahuah Tsidkenu (THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS)* (Jeremiah 23:5-6); the David-the-servant-king of the restored twelve tribes at *I will set up one shepherd over them... even my servant David* (Ezekiel 34:23) and *David my servant shall be king over them; and they all shall have one shepherd... and my servant David shall be their prince for ever* (Ezekiel 37:24-25).

The blind men recognize what the surrounding multitude cannot see. Physically without sight, they see the substance the seeing multitude misses — the cause-and-effect of hearing the King's own mouth names at John 10:26-27, operating beneath physical sight or blindness. The sheep hear the Shepherd by being sheep; the blind men who are sheep hear and call. The Matthean gospel has preserved this pattern of Davidic-Messianic recognition by the marginalized — the Galilean two blind men of Matthew 9:27-31, the Canaanite woman's cry of Matthew 15:22, here the Yericho (Jericho) two blind men — anticipating the triumphal-entry *Hoshanna (Hosanna) to the Son of David* of Matthew 21:9, the children in the temple at Matthew 21:15, and the King's own question about the Son of David at Matthew 22:41-46.

*The multitude rebuked them, because they should hold their peace.* The system-substance does not want the recognition disturbed — the same pre-rejection the disciples walked toward at the children the King rebuked at Matthew 19:13-15. *But they cried the more.* The blind men's persistence is the Tanakh substance of the psalms of distress: *out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O Yahuah (LORD)* (Psalm 130:1); *have mercy upon me, O Yahuah (LORD); for I am weak* (Psalm 6:2).

*And Yahusha (Jesus) stood still, and called them, and said, What will ye that I shall do unto you? They say unto him, Lord, that our eyes may be opened. So Yahusha (Jesus) had compassion on them, and touched their eyes: and immediately their eyes received sight, and they followed him.* (Matthew 20:32-34)

The King stops. Against the multitude that would have the blind men hold their peace, he walks into the attention to the marginalized the surrounding substance dismisses. He stops, calls, and asks — *what will ye that I shall do unto you?* — honoring them with the substance of his own attention to their petition. *Lord, that our eyes may be opened.* The petition asks for the Messianic credential the prophet Yeshayahu (Isaiah) named for the Messianic day — *then the eyes of the blind shall be opened* (Isaiah 35:5); *I... give thee for a covenant of the people, for a light of the goyim (Gentiles); To open the blind eyes* (Isaiah 42:6-7); *Yahuah (the LORD) openeth the eyes of the blind* (Psalm 146:8). The same credential the King had pointed Yochanan (John) to at Matthew 11:4-5 — *the blind receive their sight* — he fulfills here.

*So Yahusha (Jesus) had compassion on them.* The Greek *splanchnistheis* — moved in the inward parts. He touches their eyes; the blindness is removed; the sight is given. *And they followed him.* The chapter closes at the discipleship the hearing-and-seeing produces. The cause-and-effect of hearing the parable laid down at *many be called, but few chosen* lands here at the substance: the blind men who heard, hear and call; the blind men who saw, see and follow. The next chapter will open with the triumphal entry into Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — the colt and the foal, the multitudes spreading garments and branches and crying *Hoshanna (Hosanna) to the Son of David: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Matthew 21:9), the cleansing of the temple at the house-of-prayer substance of Jeremiah 7, the cursing of the fig tree, and the question of the King's authority that opens the controversy-section of the Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) week.
