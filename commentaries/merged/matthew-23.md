## The system, not the people — the lens that governs the whole chapter

Matthew 23 is the King of Yashar'el (Israel)'s final public discourse against the scribes and the Pharisees — the longest direct condemnation in the gospel — and before the first woe lands, the lens the whole chapter turns on has to be set, because the inherited Christian reading flattens it into something it is not. The chapter is spoken *against a system,* not against a people. The leadership the King names out loud is the Pharisaic-Sadducean shepherding system that victimizes the very people it claims to feed; it is not the Yahudim (Jews) as a people, and it cannot be read that way without contradicting the chapter's own ending, where the King weeps over Yerushalayim (Jerusalem)'s children and longs to gather them.

The King himself is a Yahudi (Jew) — born of Miryam (Mary) of the tribe of Yahudah (Judah), circumcised on the eighth day per the Torah, who kept the appointed feasts, the Shabbat (Sabbath), and the Torah his whole life. The disciples standing with him are Yahudim (Jews). The multitudes he addresses in the opening verse are Yahudim (Jews). What he stands against is exactly what the prophets stood against before him: the leadership corruption that makes victims of the flock. Yeshayahu (Isaiah) stood in the gate against the leadership of the First Temple; Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) stood in the gate against it; Mikhah (Micah) and Amos did the same. The King steps into that same prophetic line and turns the same indictment on the leadership of the Second Temple. This is not anti-Yahudi (anti-Jewish). It is anti-system, and the people are the ones the system has wounded.

And the same diagnostic the chapter aims at the Pharisaic-Sadducean system reaches forward to four costumes that come after it: the Galatian agitators of the apostle Sha'ul (Paul)'s letters, modern institutional Christianity, modern Hebrew Roots' false-inclusion teaching, and Islam. Five costumes, one architecture. Same flesh-credential gospel, same voiding of the Torah, same denial of the Father's prophetic gathering of the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel). The chapter walks the woes at the leadership of the Second Temple, and the same woes walk at every leadership system since that wears the costume.

## The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Mosheh's seat

*Then spake Yahusha (Jesus) to the multitude, and to his disciples, Saying, The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Mosheh's (Moses's) seat: All therefore whatsoever they bid you observe, that observe and do; but do not ye after their works: for they say, and do not.* (Matthew 23:1-3)

The King opens by addressing the multitudes and the disciples — not by turning first on the scribes and the Pharisees, though that turn will come at verse 13. The address-frame matters: the people are the audience he walks toward, the leadership the thing he names in their hearing. This is the prophet's posture exactly — Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) standing in the gate of Yahuah's (the LORD's) house and proclaiming the word there (Jeremiah 7:1-15), the corruption named aloud in the hearing of the flock it has plundered.

*Mosheh's (Moses's) seat* — the *moshav Mosheh* — is the teaching seat from which the Torah was read aloud and expounded in the synagogue. The King acknowledges the legitimate thing the scribes and the Pharisees were entrusted with: the public reading and exposition of the Torah is a real office, and the Torah they read from that seat is the same Torah the Father gave at Sinai through the hand of Mosheh (Moses). That Torah is to be walked. The carefully drawn line is between the Torah they read from the seat and the works they walk in their own lives — *they say, and do not.*

This is not a blanket endorsement of the Pharisaic oral tradition. The King had already walked against that tradition directly, naming it as *making the commandment of Elohim (God) of none effect by your tradition* and citing Yeshayahu (Isaiah): *this people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men* (Matthew 15:8-9). The oral tradition — *the traditions of my fathers* Sha'ul (Paul) names that he himself once walked in (Galatians 1:14), the *yoke ... which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear* the apostles will name at the Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) council (Acts 15:10) — is a different thing from the Torah read from the seat. The instruction here is to walk the Torah the leadership reads, not the works the leadership lives. What the chapter is pointing toward is the doing of the Father's will: *not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven* (Matthew 7:21).

*For they bind heavy burdens and grievous to be borne, and lay them on men's shoulders; but they themselves will not move them with one of their fingers.* (Matthew 23:4)

The heavy burden stands against what the King had already offered: *come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light* (Matthew 11:28-30). The contrast is direct — the oral tradition is heavy; the Father's Torah is light. The apostle Yochanan (John) carries the same line: *for this is the love of Elohim (God), that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous* (1 John 5:3). The *yoke* the apostles refused at the council was never the Torah — the apostles after the council still walked the Torah themselves; Sha'ul (Paul) went into the Temple to pay for four men's offerings to demonstrate it (Acts 21:20-26). The yoke they refused to lay on the scattered seed coming home was the Pharisaic burden the agitators were trying to impose. The Torah the Father gave is light; the additions to it are heavy.

*But all their works they do for to be seen of men: they make broad their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments, And love the uppermost rooms at feasts, and the chief seats in the synagogues, And greetings in the markets, and to be called of men, Rabbi, Rabbi.* (Matthew 23:5-7)

The *phylacteries* are the Tefillin — the binding of the Torah words on the hand and between the eyes that the Torah itself commanded: *thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes* (Deuteronomy 6:8; and Deuteronomy 11:18; Exodus 13:9, 16). The *borders of their garments* are the Tsitsit, the fringes the Torah commanded: *bid them that they make them fringes in the borders of their garments throughout their generations, and that they put upon the fringe of the borders a ribband of blue: And it shall be unto you for a fringe, that ye may look upon it, and remember all the commandments of Yahuah (the LORD), and do them* (Numbers 15:38-40; and Deuteronomy 22:12). The King walked the Tsitsit himself — the *hem of his garment* the woman with the issue of blood touched (Matthew 9:20) and the multitudes touched (Matthew 14:36) was the fringe the Torah commanded. He is not naming the Tefillin or the Tsitsit as the problem; he is naming the *making broad* and the *enlarging* of them for display — the turning of the Torah into a stage prop.

The *uppermost rooms at feasts, and the chief seats in the synagogues, And greetings in the markets, and to be called of men, Rabbi, Rabbi* is the appetite for religious status the King had already walked against — at the *except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven* of Matthew 18:3, and the *whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister; And whosoever will be chief among you, let him be your servant* of Matthew 20:26-27. The leadership reaches for the very status the kingdom inverts. *Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall* (Proverbs 16:18).

## Call no man your father upon the earth

*But be not ye called Rabbi: for one is your Master, even Messiah (Christ); and all ye are brethren. And call no man your father upon the earth: for one is your Father, which is in heaven. Neither be ye called masters: for one is your Master, even Messiah (Christ). But he that is greatest among you shall be your servant. And whosoever shall exalt himself shall be abased; and he that shall humble himself shall be exalted.* (Matthew 23:8-12)

The line is drawn carefully. The King is not categorically forbidding the word *rabbi* as a marker of a teaching relationship — Sha'ul (Paul) names himself *a teacher of the Gentiles* (1 Timothy 2:7), and the disciples call the King *Rabbi* throughout the Yochanan (John) account without correction (John 1:38, 1:49, 3:2, 4:31, 6:25, 9:2, 11:8). What he walks against is the appetite for *Rabbi* as a badge of elevated status. The same with *call no man your father upon the earth.* The Torah's fifth commandment carries the natural father the King does not overturn — *honour thy father and thy mother* (Exodus 20:12), and the King himself honored his mother Miryam (Mary) from the cross, charging Yochanan (John) to take her into his own home (John 19:26-27). Sha'ul (Paul) names himself a spiritual father — *for in Messiah (Christ) Yahusha (Jesus) I have begotten you through the gospel* (1 Corinthians 4:15) — without contradicting a word of this. What the King strikes at is *religious paternity* — the man who installs himself as religious father over the people he shepherds. It is the substance the Roman Catholic priestly paternity walks, and the substance the modern Christian pastoral office walks wherever the pastor sets himself up as the people's religious father between them and the Father in heaven.

The kingdom inversion closes the introduction: *he that is greatest among you shall be your servant. And whosoever shall exalt himself shall be abased; and he that shall humble himself shall be exalted.* This is the spine the whole chapter rests on. *A man's pride shall bring him low: but honour shall uphold the humble in spirit* (Proverbs 29:23). *Yahuah (the LORD) resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble... Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up* (James 4:6, 10). *Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of Elohim (God), that he may exalt you in due time* (1 Peter 5:6). The King walked it himself at the foot-washing: *ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet* (John 13:13-14). The leadership reaches for status; the kingdom is built on the inversion of it.

## The seven woes — one continuous prophetic indictment

What follows is not seven separate teachings but one continuous direct condemnation moving across seven discrete strokes. The Greek word for *woe* — *ouai* — is the prophetic word Yeshayahu (Isaiah) walked across the six woes of Isaiah 5:8-23 against the leadership of his day, the same word Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) walked (Jeremiah 22:13; 23:1), the same word Habakkuk walked (Habakkuk 2:6-19). The seven woes are not an innovation; they are the prophetic indictment carried forward against the leadership of the current generation. And *hypocrites* — *hypokritai,* the stage-actors, those who play a part the heart does not walk — names the gap the introduction already named: *they say, and do not.*

**The first woe — they shut the kingdom in men's faces.**

*But woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye shut up the kingdom of heaven against men: for ye neither go in yourselves, neither suffer ye them that are entering to go in.* (Matthew 23:13)

The leadership was entrusted with the stewardship of the kingdom — the same stewardship the wicked-husbandmen parable of the prior chapter walked out (Matthew 21:33-46) — and it refuses to enter and refuses to let others enter. Luke carries the same stroke: *woe unto you, lawyers! for ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered* (Luke 11:52). The leadership holds the key it will not use, and will not pass to others. Layer on layer of oral tradition has been built between the people and the Torah, layer on layer of credential between the people and the kingdom the Father is bringing in through the King. The kingdom doors close. And the system, not the people, is what the King names.

**The textual variant — they devour widows' houses.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye devour widows' houses, and for a pretence make long prayer: therefore ye shall receive the greater damnation.* (Matthew 23:14)

The verse stands in some manuscripts and not in others; the substance is preserved in the parallels regardless (Mark 12:40; Luke 20:47). It names the leadership devouring the economic life of the most vulnerable — the widow — while making a show of piety in long public prayer. The Torah has guarded the widow from its opening pages: *ye shall not afflict any widow, or fatherless child* (Exodus 22:22); *cursed be he that perverteth the judgment of the stranger, fatherless, and widow* (Deuteronomy 27:19); through Yeshayahu (Isaiah) — *seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow* (Isaiah 1:17) — through Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) (Jeremiah 7:6; 22:3), Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) (Ezekiel 22:7), Zekharyah (Zechariah) (Zechariah 7:10), and Mal'akhi (Malachi): *I will be a swift witness... against those that oppress the hireling in his wages, the widow, and the fatherless* (Malachi 3:5). The leadership walks against the very Torah it reads from Mosheh's (Moses's) seat, and uses the religious display as the cover for the economic devouring.

**The second woe — the proselyte made twofold more a child of Gehinnom.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye compass sea and land to make one proselyte, and when he is made, ye make him twofold more the child of gehenna than yourselves.* (Matthew 23:15)

This is the load-bearing diagnostic of the chapter — the false-inclusion gospel named at its root. The Pharisaic system ran an active conversion mission across the diaspora, *compassing sea and land* to bring outsiders under its system through circumcision and the keeping of the oral tradition — categorical conversion into the Pharisaic credential-system. This is not the broader stranger Yeshayahu (Isaiah) names — *neither let the son of the stranger, that hath joined himself to Yahuah (the LORD), speak, saying, Yahuah (the LORD) hath utterly separated me from his people... Even them will I bring to my holy mountain* (Isaiah 56:3, 7) — for the Isaiah 56 stranger is the scattered seed coming home into the covenant identity the exile cost them, not a categorical conversion of the nations. The proselyte the Pharisaic system made was converted into the counterfeit, and *when he is made,* he is *twofold more the child of gehenna* — doubled in the very system the woes condemn, because the convert walks it with a zeal that long familiarity has not yet tempered.

This is the architecture Sha'ul (Paul) wrote his whole letter to the Galatians against — the agitators trying to impose circumcision and the oral tradition on the scattered seed coming home through the apostolic mission. *I marvel that ye are so soon removed from him that called you into the grace of Messiah (Christ) unto another gospel: Which is not another; but there be some that trouble you, and would pervert the gospel of Messiah (Christ)* (Galatians 1:6-7). *Behold, I Sha'ul (Paul) say unto you, that if ye be circumcised, Messiah (Christ) shall profit you nothing* (Galatians 5:2). *As many as desire to make a fair shew in the flesh, they constrain you to be circumcised; only lest they should suffer persecution for the cross of Messiah (Christ). For neither they themselves who are circumcised keep the law* (Galatians 6:12-13). The apostles named the same lie at the council: *certain men which came down from Yahudah (Judaea) taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Mosheh (Moses), ye cannot be saved* (Acts 15:1). The error was never circumcision itself — Sha'ul (Paul) circumcised Timothy with his own hand (Acts 16:1-3). The error is making the flesh-credential the doorway to salvation, the conversion-into-the-system standing in for the Father's prophetic gathering of the twelve tribes.

The same architecture wears five costumes. The Pharisaic-Sadducean system voids the Torah by **addition** — piling oral tradition on top of it. The Galatian agitators void it by **inversion** — turning the apostolic gospel of the gathering into a flesh-credential conversion. Modern institutional Christianity voids it by **subtraction** — declaring the Torah the King walks as light abolished, treating the covenant people as replaced by the church, and offering the believer's confession as the new proselyte-credential. Modern Hebrew Roots' false-inclusion voids it by **collapse** — *anyone who Torah-keeps is Yashar'el (Israel),* the Pharisaic proselyte pattern in Torah language, dissolving the paternal-bloodline distinction the prophets maintain. Islam voids it by **transfer** — moving the covenant to a different bloodline through the Shahada confession. Five costumes, one substance: same flesh-credential gospel, same voiding of the Torah, same denial of the gathering of the twelve tribes.

And each fails the one standing test. *And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him* (1 John 2:3-4). The Pharisaic proselyte keeps the oral tradition, not the Torah of the Father, and fails it. Institutional Christianity teaches the believer not to keep the Shabbat (Sabbath) the Father hallowed (Genesis 2:3), not to keep the feasts the Father commanded (Leviticus 23), not to walk the Torah the King walked himself, and fails it. The Hebrew Roots collapse and the Islamic transfer fail it in their own forms. The King names the prototype here, and hands the reader the test to apply to whatever system stands in front of him next.

**The third woe — blind guides and the oath-games.**

*Woe unto you, ye blind guides, which say, Whosoever shall swear by the temple, it is nothing; but whosoever shall swear by the gold of the temple, he is a debtor! Ye fools and blind: for whether is greater, the gold, or the temple that sanctifieth the gold?... And he that shall swear by heaven, sweareth by the throne of Elohim (God), and by him that sitteth thereon.* (Matthew 23:16-22)

The Torah's word on oaths is direct: every vow binds, every oath counts. *Ye shall not swear by my name falsely* (Leviticus 19:12). *If a man vow a vow unto Yahuah (the LORD), or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond; he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth* (Numbers 30:2; and Deuteronomy 23:21-23). The oral tradition built a casuistry of binding and non-binding oaths around it — swear by the temple and it counts for nothing, but swear by the gold of the temple and you are bound; swear by the altar and nothing, but swear by the gift on it and you are bound. The system handed the leadership a verbal escape hatch, words shaped like an oath that could be evacuated of their substance by the technical form. The King had already settled it: *Swear not at all; neither by heaven; for it is Elohim's (God's) throne... But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil* (Matthew 5:34-37). Ya'aqov (James) carries the same: *swear not, neither by heaven, neither by earth, neither by any other oath: but let your yea be yea; and your nay, nay* (James 5:12). They are *blind guides* — Yeshayahu (Isaiah)'s *his watchmen are blind* (Isaiah 56:10) — the blind leading the blind, both falling into the ditch (Matthew 15:14).

**The fourth woe — tithing herbs while omitting the weight of the Torah.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. Ye blind guides, which strain at a gnat, and swallow a camel.* (Matthew 23:23-24)

The tithe is a real command — *all the tithe of the land... is Yahuah's (the LORD's): it is holy unto Yahuah (the LORD)* (Leviticus 27:30; and Deuteronomy 14:22-23) — and the Pharisaic system extended it meticulously to the smallest garden herbs. The King does not strike the tithe. He strikes the tithe walked while *judgment, mercy, and faith* are abandoned — the prophetic summary Mikhah (Micah) had already named: *what doth Yahuah (the LORD) require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy Elohim (thy God)?* (Micah 6:8). The same word ran through Hosheia (Hosea) — *I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of Elohim (God) more than burnt offerings* (Hosea 6:6), which the King had already cited (Matthew 9:13; 12:7) — through Yeshayahu (Isaiah): *Wash you, make you clean... cease to do evil; Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed* (Isaiah 1:16-17) — through Amos: *let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream* (Amos 5:24) — through Zekharyah (Zechariah): *execute true judgment, and shew mercy and compassions every man to his brother* (Zechariah 7:9).

The line that breaks the inherited reading is *these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.* The post-Reformation collapse reads the weightier matters as having *replaced* the rest of the Torah — judgment-and-mercy-and-faith now standing in for the tithe and the commandment. The King says the opposite to its face: do these, and do not leave the other undone. The Torah is both-and, not either-or. The tithe of mint is to be walked, and judgment, mercy, and faith are to be walked. This is the heart-Torah the prophets named — *Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God) will circumcise thine heart... to love Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God) with all thine heart* (Deuteronomy 30:6); *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts* (Jeremiah 31:33); *a new heart also will I give you... and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them* (Ezekiel 36:26-27) — the whole Torah written on the heart, from the tithe of the herb to the mercy of the heart, one substance, not a weightier slice that cancels the rest. *Strain at a gnat, and swallow a camel* closes the woe in irony: both the gnat and the camel are unclean (Leviticus 11:4-7; 11:20-23), and the leadership strains the wine to catch the smallest uncleanness while swallowing the largest one whole.

**The fifth woe — the clean cup and the foul inside.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye make clean the outside of the cup and of the platter, but within they are full of extortion and excess. Thou blind Pharisee, cleanse first that which is within the cup and platter, that the outside of them may be clean also.* (Matthew 23:25-26)

This is the heart-defilement the King had already taught: *not that which goeth into the mouth defileth a man; but that which cometh out of the mouth, this defileth a man* (Matthew 15:11). The same Pharisaic ritual-purity that had challenged him over unwashed hands (Matthew 15:1-9) is the outside of the cup; the heart is the inside. *For Yahuah (the LORD) seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but Yahuah (the LORD) looketh on the heart* (1 Samuel 16:7). The *extortion and excess* — *harpagēs kai akrasias,* plunder and unrestraint — is what the leadership walks in private while walking ritual purity in public, the same private devouring the widows'-houses woe named. *Cleanse first that which is within,* and the outside follows. Sha'ul (Paul) carries the same line straight at the Pharisaic substance: *he is not a Yahudi (Jew), which is one outwardly... but he is a Yahudi (Jew), which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of Elohim (God)* (Romans 2:28-29). That is not a redefinition of *Yahudi (Jew)* into a spiritual category — it is the same point the King makes here: the Father reads the inside, and the leadership has polished only the outside.

**The sixth woe — whitewashed tombs.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye are like unto whited sepulchres, which indeed appear beautiful outward, but are within full of dead men's bones, and of all uncleanness. Even so ye also outwardly appear righteous unto men, but within ye are full of hypocrisy and iniquity.* (Matthew 23:27-28)

The image draws on the Torah the leadership reads from the seat — corpse defilement: *he that toucheth the dead body of any man shall be unclean seven days* (Numbers 19:11). The Pharisaic practice was to whitewash the tombs around Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) before the Pesach (Passover) feast so pilgrims would not accidentally touch an unmarked grave and be defiled for the feast. The whitewash was a visible warning — *here is a tomb, do not touch.* The King reads the image straight into the leadership: they are the whitewashed tomb. The outside is beautiful — the broadened Tefillin, the enlarged Tsitsit, the chief seats — and the inside is dead bones and uncleanness. The same covering Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named: *one built up a wall, and, lo, others daubed it with untempered morter: Say unto them which daub it with untempered morter, that it shall fall* (Ezekiel 13:10-11). The whitewash falls; the leadership is itself the corpse-defilement the whitewash was warning against.

**The seventh woe — the measure of the fathers, and the serpent's seed.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous, And say, If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets. Wherefore ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets. Fill ye up then the measure of your fathers. Ye serpents, ye generation of vipers, how can ye escape the damnation of gehenna? Wherefore, behold, I send unto you prophets, and wise men, and scribes: and some of them ye shall kill and crucify; and some of them shall ye scourge in your synagogues, and persecute them from city to city: That upon you may come all the righteous blood shed upon the earth, from the blood of righteous Hevel (Abel) unto the blood of Zecharyah (Zechariah) son of Berekhyahu (Barachias), whom ye slew between the temple and the altar. Verily I say unto you, All these things shall come upon this generation.* (Matthew 23:29-36)

This is what the chapter has been walking toward. The leadership builds the tombs of the prophets their own fathers killed, and disowns the killing with the mouth — *if we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers* — while owning it with the life. The disowning is itself the witness: *ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.* Their own words name their paternity — *the days of our fathers* — and they walk the same prophet-killing now, at the prophets the Father is sending in their own generation.

*Fill ye up then the measure of your fathers* is the Father's patience named in the Torah's own grammar: *the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full* (Genesis 15:16). The Father extends the time for the measure to fill before the judgment falls — *To me belongeth vengeance and recompence; their foot shall slide in due time* (Deuteronomy 32:35). Sha'ul (Paul) carries the same prophetic line — *the Yahudim (Jews): Who both killed the Lord Yahusha (Jesus), and their own prophets, and have persecuted us... to fill up their sins alway* (1 Thessalonians 2:14-16) — and the system-not-people posture holds even there: Sha'ul (Paul) is himself a Yahudi (Jew), and the indictment is of the leadership that walked the killing, not of the Yahudim (Jews) as a people.

*Ye serpents, ye generation of vipers* — *opheis, gennēmata echidnōn* — names the seed of the serpent. This is the seed-war the Tanakh opens at *I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed* (Genesis 3:15) — two seeds, two lineages, running across the whole of scripture: Hevel (Abel) and Qayin (Cain) in Genesis 4, the watcher-rebellion of Genesis 6:1-4 that 1 Enoch names, Noach (Noah) preserved in his generations through an unmixed bloodline. It is the wheat and the tares the King had already taught: *the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil* (Matthew 13:38-39). The tares are not always outside the camp; they operate from within. The King names the same seed at the leadership in the Yochanan (John) account — *ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning* (John 8:44) — and Yochanan (John) the Witness had named it at the leadership coming to his baptism: *O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come?* (Matthew 3:7).

This is the place the inherited Christian reading goes wrong, and the lens has to hold. *Ye serpents, ye generation of vipers* is not a condemnation of the Yahudim (Jews) as a people. It names the seed of the serpent woven *into* the leadership — the tares among the wheat. The seed-war runs within the people of Yashar'el (Israel) exactly the way it runs within every people of the earth. The King is a Yahudi (Jew); the apostles are Yahudim (Jews); the multitudes he addresses in verse 1 are Yahudim (Jews); and the chapter's final movement weeps over gathering Yerushalayim (Jerusalem)'s children. The serpent's seed is a lineage within the people, not the people themselves.

*From the blood of righteous Hevel (Abel) unto the blood of Zecharyah (Zechariah)* spans the whole Hebrew canon. Hevel (Abel) is the first righteous blood the serpent's seed shed: *Qayin (Cain) rose up against Hevel (Abel) his brother, and slew him... the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground* (Genesis 4:8, 10) — the blood the writer of Hebrews names *that speaketh better things* (Hebrews 12:24). Zecharyah (Zechariah) is the last prophet killed in the Hebrew canon order, stoned in the Temple court: *they conspired against him, and stoned him with stones at the commandment of the king in the court of the house of Yahuah (the LORD)* (2 Chronicles 24:21). The Hebrew canon closes with Chronicles, so the King names the first prophet-blood and the last — the whole sweep of the prophet-killing pattern, beginning to end. The patronymic *son of Berekhyahu (Barachias)* some readers stumble over — the Zecharyah (Zechariah) of 2 Chronicles 24 is named son of Yehoyada (Jehoiada), while the writing prophet Zecharyah (Zechariah) is named son of Berekhyahu (Berechiah) at Zechariah 1:1. The King's claim is not a canon-trivia puzzle; it is one substance walked across both: the leadership has walked the prophet-killing from the first blood of the canon to the last, and now walks it at the prophets the Father is sending.

*Behold, I send unto you prophets, and wise men, and scribes: and some of them ye shall kill and crucify* walks forward into the apostolic age. Stephen named it — *which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One; of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers* (Acts 7:52) — and was stoned by the leadership for it (Acts 7:59). Ya'aqov (James) the brother of the King was killed under the same leadership. Sha'ul (Paul) carried the scourgings in his own body: *of the Yahudim (Jews) five times received I forty stripes save one. Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned* (2 Corinthians 11:24-25).

And *all these things shall come upon this generation* names the leadership of the current generation directly. It points forward to what the King will make explicit when he leaves the Temple — *there shall not be left here one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down* (Matthew 24:2) — the 70 CE Roman destruction of the Second Temple, a substantial judgment of the leadership. But it is not the completion of the Day of Yahuah (the LORD). The Tanakh's Day of Yahuah — *the great and the terrible day of Yahuah (the LORD)* of Joel 2-3, *the day of Yahuah (the LORD)... cruel both with wrath and fierce anger* of Isaiah 13, the Gog-and-Magog day of Ezekiel 38-39, the day Zekharyah (Zechariah) sets on the Mount of Olives in Zechariah 14 — remains future. The preterist collapse that reads the whole Olivet discourse as finished at 70 CE fails the substance. The judgment of 70 CE points forward; it does not complete.

## The lament — how often would I have gathered thy children

*O Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not! Behold, your house is left unto you desolate. For I say unto you, Ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD).* (Matthew 23:37-39)

The chapter lands its second great moment at the lament. Everything the seven woes walked is now turned toward the Father's heart — the gathering longed for at the very moment of judgment.

The doubled address — *O Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), Yerushalayim (Jerusalem)* — is the Tanakh's pattern of tender naming. The Father's angel calling *Avraham (Abraham), Avraham (Abraham)* to stay the hand at the binding (Genesis 22:11); the Father calling *Ya'aqov (Jacob), Ya'aqov (Jacob)* at the going down to Mitsrayim (Egypt) (Genesis 46:2); *Mosheh (Moses), Mosheh (Moses)* at the burning bush (Exodus 3:4); *Shemu'el (Samuel), Shemu'el (Samuel)* at the prophet's commissioning (1 Samuel 3:10); the King calling *Marta (Martha), Marta (Martha)* (Luke 10:41) and *Shimon (Simon), Shimon (Simon)* (Luke 22:31); the risen King calling *Sha'ul (Paul), Sha'ul (Paul)* on the Damascus road (Acts 9:4). The doubled name is the voice of a tender heart turned toward the one it loves. The King speaks it over the city the Father set his Name upon — *the place which Yahuah your Elohim (the LORD your God) shall choose out of all your tribes to put his name there* (Deuteronomy 12:5) — the city the Shekinah filled (1 Kings 8:10-11) and departed from (Ezekiel 10:18-19). The Father does not walk this judgment in detachment. He walks it in the voice of the tender doubled name.

*Thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee* names what the seven woes have just walked. Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) walked the same killing the First Temple leadership walked — Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) thrown into the pit (Jeremiah 38), the prophets of Yahuah (the LORD) hunted under Ach'av (Ahab) and Yezevel (Jezebel) (1 Kings 18:4). *Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted?* (Acts 7:52). The King at the lament speaks the indictment the prophets already spoke.

*How often would I have gathered thy children together* — *posakis ēthelēsa episynagagein* — is the Father's repeated extension of the gathering across the whole Tanakh. He sent prophet after prophet to gather the people back, and the leadership refused them one after another. *How often* — many times. *Since the day that your fathers came forth out of the land of Mitsrayim (Egypt) unto this day I have even sent unto you all my servants the prophets, daily rising up early and sending them: Yet they hearkened not unto me* (Jeremiah 7:25-26; and 25:4; 35:15). *They slew thy prophets which testified against them to turn them to thee* (Nehemiah 9:26). *I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people* (Isaiah 65:2).

The image is the Father as the mother bird gathering her young — *even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings.* It is the Tanakh's own picture. *As an eagle stirreth up her nest, fluttereth over her young, spreadeth abroad her wings, taketh them, beareth them on her wings: So Yahuah (the LORD) alone did lead him* (Deuteronomy 32:11-12). It runs through the Psalms — *hide me under the shadow of thy wings* (Psalm 17:8); *the children of men put their trust under the shadow of thy wings* (Psalm 36:7); *in the shadow of thy wings will I make my refuge* (Psalm 57:1); *he shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust* (Psalm 91:4) — and through Ruth: *under whose wings thou art come to trust* (Ruth 2:12) — and through Yeshayahu (Isaiah): *as birds flying, so will Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts) defend Yerushalayim (Jerusalem); defending also he will deliver it* (Isaiah 31:5). The King speaks at the lament with the Father's own voice, because the King is the One the Tanakh has been speaking from all along — the Formed who walked with the patriarchs, stood on Sinai, and filled the Temple, now standing in the Temple court in the flesh, longing over the city with the same heart that hovered over the young.

*And ye would not* — *ouk ēthelēsate* — is the leadership's flat refusal set against the Father's repeated reaching. And here the framework's cause-and-effect of hearing surfaces. *Ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me* (John 10:26-27). The leadership refused because the leadership the seven woes named out loud is the serpent's seed woven into the office — the tares, not the sheep the Father's voice gathers. The hearing reveals what was already true: the sheep hear because they are sheep; the tares do not hear because they are not. The lament is not the Father's arbitrary rejection of the city. It is the Father's tender heart over children who could have been gathered, refused by the leadership that kept them.

This is why the judgment and the gathering are not in tension. The judgment of the false shepherds is the thing that *clears the way* for the True Shepherd to gather. Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) walked the whole motion in one chapter: the false shepherds of Yashar'el (Israel) are judged, *and* the Father himself comes to shepherd the sheep (Ezekiel 34). The judgment of the false shepherds makes the room for the gathering.

*Behold, your house is left unto you desolate* — *aphietai hymin ho oikos hymōn erēmos.* It is Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah)'s word: *I have forsaken mine house, I have left mine heritage* (Jeremiah 12:7); *this house shall become a desolation* (Jeremiah 22:5). It is the departure of the Shekinah Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) watched: *the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) went up from the midst of the city, and stood upon the mountain which is on the east side of the city* (Ezekiel 11:23). The Shekinah departed the First Temple before Babylon destroyed it; the Second Temple never saw that glory fully return — *who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory? and how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?* (Haggai 2:3). Now the King walks out of it, and the house is named *your house* — no longer the Father's house. The Father's presence leaves with the King, and the next chapter opens with the disciples showing him the Temple buildings he has just abandoned.

*Ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD)* — *eulogēmenos ho erchomenos en onomati Kyriou* — is the cry of Psalm 118:26 the multitudes and the children shouted at the triumphal entry: *Hosanna to the Son of David: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Matthew 21:9, 15). The leadership rebuked that praise then. The cry the leadership refused at the first coming is the cry it will have to speak at the second. It points toward the recognition Zekharyah (Zechariah) named: *they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son* (Zechariah 12:10) — and toward the surviving third: *the third shall be left therein. And I will bring the third part through the fire, and will refine them as silver is refined... they shall call on my name, and I will hear them: I will say, It is my people; and they shall say, Yahuah (the LORD) is my Elohim (my God)* (Zechariah 13:8-9).

This is the gathering Sha'ul (Paul) names at the end of Romans 11: *blindness in part is happened to Yashar'el (Israel), until the fulness of the goyim (Gentiles) be come in. And so all Yashar'el (Israel) shall be saved: as it is written, There shall come out of Tsion (Zion) the Deliverer, and shall turn away ungodliness from Ya'aqov (Jacob)* (Romans 11:25-26). The *fulness of the goyim (Gentiles)* is not the nations added to Yashar'el (Israel) as new branches; it is the scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel) among the nations being gathered home — the wild olive branches descended from the broken-off, gathered out of the nations, brought into the bond of the new covenant, and grafted into the natural root of the fathers. The lament walks toward exactly that gathering.

So the chapter that opens with the longest condemnation in the gospel closes on the Father's tenderest grief — and the two are one motion. The judgment of the leadership is not the Father's rejection of his people; it is the clearing of the false shepherds so the True Shepherd can gather the children they kept from him. The King's public engagement with the leadership of the Second Temple ends here. He walks out of the Temple, and from this point the road runs toward the Mount of Olives, the Olivet discourse the next chapter opens, and the cross.
