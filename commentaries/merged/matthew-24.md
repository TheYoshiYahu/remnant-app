## The lens for the whole discourse — four readings the inherited church gets wrong

Matthew 24 opens the Olivet discourse, the longest sustained teaching on the end of the age in the gospel, spoken from the Mount of Olives in answer to the disciples' question as the King of Yashar'el (Israel) walks out of the Temple he has just left desolate. Before the first sign is read, four readings the inherited church carries have to be set down, because each of them bends the chapter into a shape the text refuses.

The chapter rules out the **preterist collapse** — the reading that the whole discourse was finished at the Roman destruction of 70 CE. The 70 CE destruction was real, and it was the judgment of the Second Temple and of the Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership the King had named from Matthew 21 onward. But it was not the completion of the Day of Yahuah (the LORD). The cosmic signs did not land; the gathering of the twelve tribes did not land; the Father's feet did not stand on the Mount of Olives; the surviving third of Zekharyah (Zechariah) did not call on the Name. The judgment of 70 CE points forward; it does not close the book.

The chapter rules out the **Christian-Zionist reading** that treats the modern state founded in 1948 as the prophetic restoration. The restoration the prophets named is the gathering of all twelve tribes — *Yahuah (the LORD) shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people... and shall assemble the outcasts of Yashar'el (Israel), and gather together the dispersed of Yahudah (Judah) from the four corners of the earth* (Isaiah 11:11-12) — the two houses made one stick in the Father's hand (Ezekiel 37:15-28). A visible Yahudi (Jewish) presence in the land while the body of the covenant people remains scattered and the ingathering of all twelve tribes is still future is the same counterfeit pattern Herod's Temple was at the first coming. The modern state is that pattern again, not the prophetic fulfillment.

The chapter rules out the **pre-tribulation rapture.** The King places the cosmic signs *immediately after the tribulation of those days* (verse 29), and he describes his coming as *the lightning... out of the east* shining *even unto the west* (verse 27) — public, visible, unmissable. There is no secret removal of the saints before the trouble; the citizens of the kingdom walk through the tribulation and the signs follow it.

And the chapter rules out both the **Trinitarian three-co-equal-persons formula and the modalist collapse** that flattens Father and Son into one. The King's own word — *of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only* (verse 36) — preserves the distinction the canon has carried all along: the Father is the Formless source; the Son is the Formed expression drawn from him, the One the Tanakh shows in every theophany, now in flesh and still submitted to the Father.

Hold those four, and the discourse reads itself.

## The Temple prophecy and the three questions

*And Yahusha (Jesus) went out, and departed from the temple: and his disciples came to him for to shew him the buildings of the temple. And Yahusha (Jesus) said unto them, See ye not all these things? verily I say unto you, There shall not be left here one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down. And as he sat upon the mount of Olives, the disciples came unto him privately, saying, Tell us, when shall these things be? and what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?* (Matthew 24:1-3)

The King departs the Temple, carrying forward the *behold, your house is left unto you desolate* he had just spoken over Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) (Matthew 23:38). The geography is the whole point. He crosses the Kidron valley and sits on the Mount of Olives — the very mountain the Shekinah stood over when it departed the First Temple before Babylon: *the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) went up from the midst of the city, and stood upon the mountain which is on the east side of the city* (Ezekiel 11:23). And it is the same mountain Zekharyah (Zechariah) named for the return: *his feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives, which is before Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) on the east* (Zechariah 14:4). The presence departs the house, and the One who is the presence sits on the mountain of departure to teach about the mountain of return.

The disciples point out the Herodian buildings — the white-marble and gold-overlaid wonder of that world, with stones in its foundations weighing hundreds of tons. The King answers with total judgment: *there shall not be left here one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down.* This is the Tanakh's Temple-judgment pattern. Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) stood in the same gate and named the same thing: *Is this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes?... Therefore will I do unto this house, which is called by my name, wherein ye trust... as I have done to Shiloh* (Jeremiah 7:11, 14). Mikhah (Micah) had walked it: *Tsion (Zion) shall be plowed as a field, and Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) shall become heaps* (Micah 3:12). The Temple is never protected by its own stones; it is protected by what the leadership walks in it. When the leadership corrupts the substance, the house is judged. The First Temple fell in 586 BCE; the Second fell in 70 CE.

The disciples then ask three things they have not yet learned to separate, treating them as one event: *when shall these things be?* — the Temple's destruction; *what shall be the sign of thy coming?* — the second coming; *and of the end of the world (Greek aiōnos, age)?* — the close of the present age and the beginning of the Messianic kingdom on the earth the prophets named (Isaiah 65:17-25; Ezekiel 40-48). The discourse that follows untangles the three across the chapter.

## The beginning of sorrows

*And Yahusha (Jesus) answered and said unto them, Take heed that no man deceive you. For many shall come in my name, saying, I am Messiah (Christ); and shall deceive many. And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet. For nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom: and there shall be famines, and pestilences, and earthquakes, in divers places. All these are the beginning of sorrows.* (Matthew 24:4-8)

The discourse opens with a pastoral warning — *take heed that no man deceive you* — and the deception is not a single moment but an architecture that runs across the whole age between the first coming and the second. Across the centuries the false-Messiah claims came: Shimon (Simon) bar Kosiba acclaimed in 132-135 CE, Sabbatai Tsvi sweeping the diaspora in 1666 before converting to Islam under coercion, and every modern Messianic cult since. And underneath the claims runs the same five-costume architecture the whole framework names: the Pharisaic-Sadducean system voiding the Torah by **addition,** the Galatian agitators by **inversion** (turning *if you are covenant, you respond when called* into *if you respond, you become covenant*), modern institutional Christianity by **subtraction** (the antinomian gospel Sha'ul (Paul) never preached), modern Hebrew Roots' false-inclusion by **collapse** (the paternal-blood remnant flattened into a spiritual category open to anyone who believes and Torah-keeps), and Islam by **transfer** (the covenant moved from Yitschaq (Isaac) and Ya'aqov (Jacob) to Yishma'el (Ishmael)). Each fails the one test: *and hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him* (1 John 2:3-4).

The wars and famines and pestilences and earthquakes are the Tanakh's judgment-pattern. *I will set the Mitsrim (Egyptians) against the Mitsrim (Egyptians)... city against city, and kingdom against kingdom* (Isaiah 19:2). *I will consume them by the sword, and by the famine, and by the pestilence* (Jeremiah 14:12). The four sore judgments: *the sword, and the famine, and the noisome beast, and the pestilence* (Ezekiel 14:21).

*But the end is not yet* — *all' oupō estin to telos* — is the direct rule-out of the preterist collapse. The wars and the famines are not the end of the age; they are *the beginning of sorrows* — *archē ōdinōn,* the beginning of birth pangs. The Tanakh walked the birth-pang image across the prophets: *they shall be in pain as a woman that travaileth* (Isaiah 13:8); *we have been with child, we have been in pain, we have as it were brought forth wind* (Isaiah 26:18); *I have heard a voice as of a woman in travail* (Jeremiah 4:31); *Be in pain, and labour to bring forth, O daughter of Tsion (Zion), like a woman in travail* (Micah 4:10). The pangs increase in intensity and frequency as the delivery nears. The end walks *after* the tribulation, with the cosmic signs of verse 29.

## Persecution, the cooling of love, and the witness to the nations

*Then shall they deliver you up to be afflicted, and shall kill you: and ye shall be hated of all nations for my name's sake. And then shall many be offended, and shall betray one another, and shall hate one another. And many false prophets shall rise, and shall deceive many. And because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold. But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved. And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come.* (Matthew 24:9-14)

The persecution lands first in the apostolic age and runs on across the centuries. Stephen was stoned while *the witnesses laid down their clothes at a young man's feet, whose name was Sha'ul (Saul)* (Acts 7:58). Ya'aqov (James) son of Zavdai (Zebedee) was killed with the sword (Acts 12:2). Sha'ul (Paul) carried the scourgings in his own body: *of the Yahudim (Jews) five times received I forty stripes save one. Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned* (2 Corinthians 11:24-25). *Ye shall be hated of all nations for my name's sake* — the citizen of the kingdom stands against kingdoms under the administration of the watcher rebellion: *we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world* (Ephesians 6:12). And the pressure breaks fellowship from within — *many be offended, and shall betray one another* — the same forsaking Sha'ul (Paul) knew: *Demas hath forsaken me, having loved this present world* (2 Timothy 4:10).

The false prophets fail the Torah's own test: *if there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder... saying, Let us go after other elohim (gods)... thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet* (Deuteronomy 13:1-3). The sign-and-wonder is not the mark of a true prophet; walking with the Torah is. *Believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of Elohim (God): because many false prophets are gone out into the world* (1 John 4:1).

*Because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold* is the chapter's sharpest diagnostic of the modern antinomian gospel. The Greek is *anomia* — torahlessness, life *without the Torah* — the same word in the verdict the King already spoke: *I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity (anomia, torahlessness)* (Matthew 7:23). Sha'ul (Paul) names it as a force already at work: *for the mystery of iniquity (anomia, torahlessness) doth already work... and then shall that Wicked one be revealed* (2 Thessalonians 2:7-8). And the cooling of love is no accident, because love and the commandments are one thing: *by this we know that we love the children of Elohim (God), when we love Elohim (God), and keep his commandments. For this is the love of Elohim (God), that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous* (1 John 5:2-3). When torahlessness abounds — when the Reformation grammar that pits grace against the Torah is preached as the gospel — love grows cold, because love was always the keeping of the commandments. A faith that claims the Father's love while walking without the Father's commandments cools into exactly the cold the King names here.

*He that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved* is load-bearing at the salvation level, and it stands directly against the inherited eternal-security reading. The King had already said it at the apostolic mission (Matthew 10:22); he restates it here at the eschatological scale. The endurance is conditional. The name can be blotted out: *I will not blot out his name out of the book of life* (Revelation 3:5), the promise spoken to the one who overcomes, against the Torah's own word — *whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book* (Exodus 32:33). The book holds the one who endures.

*This gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come.* The good news is the news of the gathering of the scattered seed of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) — *in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living Elohim (God)* (Hosea 1:10). The witness goes broad among all the nations because the citizens of the kingdom are scattered among all the nations: *I will gather you from the people, and assemble you out of the countries, where ye have been scattered* (Ezekiel 11:17). This is not the inclusion gospel that collapses the paternal-blood remnant into a self-selected category. The witness goes wide because the sheep are wide, and the hearing reveals what was already true: *my sheep hear my voice... ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep* (John 10:27, 26) — those named in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8; 17:8). *And then shall the end come* — after the witness has gone broad and the Father has called his citizens home.

## The abomination and the great tribulation

*When ye therefore shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place, (whoso readeth, let him understand:) Then let them which be in Yahudah (Judaea) flee into the mountains... But pray ye that your flight be not in the winter, neither on the Shabbat day: For then shall be great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be. And except those days should be shortened, no flesh should be saved: but for the elect's sake those days shall be shortened.* (Matthew 24:15-22)

The King names Daniel directly — the *abomination of desolation* of Daniel 9:27, 11:31, and 12:11: *in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease, and for the overspreading of abominations he shall make it desolate* (Daniel 9:27). The historical prototype is Antiochus IV Epiphanes in 167 BCE, who erected an altar to Zeus in the Temple and sacrificed swine on it: *they set up the abomination of desolation upon the altar* (1 Maccabees 1:54). It pre-figured again in Caligula's statue attempt of 40 CE, and again in the Roman armies under Titus encircling Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — *when ye shall see Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh* (Luke 21:20), the moment the apostolic community fled to Pella.

But the substance is not exhausted by any of these. The future reaches into the man of sin Sha'ul (Paul) names: *that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition; who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called Elohim (God)... so that he as Elohim (God) sitteth in the temple of Elohim (God), shewing himself that he is Elohim (God)* (2 Thessalonians 2:3-4), and into the beast of Revelation 13:5-18 treading the holy city forty-two months (Revelation 11:2). This is where the preterist collapse and the Christian-Zionist reading both fail: the future abomination requires a future Temple, and the modern state's celebration of a coming third Temple is the very stage the man of sin steps onto. A visible presence in the land while the body remains scattered is Herod's counterfeit walking again.

*Pray ye that your flight be not in the winter, neither on the Shabbat day* is a quiet but devastating line against the church that calls the Shabbat (Sabbath) abolished. The King assumes the citizens of the kingdom are still keeping the seventh-day Shabbat (Sabbath) the Father gave at creation (Genesis 2:1-3) and at Sinai (Exodus 20:8-11) into the far future of the flight — not the first day Constantine moved the day to in 321 CE. The Shabbat (Sabbath) is not abolished at the cross; it is kept all the way to the second coming.

*Great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time* is Daniel's future trouble: *there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time: and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book* (Daniel 12:1). And *for the elect's sake those days shall be shortened* — the Father's mercy cutting the tribulation short for the elect, the paternal-blood remnant of the twelve tribes named from the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8; 17:8), not the spiritual category the inclusion gospel substitutes.

## The false Messiahs and the lightning from the east

*Then if any man shall say unto you, Lo, here is Messiah (Christ), or there; believe it not... Wherefore if they shall say unto you, Behold, he is in the desert; go not forth: behold, he is in the secret chambers; believe it not. For as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of Adam be. For wheresoever the carcase is, there will the eagles be gathered together.* (Matthew 24:23-28)

The *desert* and the *secret chambers* are both costumes the second coming will not wear. The desert is where the false Messiahs led their followers — the Theudas of Acts 5:36, the Egyptian of Acts 21:38, leading thousands into the wilderness. The secret chambers are the hidden-revelation move, the special knowledge reserved for the initiated. The second coming is neither hidden nor private: *if it were possible, they shall deceive the very elect* — but the *if it were possible* preserves that the elect are not deceived, because *they went out from us, but they were not of us* (1 John 2:19). The coming is *as the lightning* — public, visible from east to west, seen by all who look. This is the direct rule-out of the secret rapture: there is no quiet removal, only the lightning that fills the sky. And *wheresoever the carcase is, there will the eagles be gathered together* names the judgment that follows — the carrion birds Yochanan (John) sees gathering to *the supper of the great Elohim (God)* (Revelation 19:17).

## The cosmic signs, the Son of Adam in the clouds, and the gathering of the elect

*Immediately after the tribulation of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be shaken: And then shall appear the sign of the Son of Adam in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of Adam coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other.* (Matthew 24:29-31)

This is the center of the chapter, the load-bearing eschatological architecture of the whole gospel. And it opens with the phrase that ends the rapture reading: *immediately after the tribulation of those days* — *eutheōs meta tēn thlipsin.* There is no gap, no pre-tribulation removal, no secret catching-away between the tribulation and the signs. *Immediately.* The citizens of the kingdom walk through the tribulation, and the signs follow it directly.

The cosmic signs are the Tanakh's Day of Yahuah (the LORD) cluster, walked across the centuries by prophet after prophet. *The sun shall be darkened in his going forth, and the moon shall not cause her light to shine* (Isaiah 13:10). *The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and the terrible day of Yahuah (the LORD) come* (Joel 2:31). *I will cover the sun with a cloud, and the moon shall not give her light* (Ezekiel 32:7). *The day of Yahuah (the LORD) is darkness, and not light* (Amos 5:18). *A day of darkness and gloominess, a day of clouds and thick darkness* (Zephaniah 1:15). These signs have not landed in history — not in 70 CE, not since. The sun and moon have not darkened as the prophets describe, the stars have not fallen, the powers of the heavens have not been shaken. Kefa (Peter) cited Joel at Pentecost (Acts 2:19-20) as the inauguration of the age; the completion is still future. Yochanan (John) sees the same signs at the sixth seal: *the sun became black as sackcloth of hair, and the moon became as blood; and the stars of heaven fell unto the earth* (Revelation 6:12-13). The preterist collapse breaks here: the signs the prophets named did not happen at 70 CE.

*And then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of Adam coming in the clouds.* The mourning is Zekharyah (Zechariah)'s recognition: *they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son* (Zechariah 12:10). It is the answer to the cry the King left at the end of the prior chapter — *ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Matthew 23:39) — and the survival of the refined third: *the third shall be left therein... they shall call on my name, and I will hear them* (Zechariah 13:8-9).

*They shall see the Son of Adam coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory* is Daniel 7:13-14: *one like the Son of Adam came with the clouds of heaven... and there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom... his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away* (Daniel 7:13-14). A point of careful reading carries here. Daniel 7:13 preserves the kaph-comparative — *kbar enash,* *like the Son of Adam* — and so do Revelation 1:13 and Revelation 14:14. The kaph honors the incarnation: the figure *resembled* mortal-man because the Formed took on flesh, while remaining the Formed cloud-rider. But Matthew 24:30 does not carry the comparative — the Greek has no *hōs,* no *homoion* — and reads the title directly: *the Son of Adam.* The King names himself, in plain title, as the One coming on the clouds. The harmonized figure across all four verses is the same: Yahusha (Jesus), the Formed expression of the Father, who took on flesh and remains who he was.

And the cloud-riding is the Father's own signature across the Tanakh. *Who maketh the clouds his chariot* (Psalm 104:3). *Behold, Yahuah (the LORD) rideth upon a swift cloud, and shall come into Mitsrayim (Egypt)* (Isaiah 19:1). *The clouds are the dust of his feet* (Nahum 1:3). The Son coming on the clouds is the Formed expression doing what the Formless source's expression has always done — the same Formed One the Tanakh shows in every theophany with the patriarchs and prophets, now coming in glory. Yochanan (John) restates it whole: *behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him* (Revelation 1:7) — public, visible, every eye.

*A great sound of a trumpet* is the trumpet of Yom Teru'ah (the Feast of Trumpets) the Torah gave: *a memorial of blowing of trumpets, an holy convocation* (Leviticus 23:24; Numbers 29:1). Yeshayahu (Isaiah) named its gathering work: *the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish in the land of Ashshur (Assyria), and the outcasts in the land of Mitsrayim (Egypt)* (Isaiah 27:13). Sha'ul (Paul) carries the same last trumpet: *the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout... with the trump of Elohim (God): and the dead in Messiah (Christ) shall rise first* (1 Thessalonians 4:16); *at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible* (1 Corinthians 15:52).

*Gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other* is the gathering the whole Tanakh has been building toward — the ingathering of the twelve tribes. The prophets named it again and again. *From thence will Yahuah (the LORD) thy Elohim (God) gather thee, and from thence will he fetch thee* (Deuteronomy 30:4). *He shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Yashar'el (Israel), and gather together the dispersed of Yahudah (Judah) from the four corners of the earth* (Isaiah 11:12). *I will bring my sons from far, and my daughters from the ends of the earth* (Isaiah 43:6, paraphrasing the gathering). *He that scattered Yashar'el (Israel) will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd doth his flock* (Jeremiah 31:10). *Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out... and will deliver them out of all places where they have been scattered* (Ezekiel 34:11-12). *I will take the children of Yashar'el (Israel) from among the heathen, whither they be gone, and will gather them on every side* (Ezekiel 37:21). *Then shall the children of Yahudah (Judah) and the children of Yashar'el (Israel) be gathered together, and appoint themselves one head* (Hosea 1:11). *I will surely gather the remnant of Yashar'el (Israel)* (Micah 2:12). *I will hiss for them, and gather them; for I have redeemed them* (Zechariah 10:8).

This is the *second time* of Isaiah 11:11 — the first recovery was the partial return from Babel (Babylon) under the Persians, in Ezra and Nechemyah (Nehemiah); the second is this future gathering of all twelve tribes, both houses, the house of Yahudah (Judah) and the house of Yosef (Joseph) / Ephrayim (Ephraim), the two sticks made one in the Father's hand (Ezekiel 37:15-28). The elect gathered here are the paternal-blood remnant of the twelve tribes — the 144,000 sealed of the twelve tribes Yochanan (John) preserves by name (Revelation 7:1-8; 14:1) — not the spiritual category the inclusion gospel substitutes. The four winds are the territories of the dispersion: Ashshur (Assyria) and Mitsrayim (Egypt), the north country and the coasts of the earth, all the nations the scattering drove them into. The Father set his hand a second time, and the gathering is the fulfillment everything in the discourse has been moving toward.

## The fig tree and the lineage that does not pass

*Now learn a parable of the fig tree; When his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh: So likewise ye, when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors. Verily I say unto you, This generation shall not pass, till all these things be fulfilled. Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away.* (Matthew 24:32-35)

The fig tree is a natural, seasonal sign — when the branch softens and leafs, summer is near; when these signs appear, the coming is near. It is not the modern-Christian-Zionist symbol of the 1948 state. That reading turns a lesson about reading seasons into a date-stamp on a counterfeit gathering, the same counterfeit Herod's Temple was at the first coming.

*This generation shall not pass, till all these things be fulfilled.* The Greek *genea* carries more than a forty-year span; it carries *race, kindred, lineage, kin* — the Tanakh's *dor,* the lineage the Father preserves across the whole age. The preterist reading needs *genea* to mean the forty years to 70 CE so the whole discourse collapses there. But the King is naming Yashar'el (Israel) as lineage, kept by the Father until all is fulfilled — the covenant promised to Avraham (Abraham) *in their generations for an everlasting covenant* (Genesis 17:7), the lineage guaranteed against extinction: *if those ordinances depart from before me, saith Yahuah (the LORD), then the seed of Yashar'el (Israel) also shall cease from being a nation before me for ever* (Jeremiah 31:36). The lineage does not pass until the gathering is complete.

*Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away* binds the King's words to the Torah's permanence, exactly as he had said at the Sermon on the Mount: *Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled* (Matthew 5:18). The Father's word and the King's word carry the same eternity: *the grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our Elohim (God) shall stand for ever* (Isaiah 40:8); *my salvation shall be for ever, and my righteousness shall not be abolished* (Isaiah 51:6).

## Of that day and hour — the Father only

*But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.* (Matthew 24:36)

This is the second great moment of the chapter, and it carries the nature of God. The Father knows the day and hour; the Son, in the flesh, does not — and the inherited Trinitarian formula has nowhere to put that. The Father is the Formless source, the infinite from which all proceeds, and the prerogative of the day and hour belongs to the source. The Son is the Formed expression drawn from the source, *the Word... made flesh* (John 1:14) — and the eternal submission of the Formed to the Formless holds in the incarnation. The King had already walked it: *to sit on my right hand, and on my left, is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared of my Father* (Matthew 20:23); *the Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do* (John 5:19); *I do nothing of myself; but as my Father hath taught me, I speak these things* (John 8:28).

This rules out the three-co-equal-persons formula of Nicaea and Constantinople — three persons of one undivided essence with no distinction of prerogative cannot carry a Son who does not know what the Father knows. And it rules out the modalist collapse — the Father and the Son are genuinely distinct here, not one being in two modes; the Father knows what the Son does not. The simpler, older thing the canon shows: one Yahuah (God), Formless in source and Formed in expression, the same Formed One the Tanakh shows in every theophany, now in flesh, still submitted, still drawing his knowing from the Father. Sha'ul (Paul) holds it: *who, being in the form of Elohim (God)... made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant* (Philippians 2:6-7); *then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that Elohim (God) may be all in all* (1 Corinthians 15:28). And the King held it after the resurrection too, post-glorification: *it is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power* (Acts 1:7). The submission held before the flesh, holds in the flesh, and holds into the eschaton.

## The days of Noach and the watchfulness

*But as the days of Noach (Noah) were, so shall also the coming of the Son of Adam be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noach (Noah) entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of Adam be. Then shall two be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Two women shall be grinding at the mill; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come... Therefore be ye also ready: for in such an hour as ye think not the Son of Adam cometh.* (Matthew 24:37-44)

*As the days of Noach (Noah) were* reaches back to the watcher rebellion the Adam-and-the-tares architecture turns on. *The sons of Elohim (God) saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives... There were giants (Nephilim) in the earth in those days... And Elohim (God) saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth* (Genesis 6:2, 4-5). The fallen watchers descended on Mount Hermon, took wives of the daughters of Adam, and begat the Nephilim — a corrupted seed mixed into the flesh, the very corruption the flood was the judgment against. 1 Enoch carries it fully, and Yehudah (Jude) quotes it (Jude 14-15); Kefa (Peter) walks it: *spared not the old world, but saved Noach (Noah) the eighth person, a preacher of righteousness, bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly* (2 Peter 2:5).

The point of the comparison is the unawareness, not a condemnation of the natural acts of eating and marrying. They went on with ordinary life while Noach (Noah) preached, and *knew not until the flood came, and took them all away.* And here the inherited rapture reading inverts the text. *Took them all away* — *ēren hapantas* — is the *judgment.* The flood took the wicked away into judgment; Noach (Noah) and the eight were the ones *left,* preserved into the Father's mercy. So when verses 40-41 say *the one shall be taken, and the other left,* the *taken* (*paralambanetai*) ties straight back to the flood that *took them all away* — taken into judgment, not rescued. Luke confirms it: when the disciples ask *where, Lord?* the King answers, *Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together* (Luke 17:37) — the carcass and the carrion birds, the judgment. The *left* are the Noach (Noah) ones, kept into restoration. The pre-tribulation rapture has the taking exactly backward.

This is the cause-and-effect of hearing once more: *ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep... My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me* (John 10:26-27). The ones taken in judgment are the tares the watcher rebellion wove in among the sons of Adam; the ones kept are the sheep named from the foundation of the world — the wheat and the tares of Matthew 13 reaching their post-harvest sorting. *Watch therefore... if the goodman of the house had known in what watch the thief would come, he would have watched.* The thief is the Tanakh-and-apostolic image of the unannounced day: *the day of Yahuah (the LORD) so cometh as a thief in the night* (1 Thessalonians 5:2); *the day of Yahuah (the LORD) will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away* (2 Peter 3:10). Unannounced to the unaware; watched-for by the ready.

## The faithful and the evil servant

*Who then is a faithful and wise servant, whom his lord hath made ruler over his household, to give them meat in due season?... But and if that evil servant shall say in his heart, My lord delayeth his coming; And shall begin to smite his fellowservants, and to eat and drink with the drunken; The lord of that servant shall come in a day when he looketh not for him... And shall cut him asunder, and appoint him his portion with the hypocrites: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.* (Matthew 24:45-51)

The chapter closes on a stewardship parable that carries the same weight as the wicked-husbandmen of Matthew 21:33-46 — the kingdom *given to a nation bringing forth the fruits thereof* (Matthew 21:43). The faithful servant feeds the household *in due season* — the shepherding the Father always meant, against the false shepherds Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) named: *woe be unto the pastors that destroy and scatter the sheep of my pasture* (Jeremiah 23:1). The evil servant says in his heart *my lord delayeth his coming* and turns to beating his fellows and feasting with the drunken — the presumption of delay Kefa (Peter) named: *Where is the promise of his coming?... The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering* (2 Peter 3:4, 9). The Father's longsuffering is not license for the servant's presumption.

And the verdict lands the chapter back on the prior one. The evil servant is cut off and given *his portion with the hypocrites* — *hypokritōn,* tying straight back to the seven woes of Matthew 23 against the hypocrite leadership. The evil servant walks the very substance the Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership walked, and shares its end. *Weeping and gnashing of teeth* is the King's recurring image of the post-harvest sifting (Matthew 8:12; 13:42, 50; 22:13; 25:30) — the same sifting as *I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity (anomia, torahlessness)* (Matthew 7:23). And it rules out the eternal-security reading directly: the evil servant *had been made ruler over the household.* He was trusted with the stewardship. He had it, and he lost it — through presumption and through walking off the way. *When the righteous turneth away from his righteousness, and committeth iniquity... All his righteousness that he hath done shall not be mentioned* (Ezekiel 18:24). The gift was real; the access was conditional; what was done between the giving and the gate determined the end.

The discourse opened on the Mount of Olives and runs straight into the next chapter — the ten virgins, the talents, the judgment of the nations — where the watchfulness, the stewardship, and the sifting the King has named here are unfolded in three more parables before the road turns toward the cross.
