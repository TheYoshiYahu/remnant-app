## The close of the Olivet discourse — three parables of who is ready

Matthew 25 closes the Olivet discourse the King of Yashar'el (Israel) opened a day before, and it closes his public teaching altogether — the next chapter turns toward the Pesach (Passover) and the cross. Three movements carry it: the ten virgins, the talents, and the judgment of the nations. And one lens governs all three, because the inherited Christian reading flattens each of them. Every one of these parables is about who is *ready* when the King comes — and readiness, in all three, is not the bare confession but the fruit of a life walked in the new heart. The chapter rules out, point by point, the eternal-security reading that says a confession once made can never be lost; it rules out the inclusion gospel that reads *all nations* and *my brethren* as everyone-by-belief; it rules out the universalism that empties the shut door and the everlasting fire; and it holds the system-not-people posture throughout — the systems are dismantled, never the people inside them, who inherited their deception in good faith and are invited home.

## The ten virgins — the oil that cannot be borrowed

*Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom. And five of them were wise, and five were foolish... And while they went to buy, the bridegroom came; and they that were ready went in with him to the marriage: and the door was shut. Afterward came also the other virgins, saying, Lord, Lord, open to us. But he answered and said, Verily I say unto you, I know you not. Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour wherein the Son of Adam cometh.* (Matthew 25:1-13)

The whole parable is the post-harvest sifting the King already walked at the Sermon on the Mount, drawn out into a story. *Lord, Lord, open to us* and *I know you not* are the direct echo of *not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven... And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity (anomia, torahlessness)* (Matthew 7:21, 23). The foolish virgins carried the form of the confession without the substance of the doing.

The bridegroom is the King. He had already named himself so — *can the children of the bridechamber mourn, as long as the bridegroom is with them?* (Matthew 9:15) — and the marriage runs back through the whole Tanakh. *As the bridegroom rejoiceth over the bride, so shall thy Elohim (God) rejoice over thee* (Isaiah 62:5). *Thy Maker is thine husband; Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts) is his name* (Isaiah 54:5). *I will betroth thee unto me for ever* (Hosea 2:19). The King standing as the bridegroom is the Formed expression of the Father doing what the Father has always done — for the Father is the husband of his people in the Tanakh, and the One in flesh is that same One. The marriage runs forward to *the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready... for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints* (Revelation 19:7-8).

The ten virgins are the called — the virgin of Yashar'el (Israel) the prophets named: *again I will build thee... O virgin of Yashar'el (Israel)* (Jeremiah 31:4). The lamps are the outward form; the oil is the substance. The lamps are the *Lord, Lord* on every lip; the oil is the heart-Torah-walking life that only the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) gives, the new heart the prophets promised: *a new heart also will I give you... and I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them* (Ezekiel 36:26-27); *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts* (Jeremiah 31:33). The oil is the Spirit, the same oil Zekharyah (Zechariah) saw fueling the lampstand: *Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts)* (Zechariah 4:6). The wise carry the oil; the foolish carry only the lamp.

And here the parable lands its hardest blow on the eternal-security reading: *the foolish said unto the wise, Give us of your oil... But the wise answered, saying, Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you.* The oil cannot be loaned. The heart-Torah-walking life is the individual's own relationship with the Father; one cannot borrow another's walk, cannot inherit another's new heart. The foolish virgins had the lamps, were called virgins, walked out toward the bridegroom — and still the oil was absent, and still the door was shut. *They that were ready went in with him to the marriage: and the door was shut.* It is the door the Father shut on the ark before the flood: *and Yahuah (the LORD) shut him in* (Genesis 7:16) — the same flood the King had just invoked. It is the door of Luke 13:25: *when once the master of the house is risen up, and hath shut to the door... I know you not whence ye are.* The shut door is real, and it rules out the universalism that would leave it open.

*I know you not* is *I never knew you* again. The test underneath is the same: *and hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him* (1 John 2:3-4). This is the post-harvest sifting of the wheat and the tares — and the warning is given to the wheat, not the tares. The name can be blotted out: *whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book* (Exodus 32:33); *I will not blot out his name out of the book of life* (Revelation 3:5), the promise to the one who overcomes. But the tares were never written — *whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world* (Revelation 17:8); the Father does not threaten to remove what was never there. The fruitless wheat loses what it had. And the hearing reveals which is which: *my sheep hear my voice... ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep* (John 10:27, 26). *Watch therefore... ye know neither the day nor the hour* — the same watchfulness against the unannounced day: *the day of Yahuah (the LORD) so cometh as a thief in the night* (1 Thessalonians 5:2).

## The talents — the gift that was real and was lost

*For the kingdom of heaven is as a man travelling into a far country, who called his own servants, and delivered unto them his goods... And cast ye the unprofitable servant into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.* (Matthew 25:14-30)

Between the two great parables stands the parable of the talents, and it carries the same weight: grace given, opportunity given, and both genuinely lost. The man travelling into the far country is the King departing at the ascension — *this same Yahusha (Jesus), which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner* (Acts 1:11) — to the Father's right hand: *Yahuah (the LORD) said unto my Adonai (Lord), Sit thou on my right hand* (Psalm 110:1). The far country is the present age, the same tarrying the bridegroom kept.

The talents are not the inherited reading's natural abilities. The talent is the stewardship the Father entrusted to the citizens of the kingdom in this age — the gospel of the kingdom witness the King named (Matthew 24:14), the proclamation of the gathering, the heart-Torah-walking life the Spirit gives. *To every man according to his several ability* is the Father's individual knowing of each one — *the manifestation of the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit) is given to every man to profit withal* (1 Corinthians 12:7); *Elohim (God) hath dealt to every man the measure of faith* (Romans 12:3). The two faithful servants both double what they were given, and both receive the identical word — *Well done, thou good and faithful servant... enter thou into the joy of thy lord.* Faithfulness is the criterion, not quantity; this rules out the modern success gospel that measures the Father's approval by the size of the return. The reward is the millennial reign: *I will make thee ruler over many things* is the *power over the nations* of Revelation 2:26, the reigning of Revelation 5:10, the twelve thrones the King already promised (Matthew 19:28). *Enter thou into the joy of thy lord* is the marriage of the Lamb and the everlasting joy: *the ransomed of Yahuah (the LORD) shall return, and come to Tsion (Zion) with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads* (Isaiah 35:10).

The unprofitable servant misread the master entirely: *I knew thee that thou art an hard man... and I was afraid, and went and hid thy talent.* He saw a hard taskmaster where there was a Father — the fear that has no love in it: *there is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear* (1 John 4:18). His sin was not theft or waste; it was non-action. He buried the gift. And the master's answer — *thou oughtest therefore to have put my money to the exchangers* — shows the minimum was required, not the impossible. *Thou wicked and slothful servant* names the sloth the proverbs warn against: *How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard?... so shall thy poverty come* (Proverbs 6:9, 11). *Unto every one that hath shall be given... but from him that hath not shall be taken away even that which he hath* is the cause-and-effect of hearing again, the same word the King spoke over the parables (Matthew 13:12): the one who walks the gift is given more; the one who buries it loses even that.

And the verdict — *cast ye the unprofitable servant into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth* — is the King's recurring image of the sifting (Matthew 8:12; 13:42, 50; 22:13; 24:51). This is the direct rule-out of the eternal-security reading, and all three pieces stand at once. The unprofitable servant *was given the talent* — that is the grace. He *was named one of the master's own servants* — that is the relationship. And he was *cast into outer darkness for the failure of stewardship* — that is the loss. Grace, relationship, and loss, all real, all in the same servant. The gift was free; the access was conditional; what was done with it between the giving and the reckoning determined the end. *When the righteous turneth away from his righteousness... All his righteousness that he hath done shall not be mentioned* (Ezekiel 18:24).

## The judgment of the nations — the Son of Adam on the throne of his glory

*When the Son of Adam shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory: And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as a shepherd divideth his sheep from the goats: And he shall set the sheep on his right hand, but the goats on the left.* (Matthew 25:31-33)

The third movement is the load-bearing one — and the inherited reading turns it into a universal individual judgment of every human by his confession, which is exactly what the text rules out. *When the Son of Adam shall come in his glory* is the title direct: the Greek carries no *hōs,* no *homoion,* no comparative. The kaph-comparative — *like the Son of Adam* — is preserved only at Daniel 7:13, Revelation 1:13, and Revelation 14:14, where it honors the incarnation, the Formed One who resembled mortal-man because he took on flesh. Here the King names himself outright, in title: *the Son of Adam,* the seed-of-Adam lineage weight intact. He comes in the glory Daniel saw given to him: *there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom... his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away* (Daniel 7:14). The holy angels come with him — *he came with ten thousands of saints* (Deuteronomy 33:2); *Behold, Yahuah (the LORD) cometh with ten thousands of his saints* (Jude 14, preserving the prophecy of Chanok (Enoch) the seventh from Adam). He sits on *the throne of his glory* — the Davidic throne the covenant promised: *I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever* (2 Samuel 7:13); *I will set up one shepherd over them... even my servant David* (Ezekiel 34:23) — the same throne that seats the twelve apostles on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes (Matthew 19:28).

And then the crux: *before him shall be gathered all nations.* The Greek *panta ta ethnē* here is not the inherited reading's individual believers gathered from every people. It is the surviving nations — the third category of the framework's three — the nations the Day of Yahuah (the LORD) has *not* burned. The order of the discourse demands it. The house of Yahudah (Judah) has already passed through the refining third: *the third shall be left therein... they shall call on my name, and I will hear them* (Zechariah 13:9), looking on the One they pierced (Zechariah 12:10), saying at last *Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Matthew 23:39). The scattered seed of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) has already been gathered from the four winds at the coming of the Son of Adam in the prior chapter (Matthew 24:31), the wild olive branches grafted back into their own root (Romans 11:24). Both houses are the *elect* already gathered. So the *all nations* judged here is the third category — the surviving nations of the prophets' own picture: *I will also gather all nations, and will bring them down into the valley of Yehoshafat (Jehoshaphat), and will plead with them there for my people and for my heritage Yashar'el (Israel)* (Joel 3:2); *every one that is left of all the nations which came against Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) shall even go up from year to year to worship the King... and to keep the feast of tabernacles* (Zechariah 14:16). This rules out both the inclusion gospel that flattens *all nations* into universal individual believers, and the universalism that would empty the judgment of consequence.

*As a shepherd divideth his sheep from the goats* is not the believer-versus-unbeliever line the inherited reading draws. It is Yechezk'el (Ezekiel)'s shepherd, dividing within the flock: *Behold, I judge between cattle and cattle, between the rams and the he goats... I will judge between the fat cattle and between the lean cattle* (Ezekiel 34:17, 20). The same chapter that has the Father seeking out his scattered sheep (Ezekiel 34:11-12) has him judging between cattle and cattle — and naming the Davidic shepherd who does it: *my servant David a prince among them* (Ezekiel 34:24). The King is that Davidic shepherd, doing exactly what Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) said he would. The sheep go to the right hand — the side of favor and life: *at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore* (Psalm 16:11) — the goats to the left.

## The criterion of mercy and the King's brethren

*Then shall the King say unto them on his right hand, Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world: For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat... And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.* (Matthew 25:34-40)

This is the heart of the judgment, and the two phrases the inherited reading misses are *the foundation of the world* and *my brethren.* The kingdom is *prepared for you from the foundation of the world* — the same foundation where the names were written: *whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world* (Revelation 17:8); *he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world* (Ephesians 1:4). The mercy these nations showed does not *earn* the inheritance; it *reveals* a status the Father set before the world began. The cause-and-effect of hearing once more: the action of mercy reveals what the foundation already held.

The six-fold criterion — *I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat; I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink; I was a stranger, and ye took me in; naked, and ye clothed me; I was sick, and ye visited me; I was in prison, and ye came unto me* — is the Tanakh's own criterion of righteousness. *Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to thy house? when thou seest the naked, that thou cover him* (Isaiah 58:7). *He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto Yahuah (the LORD)* (Proverbs 19:17). *Love ye therefore the stranger: for ye were strangers in the land of Mitsrayim (Egypt)* (Deuteronomy 10:19). It is the very work the King named as his own Messianic credential: *the Spirit of Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD) is upon me; because Yahuah (the LORD) hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted* (Isaiah 61:1).

But this is not a generic charity program, and the context fixes it. The mercy is mercy shown to the King's *brethren* during the tribulation he named in the prior chapter — *then shall they deliver you up to be afflicted, and shall kill you: and ye shall be hated of all nations for my name's sake* (Matthew 24:9). And the surviving nations are judged on how they treated those persecuted brethren when they had nothing to gain by it. The righteous do not even know they served the King — *Lord, when saw we thee?* — the mercy was walked without the self-conscious credential-claim, the very opposite of the flesh-credential the false-inclusion gospel rests on.

And *my brethren* — *tōn adelphōn mou* — is the King's own term for the gathered remnant of the twelve tribes, the seed of promise. He defined it himself: *whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother* (Matthew 12:50). After the resurrection: *go tell my brethren* (Matthew 28:10); *go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father* (John 20:17). And Sha'ul (Paul) names exactly whose brethren they are: *he is not ashamed to call them brethren... For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Avraham (Abraham). Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren* (Hebrews 2:11, 16-17). The brethren are the seed of Avraham (Abraham), the paternal-blood lineage — not categorical humanity. *That he might be the firstborn among many brethren* (Romans 8:29) — the King is the firstborn, and the many brethren are the gathered remnant the Father foreknew. The *least of these my brethren* are the marginalized among that gathered remnant during the tribulation. *Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me* — the same identification the King made on the Damascus road: *Sha'ul (Saul), Sha'ul (Saul), why persecutest thou me?... I am Yahusha (Jesus) whom thou persecutest* (Acts 9:4-5). What is done to the brethren is done to the King.

## The everlasting fire and the two eternities

*Then shall he say also unto them on the left hand, Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels... And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.* (Matthew 25:41-46)

The fire was *prepared for the devil and his angels* — not for the nations categorically. It is the lake of fire the dragon, the beast, and the false prophet are cast into (Revelation 20:10), the punishment the watcher rebellion stored up for itself: *the angels which kept not their first estate... suffering the vengeance of eternal fire* (Jude 6-7); *the angels that sinned... cast them down to Tartaros (hell)* (2 Peter 2:4). The goat-nations walk into it through their alignment with that rebellion, by the failure of mercy. The reverse six-fold is the failure of action as the criterion: *to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin* (James 4:17); *let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth* (1 John 3:18). And the goats ask the same honest question — *Lord, when saw we thee?* — and receive the same answer in reverse: *inasmuch as ye did it not to one of the least of these, ye did it not to me.*

The closing line sets two eternities side by side: *these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.* The Greek binds them to the same duration — *kolasin aiōnion* and *zōēn aiōnion,* everlasting punishment and everlasting life. This is the direct rule-out of both the universalism that would empty the punishment and the annihilationism that would end it. It is Daniel's two awakenings: *some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt* (Daniel 12:2). The *life eternal* is the millennial reign and what lies beyond it — *they lived and reigned with Messiah (Christ) a thousand years* (Revelation 20:4) — into the new heavens and new earth (Revelation 21:1-7) and the tree of life whose leaves are for the healing of the nations (Revelation 22:2). And the surviving sheep-nations enter that reign to receive the priestly instruction of the gathered remnant: *the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it* (Revelation 21:24) — the remnant *named the Priests of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Isaiah 61:6), reigning on the earth (Revelation 5:10). That is when the priestly relationship between the seed and the nations begins. Not now — then, in the reign.

So the King closes his public teaching with the whole arc laid out: the sifting of the wheat that bore no fruit, the gift given and lost, and the judgment of the surviving nations on their mercy to his brethren — and the everlasting life of those whose lives showed they loved him and his ways. The next words out of his mouth turn the road toward the Pesach (Passover): *ye know that after two days is the feast of the passover, and the Son of Adam is betrayed to be crucified* (Matthew 26:2). The teaching is finished. The Lamb walks toward the altar.
