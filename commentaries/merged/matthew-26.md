## The Lamb walks toward the altar

Matthew 26 opens the passion, and the lens that governs it is the oldest one in the Torah: the King of Yashar'el (Israel) walks into the Pesach (Passover) as the Pesach lamb. The Father set the pattern at the exodus — *the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you* (Exodus 12:13) — and now the lamb the pattern always pointed to walks toward the altar. Sha'ul (Paul) names it: *Messiah (Christ) our passover is sacrificed for us* (1 Corinthians 5:7). Yochanan (John) the Witness named it the first time he saw him: *Behold the Lamb of Elohim (God), which taketh away the sin of the world* (John 1:29). The chapter holds several inherited readings in check — the eternal-security reading, the Torah-abrogation reading, the transubstantiation and memorial-only readings of the supper, and all three collapses of the nature of God (the three-co-equal-persons formula, the modalist collapse, and the Arian created-being reading). And the system-not-people posture holds without exception through the Sanhedrin trial: the indictment is against the corrupt leadership the council had become, never against the Yahudi (Jewish) people, for the King himself is a Yahudi (Jew) of the tribe of Yahudah (Judah), the Davidic Messiah the prophets named.

## The prologue and the conspiracy

*And it came to pass, when Yahusha (Jesus) had finished all these sayings, he said unto his disciples, Ye know that after two days is the feast of the passover, and the Son of Adam is betrayed to be crucified. Then assembled together the chief priests, and the scribes, and the elders of the people, unto the palace of the high priest, who was called Kaiapha (Caiaphas)... But they said, Not on the feast day, lest there be an uproar among the people.* (Matthew 26:1-5)

*When Yahusha (Jesus) had finished all these sayings* is the Matthean formula that closes each of the five major discourses (Matthew 7:28; 11:1; 13:53; 19:1) — five discourses echoing the five books of Mosheh (Moses). The Olivet discourse is finished; the narrative turns to the cross. And the fourth time now the King foretells it: *the Son of Adam is betrayed to be crucified.* He names himself in the title direct — *the Son of Adam,* the Hebrew *ben adam,* the seed-of-Adam lineage weight the translators flattened into *son of man* — the King of the Davidic line walking himself toward the altar at the Father's appointed time.

The conspiracy at Kaiapha (Caiaphas)'s palace is the Tanakh's counsel against the Father's anointed walked into history: *the kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against Yahuah (the LORD), and against his anointed* (Psalm 2:2). And the irony of the Father's timing is in their own words — *Not on the feast day.* The leadership wanted the arrest away from the Pesach (Passover); the Father walked it straight into the Pesach itself, because the Lamb keeps the appointed time the Father set at Sinai. The leadership does not control the hour; the Father does.

## The anointing at Beit-Anyah

*Now when Yahusha (Jesus) was in Beit-Anyah (Bethany), in the house of Shim'on (Simon) the leper, there came unto him a woman having an alabaster box of very precious ointment, and poured it on his head... For ye have the poor always with you; but me ye have not always. For in that she hath poured this ointment on my body, she did it for my burial. Verily I say unto you, Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached in the whole world, there shall also this, that this woman hath done, be told for a memorial of her.* (Matthew 26:6-13)

The woman is Miryam (Mary) of Beit-Anyah (Bethany), sister of the Lazaros (Lazarus) the King had raised (John 12:1-8) — and she has walked the recognition the disciples have not. The anointing is the Tanakh's anointing of king and priest: *Shmuel (Samuel) took a vial of oil, and poured it upon his head... Is it not because Yahuah (the LORD) hath anointed thee to be captain over his inheritance?* (1 Samuel 10:1); *he poured of the anointing oil upon Aharon (Aaron)'s head, and anointed him, to sanctify him* (Leviticus 8:12). The King is both King and High Priest, and *Messiah* (Hebrew *Mashiach,* Greek *Christos*) means the Anointed One. She anoints the Anointed for his burial before he is dead.

The disciples' indignation was instigated by Yahudah (Judas) Iscariot — *not that he cared for the poor; but because he was a thief, and had the bag* (John 12:6). And here the inherited reading misuses verse 11. *For ye have the poor always with you* is the King quoting the Torah: *the poor shall never cease out of the land: therefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land* (Deuteronomy 15:11). This is not a dismissal of the care of the poor — it is a citation of the very command that the care of the poor is ongoing. Both stand together: the ongoing Torah-command to open the hand to the poor, and the once-in-history priority of anointing the Father's anointed for burial at this single moment. *Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached in the whole world* — the good news of the gathering of the scattered seed, going broad among all the nations where the citizens are scattered — *there shall also this... be told for a memorial of her,* the Tanakh's *zikkaron* (Exodus 12:14) carrying her act forward with the proclamation itself.

## The thirty pieces of silver

*Then one of the twelve, called Yahudah (Judas) Iscariot, went unto the chief priests, and said unto them, What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver. And from that time he sought opportunity to betray him.* (Matthew 26:14-16)

Yahudah (Judas) was one of the twelve — *have not I chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil?* (John 6:70) — and the betrayal by the familiar friend is the Tanakh's own grief: *mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me* (Psalm 41:9), which the King cites of him (John 13:18). The thirty pieces of silver are Zekharyah (Zechariah)'s prophecy walked into the open: *they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver. And Yahuah (the LORD) said unto me, Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price that I was prised at of them* (Zechariah 11:12-13) — fulfilled completely when the silver is later cast to the potter's field (Matthew 27:3-10). And thirty pieces is the Torah's price of a slave: *he shall give unto their master thirty shekels of silver* (Exodus 21:32). The leadership prices the King at the price of a slave, and the Father's prophetic irony stands over the bargain.

Two things hold at once here, and the inherited determinism collapses one of them. The Father's purpose was spoken through Zekharyah (Zechariah) and David centuries before, and Yahudah's (Judas's) individual choice was genuinely his — *the devil having now put into the heart of Yahudah (Judas) Iscariot... to betray him* (John 13:2), but the heart was his to give. The Father's prophetic purpose and the man's real choice both stand. The prophecy did not force the man; the man fulfilled the prophecy.

## The Last Supper — the new covenant in his blood

*And as they were eating, Yahusha (Jesus) took bread, and blessed it, and brake it, and gave it to the disciples, and said, Take, eat; this is my body. And he took the cup, and gave thanks, and gave it to them, saying, Drink ye all of it; for this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins. But I say unto you, I will not drink henceforth of this fruit of the vine, until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom.* (Matthew 26:26-29)

The Last Supper *is* the Pesach (Passover) meal the Torah commanded (Exodus 12; Deuteronomy 16:1-8) — not a replacement of it. The King keeps the feast the Father ordained at Sinai and invests it with the Lamb the feast always pointed toward. *This is my body* — the matzah broken — carries what he had already taught: *the bread that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world* (John 6:51). And here two inherited readings both fail. It is not the Roman Catholic transubstantiation, the bread turned into physical flesh; and it is not the Reformation memorial-only, the bread reduced to bare symbol. It is the Pesach meal carrying the Pesach-lamb fulfillment — the bread of the Father's own feast bearing the Lamb who keeps it.

*This is my blood of the new testament* — *kainēs diathēkēs,* the new covenant — is Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah)'s prophecy at its source: *I will make a new covenant with the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and with the house of Yahudah (Judah)... I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people* (Jeremiah 31:31, 33). This is the place the inherited church reads abrogation, and it is exactly backward. The new covenant is not the Torah replaced — it is the Torah *internalized,* the same Torah written on the heart, the new heart the Father promised: *a new heart also will I give you... and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them* (Ezekiel 36:26-27). And it is the Sinai covenant carried forward, not abolished — for the Sinai covenant too was inaugurated in blood: *Mosheh (Moses) took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant* (Exodus 24:8). The first covenant was sealed in the blood of bulls; the new is sealed in the blood of the Lamb, the same Torah now written inside.

*Shed for many for the remission of sins* is the suffering servant — *he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors* (Isaiah 53:12) — and the Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement) blood of atonement (Leviticus 16). The *many* (Greek *pollōn*) is the word the King used of the ransom (Matthew 20:28): the twelve tribes of Yashar'el (Israel), both the house of Yahudah (Judah) and the scattered seed, gathered from the scattering the new covenant of Jeremiah 31 names. This rules out the universalism that reads *many* as all-without-exception, and it rules out the Calvinist limited atonement that reads it as a pre-determined elect-count — it is the gathered houses of the covenant. And *until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom* sets the Pesach cup forward to the marriage of the Lamb (Revelation 19:7-9) and the Messianic banquet: *in this mountain shall Yahuah Tseva'ot (LORD of hosts) make unto all people a feast of fat things... He will swallow up death in victory* (Isaiah 25:6, 8). They sing the Hallel (Psalms 113-118) and go out *into the mount of Olives* — the very mountain Zekharyah (Zechariah) named for the return: *his feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives* (Zechariah 14:4).

## The shepherd smitten, the sheep scattered

*Then saith Yahusha (Jesus) unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad... Kefa (Peter) said unto him, Though I should die with thee, yet will I not deny thee.* (Matthew 26:31-35)

The scattering is Zekharyah (Zechariah)'s prophecy: *Awake, O sword, against my shepherd... smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered* (Zechariah 13:7). The shepherd is the King himself — the Davidic shepherd Yechezk'el (Ezekiel) named, *my servant David... he shall be their shepherd* (Ezekiel 34:23), the good shepherd who *giveth his life for the sheep* (John 10:11). *I will smite the shepherd* is the Father's own act — *it pleased Yahuah (the LORD) to bruise him* (Isaiah 53:10) — for the Pesach-lamb fulfillment. The scattering of the sheep lands when the disciples flee at the arrest (Matthew 26:56). And against it the King sets the resurrection: *after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galil (Galilee)* (fulfilled at Matthew 28:7, 16). Kefa (Peter) answers with the confidence of the flesh, and the King foretells the threefold denial — a failure that runs into restoration, not into the loss Yahudah (Judas) walked.

## Gethsemane — not as I will, but as thou wilt

*And he went a little further, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt... He went away again the second time, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if this cup may not pass away from me, except I drink it, thy will be done.* (Matthew 26:36-46)

*Gethsemane* — *Gat-Shemanim,* the oil-press — is where the olive is crushed for its oil, and on that ground the King is pressed under the Father's will. He takes the three of the inner circle — Kefa (Peter), Ya'aqov (James), and Yochanan (John) — the same three who saw the transfiguration (Matthew 17:1-8), where the Formed glory of the Father's expression was seen. *My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death* is the psalmist's cast-down soul: *why art thou cast down, O my soul?... hope thou in Elohim (God)* (Psalm 42:5).

The cup he asks to pass is not physical suffering alone — that is the modern collapse. It is the Father's cup of wrath, the cup the whole Tanakh holds: *in the hand of Yahuah (the LORD) there is a cup, and the wine is red... the dregs thereof, all the wicked of the earth shall wring them out, and drink them* (Psalm 75:8); *thou hast drunken the dregs of the cup of trembling* (Isaiah 51:17); *take the wine cup of this fury at my hand* (Jeremiah 25:15); *the cup of astonishment and desolation* (Ezekiel 23:33); *the cup of his indignation* (Revelation 14:10). The King as the Pesach lamb drinks the Father's cup of wrath for the remission of sins — the Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement) blood (Leviticus 16) carried to the cross. It is the cup he had already named to the sons of Zavdai (Zebedee): *are ye able to drink of the cup that I shall drink of?* (Matthew 20:22).

And *not as I will, but as thou wilt* carries the nature of God at its clearest, and it rules out three inherited collapses at once. The Father is the Formless source, the infinite the eye does not see — *whom no man hath seen, nor can see* (1 Timothy 6:16). The King is the Formed expression drawn from him — *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with Elohim (God), and the Word was Elohim (God)... and the Word was made flesh* (John 1:1, 14) — who had already said it plainly: *I seek not mine own will, but the will of the Father which hath sent me* (John 5:30). The submission in the garden is not a thing that began at the incarnation; it is the eternal relation of the Formed to the Formless, held in the flesh. The Son has real volition — *let this cup pass from me* — and real submission — *not as I will, but as thou wilt* — both at once. The three-co-equal-persons formula has nowhere to put a Son who submits his will to another; the modalist collapse cannot hold two genuinely distinct wills; and the Arian reading cannot make a created being out of the eternal Word who was *with* Elohim (God) and *was* Elohim (God). The simpler, older thing the verse shows is one Yahuah (God), Formless in source and Formed in expression, the Formed One submitting in the garden as he submitted in every theophany before the flesh. This is the Aqedat-Yitschaq (binding of Isaac) walked to its end: *Elohim (God) will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering* (Genesis 22:8) — the Father providing the Lamb, the Son submitting as Yitschaq (Isaac) submitted to his father.

The disciples sleep — *the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak* — for the Father *knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust* (Psalm 103:14). The King prays three times, the Tanakh pattern of devoted prayer (Daniel 6:10), and rises to meet the betrayer: *the Son of Adam is betrayed into the hands of sinners.*

## The arrest — twelve legions of angels

*And while he yet spake, lo, Yahudah (Judas)... came, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves... And, behold, one of them which were with Yahusha (Jesus) stretched out his hand, and drew his sword... Then said Yahusha (Jesus) unto him, Put up again thy sword into his place: for all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword. Thinkest thou that I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels? But how then shall the scriptures be fulfilled, that thus it must be?... But all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples forsook him, and fled.* (Matthew 26:47-56)

The kiss is the Tanakh's deceitful friend: *the kisses of an enemy are deceitful* (Proverbs 27:6); *it was thou, a man mine equal, my guide, and mine acquaintance* (Psalm 55:13). And the King's word to him — *Friend, wherefore art thou come?* — carries the Father's grief over one who walked into his own loss. The sword the King rebukes is not categorical pacifism; *all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword* invokes the Noach (Noah) covenant — *whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed* (Genesis 9:6) — and the rule that vengeance belongs to the Father, not to the disciple taking up arms for the Father's cause: *Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith Yahuah (the LORD)* (Romans 12:19; Deuteronomy 32:35). At this moment, the Father's purpose is the cross, and the sword does not serve it.

*Twelve legions of angels* — a Roman legion near six thousand, twelve legions near seventy-two thousand — carries the twelve-tribe restoration architecture: twelve, for the twelve tribes the King's twelve apostles answer to (Matthew 19:28). The King is not overcome; he could call the host at a word. He goes to the cross *voluntarily* — *no man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again* (John 10:18). And *that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled* names what is happening: the whole Tanakh cluster — Isaiah 53, Psalm 22, Zechariah 11 and 13, Psalm 41 — walking into the open. The disciples flee, and Zekharyah (Zechariah)'s scattered sheep are scattered.

## The Sanhedrin trial — the Son of Adam on the right hand of power

*And the high priest answered and said unto him, I adjure thee by the living Elohim (God), that thou tell us whether thou be the Messiah (Christ), the Son of Elohim (God). Yahusha (Jesus) saith unto him, Thou hast said: nevertheless I say unto you, Hereafter shall ye see the Son of Adam sitting on the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven.* (Matthew 26:57-68)

This is the chapter's load-bearing moment — the King's own self-identification before the council, the confluence of Psalm 110 and Daniel 7 at his own mouth. And the system-not-people posture holds here without flinching. The Sanhedrin — the chief-priest Sadducean party, the scribes, the Pharisaic elders — is the corrupt leadership the King has named since Matthew 21, the same leadership the seven woes of Matthew 23 exposed. The indictment is of the corrupt council, never of the Yahudi (Jewish) people; the King standing before it is himself a Yahudi (Jew) of the tribe of Yahudah (Judah).

The trial is itself a violation of the Torah it claims. They sought false witness — *at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established* (Deuteronomy 19:15) — and the false witnesses they found contradicted each other. The charge they seized on — *I am able to destroy the temple of Elohim (God), and to build it in three days* — twists what the King said of his own body: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up... But he spake of the temple of his body* (John 2:19, 21). His body is the Temple where the Father dwells, the Formed expression in flesh; the cross is its destruction, the third-day resurrection its rebuilding. And he holds his peace before the witnesses — *he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter... so he openeth not his mouth* (Isaiah 53:7) — the Lamb's silence walked into the room.

Then the high priest puts the question on oath, and the King answers. *Thou hast said* is the Hebraic affirmation, the *yes.* And *nevertheless I say unto you, Hereafter shall ye see the Son of Adam sitting on the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven* fuses the two greatest Messianic texts of the Tanakh at his own mouth. *The Son of Adam* here is the title direct — the Greek carries no comparative, no *hōs,* no *homoion,* unlike Daniel 7:13, Revelation 1:13, and Revelation 14:14 where the kaph is preserved. He names the title outright, as at Matthew 24:30 and 25:31. *Sitting on the right hand of power* is Psalm 110:1: *Yahuah (the LORD) said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.* And there the nature of God surfaces again — *Yahuah (the LORD) said unto my Lord* is the Father, the Formless source, speaking to the Formed expression, the *my Lord* of David's psalm, the King at the right hand of power. This is the same enthronement the apostles preach — *Elohim (God) hath made that same Yahusha (Jesus), whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Messiah (Christ)* (Acts 2:36) — and that Stephen sees: *I see the heavens opened, and the Son of Adam standing on the right hand of Elohim (God)* (Acts 7:56). The Father and the Son stand genuinely distinct, the Formed One submitted to the Formless source even in glory — which rules out the three-co-equal-persons formula, the modalist collapse, and the Arian created-being reading all at once.

*Coming in the clouds of heaven* is Daniel 7:13-14: *one like the Son of Adam came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days... his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away.* Daniel preserves the kaph — *like* the Son of Adam — honoring the incarnation, the Formed cloud-rider who took on flesh. But the King at the trial does not quote it with the comparative; he identifies *directly* with the figure: the everlasting dominion and the kingdom that shall not be destroyed are his. The cloud-riding is the Father's own signature — *who maketh the clouds his chariot* (Psalm 104:3); *Behold, Yahuah (the LORD) rideth upon a swift cloud* (Isaiah 19:1) — and the King names himself the cloud-rider. The Ancient of Days in the vision is the Father, shown in vision-form, transferring the everlasting kingdom to the Son. *Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him* (Revelation 1:7).

The high priest rends his clothes — itself a Torah violation, for the high priest *shall not uncover his head, nor rend his clothes* (Leviticus 21:10) — and the council pronounces blasphemy and death. But the irony stands: the King spoke the truth Psalm 110 and Daniel 7 preserved; the blasphemy is the council's, rejecting the Father's anointed. And the mocking fulfills the servant prophecy: *I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting* (Isaiah 50:6).

## Peter's denial — and he wept bitterly

*Then began he to curse and to swear, saying, I know not the man. And immediately the cock crew. And Kefa (Peter) remembered the word of Yahusha (Jesus), which said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And he went out, and wept bitterly.* (Matthew 26:69-75)

Kefa (Peter)'s threefold denial fulfills the King's word (Matthew 26:34), and it is the failure of the flesh the King warned of in the garden — the same Kefa (Peter) who confessed *thou art the Messiah (Christ), the Son of the living Elohim (God)* (Matthew 16:16), who walked on the sea, who saw the transfiguration, who drew the sword. But his denial is not Yahudah's (Judas's) betrayal. Where Yahudah (Judas) walked into loss, Kefa (Peter) walks into restoration — the threefold affirmation that will answer the threefold denial, and the commission *feed my sheep* (John 21:15-19). And the difference is in what he does with the failure: *he went out, and wept bitterly.* It is the broken and contrite heart the Father will not despise: *the sacrifices of Elohim (God) are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O Elohim (God), thou wilt not despise* (Psalm 51:17). It is the rending Yo'el (Joel) called for: *rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto Yahuah (the LORD) your Elohim (God)* (Joel 2:13). Kefa (Peter) rends his heart, while Kaiapha (Caiaphas) rent only his garments. This rules out the eternal-security reading at the level of the man: Kefa (Peter) genuinely failed, and was genuinely restored — through repentance, not automatically, not because the failure could not have cost him.

The chapter ends with the Lamb bound and delivered up, and the road runs straight into the next — the trial before Pilatos (Pilate), the silver cast to the potter's field that completes Zekharyah's (Zechariah's) prophecy, the scourging, the cross, the cry, the torn veil, and the burial. The whole Tanakh prophetic cluster the Father spoke centuries before is walking into the open at once, and the Pesach lamb walks toward the altar to take the cup of wrath for the *many* — the twelve tribes gathered from the scattering, into the new covenant written on the heart.
