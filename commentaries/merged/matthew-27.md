## The cross — every prophecy walking into the open at once

Matthew 27 is the crucifixion, and the lens that holds it is the one the whole passion opened on: the King of Yashar'el (Israel) is the Pesach (Passover) lamb walked to the altar — *Messiah (Christ) our passover is sacrificed for us* (1 Corinthians 5:7). What the chapter does, verse by verse, is walk the Tanakh's prophetic cluster into the open all at once: Psalm 22 in full, Isaiah 53, Zekharyah (Zechariah) 11 and 12, Amos 8, Genesis 3, Daniel 12, the Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement) architecture, the exodus Pesach. The chapter holds five inherited readings in check — the Father forsaking the Son at verse 46, the anti-Yahudi (anti-Jewish) misuse of verse 25, the Torah-abrogation reading of the torn veil, the categorical nations-inclusion reading of the centurion, and the determinism reading of Yahudah (Judas) and Pilatos (Pilate). And the system-not-people posture holds throughout: the indictment is of the corrupt Sanhedrin leadership and the Roman imperial machine, never of the Yahudi (Jewish) people, for the King is himself a Yahudi (Jew) of the tribe of Yahudah (Judah), the Davidic Messiah the prophets named.

## The morning council and the delivery to Pilatos

*When the morning was come, all the chief priests and elders of the people took counsel against Yahusha (Jesus) to put him to death: and when they had bound him, they led him away, and delivered him to Pontius Pilatos (Pilate) the governor.* (Matthew 27:1-2)

The morning council is the daylight ratification of a night trial that had already violated the Torah's own procedure — capital cases were to be tried by day, with a delay before sentencing. It is *the kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against Yahuah (the LORD), and against his anointed* (Psalm 2:2) walked into the open, the counsel the early assembly named in prayer: *the kings of the earth stood up, and the rulers were gathered together against Yahuah (the LORD), and against his Messiah (Christ)* (Acts 4:26). They hand him to Pilatos (Pilate) because the Sanhedrin lacked the capital jurisdiction Rome reserved: *It is not lawful for us to put any man to death* (John 18:31). And the Roman cross carries its own Torah weight — *he that is hanged is accursed of Elohim (God)* (Deuteronomy 21:23), which Sha'ul (Paul) cites: *Messiah (Christ) hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree* (Galatians 3:13). The curse is the Deuteronomy 28 exile judgment the twelve tribes walked into, never the Torah itself, which is the covenant instruction of the living Elohim (God). The King takes the curse as the Pesach lamb so the tribes can be gathered home.

## Yahudah's remorse and the field of blood

*Then Yahudah (Judas), which had betrayed him, when he saw that he was condemned, repented himself, and brought again the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and elders, saying, I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood... And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed, and went and hanged himself.* (Matthew 27:3-10)

The word for Yahudah's (Judas's) *repentance* here is *metamelētheis* — remorse, the realization that something has gone wrong — not *metanoia,* the turning to the Father the prophets called for: *rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto Yahuah (the LORD) your Elohim (God)* (Joel 2:13). Sha'ul (Paul) names the difference directly: *godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death* (2 Corinthians 7:10). Yahudah (Judas) confesses the innocent blood but does not turn to the Father — and that confession itself rules out the determinism reading: he knew, he could have turned, he did not. Where Kefa (Peter) wept bitterly and was restored, Yahudah (Judas) feels remorse and dies in it. The grace and the opportunity were real; both could be, and one was, lost.

And the silver completes Zekharyah's (Zechariah's) prophecy: *they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver. And Yahuah (the LORD) said unto me, Cast it unto the potter... and I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Zechariah 11:12-13). Yahudah (Judas) casts the silver in the Temple; the priests, scrupulous about blood-money in the treasury while careless about the innocent blood itself — *ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law* (Matthew 23:23) — buy the potter's field, the Akeldama, *the field of blood* (Acts 1:19). Matthew anchors the citation to Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah), combining the thirty pieces of Zekharyah (Zechariah) with the potter's-field judgment of Jeremiah 19 and the field-purchase of Jeremiah 32 — the prophetic cluster fulfilled completely. *As Yahuah (the LORD) appointed me* names the Father's purpose; the betrayal and the deflection name the men. Both walk at once.

## The trial before Pilatos — Bar-Abba and the two goats

*And the governor asked him, saying, Art thou the King of the Yahudim (Jews)? And Yahusha (Jesus) said unto him, Thou sayest... Whom will ye that I release unto you? Bar-Abba (Barabbas), or Yahusha (Jesus) which is called Messiah (Christ)?... Then answered all the people, and said, His blood be on us, and on our children.* (Matthew 27:11-26)

*Thou sayest* is the affirmation — yes — and the King is the King of the Yahudim (Jews), the Davidic Messiah, though *my kingdom is not of this world* (John 18:36). Before the accusations he is silent: *he was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth* (Isaiah 53:7) — the Lamb's silence in the governor's hall, so that Pilatos (Pilate) marvels.

The Bar-Abba (Barabbas) substitution carries the Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement) architecture, and the name itself sharpens it — *bar abba,* son of the father. The innocent Son of the Father is condemned; the guilty son-of-a-father goes free. This is the two goats of Leviticus 16: *Aharon (Aaron) shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for Yahuah (the LORD), and the other lot for the scapegoat* (Leviticus 16:8). The guilty Bar-Abba (Barabbas) is released like the scapegoat into the wilderness; the King goes to the cross like the goat for Yahuah (the LORD), whose blood is carried into the Holy of Holies — *the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited* (Leviticus 16:22). Pilatos (Pilate) knew *for envy they had delivered him* (verse 18) — *who is able to stand before envy?* (Proverbs 27:4).

Pilatos's (Pilate's) wife sends her dream warning — *Have thou nothing to do with that just man* — the Tanakh pattern of the Father warning an individual figure of the nations, as he warned Avimelekh (Abimelech) (Genesis 20:3), Lavan (Laban) (Genesis 31:24), Pharaoh (Genesis 41), and Nevukhadnetsar (Nebuchadnezzar) (Daniel 2). And Pilatos (Pilate) washes his hands by the Torah's own rite over untraced blood (Deuteronomy 21:1-9) — but he is not innocent. He held the authority, ordered the execution, and rejected the warning. *Ye denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you* (Acts 3:14). His cowardice is his own; the Father's purpose runs through it; both walk at once.

*His blood be on us, and on our children* is the verse the inherited church misused for centuries to justify anti-Yahudi (anti-Jewish) hatred, and it must be read against that misuse. This is the Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) crowd at the Sanhedrin-leadership moment, manipulated — *the chief priests and elders persuaded the multitude* (verse 20) — and it speaks the immediate judgment that fell on the Second Temple at 70 CE: *all these things shall come upon this generation* (Matthew 23:36); *there shall not be left here one stone upon another* (Matthew 24:2). It is not a categorical curse on the Yahudah (Judah) as a people. The Father has not cast away his people — *I say then, Hath Elohim (God) cast away his people? Elohim (God) forbid* (Romans 11:1) — and the very blood the crowd names is the blood that redeems them and their children, the Yom Kippur blood of the new covenant. *They shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him* (Zechariah 12:10); *the third shall be left therein... they shall call on my name, and I will hear them* (Zechariah 13:9). The blood is *for* them. The scourging fulfills the servant: *with his stripes we are healed* (Isaiah 53:5).

## The Roman mocking — the crown of thorns and the reed

*And when they had platted a crown of thorns, they put it upon his head, and a reed in his right hand: and they bowed the knee before him, and mocked him, saying, Hail, King of the Yahudim (Jews)!* (Matthew 27:27-31)

The soldiers' mockery walks the servant prophecies — *I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting* (Isaiah 50:6); *they shall smite the judge of Yashar'el (Israel) with a rod upon the cheek* (Micah 5:1). And the crown of thorns carries the curse of the ground: *thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee* (Genesis 3:18). The King wears the curse of the first Adam's ground on his own head — the Last Adam taking up what the first Adam brought, to restore it. The reed they hand him as a mock scepter is the scepter of Yahudah (Judah): *the sceptre shall not depart from Yahudah (Judah)... until Shiloh come* (Genesis 49:10). The soldiers speak the truth in mockery — he *is* the Shiloh, the everlasting scepter of Daniel 7:14 — and *Hail, King of the Yahudim (Jews)!* is the imperial greeting turned ironic over the true King.

## The procession to Golgotha

*And when they were come unto a place called Golgotha (Place of a Skull)... they gave him vinegar to drink mingled with gall: and when he had tasted thereof, he would not drink.* (Matthew 27:32-34)

Shim'on (Simon) of Cyrene is compelled to bear the cross — and the Father's providence is in the compulsion: he is *the father of Alexandros (Alexander) and Rufus* (Mark 15:21), and Sha'ul (Paul) later greets *Rufus chosen in Yahuah (the LORD), and his mother* (Romans 16:13). The family of the man who carried the cross came into the assembly. He walks, unknowing, the very thing the King taught: *let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me* (Matthew 16:24). Golgotha — *gulgolta,* the skull — is outside the gate (Hebrews 13:12), and it is the ground of Genesis 3:15: *it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.* The heel of the Seed is bruised at the cross; the head of the serpent is bruised at the cross and the resurrection. The vinegar mingled with gall — *they gave me also gall for my meat; and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink* (Psalm 69:21) — was an anesthetic, and the King refuses it; he walks the cross fully conscious, the cup he said he would not drink again until the Father's kingdom (Matthew 26:29).

## The crucifixion — Psalm 22 walking into the open

*And they crucified him, and parted his garments, casting lots... Then were there two thieves crucified with him... If thou be the Son of Elohim (God), come down from the cross... He trusted in Elohim (God); let him deliver him now, if he will have him.* (Matthew 27:35-44)

As a Pesach lamb, not a bone is broken (Exodus 12:46; John 19:36). And Psalm 22 walks into the open in detail. The parting of the garments: *they part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture* (Psalm 22:18). The two thieves: *he was numbered with the transgressors* (Isaiah 53:12) — and the one who turns even at the last receives the kingdom (Luke 23:43), grace at the very end. The superscription *KING OF THE YAHUDIM (JEWS)* — Pilatos's (Pilate's) own irony, *what I have written I have written* (John 19:22) — names the Davidic throne (2 Samuel 7:13; Isaiah 9:6-7). The mocking: *all they that see me laugh me to scorn: they shoot out the lip, they shake the head, saying, He trusted on Yahuah (the LORD) that he would deliver him: let him deliver him* (Psalm 22:7-8). And *if thou be the Son of Elohim (God), come down from the cross* is the wilderness temptation come back: *if thou be the Son of Elohim (God), command that these stones be made bread* (Matthew 4:3). The temptation to step out of the Father's purpose, spoken now through the mockers — and refused. *He saved others; himself he cannot save* gets it exactly backward: he *could* save himself — *I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels?* (Matthew 26:53); *No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself* (John 10:18) — and chooses the Lamb's death instead.

## The darkness and the cry — Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani

*Now from the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land unto the ninth hour. And about the ninth hour Yahusha (Jesus) cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My Elohim (God), my Elohim (God), why hast thou forsaken me?... Yahusha (Jesus), when he had cried again with a loud voice, yielded up the ghost.* (Matthew 27:45-50)

The three hours of darkness from noon to the ninth hour are the Tanakh's own sign: *I will cause the sun to go down at noon, and I will darken the earth in the clear day* (Amos 8:9). It echoes the darkness over Mitsrayim (Egypt) before the first Pesach (Exodus 10:21-23) and the Day of Yahuah (the LORD): *the sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and the terrible day of Yahuah (the LORD) come* (Joel 2:31). And the ninth hour is the Pesach evening sacrifice — *the whole assembly of the congregation of Yashar'el (Israel) shall kill it in the evening* (Exodus 12:6), the *bein ha-arbayim,* between the evenings. The Lamb dies at the hour the Pesach lambs were slaughtered.

*Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani* is the King citing the opening line of Psalm 22 — *my Elohim (God), my Elohim (God), why hast thou forsaken me?* (Psalm 22:1). And here the inherited reading reaches for a catastrophe the verse does not carry: the Father, it says, turned his face away and forsook the Son. That reading cannot stand, for two reasons. First, it would split the Formless and the Formed at the cross — but the Father is the Formless source and the King is the Formed expression drawn from him, eternally, and the Gethsemane submission of the night before — *not as I will, but as thou wilt* — was the relation holding, not breaking. *I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee* (Hebrews 13:5) is the Father's own word. Second, it ignores how the citation works. In the rabbinic pattern, naming a psalm's opening line summons the whole psalm — and Psalm 22 does not stay in the suffering. It walks *through* the suffering (verses 1-21) into vindication (verses 22-31), and it says of the Father, in the very psalm the King is invoking: *for he hath not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted; neither hath he hid his face from him; but when he cried unto him, he heard* (Psalm 22:24). The Father did not hide his face. He heard. The King cites the psalm of suffering that ends in vindication, and the cry is the beginning of a psalm the resurrection completes — *I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee... all the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto Yahuah (the LORD): and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before thee* (Psalm 22:22, 27). The brethren are the seed of promise (Hebrews 2:11-12); the worship of the nations is the millennial reign. The suffering is real and full; the vindication is real and full; the Father is present through both.

What the cross *is,* then, is the Lamb bearing the iniquity: *all we like sheep have gone astray... and Yahuah (the LORD) hath laid on him the iniquity of us all* (Isaiah 53:6); *he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin* (2 Corinthians 5:21) — the Pesach lamb taking the Deuteronomy 28 curse so the tribes can come home into the new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). And *yielded up the ghost* — *aphēken to pneuma* — is voluntary: *I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down* (John 10:18); *it is finished* (John 19:30); *Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit* (Luke 23:46; Psalm 31:5). The Roman execution did not take the life; the King laid it down.

## The torn veil, the earthquake, and the saints arising

*And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent; and the graves were opened; and many bodies of the saints which slept arose, and came out of the graves after his resurrection, and went into the holy city, and appeared unto many.* (Matthew 27:51-53)

The veil divided the holy place from the holy of holies (Exodus 26:33), the curtain the high priest passed once a year on Yom Kippur (Day of Atonement), *not without blood* (Hebrews 9:7). It is rent *from the top to the bottom* — the Father's act, from above, not a human hand. And this is the place the inherited church reads the Torah abolished, which is exactly wrong: the rending is the Yom Kippur architecture *fulfilled,* not the Torah cancelled. *Boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Yahusha (Jesus), by a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh* (Hebrews 10:19-20). The veil is his flesh, the Formed expression in the incarnation; the rending opens access to the Father. And the Torah is not abolished — it is fulfilled at the cross and internalized in the new covenant: *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts* (Jeremiah 31:33); *cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments* (Ezekiel 36:27). Still the covenant instruction of the living Elohim (God), now written on the heart.

The earthquake is the Tanakh's sign of the Father's coming — *then the earth shook and trembled* (Psalm 18:7); *I will shake the heavens, and the earth* (Haggai 2:6). And the graves opened and the saints arose — *many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake* (Daniel 12:2); *I have opened your graves, O my people, and brought you up out of your graves* (Ezekiel 37:13). They arose *after his resurrection,* because the King is the firstfruits — *Messiah (Christ) the firstfruits; afterward they that are Messiah's (Christ's) at his coming* (1 Corinthians 15:23). These are the faithful of the Tanakh, the fathers who walked the covenant — *these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise* (Hebrews 11:39) — a pre-figuring of the first resurrection (Revelation 20:4-6), the natural branches the Father is able to graft in again.

## The centurion's confession

*Now when the centurion, and they that were with him, watching Yahusha (Jesus), saw the earthquake, and those things that were done, they feared greatly, saying, Truly this was the Son of Elohim (God).* (Matthew 27:54)

The centurion's confession is an individual of the nations recognizing the King — and the inherited reading wants to make it a categorical grafting-in of the nations. It is not. It is the Tanakh pattern of the individual figure of the nations granted recognition: Rachav (Rahab) — *I know that Yahuah (the LORD) hath given you the land* (Joshua 2:9); Ruth — *thy people shall be my people, and thy Elohim (God) my Elohim (God)* (Ruth 1:16); Naaman — *now I know that there is no Elohim (God) in all the earth, but in Yashar'el (Israel)* (2 Kings 5:15); the widow of Tsarephath (Zarephath) — *now by this I know that thou art a man of Elohim (God)* (1 Kings 17:24); Pilatos's (Pilate's) wife; and now the centurion. The three categories hold — the house of Yahudah (Judah), the scattered seed of the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and the nations — and the individual of the nations receives recognition without the categorical grafting the inclusion gospel reads in. The nations' place is the millennial reign, receiving priestly instruction from the gathered remnant: *ye shall be named the Priests of Yahuah (the LORD)* (Isaiah 61:6). And the title *Son of Elohim (God)* was Caesar's imperial claim — the centurion speaks it over the crucified King, against Caesar.

## The faithful women and the burial

*And many women were there beholding afar off, which followed Yahusha (Jesus) from Galilee... among which was Miryam (Mary) Magdalene... When the even was come, there came a rich man of Arimathea (Arimathaea), named Yosef (Joseph)... and laid it in his own new tomb, which he had hewn out in the rock.* (Matthew 27:55-61)

The women kept the faithful witness from the cross through the burial to the resurrection (Matthew 28:1-10), while the male disciples had fled (Matthew 26:56). They followed from Galilee, ministering of their own substance (Luke 8:1-3) — the Tanakh pattern of the faithful women, Shifrah (Shiphrah) and Pu'ah (Puah) (Exodus 1:15-22), Miryam (Miriam) the prophetess, Devorah (Deborah), Channah (Hannah) — and they will be the first witnesses of the resurrection.

The burial fulfills the servant: *he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death* (Isaiah 53:9) — with the wicked, the two thieves; with the rich, Yosef (Joseph) of Arimathea. And Yosef (Joseph) is the system-not-people posture in flesh: a member of the Sanhedrin itself — *an honourable counsellor, which also waited for the kingdom of Elohim (God)* (Mark 15:43) — who *had not consented to the counsel and deed of them* (Luke 23:51). The indictment is of the corrupt council, and here a man within it recognizes the King and buries him with honor, as Nikodimos (Nicodemus) does beside him (John 19:39). The new tomb where no one had been laid (John 19:41) is the resting place of the firstfruits before the third day.

## The guard at the tomb

*Now the next day, that followed the day of the preparation, the chief priests and Pharisees came together unto Pilatos (Pilate)... So they went, and made the sepulchre sure, sealing the stone, and setting a watch.* (Matthew 27:62-66)

The leadership comes to Pilatos (Pilate) on the Shabbat (Sabbath) itself — the same leadership that had hedged the Shabbat (Sabbath) with fences against the King's healing on it (Matthew 12:1-14), now breaking it for political ends to guard a tomb. The hypocrisy is plain. And they remember the prophecy they reject: *that deceiver said, while he was yet alive, After three days I will rise again* — the three-day sign the King had given (Matthew 12:40; 16:21). The Father's irony closes the chapter: the Roman watch they set to prevent a resurrection witness becomes the Roman imperial witness *of* the resurrection (Matthew 28:11-15). The guard guarantees the empty tomb.

So the cross stands as the Father's purpose carried from suffering into vindication — the Pesach lamb who took the curse, the Yom Kippur blood that opened the veil, the firstfruits laid in a new tomb. The third day waits: *after two days will he revive us: in the third day he will raise us up* (Hosea 6:2). The Lamb has died for the *many* — the twelve tribes gathered home into the new covenant written on the heart — and the road runs into the morning of the empty tomb.
