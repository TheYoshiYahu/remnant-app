## The second-Sinai mountain — the heart-Torah in the King's own voice

Matthew 5 opens the Sermon on the Mount, and the framework reads the setting deliberately. The mountain is not incidental scenery. Mosheh (Moses) ascended a mountain to receive Torah from Yahuah (the LORD) and brought it down to the people at the base; Matthew 5 inverts the spatial frame — the Messiah goes up, and the people come up to him. The teacher does not descend with a tablet; the teacher sits, the rabbinic posture of teaching with authority, and the disciples draw near. The Torah is now spoken in the King's own voice, from the mountain, to those gathering to hear.

This is the second-Sinai motif Matthew has been preparing since chapter 2. Yahusha (Jesus) recapitulates Yashar'el (Israel) in his own body — the Egypt-sojourn of chapter 2, the Yarden (Jordan) immersion of chapter 3, the wilderness-testing of chapter 4 — and now arrives at the mountain. But this is not a repetition of Sinai; it is its continuation by the same Voice. By the Formless-Formed reading established at the *Immanuel — Elohim (God) with us* of Matthew 1:23 and the immersion-voice of Matthew 3:17, Yahuah who spoke at the burning bush, who descended on Sinai in fire, who spoke the ten words to Mosheh, is the Formed — the Son drawn from the Formless, the God of every theophany. He did not begin at this mountain or at Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem); he came *in* the flesh and stands on the mountain teaching, the same who once stood on it in fire. Same Voice. Same Torah. The intensification is in the unveiling of the heart-Torah the Sinai-Torah always carried in its substance.

The audience-structure matters. The disciples *came unto him* — those who left their nets, who stepped out of the vocational frame into the prophetic frame at Matthew 4:18-22. The multitudes follow and hear, and the sermon closes with them astonished.

*And it came to pass, when Yahusha (Jesus) had ended these sayings, the people were astonished at his doctrine: For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.* (Matthew 7:28-29)

The teaching is for the disciples in primary scope and for the multitudes in overhearing scope — the gathered remnant, the lost sheep being drawn, with the multitudes pulled toward the same gathering. The King is on the mountain because the kingdom is at hand and he speaks from the throne-place; the disciples are close because they answered the call; the multitudes are present because the Isaiah 9 light has dawned in Galilee-of-the-goyim and they are being drawn.

## The Beatitudes — the citizens-of-the-kingdom roster, not entry-conditions

*Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted. Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth. Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled. Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see Elohim (God). Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of Elohim (God). Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you, and persecute you, and shall say all manner of evil against you falsely, for my sake. Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets which were before you.* (Matthew 5:3-12)

The Beatitudes are not a how-to ladder of dispositions to attain the kingdom; they are a roster of the citizens already in it. They are how the lost sheep recognize themselves — they read the list and find their own posture named. The cause-and-effect the canon teaches applies here too: *if you hear, you are sons of Avraham (Abraham); hearing reveals, hearing does not make.* The poor in spirit hear *theirs is the kingdom of heaven* and know themselves named. The Beatitudes reveal; they do not manufacture.

**Poor in spirit.** The *anawim* of the Tanakh — the broken, the contrite, the destitute-in-self.

*For all those things hath mine hand made, and all those things have been, saith Yahuah (the LORD): but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word.* (Isaiah 66:2)

The kingdom belongs to such because they are the only ones who can receive it on its own terms. The Pharisaic-Sadducean righteousness-credential at Matthew 3:9 — *we have Avraham to our father* — is the opposite posture: full of credential, empty of contrition. The kingdom is for the empty.

**Mourn.** The mourners are those whose hearts are broken by the abominations in the holy city — who weep over what the system has become, who carry the prophetic sorrow that the leadership-pulpit has voided the heart-Torah. They are the ones marked for preservation through the day of judgment in Ezekiel 9, and the comfort they receive is Isaiah's.

*To proclaim the acceptable year of Yahuah (the LORD), and the day of vengeance of our Elohim (God); to comfort all that mourn; To appoint unto them that mourn in Tsion (Zion), to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of Yahuah (the LORD), that he might be glorified.* (Isaiah 61:2-3)

This closes the door on the Christian sentimentalism that reads *blessed are they that mourn* as generic grief-comfort. The mourning Yahusha names is the prophetic mourning over abominations.

**Meek.** Yahusha quotes Psalm 37 directly. The Hebrew is *anavim* — close cousin of *anawim,* the humble, the bowed, the unviolent-in-strength — and Psalm 37 is the frame.

*For evildoers shall be cut off: but those that wait upon Yahuah (the LORD), they shall inherit the earth. For yet a little while, and the wicked shall not be: yea, thou shalt diligently consider his place, and it shall not be. But the meek shall inherit the earth; and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace.* (Psalm 37:9-11)

The Beatitude is a gathering-prophecy: the meek inherit the land at the end of the age. The earth-inheritance is not a vague heavenly-realm aspiration; it is the prophetic land-inheritance of the gathered remnant in the millennial kingdom and beyond — the same earth, restored, after the dreadful day has cleansed the abominations.

**Hunger and thirst after righteousness.** *Tsedaqah* — covenant righteousness. The Pharisaic righteousness Yahusha will contrast at verse 20 is the counterfeit *tsedaqah,* outside-only performance for credential; the hunger he names is for the real thing.

*As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O Elohim (God). My soul thirsteth for Elohim (God), for the living Elohim (God): when shall I come and appear before Elohim (God)?* (Psalm 42:1-2)

The hungering one shall be *filled* — the *plēroō* root that returns at verse 17 in *fulfil.* What fills is the heart-Torah, the new-heart promise.

*Behold, the days come, saith Yahuah (the LORD), that I will make a new covenant with the house of Yashar'el (Israel), and with the house of Yahudah (Judah): ... But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Yashar'el (Israel); After those days, saith Yahuah (the LORD), I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people.* (Jeremiah 31:31, 33)

**Merciful.** Covenant *chesed* — steadfast loyalty in love, not Christian-sentimental softness. The merciful walk the prophetic *chesed* posture.

*For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of Elohim (God) more than burnt offerings.* (Hosea 6:6)

*He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth Yahuah (the LORD) require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy Elohim (God)?* (Micah 6:8)

The merciful obtain mercy — measure for measure. The Pharisaic-Sadducean system Yahusha will indict at Matthew 23 is the antithesis: outside-only Torah-keeping without the *chesed*-substance — *ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith* (Matthew 23:23).

**Pure in heart.** This is the heart-Torah Beatitude, the direct echo of the new-heart promises. The pure-in-heart shall see Elohim (God) — and the Formless-Formed reading holds here too: *No man hath seen Elohim (God) at any time* (John 1:18) of the Father, the Formless; the pure-in-heart see the Formed — the Son who is the visible image of the Formless — and through him know the Father. Psalm 24 names the ascent.

*Who shall ascend into the hill of Yahuah (the LORD)? or who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully.* (Psalm 24:3-4)

**Peacemakers.** *Shalom*-makers — those who carry the kingdom's wholeness-and-restoration ministry. *They shall be called the children of Elohim (God)* — the lineage-identification surfacing in plain text. *Children of Elohim* is the seed-of-Adam line-of-promise identification, the called-out for whom names were written from the foundation of the world. The peacemakers are not made children by peacemaking; they are children who make peace because that is what the children do. The Beatitude reveals; it does not manufacture.

**Persecuted for righteousness' sake.** The closing Beatitude lands on the cost of the gathered-remnant posture. The system that voided the heart-Torah persecutes those who walk it. *So persecuted they the prophets which were before you* — the prophetic-witness lineage from Hevel (Abel) through the Tanakh prophets through Yochanan (John) the Immerser. The framework reads this in the seed-war terms of Genesis 3:15 — the persecution-pattern runs through the seed-of-the-serpent against the seed-of-the-woman across the ages, and Yahusha traces the lineage of prophetic blood explicitly.

*Wherefore, behold, I send unto you prophets, and wise men, and scribes: and some of them ye shall kill and crucify; and some of them shall ye scourge in your synagogues, and persecute them from city to city: That upon you may come all the righteous blood shed upon the earth, from the blood of righteous Hevel (Abel) unto the blood of Zechariah son of Berekhyah, whom ye slew between the temple and the altar.* (Matthew 23:34-35)

The disciples are joining a lineage of suffering witnesses, and the reward is the prophets' own. The Christian commentary tradition has often listed the eight Beatitudes as virtues to cultivate. The framework reads them as identification-markers of those the Father has drawn — the family-resemblance of the citizens already in the kingdom, not the ladder into it.

## Salt of the earth, light of the world — the covenant-preservation and gathering-light of the gathered

*Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men. Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid. Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth light unto all that are in the house. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.* (Matthew 5:13-16)

The Greek *este* is indicative, not imperative — *you ARE the salt of the earth ... you ARE the light of the world.* The disciples are not commanded to become salt and light; they are named as the salt and light they already are because the Father has drawn them. And each term carries a Tanakh-substance the Christian commentary tradition has flattened into generic personal-witness. Salt is the preserving-of-covenant substance — the *covenant of salt* names a forever-standard.

*All the heave offerings of the holy things, which the children of Yashar'el (Israel) offer unto Yahuah (the LORD), have I given thee, and thy sons and thy daughters with thee, by a statute for ever: it is a covenant of salt for ever before Yahuah (the LORD) unto thee and to thy seed with thee.* (Numbers 18:19)

*Ought ye not to know that Yahuah Elohim (the LORD God) of Yashar'el (Israel) gave the kingdom over Yashar'el (Israel) to David for ever, even to him and to his sons by a covenant of salt?* (2 Chronicles 13:5)

Every offering carries salt; the Davidic kingdom is sealed by it. The disciples are salt of the earth because they preserve the covenant the Father gave — the heart-Torah, the Sabbath, the feasts, the names. The system that voids the heart-Torah is the salt that has lost its savour and is cast out: the leadership-pulpit that voids the commandments has lost the preserving function.

Light in the Tanakh carries the revealing-and-instructing register, particularly in the gathering-of-Yashar'el frame of Isaiah — and the sequence there is precise.

*And he said, It is a light thing that thou shouldest be my servant to raise up the tribes of Ya'aqov (Jacob), and to restore the preserved of Yashar'el (Israel): I will also give thee for a light to the goyim (Gentiles), that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth.* (Isaiah 49:6)

The raising-up of the tribes of Ya'aqov and the restoring of the preserved of Yashar'el is the first mission; the light to the goyim is the second — reaching the lost sheep in the lands of their scattering, where the seed of Yashar'el has been dispersed among the nations. The disciples on the second-Sinai mountain are the present-age witness of the gathered remnant carrying that gathering-light onward, not a generic Christian-evangelism motif but the gathering-of-the-scattered-seed mission the Messiah came to walk. Isaiah 60 widens the same scene at the consummation — *the goyim (Gentiles) shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising* (Isaiah 60:3) — and *a city that is set on an hill cannot be hid* lands against the eschatological gathering-place.

*And it shall come to pass in the last days, that the mountain of Yahuah's (the LORD's) house shall be established in the top of the mountains, and shall be exalted above the hills; and all nations shall flow unto it. And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of Yahuah (the LORD), to the house of the Elohim (God) of Ya'aqov (Jacob); and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Tsion (Zion) shall go forth the law, and the word of Yahuah (the LORD) from Yerushalayim (Jerusalem).* (Isaiah 2:2-3)

The visible city is the eschatological gathering-place; the disciples on the mountain are its present-age foreshadowing. And *let your light so shine ... that they may see your good works* reads in the Tanakh-substance of *ma'asim tovim* — covenant-loyal action, heart-Torah lived out. The light is the lived life of the gathered: not preaching alone, not credential-claim, but the visible covenant-walk, with the glory going to the Father whose Torah the action carries. The Christian commentary that reads *good works* against a *faith-not-works* framing has the grammar inverted.

## Think not that I am come to destroy the law — the load-bearing declaration

*Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled. Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven: but whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.* (Matthew 5:17-19)

These three verses are the load-bearing centerpiece of Matthew 5. The institutional-Christian system that voids the commandments has either ignored them entirely or built elaborate exegesis to neutralize them. The framework reads them plainly.

**Verse 17.** Yahusha opens with a preemptive denial — *think not,* *me nomisēte,* *do not even suppose.* He knows the misreading is coming. He is teaching the heart-Torah at a depth no one before him has unfolded, and he knows some will read his teaching as an attack on the written Torah, so he closes the door before it can be opened. *To destroy* — *kataluō* — means to dismantle, to take down stone by stone, to abrogate. He is not here to dismantle the Torah or the prophets; they stand.

*To fulfil* — *plēroō* — does not mean *to exhaust-and-thereby-end,* the antichrist reading the pulpit installed to retire the Torah. It means to fill up, to bring to fullness, to bring to pass. Throughout the New Testament *plēroō* regularly means *to fill* — to fill a net, to fill a heart with sorrow, to fill a house with sound. Applied to the law and the prophets, it means to bring their substance to its intended fullness — to fill the form with its substance. Yahusha fills the Torah with the heart-Torah; he does not empty it. The prophetic stream itself names the Messianic mission as Torah-magnification.

*Yahuah (the LORD) is well pleased for his righteousness' sake; he will magnify the law, and make it honourable.* (Isaiah 42:21)

And there is a deeper layer in *to fulfil* — the layer that is the gospel of the gathering said in one Greek word. What the Torah and the Prophets prophesied is the gathering of the scattered seed of Yashar'el home through redemption. The Torah itself wrote the curse for covenant-breaking — the scattering that would land both houses outside the land.

*And I will scatter you among the heathen, and will draw out a sword after you: and your land shall be desolate, and your cities waste.* (Leviticus 26:33)

The prophets confirmed it in execution. The northern house was divorced and renamed *Lo-Ammi,* not my people.

*Then said Elohim (God), Call his name Lo-ammi: for ye are not my people, and I will not be your Elohim (God).* (Hosea 1:9)

The southern house was cast out, favour withdrawn in the scattering.

*Therefore will I cast you out of this land into a land that ye know not, neither ye nor your fathers; and there shall ye serve other gods day and night; where I will not shew you favour.* (Jeremiah 16:13)

But the same Torah that wrote the curse wrote the return path.

*If they shall confess their iniquity, and the iniquity of their fathers, with their trespass which they trespassed against me, and that also they have walked contrary unto me; ... if then their uncircumcised hearts be humbled, and they then accept of the punishment of their iniquity: then will I remember my covenant with Ya'aqov (Jacob), and also my covenant with Yitschaq (Isaac), and also my covenant with Avraham (Abraham) will I remember; and I will remember the land.* (Leviticus 26:40-42)

Confession of sins — own and fathers' — plus a humbled heart plus *accept of the punishment of their iniquity* (saying *he was just* in the scattering) is the Torah-named door home. *Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand* (Matthew 3:2; 4:17) is the gospel-grammar of that same door. The return is then completed in the prophetic ingathering.

*And it shall come to pass, when all these things are come upon thee, the blessing and the curse, which I have set before thee, and thou shalt call them to mind among all the nations, whither Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God) hath driven thee, and shalt return unto Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God), and shalt obey his voice according to all that I command thee this day, thou and thy children, with all thine heart, and with all thy soul; that then Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God) will turn thy captivity, and have compassion upon thee, and will return and gather thee from all the nations, whither Yahuah thy Elohim (the LORD thy God) hath scattered thee.* (Deuteronomy 30:1-3)

But the scattered could not walk the return path on their own — divorced, scattered, no longer his people, no longer under his favour in the lands of the scattering. To open the door home, the Messiah had to redeem them: to come where they were, to bear what the Torah's curse-system named, to break the divorce by going through death and rising the third day, raised in flesh as the firstfruits of the same gathering. That death and resurrection is what makes the gathering possible. *To fulfil* the Torah and the Prophets is to bring to pass the redemption that opens the gathering — and the Sermon on the Mount is then not the abolition of the Torah for a new dispensation but the heart-Torah teaching for the redeemed scattered seed being gathered home, given new hearts, and instructed to walk in the statutes their fathers broke.

**Verse 18.** The eschatological terminus is the dissolution of the present heavens and earth — the consummation.

*But the day of Yahuah (the Lord) will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up. ... Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.* (2 Peter 3:10, 13)

Until then, every letter stands. The *jot* is the *yod,* the smallest letter of the Hebrew alphabet; the *tittle* is the *kotz,* the small ornamental stroke on certain letters, the smallest mark on the page. Yahusha binds himself to the consonantal Hebrew text at the smallest-possible-mark level — not one letter passes, not one stroke of one letter — until the consummation. The Lukan parallel locks it: *And it is easier for heaven and earth to pass, than one tittle of the law to fail* (Luke 16:17). Some Christian commentary reads *till all be fulfilled* as *until I, on the cross, fulfill it.* That violates the verse twice over: the parallel clause is *till heaven and earth pass* — the terminus is the consummation, not the crucifixion — and verse 19 presupposes the commandments are still operative in the kingdom Yahusha is establishing. If verse 18 meant *until the cross,* verse 19's binding force would evaporate the moment the cross was reached.

**Verse 19** is the direct diagnostic for the modern Judaizer movement — institutional Christianity's leadership-pulpit. The diagnostic has two components: *break* plus *teach men so.* The personal breaking is one thing; the breaking-coupled-with-teaching-others-to-break is what Yahusha names. The horn's pulpit teaches the congregation that the commandments are abolished — the Sabbath moved, the dietary laws voided, the feasts replaced, the heart-Torah of obedience pitted against grace. This is *break and teach men so,* and Yahusha names them *least in the kingdom of heaven.* Two observations. First, he does NOT say *not in the kingdom at all* — he says *least.* Mercy is held open. The congregation handed the antinomian gospel by the pulpit is not categorically out; some hear the Shepherd's voice and turn. The teachers are diagnosed first; the followers are victims of inherited interpretation, handed this in good faith, also the lost sheep. Second, the *least* designation still indicts — the framework does not soften the verse into *all paths lead home.* The dropping of the commandments from public teaching is named by the King himself as the kingdom-status-diminishing act.

The contrast is the reverse direction: *whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great.* Walk the commandments, teach the commandments, and the kingdom-status is great — Mosheh, the prophets, Yochanan the Immerser, the apostolic witnesses who did and taught, the gathered remnant doing and teaching today. Paul denies any antinomian reading of his own ministry.

*Do we then make void the law through faith? Elohim (God) forbid: yea, we establish the law.* (Romans 3:31)

*Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.* (Romans 7:12)

And Ya'aqov (James) names the same standing of the Torah.

*For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.* (James 2:10)

Matthew 5:19 is the New Testament companion of the 1234 of Truth — *he that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar* (1 John 2:4). Both say the same thing in different grammar: keeping the commandments, and teaching others to keep them, is the proof of knowing him.

## Except your righteousness exceed — the Pharisaic-righteousness contrast

*For I say unto you, That except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no case enter into the kingdom of heaven.* (Matthew 5:20)

Verse 20 bridges the load-bearing declaration to the six antitheses. Yahusha is not saying *the Pharisaic righteousness is righteousness, but you need more of it.* The Pharisaic righteousness is named here as a baseline that must be exceeded — and exceeded not by doing more of it, but by walking the heart-Torah it counterfeits. The Pharisaic righteousness is outside-only performance, oral-tradition expansion, void-of-heart compliance with display-value. The leadership-system was already named at Matthew 3:7 as the *generation of vipers,* the antichrist-front in its first-century beat; this is that same system. The flesh-credential gospel of the circumcision-party Paul will identify in his letters is the descendant of the Pharisaic righteousness Yahusha names here. Both surface the same theology: do the outside, claim the credential, void the heart-Torah.

*Knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Yahusha HaMashiach (Jesus Christ) ... for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified.* (Galatians 2:16)

Paul's *works of the law* (*erga nomou*) names the same flesh-credential performance — the circumcision-party's system for earning standing — not Torah-observance itself. The heart-Torah Yahusha will now teach in verses 21 through 48 is the exceeding righteousness: the new-heart Torah of Jeremiah 31:33, the Spirit-empowered Torah of Ezekiel 36:26-27, the commandments walked not as performance-for-credential but as the natural expression of a new heart. And the diagnostic runs to our own day with full force: institutional Christianity carries the same flesh-credential gospel (be baptized, say the prayer, join the church, and you are saved) in a different costume. Both versions are the Pharisaic righteousness, and both fail to enter the kingdom on Yahusha's explicit standard.

## The six antitheses — oral-tradition correction, not Torah supersession

The six *ye have heard ... but I say unto you* exchanges of verses 21 through 48 are the most-frequently-misread block in the Sermon. The Christian commentary tradition has often read them as Yahusha abrogating the Torah by setting his own authority above Mosheh's — which collapses him into the *destroy the law* posture he just rejected. The framework reads them on a different axis.

The methodological key: Yahusha is not contrasting himself with the written Torah of Mosheh; he is contrasting himself with the oral tradition of the Pharisaic-Sadducean leadership-system — the *takkanot* and *gezerot,* the truncations and additions laid over the Torah by the rabbinic schools. *Ye have heard* — *ēkousate* — references what was *heard* from the synagogue teachers' oral tradition, not what was *written* in the scroll. His *but I say unto you* is the corrective restoration of the original Torah's substance against the rabbinic distortion. This is the same harmonization-discipline he walked against the devil's selective Psalm 91 quote at Matthew 4 — bringing the overriding Torah-word into the conversation against a partial-citation distortion. He names the oral-tradition-over-Torah pattern in plain language elsewhere.

*Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. For laying aside the commandment of Elohim (God), ye hold the tradition of men ... Making the word of Elohim (God) of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered.* (Mark 7:7-9, 13)

And the Torah's own prohibition names the move the tradition makes.

*Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) which I command you.* (Deuteronomy 4:2)

The six antitheses are six cases of that add-or-diminish violation.

**Antithesis 1 — murder and anger.**

*Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not kill; and whosoever shall kill shall be in danger of the judgment: But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment ... Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.* (Matthew 5:21-24)

The oral tradition stopped the murder-commandment at the bodily act. The commandment always carried the heart-substance — the Torah explicitly forbids the seed-of-murder at the heart level.

*Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.* (Leviticus 19:17)

Yahusha is not innovating; he is restoring what the oral-tradition's surface-focus had left behind. This is a *diminish* case. The escalating curse — anger, *Raca,* *thou fool* — names three intensities of the heart-violation, and the altar-rule names the priority: heart-reconciliation precedes worship, the same prophetic stream that has always taught that sacrifice without heart is rejected — *I desired mercy, and not sacrifice* (Hosea 6:6).

**Antithesis 2 — adultery and lust.**

*Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not commit adultery: But I say unto you, That whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.* (Matthew 5:27-28)

Again the tradition stopped at the act. The Torah already named the heart-violation in the tenth commandment.

*Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's.* (Exodus 20:17)

Yahusha unfolds the tenth-commandment-substance into the seventh-commandment-frame; the Torah taught both, and the oral tradition had separated them — another *diminish* case. The verse 29-30 renunciation rhetoric — *if thy right eye offend thee, pluck it out* — is hyperbolic; no one has ever taken it as a literal command to mutilate, and Yahusha does not contradict the Torah's own prohibition on bodily mutilation (Leviticus 19:28). The force is gravity-naming: the heart-stake is so high that radical-renunciation imagery is the proper register.

**Antithesis 3 — divorce.**

*It hath been said, Whosoever shall put away his wife, let him give her a writing of divorcement: But I say unto you, That whosoever shall put away his wife, saving for the cause of fornication, causeth her to commit adultery: and whosoever shall marry her that is divorced committeth adultery.* (Matthew 5:31-32)

The Pharisaic schools had built a body of teaching on Deuteronomy 24:1's *because he hath found some uncleanness in her* — the Hillel school reading *some uncleanness* expansively to permit divorce for any cause, the Shammai school narrowly for sexual immorality only. Yahusha sides with the restrictive reading and grounds it in the Genesis-2 marriage-design, which he states explicitly at Matthew 19.

*Mosheh (Moses) because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so.* (Matthew 19:8)

Deuteronomy 24:1's writing of divorcement was a regulatory-concession to hardness of heart, not a Torah-mandate for permissive divorce; the oral tradition inverted the concession into a license, diminishing the original design. Yahusha restores it — divorce is permitted only for *porneia,* sexual unfaithfulness, and otherwise the *one flesh* of Genesis stands.

*Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.* (Genesis 2:24)

This is the same two-become-one the seed-covenant rests on: the marriage covenant joins the wife into the husband's identity, the bond the King is here protecting against the tradition's casual dissolution. The framework also rules out the inverse misuse — the verse is not a categorical no-divorce-ever stance, which would collapse the *porneia*-exception Yahusha himself articulates.

**Antithesis 4 — oaths.**

*Again, ye have heard that it hath been said by them of old time, Thou shalt not forswear thyself, but shalt perform unto Yahuah (the Lord) thine oaths: But I say unto you, Swear not at all ... But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.* (Matthew 5:33-37)

The oral tradition had built elaborate oath-formulas with gradations of binding — by heaven, by the earth, by Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), by the temple, by the gold of the temple, by one's own head — a tool for evading the binding-force of oaths through technical loopholes. Yahusha indicts the same gradation-system explicitly later: *Whosoever shall swear by the temple, it is nothing; but whosoever shall swear by the gold of the temple, he is a debtor! Ye fools and blind* (Matthew 23:16-17). He cuts the apparatus — *swear not at all,* eliminate the gradation-system — and restores the heart-substance: integrity of speech, a yes that means yes, a no that means no. The Torah commanded against false oaths.

*And ye shall not swear by my name falsely, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy Elohim (God): I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:12)

Yahusha does not abolish oath-taking categorically — Paul will swear oaths in his epistles, calling God to witness, the legitimate form. He cuts the gradation-evasion-system, another *diminish* case, and restores the heart-substance: speak true, and your speech needs no fortification.

**Antithesis 5 — eye for an eye.**

*Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth: But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also ... And whosoever shall compel thee to go a mile, go with him twain. Give to him that asketh thee, and from him that would borrow of thee turn not thou away.* (Matthew 5:38-42)

The framework reads this carefully, because the Christian-pacifist tradition has misused it to claim Yahusha abolished the Torah's judicial standard. The *eye for an eye* was a judicial standard — the *lex talionis* — that LIMITED retaliation to proportional justice administered by the courts.

*And if any mischief follow, then thou shalt give life for life, Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.* (Exodus 21:23-24)

It was a restraining-of-vengeance principle, preventing escalating-cycle vengeance and under-punishment alike. The oral tradition had slipped it out of the judicial sphere into the personal-revenge sphere, applying it as a green-light for personal retaliation — a sphere-mistake. Yahusha restores it to its sphere by cutting off the personal-revenge misuse: *resist not evil* on the personal level, *turn the other cheek,* *let him have thy cloke also.* The disciple does not personally administer *lex talionis* — the courts do — and Paul confirms the magistrate's continuing sword.

*For he is the minister of Elohim (God) to thee for good. But if thou do that which is evil, be afraid; for he beareth not the sword in vain: for he is the minister of Elohim (God), a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil.* (Romans 13:4)

The judicial-Torah remains operative as the courts' standard; the disciple does not retaliate personally; both stand together. The Christian-pacifist absolutization collapses one for the other. The verse 41-42 extension names the heart-Torah generosity lived out under occupation — the disciple goes beyond the demanded compliance, the *chesed* of the merciful Beatitude in action.

**Antithesis 6 — love your neighbor and hate your enemy.**

*Ye have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy: But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you; That ye may be the children of your Father which is in heaven: for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.* (Matthew 5:43-45)

This surfaces the clearest oral-tradition-addition case in the six. *Love thy neighbour* is from the Torah.

*Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:18)

*And hate thine enemy* is NOT in the Torah. There is no such commandment in the Tanakh. The oral tradition — and the Qumran community's *War Scroll* explicitly taught a *hate-the-sons-of-darkness* doctrine — had added it. Yahusha is not contradicting Mosheh; he is correcting an oral-tradition interpolation spliced onto the Torah. The Torah's actual command was love-of-neighbor and love-of-stranger.

*But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 19:34)

And the Torah commanded positive action toward the enemy — *if thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him again* (Exodus 23:4) — and the wisdom-stream the same.

*If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink.* (Proverbs 25:21)

The Torah's command was love-of-neighbor AND love-of-stranger AND positive-action toward the enemy. The oral tradition had inserted a hate-of-enemy clause; Yahusha strips the addition off and restores the Torah's actual command, extending the love-command to the enemy as the heart-completion of what the Torah always carried. The framework also rules out the Christian-pacifist misuse here, paralleling antithesis 5: the personal-disciple ethic is love-of-enemy, the judicial-Torah is preserved, and the eschatological judgment of Yahuah against the abominations of the counterfeit-system is preserved — he brings desolation on the false-system at the day; he stands on the Mount of Olives to war with the nations gathered against Yerushalayim. The disciple loves the personal enemy and does not personally administer judgment; Yahuah administers eschatological judgment on the systems and the principalities in his timing.

The six antitheses close with the heart-Torah climax.

*Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.* (Matthew 5:48)

*Perfect* — *teleios* — is the Greek for the Hebrew *tamim,* which in the Tanakh means *whole, complete, blameless* — the same *tamim* Yahuah commanded Avraham (Abraham) at Genesis 17:1 and Mosheh at Deuteronomy 18:13. The Beatitudes' *pure in heart* finds its completion here: the heart-Torah unfolded across six antitheses calls the disciple to a whole-hearted walk before the Father, the standard the Torah has always named. Kefa (Peter) names the same pattern on the apostolic side — *Be ye holy; for I am holy* (1 Peter 1:16).

The antitheses, taken together, do not abolish the Torah. They restore it. They strip the oral-tradition distortions off — the truncations of antitheses 1, 2, and 4, the inversion-of-concession of antithesis 3, the sphere-mistake of antithesis 5, and the outright addition of antithesis 6 — and they reveal the heart-Torah underneath. The Torah is intensified, never abolished. The contrary-to-Torah test stands at full force: any reading of Matthew 5 that voids the commandments is wrong, including the reading that turns the antitheses into Yahusha-versus-Mosheh.

## The Sermon's interior architecture — citizens already, walking now

The Sermon's interior architecture is the kingdom-citizens-living-as-kingdom-citizens shape. The Beatitudes name who the citizens already are; the salt-and-light verses name what they are in the world's frame; the verses 17 through 19 declaration locks the Torah as the standard they walk; verse 20 names the counterfeit-righteousness they exceed; the antitheses unfold what walking the heart-Torah looks like in six cases; verse 48 names the *tamim*-completion they grow toward. The Sermon is not a how-to manual for salvation-earning; it is a description of the gathered remnant's life and the standards by which it walks. It dismantles the counterfeit-righteousness, names the heart-Torah, and invites the gathered to walk what they are. It does not prescribe practice as the program of salvation; it describes the practice the gathered already carry because the Father has drawn them.

The Sermon is the Shepherd's voice articulated at length.

*But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.* (John 10:26-27)

The hearers who hear are the gathered; the hearers who do not are revealed by the same hearing-test — not manipulation, but the prophetic discrimination the kingdom-proclamation accomplishes by its own substance.

And the whole sits on the same eschatological filter Matthew 3 carried. The kingdom-at-hand is inaugurated with Yahusha's ministry — at hand, available, being entered — but not yet consummated; the consummation awaits the day of Yahuah, the cosmic signs, the gathering of the wheat, the burning of the tares, the millennial reign of the Messiah on the earth. The Beatitudes' future-tense promises land there: the citizens ARE now (present indicative); they shall RECEIVE the consummation-blessings then (future passive). The meek do not currently inherit the earth in a spiritualized internal sense; they shall inherit the earth at the day of Yahuah — the same earth, restored, after the dreadful day has cleansed the abominations. This rules out the amillennial collapse of the future-promises into present-spiritual-fulfillments. The kingdom is at hand, the King is sitting on the mountain, and the consummation is ahead.
