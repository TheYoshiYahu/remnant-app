## One diagnostic walked across the whole chapter — the heart the act flows from

Matthew 6 is the middle of the Sermon on the Mount, and the chapter turns on a single diagnostic carried all the way through. Yahusha (Jesus) takes three practices the covenant had always commanded — alms, prayer, and fasting — and contrasts the form that performs them *to be seen of men* with the form that does them before *thy Father which seeth in secret.* He sets the Lord's Prayer at the center as the kingdom-citizens prayer-pattern, couples the receiving of the Father's forgiveness to the citizens' own forgiveness-extension, and then turns the same diagnostic on the heart's treasure, the disciple's eye, the master the disciple actually serves, and the daily trust the kingdom-citizens walk against the anxious grasping of the present age. The chapter does not abolish a single Torah practice. It names what the practice looks like when the heart that walks it has been brought home.

This is the practical specification of the warning Yahusha (Jesus) set at the head of the Sermon's middle — that the kingdom-citizens' righteousness must exceed the righteousness of the scribes and the Pharisees. Where the six antitheses of Matthew 5:21-48 corrected the oral tradition at six commandment cruxes, moving the keeping of the commandment from the surface-act to the heart-disposition, Matthew 6 walks three practices the covenant had always commanded and contrasts the display-form with the secret-form. The same act flows from two different hearts; the motive separates them.

## The display-versus-secret diagnostic — one structure across alms, prayer, and fasting

The opening verse frames the next seventeen.

*Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them: otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven.* (Matthew 6:1)

The Greek *prosechete* — *take heed* — is the watchful imperative; the disciples are to keep watch on the motive that drives the act. What follows is a unified three-part diagnostic. Yahusha (Jesus) names three core practices of covenant-life — alms, prayer, fasting — and walks the identical contrast through each. The Pharisaic-counterfeit form practices the act *to be seen of men* and receives its reward in the seeing; the kingdom-citizens form practices the act *in secret,* and the Father who *seeth in secret* rewards openly.

The *to be seen of men* refrain names the flesh-credential gospel architecture — the religious act performed for honor from men, the surface-form without the heart-substance, the flesh-credential without the heart-Torah. This is the same architecture the Sermon has been confronting throughout, and it wears more than one costume across history. The Pharisaic-counterfeit-righteousness Yahusha (Jesus) named at Matthew 5:20 is one costume; the Galatian agitators carry it in another (*be circumcised, keep the boundary-marker, you are in the covenant* — flesh-credential without the heart-Torah); institutional Christianity carries it in another (*say the prayer, be baptized, join the church, you are saved* — flesh-credential without the heart-Torah); the modern Hebrew Roots false-inclusion gospel carries it in another (*Torah-keep and confess, and you are Yashar'el (Israel)* — flesh-credential without the paternal-blood covenant the prophets named); Islam carries it in another. The Greek the chapter uses for these figures is *hupokritai* — stage-actors playing roles for an audience — the same Pharisaic-leadership-system Yahusha (Jesus) will indict by name at Matthew 23. And he names the architecture at the level of the heart by quoting Isaiah at the Pharisees directly.

*Wherefore Adonai (the Lord) said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men.* (Isaiah 29:13)

*He answered and said unto them, Well hath Yeshayahu (Esaias) prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. For laying aside the commandment of Elohim (God), ye hold the tradition of men.* (Mark 7:6-8)

The diagnostic Yahusha (Jesus) names at Mark 7 is identical to what Matthew 6:1-18 walks at the practice-level: the surface-act honoring with the mouth, the heart far from the Father, the doctrines-of-men replacing the heart-Torah. The *they have their reward* verdict that lands three times in this chapter — at the alms, at the prayer, at the fast — lands on the same architecture wherever it appears. The contrary-to-Torah test names the lie in every costume: *He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him* (1 John 2:4).

The kingdom-citizens righteousness, by contrast, is heart-Torah for the Father's eyes alone — and the visible witness of good works flows from it as the natural outcome, not as the engineered purpose. This is critical, because the Sermon has already said:

*Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.* (Matthew 5:16)

The contradiction is only apparent. Matthew 5:16's *seen* is the visible life of covenant-faithfulness that glorifies the Father; Matthew 6:1's *seen* is the engineered public display that seeks self-glory. The disciples' lives are visible — they are a city on a hill, a lamp on a stand, the salt of the earth — and from that visibility the world sees the Father. What is forbidden is the act engineered for the visibility itself; what is required is the act done from the heart-Torah, whose visibility, if it comes, glorifies the Father.

## Alms in secret — the motive-correction, not a practice-removal

*Therefore when thou doest thine alms, do not sound a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory of men. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward. But when thou doest alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth: That thine alms may be in secret: and thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly.* (Matthew 6:2-4)

The *trumpet* image is hyperbolic — there is no historical evidence of literal trumpet-sounding for alms-giving in first-century Yerushalayim (Jerusalem) — but the rhetorical force is the visibility-engineering pattern. The Pharisaic-counterfeit form announces alms by whatever means available; the kingdom-citizens form practices alms so quietly that *the left hand* does not know what the right does — an idiom for the practice's freedom from any self-witnessing apparatus, including the disciple's own admiration of his own giving.

The Tanakh-substance behind the practice is dense. Alms-giving is not a New Testament invention; it is a Torah command and a wisdom-tradition lifeway.

*And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest. And thou shalt not glean thy vineyard, neither shalt thou gather every grape of thy vineyard; thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger: I am Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 19:9-10)

*When thou cuttest down thine harvest in thy field, and hast forgot a sheaf in the field, thou shalt not go again to fetch it: it shall be for the stranger, for the fatherless, and for the widow: that Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God) may bless thee in all the work of thine hands.* (Deuteronomy 24:19)

*Blessed is he that considereth the poor: Yahuah (the LORD) will deliver him in time of trouble.* (Psalm 41:1)

*He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto Yahuah (the LORD); and that which he hath given will he pay him again.* (Proverbs 19:17)

Any reading that turns these verses into a downgrade of alms-giving is rejected by the chapter itself. Alms-giving stands; the Torah commands it; the wisdom-tradition celebrates it; the practice continues. The motive-correction is what the verse specifies. The kingdom-citizens form is the Torah's own gleaning-and-open-handed practice extended into urban-monetary form, walked in secret because the doing of it is for the Father's eyes and the poor brother's benefit, not for the alms-giver's reputation.

The Father's reward-economy is named openly: *thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly.* This is a real exchange, not a metaphor. The Father sees the secret act, and the reward — in present-age covenant-blessing and in eschatological consummation — is his response. The Pharisaic-counterfeit form receives the reward-of-being-seen now and forfeits the Father's reward. The doubled reward-language names what that form trades away for the brief currency of being-seen.

## Prayer in secret — the closet posture and the relational frame

*And when thou prayest, thou shalt not be as the hypocrites are: for they love to pray standing in the synagogues and in the corners of the streets, that they may be seen of men. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward. But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly. But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.* (Matthew 6:5-8)

The same diagnostic specified at the prayer-practice. The Pharisaic-counterfeit form prays publicly for visibility. The kingdom-citizens form prays in the closet — the Greek *tameion,* the inner room, the storeroom, the private space.

Two distortions are named. First, the *to be seen of men* engineered-display the Pharisees walked. Second, the *vain repetitions* — the Greek *battalogeō,* to babble, to use empty repetition — the heathen practiced as if volume or repetition could manipulate the Deity into response. Both are the same underlying error: prayer as performance, prayer as transaction, prayer as a thing one does TO get an outcome FROM Elohim (God). The kingdom-citizens prayer is the opposite — relational address to the Father who already knows the need.

The misuse of this passage against public prayer is ruled out categorically. The Tanakh teaches public prayer extensively. Shlomo (Solomon) dedicated the temple with a public prayer in 1 Kings 8 that ran for forty-six verses. The psalms include corporate-prayer forms recited together. The apostolic communities prayed together as their normal practice:

*These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Miryam (Mary) the mother of Yahusha (Jesus), and with his brethren.* (Acts 1:14)

*And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.* (Acts 2:42)

*And when they heard that, they lifted up their voice to Elohim (God) with one accord, and said, Adonai (Lord), thou art Elohim (God), which hast made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all that in them is.* (Acts 4:24)

Yahusha (Jesus) himself prayed publicly at the raising of El'azar (Lazarus), and named the public posture explicitly as he did it:

*And Yahusha (Jesus) lifted up his eyes, and said, Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me. And I knew that thou hearest me always: but because of the people which stand by I said it, that they may believe that thou hast sent me.* (John 11:41-42)

The prohibition is against performative prayer — prayer engineered for self-display — not against gathered-community prayer. The motive separates the two forms; both are practiced in the kingdom-citizens community. The closet-prayer establishes the private foundation; the gathered prayer extends the corporate worship of citizens who already pray in secret.

The lock at the end of the unit frames prayer in the relational register the Lord's Prayer will articulate: *for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.* Prayer is not the conveying of information the Father lacks; prayer is the kingdom-citizens orientation of the heart toward the Father, the alignment of their wills with his kingdom, the practice of trust in his foreknowledge and provision.

*O Yahuah (LORD), thou hast searched me, and known me. Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off. Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways. For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O Yahuah (LORD), thou knowest it altogether.* (Psalm 139:1-4)

The Father knows before the citizens ask. Prayer continues anyway, because prayer is not transactional. The relational-purpose of prayer is the relation itself.

## The Lord's Prayer — the kingdom-citizens prayer-pattern in seven Tanakh-rooted phrases

*After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.* (Matthew 6:9-13)

This is the kingdom-citizens prayer-pattern, not a rote-recitation magic-formula. *After this manner* — the Greek *houtōs* — *in this way, on this pattern.* Yahusha (Jesus) is teaching the SHAPE of kingdom-citizens prayer, not commanding the words be recited verbatim as a religious-practice. The Christian commentary tradition's reduction of the prayer to a recitation-formula has often emptied its substance; each phrase is Tanakh-rooted, and each phrase walks a framework axis.

**Phrase one — *Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name.*** The Father-address is the *Avinu* address the Tanakh has already established. Three witnesses ground it:

*Doubtless thou art our father, though Avraham (Abraham) be ignorant of us, and Yashar'el (Israel) acknowledge us not: thou, O Yahuah (LORD), art our father, our redeemer; thy name is from everlasting.* (Isaiah 63:16)

*But now, O Yahuah (LORD), thou art our Father; we are the clay, and thou our potter; and we all are the work of thy hand.* (Isaiah 64:8)

*Do ye thus requite Yahuah (the LORD), O foolish people and unwise? is not he thy father that hath bought thee? hath he not made thee, and established thee?* (Deuteronomy 32:6)

The kingdom-citizens address the Father in the family-frame the Tanakh has already laid down. This is not a New Testament innovation.

*Hallowed be thy name* — the Greek *hagiazō,* the verb form of *qadosh,* set apart, sanctified. The Tanakh has already attached the sanctifying-of-the-Name to the gathering of the scattered house:

*And I will sanctify my great name, which was profaned among the heathen, which ye have profaned in the midst of them; and the heathen shall know that I am Yahuah (the LORD), saith Adonai Yahuah (the Lord GOD), when I shall be sanctified in you before their eyes. For I will take you from among the heathen, and gather you out of all countries, and will bring you into your own land.* (Ezekiel 36:23-24)

The sanctifying of the Name happens THROUGH the gathering. The Name was profaned in the lands of the scattering — every time the scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel) fell under judgment among the nations, the Name was profaned, because the nations took the scattering as evidence that the Elohim (God) of the covenant could not keep his people. The gathering reverses the profanation. When Yahuah (God) brings the scattered home, the Name is sanctified in the eyes of the nations who watched the scattering. So the kingdom-citizens prayer for the sanctification of the Name is the prayer for the gathering. *Hallowed be thy name* is a gathering-prayer.

**Phrase two — *Thy kingdom come.*** The eschatological-kingdom prayer. Daniel saw the everlasting kingdom set up at the close of the four world-kingdoms:

*And in the days of these kings shall the Elohim (God) of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever.* (Daniel 2:44)

Daniel also saw the kingdom-transfer in the night vision:

*I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of Adam came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.* (Daniel 7:13-14)

The kaph at *like the Son of Adam* — the Aramaic *kbar enash* — is preserved in the rendering because the kaph is the text's own move. The kaph honors the incarnation: the figure resembled mortal-man because he took on flesh, while remaining the Formed cloud-rider. Read in isolation, the comparative leaves the identification technically open. Read against the rest of the canon harmonized, the figure is Yahusha (Jesus) — verse 14's everlasting and indestructible kingdom belongs to him alone; the counterfeit's dominion is never everlasting; the architecture of the vision shows the Ancient of days as the Father, in vision-form for the transfer, and the *kbar enash* as the Son receiving the everlasting kingdom from him. The kingdom-citizens pray for the consummation of THIS kingdom — the same kingdom Yahusha (Jesus) refused to short-circuit when the devil offered him the kingdoms of the world from the high mountain in the wilderness just behind this Sermon. The kingdom is at hand; the citizens pray for its consummation; the timing is the Father's.

**Phrase three — *Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.*** The Torah-restoration-on-earth prayer. The Father's will is the heart-Torah the Tanakh articulates — the same Torah Yahusha (Jesus) declared not abolished at Matthew 5:17-19. In heaven this will is fully obeyed; on earth, the will is contested. The kingdoms of the present age stand under a temporary delegation that came down with the watcher-rebellion the Tanakh and the restored library both name — the powers that fell with the Watchers in Genesis 6 and 1 Enoch, the gods of the nations assigned shepherds who rebelled, the *principalities* and *powers* and *rulers of the darkness of this world* Paul names. These are the structural powers contesting the kingdoms until the consummation. The kingdom-citizens pray for the day when the earth carries the heaven-pattern — when the contested will is finally and fully done.

**Phrase four — *Give us this day our daily bread.*** The Greek *epiousios* is unusual — it appears almost nowhere else in the entire Greek corpus — and has been read as *for the coming day,* *for the existing day,* or *necessary-for-being.* The bread-petition reads against the manna-pattern of the wilderness:

*Then said Yahuah (the LORD) unto Mosheh (Moses), Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.* (Exodus 16:4)

The wilderness manna was DAILY provision; the double-portion came only the day before Shabbat. The daily-trust on the daily-provision was the design — it was the Torah-test of whether the gathered people would walk by the Father's covenant-economy. The kingdom-citizens prayer for daily-bread is the same trust-economy. The petition is for present-day provision, with the confidence that tomorrow's petition will receive tomorrow's provision. And Yahusha (Jesus) had already invoked the wilderness-bread substance against the devil's first temptation:

*But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of Elohim (God).* (Matthew 4:4)

The petition carries both registers — the physical-provision the manna prefigured, and the every-word-from-the-mouth-of-Elohim Torah-bread the Father feeds the citizens with. The disciples called from their fishing nets are now learning to pray for both — the loaves they used to secure by their own labor, and the Word they will be taught to live by.

**Phrase five — *And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.*** The Greek *opheilēmata* — debts in the moral-account sense; the Hebrew *chov* carries the same range, naming sin as a debt that must be reckoned. This reads against the Yom Kippur architecture the Torah established — the sin-debt at the heart of the covenant-relationship requires atonement, the kingdom-citizens carry the atonement-substance Yahusha (Jesus) embodies, and the citizens themselves carry the forgiveness-economy in their own dealings with debtors. The *as we forgive* parallel structure is conditional, and Yahusha (Jesus) will lock the condition explicitly in the verses immediately following. The kingdom-citizens carry a forgiveness-economy or they forfeit their own forgiveness.

**Phrase six — *And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil.*** The wilderness-testing motif from Matthew 4 surfaces directly. *Peirasmos* — testing, trial. The Tanakh and the New Testament are both clear that Elohim (God) does not tempt anyone to evil:

*Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of Elohim (God): for Elohim (God) cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man.* (James 1:13)

The petition is not *do not cause us to be tempted* but *do not lead us into the testing-place beyond what we can bear* — and it reads against the protective-care Paul names at the Corinthian assembly:

*There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but Elohim (God) is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.* (1 Corinthians 10:13)

*Deliver us from evil* — the Greek *apo tou ponērou,* possibly *from the evil one,* the substantive form pointing at the devil who tested Yahusha (Jesus) at Matthew 4. The petition is kingdom-citizens prayer for protection from the principalities-of-this-age, the powers operating under the watcher-rebellion administration. The whole world stands under that contested administration in the present age:

*And we know that we are of Elohim (God), and the whole world lieth in wickedness.* (1 John 5:19)

The disciples Yahusha (Jesus) called and is now teaching will face the same kind of testing he just faced in the wilderness; the petition is for the Father's protective leading through it.

**Phrase seven — *For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.*** The doxology. The manuscript evidence varies — some early manuscripts include this clause, others do not — but the doxology's substance is Tanakh-anchored in David's prayer at the dedication of the temple-materials:

*Thine, O Yahuah (LORD), is the greatness, and the power, and the glory, and the victory, and the majesty: for all that is in heaven and in the earth is thine; thine is the kingdom, O Yahuah (LORD), and thou art exalted as head above all.* (1 Chronicles 29:11)

The kingdom-citizens close the prayer-pattern with the kingdom-power-glory-eternity attribution to the Father — the same attribution David made publicly at the dedication of the materials for the temple he was forbidden to build.

The seven phrases together form the kingdom-citizens prayer pattern: address the Father in the family-frame; sanctify the Name through the gathering; pray for the kingdom's consummation; pray for the will done on earth as it is in heaven; petition for daily provision in the manna-trust; petition for forgiveness in the forgiveness-economy that conditions its receiving on its extension; petition for protection from the testing and the principalities; close in the doxology that names the kingdom-power-glory-eternity as the Father's. This is the framework-grammar of kingdom-citizens prayer.

## The forgiveness-economy and the grace that can be forfeited

*For if ye forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you: But if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.* (Matthew 6:14-15)

Yahusha (Jesus) appends a direct elaboration of the *as we forgive our debtors* petition. The Christian commentary tradition has frequently softened these two verses by exegetical maneuver — *if ye forgive* read as *because ye were forgiven you now show it,* the conditional collapsed into a corollary. The verses read plainly. The structure is conditional: *if ye forgive ... your heavenly Father will also forgive; if ye forgive NOT ... neither will your Father forgive.* The receiving of forgiveness from the Father is coupled to the giving of forgiveness to the brother. This is not a transactional quid-pro-quo earning-of-forgiveness; it is the relational-economy of the kingdom. The kingdom-citizens carry the forgiveness-substance in both directions — receiving from the Father, extending to the brother — or the substance is broken at the citizens' end and the receiving-side closes.

The institutional-Christian *once saved, always saved* doctrine cannot stand on this verse, and the same Matthew locks the point twice more in narrative. First, the parable of the unforgiving servant:

*Then his lord, after that he had called him, said unto him, O thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all that debt, because thou desiredst me: Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee? And his lord was wroth, and delivered him to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due unto him. So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.* (Matthew 18:32-35)

The king forgives the great debt, then REVERSES the forgiveness the moment the forgiven servant refuses to extend it. The lord's word is plain: *so likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you.* The parable is the dramatized form of these verses — the proposition of Matthew 6 walked as a story in Matthew 18, with the king's reversal confirming what the proposition stated. And the same conditional-grammar appears at the very close of the Sermon:

*Not every one that saith unto me, Adonai (Lord), Adonai (Lord), shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Adonai (Lord), Adonai (Lord), have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.* (Matthew 7:21-23)

The *I never knew you* is spoken to wheat that did not bear fruit — the prophets-in-his-name and the demon-casters-in-his-name and the workers-of-wonders-in-his-name are not random pagans; they invoked the right name and walked a counterfeit walk. This whole architecture the Tanakh has been teaching for centuries. Ezekiel walks the pattern explicitly:

*Therefore thou son of Adam, say unto the children of thy people, The righteousness of the righteous shall not deliver him in the day of his transgression: as for the wickedness of the wicked, he shall not fall thereby in the day that he turneth from his wickedness; neither shall the righteous be able to live for his righteousness in the day that he sinneth. When I shall say to the righteous, that he shall surely live; if he trust to his own righteousness, and commit iniquity, all his righteousnesses shall not be remembered; but for his iniquity that he hath committed, he shall die for it.* (Ezekiel 33:12-13)

The names of the wheat can be blotted out of the book of the living:

*And Yahuah (the LORD) said unto Mosheh (Moses), Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book.* (Exodus 32:33)

*Let them be blotted out of the book of the living, and not be written with the righteous.* (Psalm 69:28)

The warning is to the redeemed, to the righteous, to the names already written — not to the tares, who were never written in the book at all. The sons of Adam have their names written in the book of life from the foundation of the world; names can only be blotted out, never added; and the warnings of blotting-out are warnings to the wheat about the cost of lawlessness, not warnings to the tares about their failure to become wheat. The apostolic writings name the same architecture when the Hebrews-letter author warns the gathered community:

*For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins, But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries. He that despised Mosheh's (Moses's) law died without mercy under two or three witnesses: Of how much sorer punishment, suppose ye, shall he be thought worthy, who hath trodden under foot the Son of Elohim (God), and hath counted the blood of the covenant, wherewith he was sanctified, an unholy thing, and hath done despite unto the Ruach (Spirit) of grace?* (Hebrews 10:26-29)

The warning is addressed to those who have received the knowledge of the truth and been sanctified by the blood of the covenant — the redeemed, not the unevangelized — and it names the forfeit as real. The Reformation's *saved by grace through faith* sentence is true; the Reformation cut the second half of the sentence off. The conditional-grammar of these verses, the parable of the unforgiving servant, the *I never knew you* of the Sermon's close, and the Hebrews warning to the redeemed all put the second half back. Grace is the means of return to the Father's commandments, not a permanent standing outside the operation of the covenant. The kingdom-citizens carry the forgiveness-economy in both directions, or they break the connection at their own end. The standard holds at full force on this verse as on every verse: *He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him* (1 John 2:4).

## Fasting in secret — the third practice and the Isaiah 58 substance

*Moreover when ye fast, be not, as the hypocrites, of a sad countenance: for they disfigure their faces, that they may appear unto men to fast. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward. But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret: and thy Father, which seeth in secret, shall reward thee openly.* (Matthew 6:16-18)

The third unit in the display-versus-secret diagnostic. Fasting, like alms-giving and prayer, is a Tanakh-substantial practice — the Torah commands it at the day of atonement, the prophets called it at moments of national repentance, the saints practiced it at moments of personal grief and intercession. The Torah's Yom Kippur fast:

*Also on the tenth day of this seventh month there shall be a day of atonement: it shall be an holy convocation unto you; and ye shall afflict your souls, and offer an offering made by fire unto Yahuah (the LORD). And ye shall do no work in that same day: for it is a day of atonement, to make an atonement for you before Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 23:27-28)

The prophets called fasts at national moments:

*Sanctify ye a fast, call a solemn assembly, gather the elders and all the inhabitants of the land into the house of Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God), and cry unto Yahuah (the LORD).* (Joel 1:14)

David fasted at the illness of the child born of his sin with Bathsheba; Daniel fasted at the receiving of the prophetic visions; Yahusha (Jesus) himself had just fasted forty days in the wilderness. Fasting stands. The motive-correction is what the verse specifies.

The Pharisaic-counterfeit form practiced fasting with *disfigured faces* — the Greek *aphanizō,* to make unsightly, to render unrecognizable — the ostentatious pallor for visible-recognition. The kingdom-citizens form practices fasting with *anoint thine head, and wash thy face* — the normal-grooming pattern that conceals the fast from public view. The act is between the citizen and the Father.

And the Isaiah 58 fast-substance hovers over the whole passage. Where Matthew 6 corrects the form, Isaiah 58 had already corrected the substance:

*Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke? Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to thy house? when thou seest the naked, that thou cover him; and that thou hide not thyself from thine own flesh? Then shall thy light break forth as the morning, and thine health shall spring forth speedily: and thy righteousness shall go before thee; the glory of Yahuah (the LORD) shall be thy rereward.* (Isaiah 58:6-8)

Isaiah 58 is the Tanakh-charter of the kingdom-citizens fast — the fast joined to *chesed*-action toward the oppressed, the heart-Torah expressed in concrete care for the poor. The Matthew 6 form-correction sits inside the Isaiah 58 substance-correction. The kingdom-citizens fast is not the Pharisaic-display-pallor; it is the Isaiah 58 heart-fast.

The three-part diagnostic closes here. Alms, prayer, fasting — three Tanakh-substantial practices the kingdom-citizens walk; three motive-corrections against the Pharisaic-counterfeit form. The Father's reward-economy is named in all three: *thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.* The counterfeit form has its reward in the seeing-of-men; the kingdom-citizens form has its reward in the Father's seeing.

## Treasures in heaven — the heart-orientation diagnostic

*Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.* (Matthew 6:19-21)

The treasures-in-heaven teaching continues the display-versus-secret diagnostic at the broader economic-orientation level. The Pharisaic-counterfeit-righteousness accumulates visible-credential — religious-display, public-recognition, social-honor — which is treasure on earth. The kingdom-citizens-righteousness accumulates treasure in heaven — the eschatological-reward the Father stores for the citizens who walk the kingdom in secret. Earth-treasure is vulnerable to moth (the Greek *sēs*), to rust (the Greek *brōsis,* corrosion or eating-away), and to thieves; heaven-treasure is permanent. The Tanakh's wisdom-stream has named the futility of earth-treasure since the Proverbs were collected:

*Be not thou afraid when one is made rich, when the glory of his house is increased; For when he dieth he shall carry nothing away: his glory shall not descend after him.* (Psalm 49:16-17)

*For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also* is the diagnostic-verse — the heart-orientation-test. The disciple discovers what the heart actually serves by tracing where the treasure has been laid up. This is the heart-Torah diagnostic in another register. The Sermon has been working from the heart outward — the Beatitudes named the heart-conditions of kingdom-citizens; the antitheses moved the commandment-keeping from the surface-act to the heart-disposition; the secret-practices located the practice-motive at the heart-level; and now this verse names the heart-location-test that follows the treasure. The heart goes where the treasure goes. The disciple finds the heart by tracing the treasure.

## The good-eye and the evil-eye — the Hebrew idiom for generosity and stinginess

*The light of the body is the eye: if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light. But if thine eye be evil, thy whole body shall be full of darkness. If therefore the light that is in thee be darkness, how great is that darkness!* (Matthew 6:22-23)

These verses need particular care, because the Christian commentary tradition has chronically spiritualized the *single eye / evil eye* contrast into a generic spiritual-vision metaphor — *single eye* read as singleness-of-spiritual-focus, *evil eye* read as misdirected gaze. The framework recovers the Hebrew idiom the Tanakh and the wisdom-stream have used since the Torah. In Hebrew, *good of eye* (*tov-ayin*) and *evil of eye* (*ra-ayin*) describe GENEROSITY and STINGINESS — the open-handed heart that gives to the poor and the grasping heart that withholds. The idiom is not exotic; it sits in plain view across the canon.

*He that hath a bountiful eye shall be blessed; for he giveth of his bread to the poor.* (Proverbs 22:9)

The Hebrew of *bountiful eye* is *tov-ayin* — literally *good of eye.* The verse explains the idiom directly: the good-eye person is generous; he gives of his bread to the poor.

*Eat thou not the bread of him that hath an evil eye, neither desire thou his dainty meats: For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he: Eat and drink, saith he to thee; but his heart is not with thee.* (Proverbs 23:6-7)

The Hebrew of *evil eye* here is *ra-ayin* — *evil of eye.* The verse explains the idiom directly: the evil-eye person is stingy; the heart is grasping; the hospitality is performative-but-not-substantive. The evil-eye host offers food while begrudging it; the eating is unblessed because the heart is closed. The Torah itself uses the same idiom in the year-of-release context:

*Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart, saying, The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand; and thine eye be evil against thy poor brother, and thou givest him nought; and he cry unto Yahuah (the LORD) against thee, and it be sin unto thee.* (Deuteronomy 15:9)

And a fourth witness names hastening-to-be-rich as the heart-condition that produces evil-eye stinginess:

*He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye, and considereth not that poverty shall come upon him.* (Proverbs 28:22)

The Greek of the passage carries the Hebrew idiom into the Septuagint vocabulary. *Haplous* for *single* renders Hebrew *tam* — sound, whole, generous-without-double-mind. *Ponēros* for *evil* renders Hebrew *ra* — evil, including the grasping-stinginess sense in the Proverbs passages above. The Tanakh-idiom carries into the Greek of the gospel.

The reading is locked by the literary context. These verses sit BETWEEN the treasures-in-heaven teaching and the Mammon-or-Elohim binary. The whole block is about economic orientation. The single eye whose body is full of light is the GENEROUS person who lays up treasure in heaven through generous-giving; the evil eye whose body is full of darkness is the STINGY person who lays up treasure on earth through grasping. The Tanakh-idiom precisely fits the surrounding teaching on the disciple's economic orientation. The Christian-spiritualization of *single eye* into a generic spiritual-vision-purity loses the substance entirely and reads the verses as a free-floating metaphor in the middle of an economic discourse.

The *how great is that darkness!* lamentation closes the unit. The stingy-heart of the disciple who professes to serve the Father but accumulates earth-treasure is a darkness that has invaded the whole life. The light that is supposed to be in the body has become darkness through the mammon-orientation. The intensity-marker names the gravity. The disciple cannot serve the kingdom from a grasping heart, no matter how religious the surface of the practice.

## Mammon as principality — the forced choice

*No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve Elohim (God) and mammon.* (Matthew 6:24)

The Mammon-or-Elohim binary closes the economic-orientation block. Matthew leaves the Aramaic *mamona* untranslated, deliberately so, because the term names the personified-power of wealth, not just the wealth itself. Mammon is treated as a master, a competing lord. The disciple either serves Elohim (God) or serves Mammon; the two are mutually exclusive.

Mammon reads against the principalities-of-this-age architecture the apostolic writings name. The kingdoms of the present age stand under a temporary delegation that came down with the watcher-rebellion the Tanakh and the restored library both describe — the powers that fell with the Watchers, the gods of the nations assigned to the seventy peoples and the seventy shepherds who corrupted their charge, the structural powers Paul names:

*For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.* (Ephesians 6:12)

Mammon is one of the systemic powers in that administration — wealth treated as the organizing-power of life. When the devil offered Yahusha (Jesus) the kingdoms-and-the-glory-of-them on the high mountain of Matthew 4 in exchange for worship, the trade was the same trade Mammon offers every disciple in another register — wealth-as-master in exchange for whole-heart-allegiance. The disciple cannot serve both. The Shema's whole-heart standard makes the dual-allegiance impossible:

*Hear, O Yashar'el (Israel): Yahuah Eloheinu (The LORD our God) is one Yahuah (LORD): And thou shalt love Yahuah Elohayka (the LORD thy God) with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.* (Deuteronomy 6:4-5)

And Eliyahu (Elijah) at Mount Carmel named the same forced-choice in the form the prophets used to name idolatry:

*And Eliyahu (Elijah) came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if Yahuah (the LORD) be Elohim (God), follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.* (1 Kings 18:21)

Matthew 6:24 is the Carmel-confrontation in the disciple's own economic life. The kingdom-citizens cannot serve both Yahuah (God) and the principality the Aramaic names Mammon. The apostolic naming of the love-of-money as the root from which the evil flows lands the same architecture:

*For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.* (1 Timothy 6:10)

The disciple who serves Mammon has erred from the faith. The dual-allegiance breaks the whole-heart standard, and the breaking is the departure from the faith. The forced choice is real.

## Seek first the kingdom — the daily-trust of the kingdom-citizens

*Therefore I say unto you, Take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink; nor yet for your body, what ye shall put on. Is not the life more than meat, and the body than raiment? Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns; yet your heavenly Father feedeth them. Are ye not much better than they? Which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature? And why take ye thought for raiment? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow; they toil not, neither do they spin: And yet I say unto you, That even Shlomo (Solomon) in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these. Wherefore, if Elohim (God) so clothe the grass of the field, which to day is, and to morrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you, O ye of little faith?* (Matthew 6:25-30)

The take-no-thought teaching extends from the Mammon-binary. The kingdom-citizens who serve Elohim (God) and not Mammon walk a trust-economy — confidence in the Father's covenant-provision, freedom from the anxious-grasping that characterizes Mammon-service. *Take no thought* — the Greek *mē merimnaō,* *do not be anxious, do not be torn-apart-with-care.* The verb does not abolish prudent planning; it abolishes anxious-grasping. The disciple plants and harvests and labors as the Torah commands; what the disciple does not do is allow the care for tomorrow to consume the trust today.

The fowls-of-the-air and lilies-of-the-field name the Father's covenant-provision economy. The fowls do not plant-and-store, yet the Father feeds them. The lilies do not toil-or-spin, yet the Father clothes them in glory exceeding Shlomo's (Solomon's) royal robes. The Tanakh-substance is the broad creation-care testimony of the Father:

*He giveth to the beast his food, and to the young ravens which cry.* (Psalm 147:9)

*Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding. Who hath laid the measures thereof, if thou knowest? or who hath stretched the line upon it? Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened? or who laid the corner stone thereof; when the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of Elohim (God) shouted for joy?* (Job 38:4-7)

*He sendeth the springs into the valleys, which run among the hills. They give drink to every beast of the field: the wild asses quench their thirst. By them shall the fowls of the heaven have their habitation, which sing among the branches. He watereth the hills from his chambers: the earth is satisfied with the fruit of thy works. He causeth the grass to grow for the cattle, and herb for the service of man; that he may bring forth food out of the earth.* (Psalm 104:10-14)

The Father's covenant-care extends through the fowls and the lilies and the springs and the grass; how much more to the kingdom-citizens. The disciples called from their fishing nets are now being taught that the Father who feeds the ravens will feed the men who left the nets to follow the Son.

The *O ye of little faith* is kindness, not rebuke. Yahusha (Jesus) names the disciples' faith-as-small without dismissing them; the teaching is FOR the small-faith to grow. The same Yahusha (Jesus) who called fishermen out of their nets is now teaching them to trust the Father for the daily-provision they used to secure by their own labor. The shift from net-security to Father-security is the lifelong work of the kingdom-citizens; the little faith is the starting place, not the verdict.

*Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? (For after all these things do the Gentiles seek:) for your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things. But seek ye first the kingdom of Elohim (God), and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you. Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof.* (Matthew 6:31-34)

The closing verses lock the chapter's central orientation. *Seek ye FIRST the kingdom of Elohim (God), and his righteousness.* The Greek *prōton* — *first* — is not chronological-first-and-then-the-other; it is priority-first-as-organizing-principle. The kingdom-citizens orient FIRST toward the kingdom — the gathering, the consummation, the Father's will done in earth — and toward his righteousness — the heart-Torah Yahusha (Jesus) has been unfolding throughout the Sermon. The daily-provision is added in the framework of that priority. The Sermon's hungering-and-thirsting Beatitude finds its present-age practice here:

*Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.* (Matthew 5:6)

The hunger the Beatitude blessed is the hunger for the Father's righteousness — and this verse names what that hunger looks like in practice: seek the kingdom and his righteousness FIRST, walk the heart-Torah, and the Father provides what the day requires. The Pharisaic-counterfeit-righteousness of Matthew 5:20 is not what the citizens seek; the Father's righteousness — the heart-Torah, the kingdom-walking — is. The Tanakh's wisdom-stream had already named the same priority-pattern in narrative form. Shlomo (Solomon) at Gibeon asked for wisdom rather than wealth, and Yahuah (the LORD) added the wealth:

*And Elohim (God) said unto him, Because thou hast asked this thing, and hast not asked for thyself long life; neither hast asked riches for thyself, nor hast asked the life of thine enemies; but hast asked for thyself understanding to discern judgment; Behold, I have done according to thy words: lo, I have given thee a wise and an understanding heart; so that there was none like thee before thee, neither after thee shall any arise like unto thee. And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked, both riches, and honour: so that there shall not be any among the kings like unto thee all thy days.* (1 Kings 3:11-13)

Seek what the kingdom requires; the kingdom adds what the seeker did not request. The same pattern at the Psalmist's mouth:

*Delight thyself also in Yahuah (the LORD); and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart.* (Psalm 37:4)

The delighting comes first; the desires-of-the-heart follow as the kingdom-citizens' delight-in-Yahuah shapes the desires themselves into things the Father gives. The priority is the organizing principle.

The *take no thought for the morrow* and *sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof* close the Sermon-middle on the day-by-day frame. The kingdom-citizens walk one day at a time in the Father's provision — the manna-pattern of the wilderness. The wilderness-trust the Yashar'el (Israel) of the Exodus generation failed at — gathering more than the day required, hoarding against tomorrow, eating yesterday's manna full of worms — is the trust Yahusha (Jesus) walked perfectly in his own forty-day wilderness, and the disciples now learn the same daily-trust the Father has been teaching since the wilderness. Each day carries its own portion of trouble; the kingdom-citizens trust the Father's daily-grace for the daily-portion, and let the morrow take its own thought when the morrow comes.

Matthew 6 closes here. The chapter has walked the practical specification of the kingdom-citizens-righteousness against the Pharisaic-counterfeit form — alms, prayer, and fasting in secret; the Lord's Prayer as the kingdom-citizens prayer-pattern in seven Tanakh-rooted phrases; the forgiveness-economy that conditions the receiving on the giving; treasures laid up in heaven by the open hand of the good eye; the forced-choice between Yahuah (God) and Mammon-the-principality; the seek-first-the-kingdom priority and the daily-trust in the Father's covenant-provision. The chapter does not abolish a single Torah practice; it names what the practice looks like when the heart that walks it has been brought home.
