## The chapter the contrary-to-Torah test was written for

Matthew 7 closes the Sermon on the Mount, and it carries one of the most load-bearing passages in the whole New Testament for the test the rest of this work keeps returning to: a teacher, a community, a system is identified not by its name, its credentials, or its claimed lineage, but by whether what it teaches and walks stands *with* the Torah or *against* it. The King who has just bound himself and the kingdom-citizens to every jot and tittle (Matthew 5:17-19) now closes by sorting the citizens — not by their confession, not by their supernatural ministry, but by their doing of the Father's will. The whole chapter is the apostle Yochanan's (John's) master filter spoken in advance:

*And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.* (1 John 2:3-4)

Hold that filter through the chapter and the discrimination of vv.1-6, the prayer-confidence of vv.7-11, the Golden Rule of v.12, the narrow gate of vv.13-14, the false prophets of vv.15-20, the *Lord, Lord* dismissal of vv.21-23, and the two builders of vv.24-27 all fall into one line. The chapter teaches the citizens how to discern, then turns the discernment on the citizens themselves.

## *Judge not* — the standard of judgment, not the abolition of it

*Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again. And why beholdest thou the mote that is in thy brother's eye, but considerest not the beam that is in thine own eye? Or how wilt thou say to thy brother, Let me pull out the mote out of thine eye; and, behold, a beam is in thine own eye? Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye. Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you.* (Matthew 7:1-6)

The modern Christian commentary tradition has often weaponized v.1 as a categorical prohibition on moral discernment of any kind — *Jesus said do not judge, therefore the believer must extend non-judgmental acceptance to every behavior, every doctrine, every teacher, every claim.* The reading collapses against the very next sentence. *With what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged* presupposes that the disciple WILL judge — the variable is the STANDARD. The Greek *en hō krimati* — *with what KIND of judgment* — names the variable. The disciple is being instructed on HOW to judge, not commanded to abolish all judgment.

The mote-and-beam parable specifies the form. The disciple sees the *mote* (the Greek *karphos,* a splinter of dry matter) in the brother's eye while harboring a *beam* (the Greek *dokos,* a large load-bearing timber) in his own. The corrective is not *therefore stop seeing the brother's mote.* The corrective is *cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye.* The brother's-mote-removal is RESTORED — after the disciple has dealt with his own. The standard is self-examination first, then helpful correction. The Torah itself commanded the rebuke and bound it to the love-of-neighbor in the same breath:

*Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him. Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:17-18)

The rebuke is part of the love-of-neighbor; the loving includes the not-suffering-sin-upon-him. Yahusha (Jesus) made the same clarification explicit in the gospel of John:

*Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.* (John 7:24)

What is forbidden is the according-to-appearance judgment; what is required is the righteous judgment that begins with self-examination.

Then v.6 forces the matter the opposite way. *Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine.* The disciple is commanded to KNOW the difference between *the holy* and *the dogs,* between *pearls* and *swine* — active moral discernment, exactly what the tolerance-absolutization rules out. The disciple who cannot tell the holy from the dogs cannot obey v.6. No reading of vv.1-5 can be allowed to contradict v.6 in the same paragraph. The wisdom-tradition had said the same about discrimination of audience:

*Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words.* (Proverbs 23:9)

The whole teaching reads as one: judge the brother by the same standard you apply to yourself; deal with your own faults first; then help him with his; and recognize the difference between the brother-receiving-correction and the dogs-and-swine who reject the holy outright. The discernment is INTENSIFIED, not abolished. And this is the posture toward those still inside the inherited systems — the one still in the church pew, the one still in the synagogue, the one still in the Hebrew-roots false-inclusion community, the one still inside the Islamic framework. They are the deceived, not the dogs; the patience is for the people, and the naming is for the systems. The dogs and swine of v.6 are those who finally trample the pearls and turn to rend — and at that point the pursuit ends where the rejection lands, and the pearls are not cast again.

## Ask, seek, knock — and the gift at the center is the Spirit

*Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you: For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened. Or what man is there of you, whom if his son ask bread, will he give him a stone? Or if he ask a fish, will he give him a serpent? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?* (Matthew 7:7-11)

The threefold *ask / seek / knock* is the progressively-deeper prayer-posture of the kingdom-citizens, and the threefold *given / find / opened* names the Father's answering. The earthly-father / heavenly-Father comparison walks the *kal va'chomer* argument-form — the light-and-weighty: if a lesser case holds, how much more the greater. If earthly fathers, who are *evil,* know how to give good gifts, *how much more* the heavenly Father. The *being evil* is not a Calvinist total-depravity claim but the post-Eden condition of the seed of Adam living under the watcher-rebellion administration; even fallen-and-flawed fathers give good gifts to their children, and the lesser case holds.

Luke's parallel names what *the good things* most centrally are:

*If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent? Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?* (Luke 11:11-13)

THE good gift is the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit). The central prayer-petition of the gathered is for the indwelling Spirit who writes the heart-Torah on the citizens — the prophetic promise the Tanakh had held out for centuries:

*But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Yashar'el (Israel); After those days, saith Yahuah (the LORD), I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their Elohim (God), and they shall be my people.* (Jeremiah 31:33)

And the Spirit does not write a Torah only to lead away from it. The same Spirit causes the walking:

*And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* (Ezekiel 36:27)

The Father's covenant-care for the gathered is most fundamentally the gift of the Spirit who enables the kingdom-walking. The *good things* — the Greek *agatha* — name that kingdom-substance, with the Spirit at the center. This rules out the prosperity-gospel misuse of the passage: *ask / seek / knock* is not a wealth-and-blessing formula. The *good* the Father gives is the Spirit, the heart-Torah, the gathering-promise, the daily bread of Matthew 6:11, the deliverance from the evil one of Matthew 6:13 — the kingdom-substance, not a wealth template lifted out of the Sermon's frame.

## The Golden Rule — restoration of the Hebrew library, not a simpler ethic

*Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets.* (Matthew 7:12)

The Christian commentary tradition has often flattened v.12 into a Yahusha-invented ethical simplification of an alleged Tanakh complexity — *the Torah was complicated, but Jesus reduced it to one simple rule.* The teaching was already in the Hebrew library, articulated explicitly in the wisdom-stream centuries before the Sermon. The angel Raphael's instruction to Tobias names the rule in negative form at the close of Tobit's narrative:

*Do that to no man which thou hatest: drink not wine to make thee drunken: neither let drunkenness go with thee in thy journey.* (Tobit 4:15)

Hillel — the most prominent rabbi of the generation before Yahusha (Jesus) — taught the same rule in the same negative form: *what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow; this is the whole Torah, the rest is commentary — go and learn.* The Hebrew library carried the teaching; the Pharisaic tradition at its best carried it; the rule had been articulated for generations. Yahusha (Jesus) at v.12 walks it in the positive direction — *do unto others* rather than *do not do* — and binds it to the Sermon's whole architecture with the closing words *for this is the law and the prophets.*

And those closing words are the key. The Rule is the heart-substance of the Torah, NOT a replacement of the Torah's specific commandments. The King has just spent Matthew 5:17-19 binding himself and the citizens to every jot and tittle; Matthew 5:21-48 restoring the heart-Torah at six commandment cruxes; Matthew 6:1-18 specifying the kingdom-citizens form of three core practices. The Golden Rule cannot abolish what the King has just affirmed. The Tanakh's commandments toward neighbor and stranger are summed in the Rule's substance:

*Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Leviticus 19:18)

*But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Mitsrayim (Egypt): I am Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God).* (Leviticus 19:34)

The prohibitions on theft and false witness and covetousness, the prophets' demands for justice and mercy and the humble walk — all summed in the Rule's substance. The one who walks the Torah's commandments walks what the Rule names; the one who walks the Rule walks the commandments. The two are mutually coextensive at the heart-level. The supersessionist reading that treats the King as the innovator of a new ethic replacing the *complicated* Torah collapses against Tobit 4:15 and against the Sermon's own architecture. The teaching was already in the library; the King restores it.

## The narrow gate and the few — the Two-Ways the library always carried

*Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.* (Matthew 7:13-14)

The structure is binary and quantitatively asymmetric: TWO gates, TWO ways, TWO destinations. The wide gate and broad way lead to destruction, and *many* go in. The strait gate and narrow way lead to life, and *few* find it. The framework will not soften the asymmetry, and it refuses both the universalist-dilution and the institutional-Christian assumption that the church pew is automatically on the narrow way.

The teaching is not a gospel-invented dualism. Mosheh (Moses) named the Two-Ways covenant-decision at the close of Deuteronomy before the people entered the land:

*See, I have set before thee this day life and good, and death and evil... I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live.* (Deuteronomy 30:15, 19)

The Psalter opened on the same architecture — *blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly... for Yahuah (the LORD) knoweth the way of the righteous: but the way of the ungodly shall perish* (Psalm 1:1, 6) — and Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) carried it into the prophets in the same explicit framing:

*Thus saith Yahuah (the LORD); Behold, I set before you the way of life, and the way of death.* (Jeremiah 21:8)

The apocalyptic stream brought the same Two-Ways forward in the wisdom of Enoch the righteous:

*And now I say unto you, my sons, love righteousness and walk therein; For the paths of righteousness are worthy of acceptance, But the paths of unrighteousness shall suddenly be destroyed and vanish. And to certain men of a generation shall the paths of violence and death be revealed, And they shall hold themselves aloof from the paths of righteousness, And they shall not find mercy.* (1 Enoch 94:1-2)

Mosheh (Moses), the Psalter, Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah), and Enoch all stand on one Two-Ways covenant-architecture. The Christian commentary that treats the narrow gate as a gospel innovation — *the Old Testament was inclusivist, Jesus introduced the narrow way* — collapses against the library's own centuries-old architecture.

The *few* is also remnant-language at full voltage — the remnant the prophets had named:

*And it shall come to pass in that day, that the remnant of Yashar'el (Israel), and such as are escaped of the house of Ya'aqov (Jacob), shall no more again stay upon him that smote them; but shall stay upon Yahuah (the LORD), the Holy One of Yashar'el (Israel), in truth. The remnant shall return, even the remnant of Ya'aqov (Jacob), unto the mighty El (God). For though thy people Yashar'el (Israel) be as the sand of the sea, yet a remnant of them shall return: the consumption decreed shall overflow with righteousness.* (Isaiah 10:20-22)

*I will also leave in the midst of thee an afflicted and poor people, and they shall trust in the name of Yahuah (the LORD). The remnant of Yashar'el (Israel) shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies; neither shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth: for they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid.* (Zephaniah 3:12-13)

And the same prophet named the third-part survivors with even greater precision — those who look on the pierced one:

*And I will pour upon the house of David, and upon the inhabitants of Yerushalayim (Jerusalem), the spirit of grace and of supplications: and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son, and shall be in bitterness for him, as one that is in bitterness for his firstborn.* (Zechariah 12:10)

The remnant is consistently named as a minority — the third-part, the marked, the few who find. And the *find* is precise. *Find* — the Greek *heuriskō* — they are FOUND in the narrow way; the way does not simply open to any who claim it. The hearing-reveals-not-makes principle is operative: those who find are those whose names were written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world AND who walk the heart-Torah through the post-harvest sifting:

*And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.* (Revelation 13:8)

The wheat hear the Shepherd because they are sheep — *my sheep hear my voice* (John 10:27) — and the tares cannot, because they are not. Among the gathered wheat, the post-harvest sifting separates the wheat that bore fruit from the wheat that did not, and names get blotted out among the unfruitful. The narrow gate corresponds to the remnant who pass both the foundation-of-the-world identification and the post-harvest sifting. Yahusha (Jesus) himself locks the intensification in the Lukan parallel:

*Then said one unto him, Adonai (Lord), are there few that be saved? And he said unto them, Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able.* (Luke 13:23-24)

The strive — the Greek *agōnizomai,* to contend, to wrestle — is the citizens' posture toward the gate. Many will seek and not be able. The few who find — by hearing and by walking — enter the life the gate leads to.

## False prophets and the fruit-test — the seed-war at the leadership level

*Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit. A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit. Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire. Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them.* (Matthew 7:15-20)

The false prophets *come to you in sheep's clothing* — they LOOK like the gathered remnant, they wear the sheep-costume — *but inwardly they are ravening wolves.* This is the seed-war of Genesis 3:15 specified at the leadership level: the seed of the serpent walks among the seed of the woman in disguise, and the disciple's task is to discern. The *good tree / corrupt tree* dichotomy names the lineage-register — *a good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit.* The fruit reveals the nature; the fruit does not transform it. The wolf in sheep's clothing is a wolf; the costume does not make him a sheep. This is the hearing-reveals-not-makes principle again: the fruit shows what the tree IS.

And the fruit-test is concrete. The Torah's own false-prophet diagnostic runs in two directions. The first: a prophet whose sign or wonder COMES TO PASS is still false if his teaching leads after other gods.

*If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder, And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them; Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet... for Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) proveth you.* (Deuteronomy 13:1-3)

The signs are real; the prophet is still false; supernatural credential is not the proof. The second test names the inverse:

*But the prophet, which shall presume to speak a word in my name, which I have not commanded him to speak, or that shall speak in the name of other gods, even that prophet shall die. And if thou say in thine heart, How shall we know the word which Yahuah (the LORD) hath not spoken? When a prophet speaketh in the name of Yahuah (the LORD), if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which Yahuah (the LORD) hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him.* (Deuteronomy 18:20-22)

The word that does not come to pass is the second blade. And the Torah fenced the commandments against the very modifications any false prophet would have to make:

*Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of Yahuah Elohaychem (the LORD your God) which I command you.* (Deuteronomy 4:2)

This is the contrary-to-Torah test as the master filter of every teacher's fruit. The teaching that leads away from the commandments is false; the teacher who voids the Torah is a false prophet by the filter, however impressive the apparent fruit of his ministry. The diagnostic exposes the antichrist-system's teachers in every age in the same way: the Pharisaic leadership in the sheep's clothing of religious office; the post-apostolic teachers who voided the heart-Torah by subtraction across the centuries in the clothing of bishop and pope; the modern institutional-Christian pulpit teaching the antinomian gospel in the clothing of pastor and televangelist; the Hebrew-roots false-inclusion teacher who says anyone who confesses Yahusha (Jesus) and Torah-keeps becomes Yashar'el (Israel), in the clothing of restoration-teacher; the Islamic teacher who denies the gathering of the twelve tribes and transfers the covenant to Ishmael, in the clothing of Abrahamic religion. The fruit-test exposes them all — the teaching that voids the Torah, the teaching that adds or subtracts, the teaching that produces a community without the heart-Torah, the word that does not come to pass.

*Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.* (Matthew 7:19)

This is the same imagery, the same warning, Yochanan (John) the Immerser had laid down at Matthew 3:10 — *the axe is laid unto the root of the trees: therefore every tree which bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.* The unfruitful tree is hewn down; the fire is the eschatological-judgment fire of the day of Yahuah (the LORD), not a metaphor for natural consequence.

## *Lord, Lord* — the load-bearing anti-antinomian declaration of the Sermon

These three verses are the most load-bearing passage in Matthew 7 for the framework, and one of the most load-bearing in the whole New Testament. The institutional-Christian system has either passed over them or built elaborate exegesis to neutralize them. They read plainly.

**Verse 21.** *Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.*

The King opens with a direct denial of the verbal-confession-as-entry-criterion gospel. *Saying Lord, Lord* — the verbal confession of Yahusha (Jesus) as Lord — is NOT the entry criterion. The Pauline verse most often used to argue otherwise stands inside a longer passage:

*That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Yahusha (Jesus), and shalt believe in thine heart that Elohim (God) hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.* (Romans 10:9-10)

Paul harmonizes with the King; he does not contradict him. Romans 10:9-10 names the initial confession-and-belief that ENTERS the journey; the King at Matthew 7:21 names the doing-of-the-Father's-will as the kingdom-entry criterion at the judgment day. The journey begins with confession; the entry happens through the walking. The institutional-Christian collapse of Romans 10:9-10 into the entire gospel — *say the prayer, you're in forever* — is the antinomian flattening of Paul into the antichrist reading. The same Paul wrote, in the same letter, that faith does not abolish the Torah:

*Do we then make void the law through faith? Elohim (God) forbid: yea, we establish the law.* (Romans 3:31)

And Peter, writing about Paul, named the wresting of Paul's letters as a known phenomenon already destroying the unstable in his own day:

*As also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction. Ye therefore, beloved, seeing ye know these things before, beware lest ye also, being led away with the error of the wicked, fall from your own stedfastness.* (2 Peter 3:16-17)

The institutional-Christian collapse of Paul into an anti-Torah gospel is the wresting Peter warned of, two thousand years downstream. No doctrine standing on the wrested Paul can stand against the King's own teaching here. The actual entry-criterion is *doing the will of the Father* — the heart-Torah-walking, the same will the Lord's Prayer petitions when it asks *thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.* The doer-of-the-Father's-will enters; the verbal-confessor without the doing does not. The filter of 1 John 2:3-4 says the same thing in another register: the verbal-confession-without-the-walking is the *liar* of 1 John 2:4 and the *Lord, Lord* sayer of Matthew 7:21; the doer-of-the-Father's-will is the keeper-of-the-commandments.

**Verse 22.** *Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works?*

Read carefully who is being described. These are not the obvious-pagan unbelievers. These confess Yahusha (Jesus) as Lord — twice, in emphatic acclamation. They prophesied IN HIS NAME — active in supernatural-prophetic ministry under his name. They cast out devils IN HIS NAME — active in deliverance-ministry under his name. They did *many wonderful works* — the Greek *dunameis pollas,* many works of power — IN HIS NAME. This is the impressive religious-ministry-performance community. By every visible credential the modern charismatic and evangelical communities track, these people pass. And Yahusha (Jesus) describes them in the future tense — *many will say* — a prophetic warning about a community that WILL show up at the judgment day claiming the credentials.

The Tanakh's own diagnostic had already warned that the signs themselves are not the proof — Deuteronomy 13 named that the false prophet's sign-or-wonder can come to pass and the prophet still be false. Paul named the same architecture to the Corinthian assembly:

*For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the apostles of HaMashiach (Christ). And no marvel; for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers also be transformed as the ministers of righteousness; whose end shall be according to their works.* (2 Corinthians 11:13-15)

The principalities of this age can produce supernatural effects through humans who invoke the name without belonging to him. The credentials are real; the relationship is absent.

**Verse 23 — the diagnostic.** *And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.*

Read every word. *I never knew you* — the Greek *oudepote egnōn humas.* Not *I knew you once and now reject you,* but *I never knew you at all.* The relational-knowing — the *yada* of the Tanakh covenant-relationship — never existed. The entire ministry under his name was performed without his knowing of them.

*Depart from me* — the Greek *apochōreite ap' emou.* This is the post-harvest sifting of the wheat. The wheat is gathered from all nations into the wilderness for sifting; the unfruitful are purged; only the fruitful pass through to the kingdom-citizenship in the reign. Ezekiel named the architecture:

*And I will cause you to pass under the rod, and I will bring you into the bond of the covenant: And I will purge out from among you the rebels, and them that transgress against me: I will bring them forth out of the country where they sojourn, and they shall not enter into the land of Yashar'el (Israel): and ye shall know that I am Yahuah (the LORD).* (Ezekiel 20:37-38)

*Purge out from among you the rebels, and them that transgress against me* is the very sifting Matthew 7:23 echoes. The people of v.22 had the appearance of being wheat — gathered, active in supernatural ministry under the name — but the rod of the covenant reveals them as rebels among the wheat. They are not tares (tares never entered the threshing floor; they were burned in the field at the harvest); they are unfruitful wheat dismissed at the second sifting. And the formula Yahusha (Jesus) speaks is David's own:

*Depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity; for Yahuah (the LORD) hath heard the voice of my weeping.* (Psalm 6:8)

The King quotes David's psalm against the supernatural-ministry community he is dismissing — the Tanakh-anchor of the *depart from me, workers of iniquity* formula naming the same diagnostic for the same kind of community.

*Ye that work iniquity* — the Greek *hoi ergazomenoi tēn anomian,* the WORKERS OF *anomia.* The English flattens it. *Anomia* — the *a-* (without) plus *nomos* (law) — literally means WITHOUT-TORAH. The people being dismissed are the workers of TORAHLESSNESS. They prophesied, cast out demons, and did wonderful works WHILE LIVING WITHOUT THE TORAH. The ministry was active; the Torah-walking was absent. They confessed Yahusha (Jesus) verbally; they violated the commandments practically. Yochanan (John) defined sin itself with the same word:

*Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.* (1 John 3:4)

The *transgression of the law* is *anomia* in the Greek; sin is Torah-violation, defined directly. And Paul names the operative system of the present age in the same vocabulary:

*For the mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way. And then shall that Wicked be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming.* (2 Thessalonians 2:7-8)

The *mystery of iniquity* in Paul's Greek is *to mystērion tēs anomias* — the mystery of TORAHLESSNESS, already at work in the apostolic age. The system Paul named there is the system Yahusha (Jesus) identifies at Matthew 7:23. The mystery of torahlessness was already working in the first century; the institutional-Christian system that rose out of it has been its full surface manifestation for seventeen centuries.

This is the direct diagnostic for the institutional-Christian antinomian-gospel community — the modern Judaizer movement wearing a different costume. The diagnostic is exact.

And the verses are the load-bearing New Testament voltage of a pattern the Tanakh taught from the beginning: grace freely given, grace that can be forfeited, the redeemed who walked away losing what they had. Hosea named it:

*Woe unto them! for they have fled from me: destruction unto them! because they have transgressed against me: though I have redeemed them, yet they have spoken lies against me.* (Hosea 7:13)

Ezekiel named it for the righteous-turned-to-iniquity:

*When I shall say to the righteous, that he shall surely live; if he trust to his own righteousness, and commit iniquity, all his righteousnesses shall not be remembered; but for his iniquity that he hath committed, he shall die for it.* (Ezekiel 33:13)

And the Hebrews-letter named it for the redeemed who tasted the Spirit:

*For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit), And have tasted the good word of Elohim (God), and the powers of the world to come, If they shall fall away, to renew them again unto repentance.* (Hebrews 6:4-6)

These were people who had received the gift — they could prophesy, cast out demons, do wonderful works, taste the powers of the world to come — and the gift was real, and they LOST it through *anomia.* The grace was offered; the gift was active; the entry-condition — doing the Father's will, the heart-Torah-walking — was not met; the dismissal at the post-harvest sifting is the result. The institutional-Christian *once saved, always saved* doctrine collapses on this passage and is wrong at the foundation. And the dismissal itself is not the cruelty the pulpit caricature imagines — it is the mercy of releasing the unwilling. The Father will not chain those who said with their whole walk that they did not want him and did not want his ways into a kingdom built around everything they refused. The depart is the answer to the life that asked for it.

## The two builders — *na'aseh v'nishma,* hearing-AND-doing as the foundation

*Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock. And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them not, shall be likened unto a foolish man, which built his house upon the sand: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it.* (Matthew 7:24-27)

The closing parable is the Sermon's summary at the foundation level, and the differentiator is exact: *heareth ... AND doeth them.* Both the wise and the foolish HEAR; the wise hears AND does; the foolish hears AND does not. The hearing alone is not the foundation; the hearing-AND-doing is. This is the Sinai pattern. When Yashar'el (Israel) received the Torah, they answered with the doing put first:

*And Mosheh (Moses) took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that Yahuah (the LORD) hath said will we do, and be obedient.* (Exodus 24:7)

The Hebrew of *will we do, and be obedient* is *na'aseh v'nishma* — *we will do, and we will hear* — the doing placed before the hearing. The two-builders parable is the same pattern in the Sermon's frame: the citizens hear-AND-do; the *Lord, Lord* community of v.22 hears-without-doing; the difference is the kingdom-entry-criterion.

The rock-foundation walks the Tanakh-substance of Yahuah (the LORD) as the Rock:

*He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a El (God) of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he.* (Deuteronomy 32:4)

*Yahuah (The LORD) is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my Elohi (God), my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower.* (Psalm 18:2)

The foundation-rock the wise man builds on is Yahuah (the LORD) himself, and the access to that foundation is through hearing-and-doing the heart-Torah the Sermon has unfolded. The doing locks the citizen into the rock; the hearer-without-the-doing has heard the sayings — perhaps spoken them, taught them, sung them, preached them — but has not built on them, and the house stands on sand because the foundation-establishing act is the doing. The sand-foundation is the institutional-Christian pulpit-and-pew arrangement specifically: the pew HEARS the sayings every Sunday — *love your enemies, judge not, lay up treasure in heaven, seek first the kingdom, do unto others* — while the pulpit teaches that the heart-Torah's specific commandments are abolished by grace. The hearing happens; the doing does not; the foundation is sand. *And great was the fall of it* — read against the post-harvest sifting, the day of Yahuah (the LORD), the eschatological test that exposes what each builder built on.

Ya'aqov (James) carried the same diagnostic into the apostolic letters:

*But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves. For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was. But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.* (James 1:22-25)

Ya'aqov names the same hearer-AND-doer standard, names the Torah as the *perfect law of liberty,* and locks the *doer of the work* as the one who is blessed. The Sinai *na'aseh v'nishma,* the two-builders parable, and Ya'aqov's doer-of-the-word standard are one teaching in three registers. The Reformation hierarchy that elevates hearing over doing, faith over works, grace over obedience runs straight into the Sermon's closing parable and into Ya'aqov's first chapter. The doing is not a downstream fruit that may or may not follow; the doing IS the foundation-establishing act.

## Authority, not as the scribes — the second-Sinai voice at chapter-close

*And it came to pass, when Yahusha (Jesus) had ended these sayings, the people were astonished at his doctrine: For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.* (Matthew 7:28-29)

*Astonished* — the Greek *ekplēssomai,* struck out of themselves, knocked loose from the default. The teaching landed at a register the listeners had never heard. The scribes taught by tradition-chain authority — *Rabbi X said in the name of Rabbi Y who said in the name of...* — the chain was the authority-source. Yahusha (Jesus) taught DIRECTLY — *but I say unto you* — in the King's own voice. The authority IS the Torah's authority, for the King has bound himself to every jot and tittle (Matthew 5:18), but it is spoken in the voice of the One whose Voice gave the Torah at Sinai.

This is where the nature-of-God reading surfaces again. The Voice that spoke the Torah at Sinai was the Formed — the Son drawn from the Formless — the same One who walked with Adam, appeared to Avraham (Abraham), wrestled with Ya'aqov (Jacob), and spoke from the fire. He did not begin at Beit-Lechem (Bethlehem); he came IN the flesh, remaining who he had always been. The *Immanuel — Elohim (God) with us* of Matthew 1:23 is the same voice as the *but I say unto you* of the Sermon. The *authority* the multitudes recognize at v.29 is the same authority that thundered at Sinai. The scribes lacked it because the scribes lacked the relationship-with-the-Voice the Sinai-Speaker is now demonstrating in person. Mark records the same astonishment:

*And they were astonished at his doctrine: for he taught them as one that had authority, and not as the scribes.* (Mark 1:22)

The chapter closes on the recognition-of-authority, not on committed discipleship. The multitudes are astonished; some will follow, many will not. The Sermon has been delivered. The wide gate and the narrow gate have been named. The post-harvest sifting has been previewed. The two builders have been contrasted. The doing of the Father's will has been locked as the kingdom-entry-criterion. The multitudes hear the authority; what they do with it now reveals what they are.
