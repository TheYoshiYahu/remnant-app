# The Same God Says Both — Grace, the Sifting, and the End He Declared From the Beginning

You have probably been told that the Bible's two voices on grace and works cannot stand together. The pulpit you grew up with held one voice and quietly retired the other. The Reformation pulpit kept the *for my name's sake* voice and called the *depart from me, ye that work iniquity* voice an outlier. The rabbinic pulpit kept the *depart from me* voice and called the *not for your righteousness* voice a paradox to be reverently set aside. In both systems, half of what the Father said about grace and the response to it was set down, and the half that survived was preached as the whole gospel.

The reader is left with a half-architecture. He has either been told the standing is permanent the moment he prayed the prayer, or he has been told the standing is shaky every moment he might fail to perform. Neither matches what the same God said about His own work. This unit walks you through what the inherited pulpit handed you, where that reading falls short of scripture's own words, and how the framework holds the whole architecture together as one mercy.

The people inside any of these systems are not the enemy. They were handed what they hold in good faith, by sincere teachers who were handed it before them. The system is what gets dismantled here. The grandmother who prayed the prayer loved the Father. The brother in the synagogue loves the Word. What we are pulling out from under their feet is the inherited frame — not the people standing on it.

## What the pulpit handed you

The inherited Reformation pulpit teaches grace as the cancellation of the consequence-system. The reader hears *saved by grace through faith, not of works, lest any man should boast* (Ephesians 2:8–9) and the sentence stops there. The implication that gets preached: nothing the believer does after that prayer affects the believer's standing. The standing is positional. It is sealed. *Once saved, always saved* — and any verse that suggests otherwise gets reread as referring to someone who was *never truly saved* in the first place.

In this frame, the *depart from me, ye that work iniquity* of Matthew 7 is preached as a warning to those who never had the new heart — false converts, never-believers, hypocrites who only claimed to be saved. The verse never lands on the listener as a warning the listener might need to hear about himself. The verse is always about someone else.

The *whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book* of Exodus 32:33 is preached as Old Testament covenant language that has been retired by the New Covenant in Christ. The reader is told the New Covenant's grace is unconditional in a way the Old was not — that the blotting is a pre-cross arrangement no longer operative.

The *righteous man falls seven times and rises* of Proverbs 24:16 is preached as a verse about earthly resilience, not as a load-bearing diagnostic for the distinction between the just and the wicked. The verse is read for inspiration, not for theology.

The *ye shall loathe yourselves in your own sight for your iniquities* of Ezekiel 36:31 is preached around — usually skipped entirely in the same passage where Ezekiel 36:26–27 is celebrated as the new-heart promise. The verse follows the new heart by exactly five verses; the inherited pulpit reads the first half and stops at the period.

The rabbinic pulpit teaches the inverse — the same scripture filtered through a different selective hearing. Here the *depart from me, ye that work iniquity* is preached as the load-bearing standard, and the *for my own name's sake* is read as a side note about Yahuah's (God's) own glory that does not really speak to the believer's standing. The covenant performance becomes the ground of the believer's confidence. Every Sabbath kept, every feast observed, every tradition transmitted, becomes another stone in the wall of the believer's standing before the Father. The new heart given by mercy — the *I will give you a new heart* of Ezekiel 36:26 — becomes a future eschatological promise rather than a present operative reality, because if it were present and operative, the system's emphasis on accomplishment would be undermined.

In both pulpits, the architecture is split. The Reformation pulpit gives the reader a grace untethered from the new heart's response to grace. The rabbinic pulpit gives the reader a response untethered from the name's-sake mercy that makes the response possible. Neither pulpit can preach what the same God actually said: that the grace and the response stand together as one architecture, and that the architecture rests on the same name He swore by from the beginning.

## Where that reading falls short

The half-architecture collapses against scripture's own pairings, against the prophetic anchor of every grace passage in the New Testament, and against the witness of the books the Reformation cut from the canon.

### The Reformation pairings the pulpit will not preach side by side

Ephesians 2:8–9 is preached as a standalone sentence; the very next verse completes the sentence and the inherited pulpit drops it.

*For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of Elohim (God): not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Messiah (Christ) Yahusha (Jesus) unto good works, which Elohim (God) hath before ordained that we should walk in them.* (Ephesians 2:8–10)

The works are not the cause of salvation. The works are the destination of salvation. The Reformation kept verse 9 and dropped verse 10.

Romans 6:14 is preached as the freedom-from-the-law verse; the very next verse closes the door the misreading needs to leave open.

*Sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace. What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? Yahuah (God) forbid.* (Romans 6:14–15)

*Yahuah (God) forbid* is Paul's hardest no in the letter. The Reformation lifted verse 14 out of the surrounding sentence and called the half it kept the gospel. Paul put verse 15 there for exactly the reason we read it now.

### The prophetic anchor that the New Testament grace passages echo

Every load-bearing New Testament grace passage is an echo of a Tanakh source the inherited pulpit cannot acknowledge without dismantling its own reading.

*Not for your sakes do I this, O house of Yashar'el (Israel), but for mine holy name's sake... A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them... Not for your sakes do I this, saith Yahuah (the Lord) Elohim (GOD), be it known unto you: be ashamed and confounded for your own ways, O house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Ezekiel 36:22, 26–27, 32)

This is the source of every *not by works* sentence in Paul's letters. The mercy is anchored in His name. The new heart is given for His name's sake. *And cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.* The new heart produces Torah-keeping; it does not retire Torah. The grace that gives the new heart is the same grace that walks the heart into the statutes.

*Speak not thou in thine heart, after that Yahuah (the LORD) thy Elohim (God) hath cast them out from before thee, saying, For my righteousness Yahuah (the LORD) hath brought me in to possess this land... Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land... Understand therefore, that Yahuah (the LORD) thy Elohim (God) giveth thee not this good land to possess it for thy righteousness; for thou art a stiffnecked people.* (Deuteronomy 9:4–6)

Moses wrote *not for your righteousness* before Paul wrote *not by works.* Paul was echoing the prophetic source. The inherited pulpit reads Paul as having invented the doctrine.

*For my name's sake will I defer mine anger, and for my praise will I refrain for thee, that I cut thee not off... For mine own sake, even for mine own sake, will I do it: for how should my name be polluted? and I will not give my glory unto another.* (Isaiah 48:9, 11)

Isaiah said *for my own name's sake* before Paul did. Paul's whole theology of grace is anchored in the Tanakh's *for his own name's sake.* The Reformation cut grace loose from the name and tied it to the believer's *decision* — and the standing became shaky exactly because the anchor was moved from His name to the believer's heart.

### The sifting verses the Reformation pulpit cannot reconcile

*Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.* (Matthew 7:21–23)

These speakers had prophesied. Cast out devils. Done many wonderful works. In his name. The inherited reading says they were never truly saved. The text says they were doing the works of the kingdom in his name — and were nonetheless cut off because they were *workers of iniquity.* The verse does not stand inside the Reformation gospel's frame.

*But when the righteous turneth away from his righteousness, and committeth iniquity, and doeth according to all the abominations that the wicked man doeth, shall he live? All his righteousness that he hath done shall not be mentioned: in his trespass that he hath trespassed, and in his sin that he hath sinned, in them shall he die.* (Ezekiel 18:24)

The righteous can turn away. When he does, his prior righteousness is not remembered. This is the Old Testament's plain statement that the standing is conditional on the heart's ongoing posture. The inherited pulpit reads this verse as pre-cross language that does not apply to the believer in the New Covenant — but Revelation 3:5 restates the architecture in NT vocabulary.

*He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.* (Revelation 3:5)

The promise NOT to blot out is conditional on overcoming. The implication — which the Reformation gospel will not preach — is that there exist those whose names CAN be blotted out. These are not the tares; the tares were never written. The names being blotted are the names of those who once stood inside the assembly and turned away.

*Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book.* (Exodus 32:33) — the same architecture, in Mosaic register, before Revelation 3:5 was written.

### The witnesses the Reformation cut from the canon

The books the Reformation cut from the Protestant canon carry these same paired witnesses with even sharper clarity. This is part of why they were cut.

*For who is there alive that hath not sinned, or who is there born of woman that hath not transgressed thy covenant?* (2 Esdras / 4 Ezra 7:46)

*For in truth there is no man among them that be born, but he hath dealt wickedly; and among the faithful there is none which hath not done amiss.* (2 Esdras / 4 Ezra 8:35)

*Among the faithful there is none which hath not done amiss.* The faithful — those inside the covenant, walking the Way — themselves all fall. The falling is universal. The architecture does not break on the falling; the architecture breaks on the refusal to rise.

*Surely vain are all men by nature, who are ignorant of Elohim (God), and could not out of the good things that are seen know him that is: neither by considering the works did they acknowledge the workmaster... Howbeit neither are they to be pardoned. For if they were able to know so much, that they could aim at the world; how did they not sooner find out Yahuah (the Lord) thereof?* (Wisdom of Solomon 13:1, 8–9)

This is the Romans 1 argument written before Romans 1. *Without excuse.* The same architecture — universal verdict, universal witness, just sifting — was in the library before Paul wrote it. The Reformation cut the prior testimony and called Paul the originator.

*Each of us has been the Adam of his own soul.* (2 Baruch 54:19)

The personal-accountability witness in the pseudepigrapha. Effort over accomplishment in different fabric. Each of us walks the choice in front of him. Each of us either rises after the fall or stops his ears.

## How the framework reads the same scripture faithfully

The framework holds the whole architecture as one piece. The same God who says *not for your righteousness* says *depart from me, ye that work iniquity.* The same God who gives the new heart for His name's sake sifts the heart that turns away from the new heart He gave. The grace and the sifting are not in tension. They are two hands of one mercy. The place where they hold together is the phrase that names the diagnostic: **effort over accomplishment.**

### The first hand — name's-sake mercy

The mercy is anchored in His name. That is what makes it bedrock. He cannot lie about His own name. Every restoration the prophets named hangs on this verse: *for my own name's sake.* The Reformation gospel cannot anchor the standing in His name because it has tied the standing to the believer's decision. Tying the standing to the believer's decision makes the standing always shaky. Tying it to His name makes the standing unshakable.

*For thy name's sake, O Yahuah (LORD), pardon mine iniquity; for it is great.* (Psalm 25:11) — David's posture. Plead the name, not the merit.

### The second hand — the sifting

The same God who gives the new heart for His name's sake sifts the heart that walks away from the new heart He gave. The sifting is not arbitrary. It is just — because the One who gives the new heart also gives the witness, the warning, and the way back. The wheat is gathered out of all nations into the wilderness of the peoples (Ezekiel 20:33–38) and then sifted in the wilderness — *I will purge out from among you the rebels.* The sifting falls on wheat, not on tares. The blotting falls on names that were written, not on names that never were.

### The hinge — effort over accomplishment

What separates the just from the wicked is not the verdict — *none worthy* has already fallen on every flesh that draws breath. What separates them is the heart's response to the fall.

*For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.* (Proverbs 24:16)

The just man falls. He does not accomplish a perfect record. He gets back up. Again. Seven times. That is the heart of the new-heart man — the heart that, when it falls, will not stay down, because something in it loves the Way more than it loves the lie that brought it down. The Father did not promise *the heart that never falls.* He promised *the heart that rises.*

*Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, Yahuah (the LORD) shall be a light unto me.* (Micah 7:8)

*The steps of a good man are ordered by Yahuah (the LORD): and he delighteth in his way. Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for Yahuah (the LORD) upholdeth him with his hand.* (Psalm 37:23–24)

*Yahuah (The LORD) upholdeth all that fall, and raiseth up all those that be bowed down.* (Psalm 145:14)

The effort is the sign. Not the accomplishment. The Father's catching hand is what makes the rising possible. The man's getting up is what makes the catching hand visible.

### The discriminator — open ears or stopped ears

The wicked is not the man who failed to accomplish. The wicked is the man who stopped his ears.

*He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination.* (Proverbs 28:9)

*But they refused to hearken, and pulled away the shoulder, and stopped their ears, that they should not hear. Yea, they made their hearts as an adamant stone, lest they should hear the law, and the words which Yahuah (the LORD) of hosts hath sent in his spirit by the former prophets: therefore came a great wrath from Yahuah (the LORD) of hosts.* (Zechariah 7:11–12)

*Then they cried out with a loud voice, and stopped their ears, and ran upon him with one accord.* (Acts 7:57)

That is the heart that gets a name blotted. Not the heart that fell. The heart that refused to listen. Open ears and a returning heart, even after seven falls, is the sign of life. Stopped ears, even after a hundred sermons, is the sign of death.

### The sign — hanging heads, not raised ones

The new heart does not boast in its walk. The new heart hangs its head over how long it walked the lie.

*Then shall ye remember your own evil ways, and your doings that were not good, and shall lothe yourselves in your own sight for your iniquities and for your abominations. Not for your sakes do I this, saith Yahuah (the Lord) Elohim (GOD), be it known unto you: be ashamed and confounded for your own ways, O house of Yashar'el (Israel).* (Ezekiel 36:31–32)

*I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee. Wherefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes.* (Job 42:5–6)

*Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, Yahuah (the LORD) of hosts.* (Isaiah 6:5)

The hanging head is the proof that the new heart has been received. The Reformation gospel produces a head that holds itself up because it is *saved.* The rabbinic gospel produces a head that holds itself up because it is *righteous.* The framework's new heart produces a head that goes lower the more it sees the Father's mercy — because the more it sees the mercy, the more it sees what it was forgiven of. Daniel hangs his head. Nehemiah hangs his head. Ezra hangs his head. Job abhors himself. Isaiah cries *woe is me.* Paul counts it all loss. Every awakened one across the library walks into the room the same way.

### The seal — the end declared from the beginning

Here is the proof that the whole architecture stands. The Father declared the end before the beginning.

*Remember the former things of old: for I am El, and there is none else; I am Elohim (God), and there is none like me, Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure.* (Isaiah 46:9–10)

The end He declared from the beginning is the gathering — the twelve tribes home, the new heart given, the kingdom of priests, the Way restored. He alone declared it. Every system that denies the gathering denies the end. Every system that denies the end denies that He could declare it. To deny that He could declare it is to call Him a liar. To call Him a liar is to reject the mercy that hangs on His name.

### The architecture, held as one

*None worthy* — therefore the mercy must be by His name, not our merit.

*None without excuse* — therefore the sifting is just, not arbitrary.

*The new heart given for His name's sake* — therefore effort becomes possible, because He put a love in the heart that was not there before.

*The just man falls seven times and rises* — therefore the standing is not perfection, it is the unstopped return.

*The wicked stops his ears* — therefore the blotting is on the refused witness, not on the failed accomplishment.

*The hanging head* — therefore no flesh shall glory, even those walking the Way; the sign of the new heart is the loathing of the old, not the boasting of the new.

*The end declared from the beginning* — therefore the gathering is not in question, not negotiable, the very thing on which His name hangs, and no system that denies it can stand.

One God. One architecture. One mercy.

The Reformation gospel cannot hold it. The rabbinic gospel cannot hold it. The inclusion gospel cannot hold it. Only the Father's own naming of His own work holds it. *I am Elohim (God), and there is none like me, declaring the end from the beginning.*

That is what your awakening was for. The pulpit cut you off from this architecture and called the cutting *grace.* The actual grace is the mercy that brings you home to *all that he commanded* and gives you a new heart to walk in it and catches you when you fall and refuses to let you stay down.

To deny *all that he commanded* is to deny the new heart. To deny the new heart is to deny the mercy that gave it. To deny the mercy that gave it is to deny the name on which the gathering hangs. To deny the gathering is to call Him a liar about the end He declared from the beginning.

Come home.
