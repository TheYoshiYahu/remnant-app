Abram did not have the written Torah. He did not have a prophet standing over him with an open scroll. He had the heavens. And by looking at the heavens, he worked out that whatever made the sun and the moon was not the sun or the moon themselves. The sun sets. The moon waxes and wanes. The Maker of the sun does not set. Paul will later make exactly this argument in Romans 1:20 — “the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made.” Paul was not inventing that argument. He was quoting the experience of Abraham. This is how the father of the faithful came to faith. He watched the heavens and refused to worship anything less than what had made them. This is a rebuke, both to the atheist who stares at creation and sees nothing, and to the pagan who stares at creation and worships the thing instead of the Maker. Abram represents the third option — the awakened man who sees through creation to the One who made it. This is why we are told in Genesis 26:5 that Abraham “obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws.” Nobody gave him a copy of the Torah. He received the Torah the same way creation itself received it — built in, before Sinai, before Moses, before even the patriarchs were named. Torah is older than the stars. The stars keep it. The people of Babel did not simply want to build a monument. They divided themselves into three divisions of explicit hostility: one group to ascend to heaven and fight YAHUAH; one group to ascend and put their own gods there; one group to ascend and shoot YAHUAH with arrows. This is not architecture. This is an organized assault on the throne of heaven by created men who had lost their fear. The tower is a weapon. The city is a staging ground. The builders are a militia. This is the blueprint of every anti-YAHUAH system that has ever arisen. Jasher tells us what YAHUAH did: He turned their arrows back onto themselves (verse 29), He confused their tongues so they could not coordinate (verse 32), and He scattered them across the earth (verse 35). The anti-YAHUAH coalition fails every time, and every time the fracture is linguistic. The lesson is: the moment an anti-YAHUAH coalition achieves a unified tongue, YAHUAH splits the tongue. The scattered seed is still, in a sense, living inside the territories of those fallen delegates. The final gathering includes the judgment of those gods. Slow down at this verse. This is the taxonomy of anti-covenant rebellion, and Daniel 7's four beasts are that taxonomy re-deployed in four successive imperial forms. Read Jasher 9 carefully enough and the whole prophetic arc from Babel to Revelation unfolds in its right order. Jasher says the first faction of Babel wanted to ascend to heaven and fight Yahuah (God). The second faction wanted to ascend to heaven, place their own gods there, and serve them. The third faction wanted to ascend to heaven and shoot Yahuah (God) with bows and spears. Three strategies, one enemy, one assault. Label them plainly: fight Him, replace Him, shoot Him. Every anti-covenant kingdom from Babel forward has operated on some combination of these three strategies, and the Daniel 7 vision presents them in four historical costumes over the course of the Times of the Gentiles. Daniel 7 gives us four beasts — a lion with eagle's wings, a bear with three ribs in its mouth, a four-headed leopard, and a fourth dreadful beast with iron teeth and ten horns. Daniel 2 gives us the same empires in a different image — a gold head, a silver chest and arms, a bronze belly and thighs, iron legs, and iron-mixed-with-clay feet. The historical identifications are well established across both Jewish and Christian traditions: Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome. What is less often named is that each of these four is a specific costume of the one Nimrodic spirit, deploying a specific one of Babel's three strategies as its primary weapon. "After this I saw in the night visions, and behold a fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly; and it had great iron teeth: it devoured and brake in pieces, and stamped the residue with the feet of it: and it was diverse from all the beasts that were before it; and it had ten horns" (Daniel 7:7). The fourth beast is the composite, the distillation, the summary — the beast from which the ten horns will finally crown the Antichrist-figure of Revelation 13. But the lineage runs back through the three before it, and through each of those to Babel. Babylon under Nebuchadnezzar worked the replace-Him strategy. When Nebuchadnezzar conquered Yahudah (Judah), he carried the vessels of the temple into the house of his gods (Daniel 1:2) — not to destroy them but to absorb them into his own pantheon. He took the sons of the royal line and enrolled them in his own wisdom school (Daniel 1:3–5). His project was to assimilate, to translate, to absorb. Daniel 3's golden image is the whole thing compressed into one monument — a statue before which every nation, every tongue, every people would bow. Replace-Him. Put our own gods on the throne of heaven and worship them. Babel's second faction rebuilt in Babylonian iconography. Medo-Persia, the silver chest and bear, worked the legislate-around-Him strategy. Darius did not denounce the Elohim (God) of Daniel; he let himself be manipulated by his satraps into signing a decree that for thirty days no petition could be made to any Elohim (God) or man except the king (Daniel 6:7). The rebellion was administrative. The system of law itself was bent into a tool that excluded the Most High from the territory of daily petition. Daniel prayed anyway. The lion's mouth was shut. The decree failed. But the strategy is preserved in the record — administrative law as the anti-covenant weapon. Every regime since that has outlawed prayer in schools, crosses on public buildings, Sabbath observance in workplaces, and Torah in the common square is operating on the Medo-Persian playbook. Legislate-around-Him. This is fight-Him in its bureaucratic form — Babel's first faction wearing the robes of a lawful court. Greece, the bronze belly and leopard, worked the replace-Him strategy with philosophical precision. Where Babylon was crude — a statue on a plain — Greece was sophisticated. Hellenism did not ban the Most High. Hellenism subsumed Him. The Septuagint became a battleground. The gymnasium came to Jerusalem (1 Maccabees 1:14). The high priesthood was sold. The temple was rededicated to Olympian Zeus by Antiochus IV Epiphanes in 167 BCE. The Greek project was not to destroy the covenant seed but to Hellenize it, to translate it, to reinterpret every word of Tanakh through a philosophical grid until the covenant itself had been re-voiced in the grammar of the Academy. Replace-Him with Plato. Replace-Him with Aristotle. Replace-Him with a philosophical abstraction that has the same syllables but none of the same weight. Nicaea and Chalcedon and the whole creedal apparatus are downstream of Greek grammar imposed on a Hebrew covenant. Greek was the replace-Him language of Babel's second faction, refined over centuries into a system so elegant that centuries of believers have mistaken it for the faith itself. Rome, the iron legs and dreadful ten-horned beast, worked the shoot-Him strategy. Rome did not translate the covenant or absorb the gods. Rome fed the saints to lions. Rome crucified. Rome beheaded. Rome built the Colosseum on the model of the Circus Maximus and turned public entertainment into a devotional act of blood against the seed of promise. The seventy years of persecution after Nero, the intermittent purges under Decius and Valerian and Diocletian, the ritualized executions, the demand for the pinch of incense before the emperor's image — Rome's project was to make the confession of the Name itself illegal. Arrows and spears against heaven. Babel's third faction in legionary armor. Each beast worked primarily one strategy while drawing on the other two at need. Each beast had its season. Each beast fell to the next. And the fourth beast — "diverse from all the beasts that were before it" (Daniel 7:7) — did not simply represent historical Rome. It pointed forward to the composite end-beast of Revelation 13, in which all three strategies converge in one final regime. "And the beast which I saw was like unto a leopard, and his feet were as the feet of a bear, and his mouth as the mouth of a lion" (Revelation 13:2). Greek leopard, Medo-Persian bear, Babylonian lion — and the iron teeth of Rome holding them together. Every strategy Babel invented is deployed simultaneously at the end. Replace-Him through a global religious synthesis. Legislate-around-Him through a mark of the beast that governs commerce and access. Shoot-Him through the martyrdom of every saint who will not take the mark. The end-beast is Babel rebuilt with every strategy fully weaponized. Notice what Yahuah (God) did at Babel and what He does at every recurrence. "Come, let us descend and confuse their tongues, that one man shall not understand the language of his neighbor" (Jasher 9:32; compare Genesis 11:7). The fracture mechanism is linguistic. Medo-Persia fell when its legal-administrative lingua franca cracked under the weight of its own decrees. Greece fell when Hellenization's philosophical tongue could no longer bind an empire Alexander's successors had carved into four. Rome fell when Latin could no longer enforce a unified Christendom, and what scattered it into the vernaculars was, providentially, the translation of the Word into the languages of the people. Every Babel ends the same way. Every coalition against the Most High finds its tongue confused at the moment the tower is nearly complete. This pattern means something for the awakening reader of this generation. Every attempt at a unified global consciousness — whether by empire or by economy or by technology or by religion or by a technological neural integration that promises to unify human cognition — is operating on Babel's blueprint. Every attempt to rebuild the tower ends at the fracture of the tongue. The present age is tower-building at scale. The Father's answer will be the same answer He has given since Jasher 9. He will not be mocked (Galatians 6:7). The confusion of the tongue is sovereign. What man unifies in rebellion, the Father scatters in mercy — because a unified Babel is a Babel that can complete the tower, and the Father has never let the tower be completed. And none of this is grounds for despair. The Father has never been frightened by any Babel. He descended on Hermon when the Watchers swore. He descended on Babel when the tower rose. He descended in the Messiah (Christ) at Bethlehem when the ancient promise had been delayed four thousand years. The beast is the last iteration of a pattern the Father has always known how to fracture. The awakening remnant is not preparing for a battle Yahuah (God) might lose. The remnant is preparing to witness the fall of the fourth beast exactly as it witnessed the fall of the first.
