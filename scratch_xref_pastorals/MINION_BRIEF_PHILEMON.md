## THE GOVERNING FRAME (read before you author)

Paul writes from prison to Philemon, a brother whose house hosts an assembly, pleading for **Onesimus**
— Philemon's runaway servant, now Paul's son in the faith — to be received back *not now as a servant,
but above a servant, a brother beloved* (1:16). The load-bearing weight of this letter is **reconciliation
and the receiving of a brother**:

- **★ Philemon 15–17 — received no longer as a servant but as a brother beloved.** *For perhaps he
  therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever; Not now as a servant, but
  above a servant, a brother beloved... If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself*
  (1:15–17). Read through the framework: this is the **Yoseph (Joseph) pattern** — the brother sold/lost
  who is received back, the evil meant against him turned to good by Elohim's providence: *ye thought
  evil against me; but Elohim (God) meant it unto good* (Genesis 50:20), *Now therefore be not grieved...
  for Elohim (God) did send me before you to preserve life* (Genesis 45:5). *He therefore departed for a
  season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever* is the same providence — the parting turned to a
  greater reunion, the servant received as a brother. Weave the brotherhood/love-thy-brother root
  (Leviticus 19:17–18 *thou shalt not hate thy brother... thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself*) and
  the kinsman-restored frame.
- **★ Philemon 18–19 — I will repay (suretyship / the debt assumed).** *If he hath wronged thee, or
  oweth thee ought, put that on mine account; I... will repay it* (1:18–19). Read through the framework:
  this is the **surety** of the Tanakh — Yahudah (Judah) becoming surety for Binyamin (Benjamin): *I will
  be surety for him; of my hand shalt thou require him* (Genesis 43:9), *thy servant became surety for the
  lad* (Genesis 44:32) — and the type of the redeemer who assumes the debt of another. Weigh honestly
  (Proverbs on suretyship is a CAUTION, a different sense — do not force it).
- **No replacement theology. No law-vs-grace antithesis.** Brotherhood, reconciliation, the receiving of
  the lost one, the assumed debt — all belong to the one household, the one olive tree.
