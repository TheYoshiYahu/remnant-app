## What the text actually is

The **Alphabet of Ben Sira** (*Alpha-Beta de-Ben Sira* / "Aleph Beyt Siram" in the
manifest's Cepher Shamayim row) is an **anonymous medieval work**, composed in the
Islamic world roughly **700–1000 CE** — centuries after the Talmud. It is **not** the
Wisdom of Ben Sira / Sirach / Ecclesiasticus, which is a different book and is already
in the corpus (the KJV apocrypha edition and `apocrypha-charles-vol1/sirach.md`). It
only borrows the name.

Its structure: a frame-narrative birth-and-childhood legend of "Ben Sira," then two
acrostic proverb-lists (22 in Aramaic, 22 in Hebrew), each proverb carrying an aggadic
fable. The fables are framed as 22 stories Ben Sira tells at the court of Nebuchadnezzar.
Adolf Neubauer and Abraham Epstein read the work as outright **satire/parody**; Louis
Ginzberg disputed the "satire" label — but no one in the text's own tradition treats it
as a devotional or revelatory work, and it was historically condemned within Judaism as
obscene.

## The screen — five concerns through the voice lens

**1. It is not a covenant witness — it is a different category of text.** Everything
else in the extras tier — Enoch, Jubilees, the Adam-Eve books, the Apocalypse of
Abraham, the Ascension of Isaiah — presents itself, whatever its friction points, as a
witness to covenant history or revelation. The Alphabet of Ben Sira is a medieval
frame-tale anthology that makes no such claim. This is a category difference from the
rest of the shelf, not a degree-of-friction difference.

**2. The birth narrative degrades a prophet of Yahuah (God).** The frame opens by making
the prophet **Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah)** the unwitting father — and, through his own
daughter, simultaneously the grandfather — of Ben Sira, by way of a crude bathhouse
conception scenario. **Jeremiah 16:19** — *our fathers have inherited lies* — is one of
the named anchor scriptures of this entire series (Key Scriptures, `framework-deep.md`).
A study Bible built under this voice cannot carry, as part of its corpus, a text whose
opening premise is a parody-conception pinned on the very prophet whose confession
anchors the framework. This is not a "read with eyes open" friction point (the Ascension
of Isaiah standard) — it is a degradation at the level of the text's premise.

**3. The Lilith narrative is a rival origin myth that contradicts the framework's reading
of Adam.** This is the text's most famous passage and the reason Cepher and others reach
for it. The voice skill's Adam framework is specific and load-bearing: Genesis 1 created
the peoples of the earth; Genesis 2 formed Adam separately; and *instead of taking Adam a
wife from the nations, Yahuah (God) made him a woman from his own body* (`SKILL.md`,
Three Categories #3; `framework-deep.md`, "Adam, the Sons of Adam, and the Tares"). The
Alphabet's Lilith story asserts the opposite — that Adam had a *first* wife, Lilith,
formed from the earth as he was, co-equal, who rebelled and became a child-killing demon,
with an amulet practice attached. That is a competing account of the formation of Adam's
woman, cutting against the exact distinction the framework rests on. And it is precisely
the part of the text a reader would come to it for.

**4. The contrary-to-Torah test (1 John 2:3–4 / the 1234 of Truth).** Unlike the Gospel
of Mary, the Alphabet does not preach a flesh-credential gospel or an explicit anti-Torah
line — it is not Gnostic doctrine, and it does not fail the master filter the *same way*
the Gospel of Mary did. But the filter certifies *teaching*, and the Alphabet's teaching
apparatus — its proverbs and fables — is in many places cynical, bawdy, or scatological
inversion rather than covenant instruction. It does not edify toward the commandments; in
places it mocks. It fails the filter not by one heretical sentence but by not being the
kind of text the filter exists to certify — while smuggling in the Lilith origin myth,
which *does* cut against Torah's Genesis account.

**5. Tone and fitness for a reader-facing study-Bible surface.** Scatological humor,
sexual crudeness, and misogynistic fable material run through the 22 stories
structurally, not incidentally. Setting theology entirely aside, this is not material
that belongs on a study-Bible surface under a ministry voice.

## A caution on the Cepher catalog entry

The manifest puts this book on our list solely on the strength of Cepher's 87-book TOC
("Aleph Beyt Siram"). The Gospel of Mary entry turned out to be a Cepher **cataloguing
error**. The voice skill is explicit that Cepher is *data, not authority*. Before this
book is ever sourced, the Cepher entry itself should be verified — that Cepher's TOC
carries the medieval bawdy *Alphabet of Ben Sira* and not a mislabel of the canonical
Wisdom of Sirach (already in the corpus) or some other acrostic "aleph-bet" text. If the
decision is "drop," this caution is moot; if Yoshi leans "keep," it should be resolved
first.

## Source-availability picture (context — only live if Yoshi leans keep)

- The standard English translation — **Norman Bronznick**, in Stern & Mirsky,
  *Rabbinic Fantasies* (Yale Judaica Series, 1990 / 1998 reprint) — is **under
  copyright**. Not republishable.
- **No clean, complete public-domain English translation is confirmed to exist.** The
  19th-century editions (Steinschneider, 1854 / 1858; Eisenstein, *Otzar Midrashim*,
  1915) are **Hebrew**, not English.
- A possible — **unverified** — public-domain English path: Charles Taylor, "The
  Alphabet of Ben Sira," *Journal of Philology* 30 (1907), 95–132. Whether Taylor
  printed a full continuous English translation or only scholarly notes plus excerpts
  is not confirmed. The JQR 17 (1904/05) 238–239 item is a two-page note, not a
  translation.
- Net: the source is **genuinely unresolved**. Even on a "keep," sourcing would mean
  either confirming/extracting a PD English translation that may not exist as a complete
  text, or translating from 19th-century PD Hebrew in-pipeline — a substantially larger
  job than any prior extras book.

## Recommendation

**Lean DROP — retire the "Aleph Beyt Siram" row the way the Gospel of Mary row was
retired.** The reasoning is the four screen concerns above: it is a medieval satirical
frame-tale rather than a covenant witness; its premise degrades the prophet Jeremiah;
its signature passage (Lilith) is a rival origin myth contradicting the framework's Adam
reading; and its body content is crude and unfit for the surface. The unresolved source
is a *secondary* reason — but it means even a "keep" does not land this wheel; it only
converts the block from "screen + source" to "source," with real risk that no clean PD
English exists.

This is Yoshi's call. The decision options:

- **A — Drop from the manifest.** Retire the row; record it the way the Gospel of Mary
  drop was recorded. Closes the Chrome-wheel's third book as "screened out, not sourced,"
  and the three-book Chrome wheel is then complete (two landed, one screened out).
- **B — Keep, and pursue sourcing.** Accept the text into the manifest despite the
  screen concerns and open a sourcing sub-wheel — first verify the Cepher catalog entry,
  then chase the Taylor 1907 PD path or decide on in-pipeline translation from PD Hebrew.
- **C — Keep a narrowed form.** Carry only the proverb-lists, not the bawdy frame-
  narrative or the Lilith fable. Named for completeness, but flagged with a concern:
  this is a heavy editorial intervention, it still needs a PD source, and a proverb-list
  stripped of its frame is arguably no longer "the Alphabet of Ben Sira."
- **D — Hold / defer.** Leave the row parked and undecided; move the next wheel
  elsewhere.

No manifest edit, no sourcing, and no parser work happens until Yoshi rules.
