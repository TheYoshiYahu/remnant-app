18. over the multitude... i.e. the prostrate congregation.

19. And His ordinances... The Hebrew word 051, which often has the sense of ‘judgement’, is also used as
a technical term expressive of something that is due to somebody ; thus in Deut. xviii. 3, ‘this shall be the priest's due
from the people ...’, cp. Deut. xxi. 17; in Num. xv. 24 reference is made to the offerings to the Lord ‘in accordance
with what is due’, or, as the R. V. renders it, ‘according to the ordinance’ (WBWD2), cp. Job xxxvi. 6, Prov. xviii. 5.

20. Then he descended. According to Lev. ix. 22, Aaron does not descend from the altar until after he has
pronounced the Blessing, and Voma vi. 2 seems to agree with this.

And the blessing of the Lord... Cf. Voma vi. 2, ‘And the priests and the people who are standing in the
court (ANY), when they hear the “ Ineffable Name” (WADA DY) proceeding out of the mouth of the High-Priest, then
they bow themselves down and worship, falling down upon their faces, and say: ‘“‘ Blessed be the Name of the glory of
His Kingdom for ever and ever.”’ This was the only occasion on which the Holy Name was pronounced. As is well
known, in the Hebrew text of the O. T. the name 717 (‘Jehovah’= Jahveh) is written with the vowels of Adonaz
( Lord’), and pronounced so ; if the title 4donaz precedes 717 (i.e. if the text has ‘the Lord Jahveh’) it is written
with the vowels of Elohim (‘God’). This was done in order to avoid pronouncing the Holy Name. In the Hebrew

of Sirach, Jahveh is always written ™* or ‘. ‘This non-uttering of the Holy Name was not always due to reve-
rential fear, but often also because of superstitious dread. ‘The names of supernatural beings, such as gods and
spirits, are commonly believed to be endowed with marvellous virtues, and the mere utterance of them may work
wonders and disturb the course of nature’ (Frazer, The Golden Bough’, i, p. 441). This must certainly have applied
at one time to the mass of the uncultured among the Israelites. There is a curious passage in Pirge Aboth v. 14,
where it is said that ‘Noisome beasts come into the world for vain swearing, and for the profanation of the
(divine) Name’. ;
