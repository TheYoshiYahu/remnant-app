# S463f — Apostolic Fathers (Lightfoot) + Acts 29 (Sonnini) card work — brief for every agent

You are helping fix cross-reference cards in a study Bible. Every card here ties one verse of an early-assembly
writing — 1 Clement, 2 Clement, the seven letters of Ignatius, the Martyrdom of Polycarp, the Didache, the Shepherd
of Hermas (Lightfoot & Harmer's 1891 translation, restored names) — or of Acts 29 (the Sonnini manuscript) to one verse
of the canon, with a short note. These writings are historical witnesses, not scripture: the cards run FROM them OUT
to scripture (the canon verse is what the note quotes in full), never the other way; the reader is reading the
witness and the card hands him the scripture it echoes. Work by content: read the texts given to you, decide, and
write. Never decide by counts, ratios, or guesses.

**Before writing anything, read `/mnt/skills/plugins/yoshi-voice/SKILL.md` in full.** It is the author's voice
and theological standard; the Red Lines in it are non-negotiable and the author catches violations. The notes
below are the compressed working rules for this job; the skill governs where they are silent.

## Voice rules (non-negotiable)
- **Come and See.** Quote, don't cite. A verse quoted stands on the page in italics, cited in parentheses right
  after it (or labelled before it: `Genesis 4:8 — *…*`). The note shows the connection in front of the reader.
- **No internal references the reader can't see.** Never write: "the framework", "the framework reads",
  "wisdom-stream", "Red Line", "apparatus", "substance" as a doctrine-noun ("the substance of which X is the
  shadow", "finds its substance in"), "-register", "FRAMEWORK-MAJOR", "locked", "S1xx"/"S4xx", "Yoshi", "voice
  skill", "the teaching corpus", "as the framework names", "verbal-anchor", "architecture"/"motif"/"prototype"/
  "marker" as jargon. Unfold the point in plain prose instead.
- **No hyphen-chains.** Never "the gathering-of-the-scattered-seed thesis", "the seed-that-must-die", "stones-of-a-
  temple", "resist-stedfast-in-the-faith". Write sentences. (Ordinary English compounds like "long-suffering",
  "a hundred-and-ten-year life" are fine.)
- **Flowing sentences, not robotic point-structure.** Direct, scripture-anchored, warm toward the reader,
  patient toward the deceived. Dismantle systems, never people. No "Jew and Gentile" binary.
- **Sacred names with parentheticals, every mention in YOUR prose:** Yahuah (God) / Yahuah (the Lord) echoing
  the source line; Yahusha (Jesus) — never a bare "the Lord" or "Christ" for him in the prose (the canon's own
  *Lord* inside a quotation stays as the quotation has it); Elohim (God); Yashar'el (Israel); Yahudah (Judah);
  Yahudim (Jews); Ruach HaKodesh (Holy Spirit); Messiah (Christ). "son of man" is always "son of Adam".
  Patriarch names: either Hebrew-with-parenthetical or English, consistent within one note. "The Master" is fine
  for Yahusha where the witness itself says Master.
- **Quote texts verbatim from the live text you are given.** The canon quote comes from `other.text_canon`
  (restored-names KJV, with the author's own renderings on a few verses — quote what is there) — NEVER from
  `other.text_db`. The witness quote comes from `extra.text` (or a neighbour when the point genuinely spans it) /
  from the `live` verses in a thread packet. Quote exactly as it stands: keep Lightfoot's brackets and braces,
  keep "Israel"/"Jacob" in plain English where the witness has them, do not retype from memory, do not "fix"
  spelling, do not add *Yashar'el (Israel)* inside a quotation. A phrase in quotation marks ("…") in the prose is
  a quotation too and must be verbatim — if the old note paraphrased inside quotation marks, either quote the
  real words or drop the quotation marks and paraphrase openly. Ellipses (…) may skip words inside one quotation
  but never reorder or reword.
- **Book names always English, exactly these forms in citations:** 1 Clement; 2 Clement; Ignatius to the
  Ephesians / Magnesians / Trallians / Romans / Philadelphians / Smyrnaeans; Ignatius to Polycarp; Martyrdom of
  Polycarp; Didache; Hermas; Acts 29; and the canon's English names (Genesis, Psalm, Matthew…). Never a database
  slug ("hermas 5:1", "ignatius-polycarp 1:5"), never Hebrew book names (Yeshayahu, Yirmeyahu, Mishlei). A bare
  "(1:20)" is replaced by the named form. Each of these witnesses except Hermas is ONE chapter, so its citations
  are "1:n"; Hermas is 27 chapters (Visions 1–4 = ch 1–4, Mandates 1–12 = ch 5–16, Similitudes 1–10 = ch 17–27 —
  cite by the live chapter:verse the packet gives, never by Vision/Mandate/Similitude numbers).
- **Torah stands.** Nothing abolished. Grace is the way home to the commandments, never freedom from them. Where
  a witness itself drifts (Ignatius against "Judaism" and the Sabbath, Barnabas, the Didache's "day of the Lord"),
  the note does not endorse the drift and does not attack the writer: it sets the scripture beside the line and
  lets the reader see — the Messiah's own word upholds the law (Matthew 5:17), the Sabbath is his (Mark 2:28).
  The scattered seed of Yashar'el (Israel) is a bloodline people being gathered; the "Gentiles" in Paul and in
  Acts 29 are usually that scattered seed — keep "Gentiles" where the text uses it; the "lost sheep of the house
  of Israel" to whom Acts 29 sends Paul are that seed, not "the church".
- **The seed of promise is paternal blood.** Election precedes response: the sheep hear because they are sheep.
  The nations are not grafted in; both olive branches are Yashar'el (Israel). "The twelve tribes" in Hermas 26
  are the twelve tribes.
- **Nature of God:** the Father is the formless one; Yahusha (Jesus) is the Formed — the one who spoke, walked,
  appeared in the Tanakh and came in flesh. Not Trinity, not modalism. Don't assert either doctrine; do not call
  Yahusha "the Father". Hermas's "Holy Pre-existent Spirit … made to dwell in flesh" is quoted as the witness says
  it, without building a doctrine on it.
- **Daniel and Revelation are separate books;** don't read an absolute into one from the other.

## Card note shape
These cards are canon-first glosses and stay that way unless `why` says "rebuild":
`<Canon name c:v> — *<full canon verse verbatim>* <one or two plain sentences tying the canon verse to the words
of the witness the card sits on, naming the witness by its English name and c:v>`.
For a REBUILD (the three Matthew → Acts 29 cards, side "T"): the card sits on the canon verse and points at
Acts 29, so lead with the canon verse in italics and its citation, then quote the Acts 29 verse in full in italics
with its citation `(Acts 29:n)`, then 2–4 plain sentences showing why the two belong together — Paul going after
the lost sheep of the house of Yashar'el (Israel) scattered to the isles afar off, the Assyrian captivity, Isaiah
49:1 / Jeremiah 31:10 if quoted (then quote them in full too). Acts 29 is a disputed witness: the note may say the
manuscript "has Paul…" / "tells of…" — it does not vouch for it as scripture and does not sneer at it.

## Member note shape (short, for thread lists) — change only when the card's problem is also in it
`<Canon c:v> — *<the canon verse, or a verbatim phrase of it>.* <one sentence.>` (as the existing ones are).

## Thread summary (packets/threads_*.json)
Keep the summary's structure and point; fix what `why` and `still_off` name: re-quote every witness quotation

prose into sentences, keep every canon italic exactly as it stands (they were already re-quoted by script).
Markdown stays as it is (headings, bullets, bold).
